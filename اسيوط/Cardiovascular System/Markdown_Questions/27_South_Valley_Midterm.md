### Q3: Cardiac muscle differ from skeletal muscle as

- **A)** Cardiac muscle has well developed sarcoplasmic reticulum
- **B)** Cardiac muscle has a shorter action potential
- **C)** Cardiac muscle contraction depend on extracellular Ca
- **D)** Cardiac muscle can be tetanized

**Correct Answer:** C
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Unlike skeletal muscle, cardiac muscle contraction relies on Ca2+ influx from the extracellular fluid (calcium-induced calcium release) in addition to SR stores; its SR is less developed, its action potential is much longer, and its long refractory period prevents tetanization
**Tag:** External, South Valley

### Q4: All or non-rule

- **A)** Is part of extrinsic regulation of cardiac contractility
- **B)** Apply only for atrial muscle
- **C)** It describe relation between EDV and contraction
- **D)** It describe relation Strength of stimulus and power of contraction

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** The all-or-none law states that once threshold is reached, a muscle fiber (or the whole heart, functionally a syncytium) contracts maximally regardless of further increase in stimulus strength — describing the relation between stimulus strength and contraction, not EDV (that is Starling's law) or extrinsic regulation
**Tag:** External, South Valley

### Q5: Regarding Frank starling relationship

- **C)** It depend on Autonomic nervous system

**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** The Frank-Starling (heterometric) mechanism states that the force of ventricular contraction increases in direct proportion to end-diastolic volume (fiber stretch), independent of autonomic/Ca2+-mediated homometric regulation

### Q13: Which of the following is an example of passive ectopic

### Q22: About heart rate:

- **A)** The heart rate is directly proportional to the arterial blood pressure
- **B)** The heart rate is directly proportional to the atrial blood pressure
- **C)** The heart rate is indirectly proportional to high arterial CO₂ pressure
- **D)** The heart rate is indirectly proportional to skeletal muscle hypoxia

**Correct Answer:** B
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Increased atrial (venous) filling/pressure stretches the atrial wall and reflexly raises heart rate via the Bainbridge reflex — a direct relationship; rising arterial BP instead reflexly slows the heart via baroreceptors (inverse relation, as annotated in the source), and both high CO₂ and muscle hypoxia reflexly increase, not decrease, heart rate
**Tag:** External, South Valley

### Q23: Venous return increase in case of:

- **A)** Arterial vasoconstriction
- **B)** Venous vasoconstriction
- **C)** Arterial vasodilatation
- **D)** Venous vasodilation

**Correct Answer:** C
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Arterial vasodilatation lowers resistance and increases blood flow through the tissues into the venous system, increasing venous return; venous vasoconstriction (not dilation) also raises venous return by mobilizing reservoir blood, while venous vasodilation pools blood and reduces venous return
**Tag:** External, South Valley
