### Q1: Question I — Pharmacology. Answer the following: (1) Enumerate two central side effects of levodopa with long-term therapy. (2) List two drugs used in treatment of grand mal epilepsy. (3) List one antagonist of acute diazepam poisoning and one antagonist of acute morphine poisoning. (4) List four neurological adverse effects of antipsychotic drugs

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** (1) Levodopa: abnormal involuntary movements (dyskinesias); behavioral disturbances with hallucinations and confusion; the on-off phenomenon; and abrupt withdrawal may cause a malignant neuroleptic-like syndrome with hyperthermia and muscle rigidity. Measures for the on-off phenomenon include IV infusion, sustained-release formulation, equal doses every two hours, addition of other drugs, and a drug holiday. (2) Acceptable antiepileptic drugs include phenytoin, carbamazepine, valproic acid, phenobarbital, primidone, lamotrigine, topiramate, oxcarbazepine, levetiracetam, and zonisamide; any two are accepted. (3) Flumazenil antagonizes acute diazepam poisoning; naloxone antagonizes acute morphine poisoning. (4) Acute dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, tardive dyskinesia, and perioral tremors; any four are accepted

### Q2: Question II — Anatomy. Answer the following nine items: action of genioglossus; motor nerve of sternocleidomastoid; the sinus receiving the superior sagittal sinus; the skin area supplied by cutaneous branches of one spinal nerve; the thalamic termination of the trigeminal lemniscus; one cranial nerve nucleus in the medulla; the facial terminal branch of the maxillary nerve; the sinus occupying the posterior fixed margin of the falx cerebelli; and the pharyngeal arch of the anterior two-thirds of the tongue

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Genioglossus protrudes the tongue. Sternocleidomastoid is supplied by the spinal accessory nerve. The superior sagittal sinus drains into the right transverse sinus. The skin area supplied by cutaneous branches of an individual spinal nerve is a dermatome. The trigeminal lemniscus ends in the ventral posteromedial (VPM) nucleus of the thalamus. Acceptable medullary nuclei include the spinal trigeminal nucleus and tract, solitary nucleus, nucleus ambiguus, dorsal motor nucleus of vagus, and hypoglossal nucleus. The maxillary nerve terminates as the infraorbital nerve in the face. The occipital sinus occupies the posterior fixed margin of the falx cerebelli. The anterior two-thirds of the tongue develops from the first pharyngeal arch

### Q3: Question III — Anatomy. Answer the following: (1) Mention one communication of the middle ear. (2) Define projection fibers and give one example. (3) Name the main artery supplying the leg and foot areas of motor and sensory cortices. (4) Name the artery supplying the middle cerebellar peduncle. (5) Write the scientific names corresponding to the two congenital anomaly descriptions in the source. (6) Complete the two statements about the secondary olfactory cortex and optic chiasm

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** (1) The middle ear communicates anteriorly with the nasopharynx through the auditory tube, or posteriorly with mastoid air cells through the mastoid antrum; either is accepted. (2) Projection fibers are afferent and efferent nerve fibers passing to and from the cerebral cortex. Examples include the internal capsule, corona radiata, sensory radiation, optic radiation, and auditory radiation; any one is accepted. (3) Anterior cerebral artery. (4) Anterior inferior cerebellar artery. (5) Meningocele is failure of vertebral arches to close with herniation of meninges but not spinal cord; meningoencephalocele is an occipital defect with herniation of meninges and part of the brain. (6) The secondary olfactory cortex is also called the entorhinal cortex or area 28; injury of the optic chiasm causes bitemporal hemianopsia

### Q7: Question VII — Physiology. Discuss: (1) two characteristics of cerebellar ataxia; (2) the role of the nigrostriatal dopamine pathway in controlling motor function; and (3) two physiological functions of the stretch reflex

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** (1) Cerebellar ataxia may include dysmetria, dysarthria, decomposition of movement, adiadochokinesia, asynergia, kinetic or intention tremor, eyeball tremor/nystagmus, rebound phenomenon, and a zigzag-line gait; any two are accepted. (2) Nigrostriatal dopamine from substantia nigra to striatum excites the direct pathway through D1 receptors and inhibits the indirect pathway through D2 receptors, facilitating motor activity. (3) Functions include generation and maintenance of muscle tone, smoothing or damping muscle contraction to prevent jerky movement, stabilization of joint posture through the load reflex, and proprioceptive information to the brain and cerebellum; any two are accepted

### Q8: Question VIII — Histology. Answer the following five items: (1) mention two glial cells in the peripheral nervous system; (2) mention two structures forming the blood–CSF barrier; (3) enumerate the listed layers of the cerebral cortex; (4) mention two refractive media of the eye; (5) state the lining epithelium of the cornea

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** (1) Satellite cells and Schwann cells. (2) Tight junctions between choroidal epithelial cells, their basement membrane, and the capillary endothelium associated with the choroid plexus; any two are accepted. (3) Molecular layer, pyramidal cell layer, and granular layer. (4) Cornea, aqueous humor, lens, and vitreous humor; any two are accepted. (5) Stratified squamous non-keratinized epithelium
