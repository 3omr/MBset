## 2nd-week GDs — Biochemistry

### Q8: What is schizophrenia and what are its manifestations?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Schizophrenia is a severe, chronic mental disorder affecting thought, feeling, and behavior, with impaired reality monitoring. Manifestations include hallucinations, delusions, disorganized thought or speech, markedly disorganized or abnormal motor behavior, diminished emotional expression, and cognitive deficits such as impaired attention.
**Tag:** Department, GDs, Biochemistry GD 1

### Q9: What is the glutamate hypothesis of schizophrenia and what is its basis?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** The hypothesis proposes that hypofunction of NMDA glutamate receptors is an essential step in schizophrenia pathophysiology. Ketamine and phencyclidine, which antagonize NMDA receptors, can precipitate schizophrenia-like symptoms and psychotic episodes.
**Tag:** Department, GDs, Biochemistry GD 1

### Q10: Mention the role of glutamate neurotransmitter in the brain.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Glutamate is the main excitatory amino-acid neurotransmitter in the brain and is especially important in learning and memory.
**Tag:** Department, GDs, Biochemistry GD 1

### Q11: Discuss glutamate receptors and mention which one is mostly implicated in schizophrenia.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Glutamate receptors are ionotropic and metabotropic. Ionotropic receptors include AMPA, NMDA, and kainate receptors; AMPA mediates much fast excitatory transmission, NMDA is permeable to Ca²⁺ and requires glycine, and kainate receptors regulate glutamate release. Metabotropic receptors are G-protein-coupled receptors in three families. NMDA receptors are most implicated in schizophrenia.
**Tag:** Department, GDs, Biochemistry GD 1

### Q12: Describe the glutamate-glutamine cycle and mention its importance to glutamatergic neurotransmission.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** In astrocytes, glutamate is converted to glutamine by glutamine synthetase. Glutamine is transported to neurons and converted back to glutamate by phosphate-activated glutaminase. Glutamate is released, activates postsynaptic AMPA/kainate and NMDA receptors, and is rapidly taken back into astrocytes by excitatory amino-acid transporters. The cycle supplies neuronal glutamate and rapidly removes synaptic glutamate.
**Tag:** Department, GDs, Biochemistry GD 1

## 2nd-week GDs — Biochemistry

### Q13: Define depression and mention its symptoms.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Depression is a mood disorder causing persistent sadness and loss of interest that interferes with daily function. Symptoms include insomnia, hopelessness, fatigue, aches and pains, and suicidal attempts.
**Tag:** Department, GDs, Biochemistry GD 2

### Q14: What is the monoaminergic hypothesis?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** The monoaminergic hypothesis states that depression is caused by absolute or relative deficiencies and hypoactivity of monoaminergic systems, especially serotonin and norepinephrine.
**Tag:** Department, GDs, Biochemistry GD 2

### Q15: What are the functions of norepinephrine and serotonin neurotransmitters?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Norepinephrine is involved in arousal, wakefulness, attention, memory, restlessness, anxiety, and the autonomic fight-or-flight response. Serotonin contributes to mood regulation and memory, normal bowel function, appetite reduction, platelet clotting and vasoconstriction, sleep through melatonin formation, bone density, and sexual function.
**Tag:** Department, GDs, Biochemistry GD 2

### Q16: List norepinephrine and serotonin receptors.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Noradrenergic receptors include alpha1, alpha2, beta1, beta2, and beta3 receptors. Serotonin receptors include G-protein-coupled 5-HT1A/B/D/F, 5-HT2A/B/C, 5-HT4, 5-HT5A/B, 5-HT6, and 5-HT7 receptors, plus the ligand-gated 5-HT3 receptor.
**Tag:** Department, GDs, Biochemistry GD 2

### Q17: How are norepinephrine and serotonin neurotransmitters terminated, and what is their clinical significance in depression?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Norepinephrine is mainly removed by presynaptic norepinephrine transporters and metabolized by monoamine oxidase and catechol-O-methyltransferase; vanillylmandelic acid is a major final metabolite. Serotonin is taken up by the serotonin transporter and metabolized by monoamine oxidase. Depression treatment increases monoamine availability by blocking norepinephrine or serotonin reuptake or inhibiting monoamine oxidase; SSRIs block serotonin reuptake.
**Tag:** Department, GDs, Biochemistry GD 2

### Q18: List causes that lower neurotransmitter levels.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Causes include prolonged stress; an inappropriate diet lacking proteins, vitamins, or minerals; genetic and metabolic abnormalities; gastrointestinal problems impairing nutrient breakdown or absorption; toxic substances such as heavy metals; caffeine and nicotine; cholesterol-lowering medications; and hormonal changes.
**Tag:** Department, GDs, Biochemistry GD 2

### Q35: Explain the diversity of adverse effects encountered with TCAs compared with SSRIs.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key

**Tag:** Department, GDs, Pharmacology GD 1
