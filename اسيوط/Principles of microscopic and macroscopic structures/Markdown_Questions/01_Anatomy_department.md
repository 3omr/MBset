### Question 1

Functions of placenta?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Respiration: Oxygen supplied by maternal blood diffuses across the placental membrane into fetal blood, and carbon dioxide diffuses from fetal blood to maternal blood.
2. Nutrition: Water, mineral salts, carbohydrates, fats, proteins, and vitamins pass from maternal blood to fetal blood.
3. Excretion: Fetal waste products (urea, uric acid, creatinine) cross placenta into maternal blood; the placenta acts as a fetal kidney.
4. Protection: Serves as a barrier against most maternal microorganisms, though some viruses (e.g. rubella, CMV) and bacteria can cross.
5. Endocrine: Synthesizes and secretes hormones essential for pregnancy maintenance, including progesterone, estrogens, human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), and human placental lactogen (somatomammotropin).
6. Passive Immunity: Maternal antibodies (predominantly IgG) cross the placental membrane to provide passive immunity to the fetus.

### Question 2

Anomalies of placenta?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Placenta membranacea (diffuse placenta): Chorion laeve persists, producing a thin, membranous placenta covering the entire uterine cavity.
2. Placenta bipartita / tripartita: Placenta partially divided into two or three distinct lobes with shared fetal vessels.
3. Placenta duplex / multiplex: Two or more completely separated placental discs.
4. Placenta succenturiata: Small accessory lobe located at a distance from the main placenta, connected by fetal blood vessels.
5. Placenta fenestrata: A window-like defect lacking placental tissue in a central area.
6. Placenta circumvallata: A raised whitish fibrous ring on the fetal surface composed of a double fold of amnion and chorion.
7. Battledore placenta: Marginal attachment of the umbilical cord at the placental margin.
8. Placenta velamentosa: Cord attaches to the fetal membranes rather than the placental mass; vessels travel between amnion and chorion before reaching placenta.
9. Placenta previa: Implantation of the blastocyst in the lower uterine segment covering or adjoining the internal cervical os (lateralis, marginalis, or centralis).

### Question 3

Describe the full term placenta?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Dimensions and Weight: Discoid shape, approximately 15 to 20 cm in diameter, 2 to 3 cm in thickness, and weighs approximately 500 grams (about one-sixth the weight of the fetus).
2. Coverage: Covers approximately 15% to 25% of the internal surface of the uterus.
3. Fetal Surface: Smooth, glistening, covered by transparent amnion, through which the chorionic vessels are visible radiating from the umbilical cord attachment site.
4. Maternal Surface: Dull red, rough, and divided by placental septa into 15 to 20 convex cotyledons (lobes), covered by the decidua basalis.

### Question 4

List the layers of the placental barrier?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Initially (early pregnancy, 4 layers):
1. Syncytiotrophoblast
2. Cytotrophoblast (Langhans layer)
3. Extraembryonic connective tissue core of the villus
4. Endothelial lining of fetal capillaries

Late pregnancy (from the 4th month onward, 2 layers):
1. Syncytiotrophoblast (cytotrophoblast and connective tissue thin out and largely disappear)
2. Endothelial lining of fetal capillaries (which come into direct, intimate contact with the syncytium to significantly increase gas and nutrient exchange rates).

### Question 5

Mention parts and sites of the deciduas?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Decidua basalis: Lies directly deep to the implanted conceptus between the blastocyst and the myometrium; forms the maternal component of the definitive placenta.
2. Decidua capsularis: Covers the abembryonic pole of the developing embryo, separating it from the uterine cavity.
3. Decidua parietalis (decidua vera): Lines the remaining surface of the uterine cavity away from the implantation site.

### Question 6

Contents of the umbilical Cord?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Wharton's jelly: Gelatinous mucous connective tissue rich in proteoglycans that protects and cushions the umbilical vessels.
2. Umbilical vessels: Two umbilical arteries (carrying deoxygenated fetal blood to the placenta) and one umbilical vein (carrying oxygenated blood to the fetus).
3. Vitelline duct (yolk stalk): Remnant connecting the primitive midgut to the yolk sac.
4. Definitive yolk sac remnant.
5. Allantois remnant: Extraembryonic portion of the allantoic diverticulum.
6. Physiological umbilical hernia: Loop of intestine present inside the proximal umbilical cord between the 6th and 10th weeks of development.

---

### Question 7

Anomalies of umbilical cord?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Abnormally long cord: Reaches 1.5 meters or more; predisposes to knotting, looping around the fetal neck, and cord prolapse.
2. Abnormally short cord: 10 cm or less; may cause premature placental abruption, fetal distress, or delay in delivery.
3. Battledore placenta: Insertion of the umbilical cord into the margin of the placenta.
4. Velamentous insertion (placenta velamentosa): Insertion into the fetal membranes with vessels running unprotected.
5. Single umbilical artery: Absence of one umbilical artery, often associated with cardiovascular and urinary system malformations.
6. True and false knots of the cord.
7. Omphalocele: Failure of physiological umbilical hernia to return to the abdominal cavity by the 10th week.

### Question 8

Sources of amniotic fluid?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Amnioblasts: Secretion by cells of the amniotic membrane lining the amniotic cavity.
2. Kidneys of embryo / fetus: Fetal urine contributes significantly starting from the second trimester (fetus swallows fluid and urinates back into cavity).
3. Maternal tissue fluid: Transudation across the placental membrane from maternal blood.
4. Fetal respiratory tract: Secretions from the fetal respiratory tree.

---

### Question 9

Composition of amniotic fluid?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: - 98% to 99% water.
- 1% to 2% dissolved solids, consisting of:
  - Organic compounds: Glucose, proteins, urea, uric acid, creatinine, lipids, and hormones.
  - Inorganic salts: Sodium, potassium, chloride, and calcium.
  - Desquamated fetal epithelial cells (used for fetal karyotyping and genetic analysis).

### Question 10

Functions of amnion and amniotic fluid?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: During pregnancy:
1. Cushions the fetus and protects it from external mechanical trauma.
2. Maintains a constant, uniform body temperature for the fetus.
3. Allows symmetrical external growth of the embryo.
4. Permits free fetal movement, which is essential for normal musculoskeletal development.
5. Prevents adhesion of the amnion to the fetal surface.

During labor:
6. Forms a hydrostatic fluid wedge that assists in gradual, uniform dilatation of the cervical canal.
7. Rupture of membranes releases sterile amniotic fluid that cleanses and lubricates the birth canal.

### Question 11

Anomalies of amnion and amniotic fluid?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Hydramnios (Polyhydramnios): Excess volume of amniotic fluid exceeding 1500 to 2000 ml; associated with maternal diabetes, central nervous system anomalies (anencephaly), and gastrointestinal tract atresias (esophageal atresia).
2. Oligohydramnios: Abnormally low volume of amniotic fluid (less than 400 ml); associated with bilateral renal agenesis (Potter syndrome) and pulmonary hypoplasia.
3. Amniotic band syndrome: Tear in the amnion leads to fibrous bands that constrict fetal digits or extremities, causing amputations or ring constrictions.

### Question 12

Function of yolk sac?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Nutritive transport: Transmits nutritive fluids to the embryo during early development before the establishment of the uteroplacental circulation.
2. Hematopoiesis: The extraembryonic mesoderm covering the yolk sac is the primary site of earliest blood cell and vessel formation during the 3rd to 5th weeks.
3. Germ cell origin: Primordial germ cells appear in the endodermal wall of the yolk sac near the allantois and migrate to the developing genital ridges.
4. Digestive tube formation: Part of the yolk sac endoderm is incorporated into the embryo as the epithelial lining of the primitive digestive tube.

---

### Question 13

Anomalies of yolk sac (vitelline duct)?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Meckel's diverticulum: Persistence of the proximal portion of the vitelline duct as a blind outpouching of the ileum (present in ~2% of population).
2. Vitelline fistula: Complete patency of the vitelline duct connecting the ileum directly to the umbilicus, causing fecal discharge from the navel.
3. Vitelline cyst (enterocystoma): Intermediate portion of the duct remains patent and forms a fluid-filled cyst.
4. Vitelline ligament: Obliterated vitelline duct forms a fibrous cord connecting the ileum to the umbilicus, creating risk for volvulus and strangulation.

---

### Question 14

Fate of Allantois?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: The intraembryonic portion of the allantois connects the apex of the developing urinary bladder to the umbilicus. During fetal development, its lumen obliterates and it becomes a thick fibrous cord called the urachus. In the adult, the urachus persists as the median umbilical ligament extending from the bladder apex to the umbilicus.

---

### Question 15

Congenital Anomalies of Allantois (urachus)?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Urachal fistula: Complete patency of the urachus connecting the bladder lumen to the umbilicus, causing constant leakage of urine from the umbilicus.
2. Urachal cyst: Partial patency of the mid-portion of the urachus, resulting in a localized cystic dilatation.
3. Urachal sinus: Blind pocket opening either externally at the umbilicus or internally into the urinary bladder.

### Question 16

Compare between monozygotic twins and dizygotic twins?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Dizygotic (Fraternal) Twins:
- Origin: Two separate oocytes fertilized by two separate spermatozoa.
- Genetics: Different genetic constitutions; may be of the same sex or opposite sexes.
- Placentation: Each embryo implants separately with its own placenta, chorion, and amnion (though placentas and chorions may secondarily fuse).
- Heredity: Incidence influenced by maternal age, race, heredity, and ovulation stimulation treatments (~70% to 75% of twins).

Monozygotic (Identical) Twins:
- Origin: A single fertilized ovum (zygote) splits at an early stage of development.
- Genetics: Identical genetic constitutions; strictly the same sex.
- Placentation: Depends on the time of splitting:
  - Splitting at 2-cell stage: Dichorionic, diamniotic with separate placentas.
  - Splitting at blastocyst stage: Monochorionic, diamniotic with a shared placenta (most common, ~65%).
  - Splitting at bilaminar germ disc stage: Monochorionic, monoamniotic.
- Heredity: Constant frequency worldwide (~3 to 4 per 1000 births); not influenced by heredity.

### Question 17

Causes of birth defects (teratogenesis)?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Infectious agents: Rubella virus, cytomegalovirus, herpes simplex virus, toxoplasma gondii, and syphilis.
2. Radiation: Ionizing radiation causes cell death, chromosome breaks, and microcephaly.
3. Chemical agents and drugs: Thalidomide, antiepileptic drugs (phenytoin, valproate), warfarin, ACE inhibitors, and retinoic acid (vitamin A derivatives).
4. Social drugs: Alcohol (fetal alcohol syndrome), cigarette smoking, and cocaine.
5. Maternal metabolic disorders: Maternal diabetes mellitus (heart and neural tube defects), maternal phenylketonuria, and nutritional deficiencies.
6. Mechanical forces: Oligohydramnios causing compression deformities (clubfoot, facial flattening).

### Question 18

Prevention of birth defects?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Periconceptional folic acid supplementation: Reduces the incidence of neural tube defects (spina bifida and anencephaly) by up to 70%.
2. Strict glycemic control: Regulating blood glucose levels in diabetic women prior to conception and throughout pregnancy.
3. Iodine supplementation: In water and salt supplies to prevent endemic cretinism and severe intellectual disability.
4. Avoidance of teratogenic medications, alcohol, and recreational drugs throughout pregnancy.
5. Pre-pregnancy vaccination: Rubella immunization prior to conception to prevent congenital rubella syndrome.

### Question 19

Prenatal diagnosis methods?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Ultrasonography: Non-invasive technique used to assess fetal growth, gestational age, structural anomalies, and amniotic fluid volume.
2. Maternal serum screening: Non-invasive blood tests measuring markers such as alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), and unconjugated estriol.
3. Amniocentesis: Transabdominal aspiration of amniotic fluid under ultrasound guidance, performed at 15 to 18 weeks for fetal karyotyping and biochemical testing.
4. Chorionic villus sampling (CVS): Biopsy of trophoblastic tissue performed at 10 to 12 weeks for early chromosomal and genetic diagnosis.
5. Cell-free fetal DNA testing: Non-invasive screening of fetal DNA fragments present in maternal blood.

### Question 20

Indications for using prenatal diagnostic tests?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Advanced maternal age (35 years of age or older at delivery) due to increased risk of trisomy 21 (Down syndrome).
2. Previous child with a chromosomal abnormality or neural tube defect.
3. Known structural chromosomal rearrangement or balanced translocation in either parent.
4. Family history of a known single-gene disorder or X-linked disorder.
5. Abnormal findings on routine maternal serum screening or fetal ultrasound.

### Question 21

Define and compare Syndrome vs Association?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: - Syndrome: A recognized pattern of multiple congenital anomalies occurring together that are pathogenetically related and have a specific, single common etiology (e.g. Down syndrome caused by trisomy 21). Diagnosis establishes a known clinical course and recurrence risk.
- Association: The non-random occurrence in two or more individuals of multiple anomalies that occur together more frequently than expected by chance alone, but whose specific common etiology is currently unknown (e.g. VACTERL association: Vertebral, Anal, Cardiac, Tracheo-esophageal, Renal, and Limb defects).
