### Question 1

State the timing of blastocyst arrival in the uterus, the timing of partial implantation, and the cellular layers of the trophoblast and embryoblast on day 8 of development.

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Timing: Blastocyst reaches uterus at day 5; partial implantation occurs at day 8. Trophoblast divides into 2 layers: outer syncytiotrophoblast and inner cytotrophoblast. Inner cell mass (embryoblast) forms 2 layers (bilaminar embryonic disc): epiblast and hypoblast. Two cavities appear: amniotic cavity and primary yolk sac.

### Question 2

Define gastrulation, mention its timing, characteristic morphological evidence, and the embryonic fate of the notochord.

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Definition: Gastrulation is the process that establishes all three primary germ layers (ectoderm, intraembryonic mesoderm, endoderm), converting the bilaminar disc into a trilaminar embryonic disc. Timing: 3rd week of development. Evidence: Appearance of the primitive streak. Fate of notochord: 1) Nucleus pulposus of intervertebral discs; 2) Apical ligament of dens; 3) Parts of the base of the skull.

### Question 3

Enumerate congenital anomalies and teratogenic conditions associated with defective gastrulation.

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Caudal dysgenesis (Sirenomelia / Mermaid syndrome)
2. Sacrococcygeal teratoma (derived from remnants of primitive streak)
3. Holoprosencephaly
4. Situs inversus.

---

### Question 4

Enumerate the developmental anomalies associated with failure of neural tube closure (dysraphic disorders).

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Spina bifida occulta: Failure of vertebral arches to fuse, covered by intact skin (often marked by a tuft of hair).
2. Spina bifida cystica:
   - Meningocele: Protrusion of fluid-filled meninges only through the vertebral defect.
   - Meningomyelocele: Protrusion of both meninges and neural tissue (spinal cord / nerve roots).
   - Rachischisis (Myeloschisis): Failure of neural folds to elevate and close, leaving open neural plate tissue.
3. Anencephaly: Failure of closure of the cephalic (anterior) neuropore, leading to absence of major parts of the brain and calvarium.

### Question 5

Describe the structural types of chorionic villi during placental development.

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Primary villi: Composed of a cellular cytotrophoblastic core covered by an outer layer of syncytiotrophoblast.
2. Secondary villi: Primary villi invaded by an inner core of extraembryonic mesenchyme.
3. Tertiary villi: Secondary villi differentiated by the development of fetal capillaries within the mesenchymal core.

### Question 6

Enumerate the anatomical layers forming the placental barrier (membrane).

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Syncytiotrophoblast
2. Cytotrophoblast (Langhans layer - thins in late pregnancy)
3. Extraembryonic connective tissue mesodermal core
4. Fetal capillary endothelium with its basal lamina.

### Question 7

Define the embryonic period and define neurulation and its timing.

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Embryonic period: From the beginning of the 3rd week to the end of the 8th week (organogenesis). Fetal period: From the 3rd month (9th week) until birth. Neurulation: The embryonic process involving the formation, elevation, and fusion of neural folds to create the neural tube. Timing: Initiates at approximately day 18-19.

### Question 8

Enumerate the principal adult derivatives of the surface ectoderm and neural crest cells.

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Derivatives of surface ectoderm: Epidermis of skin and its appendages (hair, nails, sweat and sebaceous glands), lens and cornea of eye, internal ear, lining of oral cavity and anal canal, enamel of teeth, anterior pituitary gland.
Derivatives of neural crest cells: Melanocytes of skin, sensory (dorsal root) and autonomic ganglia, Schwann cells of peripheral nerves, chromaffin cells of suprarenal medulla, outflow tract septum of the heart (conotruncal cushions), craniofacial mesenchyme and branchial cartilages/bones.

---

### Question 9

Enumerate the three subdivisions of intraembryonic mesoderm and mention their main derivatives.

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Paraxial mesoderm: Segmented into somites, which give rise to sclerotome (vertebrae, ribs, and axial cartilage), myotome (skeletal musculature of trunk and limbs), and dermatome (dermis of the back).
2. Intermediate mesoderm: Gives rise to the urogenital system (kidneys, ureters, gonads, and genital ducts).
3. Lateral plate mesoderm: Divides into somatic (parietal) layer lining body wall and splanchnic (visceral) layer covering viscera, giving rise to serous membranes (pleura, pericardium, peritoneum), connective tissue, and smooth muscle of gut and respiratory tract.

### Question 10

State the timing of appearance of the first pair of somites, the total number of somites formed, and the fate of the sclerotome.

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Appearance: First pair of somites appears in the cephalic/occipital region at approximately day 20-21. Total number: 42 to 44 pairs (4 occipital, 8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, and 8-10 coccygeal). Fate of sclerotome: Differentiates into the axial skeleton, forming the vertebral bodies, vertebral arches, ribs, and associated cartilages and ligaments.
