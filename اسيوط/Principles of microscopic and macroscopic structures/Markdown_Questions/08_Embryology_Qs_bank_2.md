### Question 38

Describe the structure of a mature human spermatozoon.


**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: A mature sperm is about 60 microns long and consists of: 1) Head (4-5 microns): contains condensed haploid nucleus (23 chromosomes: 22 autosomes + X or Y) capped by the acrosome containing enzymes (hyaluronidase, acrosin). 2) Neck: contains centrioles. 3) Middle piece: contains axial filament surrounded by spirally arranged mitochondrial sheath (provides ATP for motility). 4) Tail (principal piece and end piece): consists of flagellum providing propulsive motility.

---

### Question 39

Describe the structure of an ovulated secondary oocyte and its coverings.


**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: The ovulated oocyte is arrested in metaphase of meiosis II and contains haploid nucleus (23, X) and first polar body in perivitelline space. It is surrounded by: 1) Oolemma (cell membrane). 2) Zona pellucida: thick translucent glycoprotein coat. 3) Corona radiata: outer radially arranged follicular cells.

---

### Question 40

Enumerate the events occurring upon sperm entrance into the oocyte cytoplasm.


**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1) Cortical and zona reaction: release of cortical granules to prevent polyspermy (zona hardening). 2) Completion of the second meiotic division of the secondary oocyte, releasing the second polar body and definitive female pronucleus. 3) Metabolic activation of the egg. 4) Swelling of sperm nucleus to form the male pronucleus. 5) Fusion of male and female pronuclei restoring diploid number (46 chromosomes).

---

### Question 41

Mention the results of fertilization.


**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1) Restoration of the diploid number of chromosomes (46). 2) Determination of chromosomal sex (XX = female, XY = male). 3) Initiation of cleavage (mitotic divisions).

---

### Question 42

Describe the process of blastocyst formation.


**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: By day 4-5 post-fertilization, fluid enters through the zona pellucida into the intercellular spaces of the morula, coalescing to form a fluid-filled cavity (blastocoele). The cells separate into: 1) Outer cell mass (trophoblast): forms the placenta and chorion. 2) Inner cell mass (embryoblast): situated at one pole, gives rise to the embryo proper.

---

### Question 43

Describe the wall of the chorionic vesicle.


**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: The chorionic vesicle wall consists of the chorion, formed by three layers: 1) Outer syncytiotrophoblast. 2) Middle cytotrophoblast. 3) Inner extraembryonic somatic mesoderm. Primary, secondary, and tertiary chorionic villi project from this wall into the surrounding maternal lacunar spaces.

---

### Question 44

Enumerate the parts and fate of the uterine decidua.


**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Parts: 1) Decidua basalis: lies deep to the blastocyst, forms the maternal component of the definitive placenta. 2) Decidua capsularis: covers the blastocyst, degenerates as the conceptus enlarges and fuses with decidua parietalis. 3) Decidua parietalis: lines the rest of the uterine cavity. Fate: Decidua capsularis degenerates. Decidua basalis and parietalis are sloughed off after delivery with bleeding as lochia.

---

### Question 45

Describe the structure, parts, and function of a tertiary chorionic villus.


**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Structure: Core of extraembryonic mesoderm containing fetal capillaries and connective tissue cells (Hofbauer cells), surrounded by inner cytotrophoblast and outer syncytiotrophoblast. Parts: Anchoring villi (attach to maternal decidua) and floating villi (bathed in maternal blood of intervillous spaces). Function: Gas exchange (O2/CO2), nutrient and electrolyte transport, waste excretion, hormone production (hCG, progesterone, estrogen), and immunologic barrier.
