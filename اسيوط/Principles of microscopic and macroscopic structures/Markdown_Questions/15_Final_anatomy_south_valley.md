### Question 1

Write short notes on the types and sites of cartilage in the human body:

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Hyaline cartilage: Articular cartilage of synovial joints, costal cartilages, thyroid/cricoid cartilages, tracheal and bronchial rings, epiphyseal growth plates.
2. Elastic cartilage: Auricle (pinna) of ear, external acoustic meatus, auditory (Eustachian) tube, epiglottis, corniculate and cuneiform cartilages of larynx.
3. Fibrocartilage: Intervertebral discs, symphysis pubis, menisci of knee joint, glenoid and acetabular labra, temporomandibular joint articular disc.

### Question 2

Write short notes on the types of exocrine glands according to the mode of secretion:

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Merocrine (eccrine) glands: Secretory vesicles release products via exocytosis without loss of cytoplasm (e.g., salivary glands, pancreas, most sweat glands).
2. Apocrine glands: The apical portion of the secretory cell cytoplasm is pinched off with the secretion (e.g., mammary glands, specialized axillary sweat glands).
3. Holocrine glands: The entire secretory cell disintegrates to release its secretory content (e.g., sebaceous glands of the skin, tarsal/Meibomian glands).

### Question 3

Describe the light and electron microscopic structure of elastic fibers:

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Under LM, elastic fibers are thin, refractive, branching fibers that stain poorly with H&E but stain dark brown with Orcein and resorcin-fuchsin. Under EM, they consist of an amorphous central core of the protein elastin surrounded by an outer sheath of 10-12 nm microfibrils composed of the glycoprotein fibrillin-1.

### Question 4

What are the types of corpus luteum and mention their function?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Corpus luteum of menstruation: Formed after ovulation when pregnancy does NOT occur. Persists for approximately 10-14 days during the luteal phase, secreting progesterone and estrogens to maintain the secretory endometrium, then regresses into the corpus albicans.
2. Corpus luteum of pregnancy: Formed when fertilization and implantation occur, maintained by hCG secreted by syncytiotrophoblast. Persists and actively secretes progesterone for the first 3-4 months of pregnancy until placental steroidogenesis takes over.

### Question 5

Define blastocyst implantation, mentioning its normal site and most common abnormal sites:

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Implantation is the process by which the blastocyst attaches to and invades into the functional layer of the endometrium, occurring on days 6 to 10 after fertilization.
- Normal site: The superior and posterior wall of the body of the uterus near the fundus in the midsagittal plane.
- Abnormal sites (ectopic pregnancy):
  1. Uterine ectopic: Lower uterine segment near the internal os (placenta previa).
  2. Extrauterine ectopic: Ampulla or isthmus of the fallopian tube (tubal pregnancy, ~95%), ovary, peritoneal/abdominal cavity (rectouterine pouch of Douglas).

### Question 6

Which of the following time intervals best describes the period of maximum susceptibility of the developing conceptus to teratogens?

- **A)** Weeks 3 to 8 (Embryonic period)
- **B)** Weeks 1 to 2 (Pre-embryonic period)
- **C)** Months 3 to 9 (Fetal period)
- **D)** At the time of fertilization

**Correct Answer**: A
**Explanation**: The embryonic period (weeks 3 to 8) is the period of active organogenesis; each organ system is forming and highly vulnerable to teratogenic disruption resulting in major structural birth defects.

### Question 7

What is the normal duration of the human menstrual cycle and name its three phases?

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: The normal duration averages 28 days (ranging from 21 to 35 days). Its three uterine phases are:
1. Menstrual phase (days 1 to 4/5): Shedding of the stratum functionale.
2. Proliferative (follicular) phase (days 5 to 14): Estrogen-driven regeneration of the functional layer.
3. Secretory (luteal) phase (days 15 to 28): Progesterone-driven glandular tortuosity and secretion.

### Question 8

A nucleosome is a unit repeating structure of which of the following?

- **A)** Ribosomes
- **B)** Chromatin
- **C)** Nuclear pore complex
- **D)** Microtubules

**Correct Answer**: B
**Explanation**: Nucleosomes are the basic structural repeating subunits of chromatin, consisting of approximately 146 base pairs of DNA wrapped around an octamer of core histone proteins (two each of H2A, H2B, H3, and H4).

### Question 9

The presence of elevated alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) in maternal serum or amniotic fluid is a diagnostic indicator for which condition?

- **A)** Down syndrome
- **B)** Turner syndrome
- **C)** Klinefelter syndrome
- **D)** Neural tube defects (such as anencephaly and spina bifida)

**Correct Answer**: D
**Explanation**: Open neural tube defects (anencephaly, open myelomeningocele) leak fetal serum proteins into the amniotic fluid, causing markedly elevated levels of alpha-fetoprotein (AFP).

### Question 11

Which of the following is classified as a permanent (fixed) cell of connective tissue proper?

- **A)** Fibroblast
- **B)** Plasma cell
- **C)** Monocyte
- **D)** Neutrophil

**Correct Answer**: A
**Explanation**: Fixed (resident) cells of connective tissue include fibroblasts, fibrocytes, pericytes, and adipocytes. Plasma cells, monocytes, and granulocytes are transient/wandering cells.
