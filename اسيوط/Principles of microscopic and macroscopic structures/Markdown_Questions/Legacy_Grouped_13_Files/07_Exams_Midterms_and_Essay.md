## Part 3: Short Essay & Written Questions Bank (Past Years Official Papers)

- **Tag**: `Exams, Written`

### Question 1 (Written)

**Question**: Define end arteries and give examples.

**Model Answer**:
End arteries are arteries that are the sole supply of oxygenated blood to a portion of tissue, with no anastomoses with adjacent arteries.
- Anatomical end arteries: Central artery of retina, splenic artery branches, renal segmental arteries.
- Functional end arteries: Coronary arteries (have small potential anastomoses that are inadequate to prevent necrosis if suddenly occluded).

---

### Question 2 (Written)

**Question**: Define spermatogenesis and enumerate its stages.

**Model Answer**:
Spermatogenesis is the process by which immature male germ cells (spermatogonia) develop into mature spermatozoa.
Stages:
1) Spermatocytogenesis: Spermatogonia (type A and B) divide by mitosis to form primary spermatocytes.
2) Meiosis: Primary spermatocytes undergo Meiosis I -> Secondary spermatocytes; Secondary spermatocytes undergo Meiosis II -> Spermatids.
3) Spermiogenesis: Transformation of spermatids into spermatozoa.

---

### Question 3 (Written)

**Question**: Enumerate the phases of fertilization.

**Model Answer**:
Phase 1: Penetration of the corona radiata.
Phase 2: Penetration of the zona pellucida (acrosome reaction).
Phase 3: Fusion of the oocyte and sperm cell membranes.

---

### Question 4 (Written)

**Question**: Enumerate the main results of fertilization.

**Model Answer**:
1) Restoration of the diploid number of chromosomes (46, 2n).
2) Determination of chromosomal sex (XX for female, XY for male).
3) Initiation of cleavage divisions.

---

### Question 5 (Written)

**Question**: Define gastrulation and state its beginning.

**Model Answer**:
Gastrulation is the formative process by which the bilaminar embryonic disc is converted into a trilaminar embryonic disc composed of three primary germ layers: ectoderm, intraembryonic mesoderm, and endoderm.
- Begins with the formation of the primitive streak on the surface of the epiblast at the beginning of the 3rd week.

---

### Question 6 (Written)

**Question**: What are the changes in the endometrium during the decidual reaction?

**Model Answer**:
Under the influence of progesterone:
1) Stromal cells enlarge, become polyhedral, and accumulate glycogen and lipids (decidual cells).
2) Endometrium becomes hyperemic, edematous, and thickened.
3) Endometrial glands become tortuous, dilated, and actively secretory.

---

### Question 7 (Written)

**Question**: Mention three differences between cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast.

**Model Answer**:
1) Cytotrophoblast: Inner layer, mononucleated discrete cells with well-defined cell boundaries, actively dividing by mitosis.
2) Syncytiotrophoblast: Outer layer, multinucleated protoplasmic mass with no cell boundaries, does not divide by mitosis.
3) Secretion: Syncytiotrophoblast secretes hCG and produces proteolytic enzymes for implantation.

---

### Question 8 (Written)

**Question**: Name two sources of amniotic fluid.

**Model Answer**:
1) Maternal blood through placenta and chorionic/amniotic membrane.
2) Fetal urine excreted into amniotic cavity (later in pregnancy).
3) Fluid secretions from fetal respiratory tract.

---

### Question 9 (Written)

**Question**: Mention two key features of apoptosis (programmed cell death).

**Model Answer**:
1) Cell shrinkage and chromatin condensation with nuclear fragmentation (karyorrhexis).
2) Formation of apoptotic bodies with intact plasma membranes, engulfed by macrophages without eliciting an inflammatory response.

---

### Question 10 (Written)

**Question**: Define the basement membrane and mention its layers under EM.

**Model Answer**:
A specialized extracellular matrix structure that anchors epithelium to underlying connective tissue.
Layers under EM:
1) Basal lamina (produced by epithelial cells): Lamina lucida and Lamina densa.
2) Reticular lamina (produced by connective tissue fibroblasts, containing type III and VII collagen).

---

### Question 11 (Written)

**Question**: Enumerate the components of the cytoskeleton.

**Model Answer**:
1) Microfilaments (actin filaments, 6-8 nm)
2) Intermediate filaments (8-10 nm: keratin, vimentin, desmin, neurofilaments, glial fibrillary acidic protein)
3) Microtubules (25 nm hollow cylinders of alpha and beta tubulin)

---

### Question 12 (Written)

**Question**: Enumerate types of lysosomes and their functions.

**Model Answer**:
1) Primary lysosomes: Newly synthesized from Golgi complex containing inactive acid hydrolases.
2) Secondary lysosomes (phagolysosomes / autophagolysosomes): Formed by fusion of primary lysosome with phagosome or autophagosome, actively digesting contents.
3) Residual bodies (tertiary lysosomes): Indigestible material retained in cell (e.g., lipofuscin pigment granules).

---

### Question 13 (Written)

**Question**: Define fertilization, name its normal anatomical site, and enumerate its results.

**Model Answer**:
- Definition: Sequence of coordinated events beginning with contact between sperm and secondary oocyte and ending with intermingling of maternal and paternal chromosomes.
- Site: Ampulla of the uterine (fallopian) tube.
- Results: Restoration of diploid number (46), determination of sex, initiation of cleavage.

---

### Question 14 (Written)

**Question**: Describe the menstrual cycle: definition and phases.

**Model Answer**:
- Definition: Cyclic series of endometrial changes occurring approximately every 28 days in response to ovarian hormones (estrogen and progesterone).
- Phases:
1) Menstrual phase (days 1-4): Shedding of stratum functionalis.
2) Proliferative / follicular phase (days 5-14): Under estrogen, regeneration of endometrium.
3) Secretory / luteal phase (days 15-28): Under progesterone, glands become tortuous and secretory, ready for implantation.

---

### Question 16 (Written)

**Question**: Enumerate types of intercellular junctions (junctional complex).

**Model Answer**:
1) Zonula occludens (tight junction) - sealing / barrier function
2) Zonula adherens (adhesion belt) - actin-anchoring junction
3) Macula adherens (desmosome) - intermediate filament-anchoring junction
4) Gap junction (nexus) - communicating junction with low electrical resistance
5) Hemidesmosome - anchors epithelial cells to basal lamina

---

### Question 17 (Written)

**Question**: Enumerate functions of the placenta.

**Model Answer**:
1) Gas exchange (oxygen and carbon dioxide)
2) Nutrient transfer (glucose, amino acids, vitamins, fatty acids)
3) Waste excretion (urea, uric acid, creatinine)
4) Hormone production (hCG, progesterone, estrogen, human placental lactogen)
5) Transmission of maternal antibodies (IgG conferring passive immunity)

---

### Question 18 (Written)

**Question**: Enumerate differences between primary and secondary cartilaginous joints.

**Model Answer**:
1) Primary cartilaginous (synchondrosis): Bones united by hyaline cartilage, temporary joint that ossifies with age (e.g., epiphyseal plate, spheno-occipital synchondrosis), allows no movement.
2) Secondary cartilaginous (symphysis): Bones united by fibrocartilage with hyaline cartilage covering bone ends, permanent joint located in median plane (e.g., pubic symphysis, intervertebral discs), allows limited movement.
