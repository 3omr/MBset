### Question 55

Name the parts of the growing long bones. Mention how it increases in length & breadth.

**Correct Answer**: -

**Explanation**: Parts of growing long bone: Diaphysis (shaft), Epiphysis (ends), Metaphysis (part of diaphysis adjacent to epiphyseal cartilage), and Epiphyseal cartilage plate.
Increase in length: By proliferation of cartilage cells in the epiphyseal plate.
Increase in breadth (thickness): By appositional bone deposition by osteoblasts in the periosteum.

### Question 56

Describe ossification of long bones.

**Correct Answer**: -

**Explanation**: Long bones ossify predominantly via intracartilaginous (endochondral) ossification from a hyaline cartilage model. Primary ossification center appears in the diaphysis before birth (around 8th week IUL) forming periosteal collar and central calcification. Secondary ossification centers appear in the epiphyses usually after birth. Epiphyseal cartilage plate persists between diaphysis and epiphysis until full skeletal maturity when it undergoes synostosis.

---

### Question 57

Describe types of ossification giving examples.

**Correct Answer**: -

**Explanation**: 1. Intramembranous ossification: Bone develops directly within condensed mesenchymal connective tissue membranes (e.g. bones of skull vault, facial bones, clavicle).
2. Intracartilaginous (endochondral) ossification: Mesenchyme first forms a hyaline cartilage model which is later replaced by bone (e.g. long bones of limbs, vertebrae, ribs, sternum).

### Question 58

Define the anatomical position. Name the 3 different planes of the body.

**Correct Answer**: -

**Explanation**: Anatomical position: Standing erect, facing forward, eyes looking to the horizon, upper limbs hanging at sides with palms facing forward (thumbs pointing laterally), and lower limbs together with feet flat and toes pointing forward.
Planes of the body:
1. Median (sagittal) plane: Vertical anteroposterior plane dividing body into right and left equal halves.
2. Coronal (frontal) plane: Vertical plane perpendicular to sagittal plane, dividing body into anterior and posterior parts.
3. Transverse (horizontal) plane: Horizontal plane perpendicular to sagittal and coronal planes, dividing body into superior and inferior parts.

### Question 136

Compare primary & secondary cartilaginous joints.

**Correct Answer**: -

**Explanation**: 1. Primary cartilaginous joint (Synchondrosis):
   - Articular surfaces united by hyaline cartilage.
   - No movement allowed.
   - Usually temporary (ossifies with age).
   - Examples: Epiphyseal plate of long bone, first sternocostal joint, spheno-occipital synchondrosis.
2. Secondary cartilaginous joint (Symphysis):
   - Articular surfaces covered by hyaline cartilage and united by a plate of white fibrocartilage.
   - Limited movement allowed.
   - Permanent (does not ossify with age).
   - Located in the median plane of the body.
   - Examples: Symphysis pubis, intervertebral discs, manubriosternal joint.

### Question 138

Classify types of skeletal muscles according to the arrangement of its fibers, giving one example for each type.

**Correct Answer**: -

**Explanation**: 1. Parallel fibers: Fibers run parallel to the line of pull (e.g. Sartorius, strap muscles).
2. Pennate (feather-like) fibers:
   - Unipennate: Tendon on one side, fibers oblique on one side (e.g. Flexor pollicis longus, extensor digitorum longus).
   - Bipennate: Central tendon, fibers converge from both sides (e.g. Rectus femoris, dorsal interossei).
   - Multipennate: Multiple central tendons with diagonal fibers (e.g. Deltoid, subscapularis).
   - Circumpennate: Cylindrical tendon with circular oblique fibers (e.g. Tibialis anterior).
3. Triangular (convergent) fibers: Broad origin converging to a narrow tendon (e.g. Temporalis, pectoralis major).
4. Spiralized / Cruciate fibers: Fibers twist or cross (e.g. Trapezius, sternocleidomastoid, latissimus dorsi).
5. Circular fibers: Sphincters surrounding orifices (e.g. Orbicularis oris, orbicularis oculi).

### Question 139

Describe the structure of a synovial joint.

**Correct Answer**: -

**Explanation**: A synovial joint is characterized by:
1. Articular surfaces: Covered by a thin layer of smooth hyaline cartilage (articular cartilage), devoid of perichondrium, blood vessels, or nerves.
2. Joint cavity: A potential space between the articulating bones filled with synovial fluid.
3. Fibrous capsule: Dense fibrous sheath completely enclosing the joint and attached to bones beyond the articular margins.
4. Synovial membrane: Vascular connective tissue lining the inner aspect of capsule and all non-articular surfaces, secreting viscous synovial fluid for lubrication and nutrition.
5. Supporting structures (accessories): Intracapsular/extracapsular ligaments, articular discs/menisci (fibrocartilage), labra (e.g. glenoid labrum), and bursae/fat pads.
