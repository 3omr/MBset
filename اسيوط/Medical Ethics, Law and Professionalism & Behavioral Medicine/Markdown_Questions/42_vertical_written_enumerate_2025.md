# Source 42 — Vertical written enumerate booklet, 2025

This booklet provides topic headings and answer outlines rather than interrogative stems. The following QROC prompts are normalized as “enumerate/describe” questions from those printed headings; the model-answer content is taken from the accompanying lists. A few unclear source spellings are flagged in the relevant answer. Source terminology and factual claims have not been independently updated.

### Q1: List the ideas into which thoughts are organized.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source lists a central idea, a following idea, an opposing idea, a parasitic idea, and an item spelled “Acentral idea” in the source.

---

### Q2: Enumerate the types of thinking according to nature, the object dealt with, and conformity with reality.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:**
- According to nature: simple thinking, trial-and-error thinking, and insight thinking.
- According to the object dealt with: egocentric thinking and objective thinking.
- According to conformity with reality: realistic, idealistic, and autistic thinking.

---

### Q3: Enumerate the factors that affect problem solving.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** How the problem is perceived; intensity of motivation; transfer of learned responses; emotional state; and physical state.

---

### Q4: List the types of imagination and the forms of free imagination.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Types of imagination: free imagination, controlled imagination, and inventive or creative production. Forms of free imagination: imaginative play, daydreaming, and autistic thinking.

---

### Q5: Describe the source's listed pathological use of daydreaming.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Escaping actual problems and responsibilities and losing contact with the environment.

---

### Q6: Enumerate the phases of invention.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Preparation, incubation, inspiration, and verification/elaboration.

---

### Q7: Classify disorders of thinking and enumerate the examples listed under each category.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:**
- Form disorders: loosening of associations, clang association, incoherence, word salad, verbigeration, perseveration, and neologism. The source has an unclear spelling beside “incoherence”; “clang association” is the standard term corresponding to its “gang association” transcription.
- Stream disorders: flight of ideas, circumstantiality, and blocking.
- Content disorders: overvalued ideas, obsessions, compulsions, and delusions.

---

### Q8: List the forms and types of delusions in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Delusions may be fixed or shakable, systematized or poorly systematized, and bizarre or non-bizarre. Types listed: persecution, grandeur, reference, guilt or self-accusation, nihilistic, somatic, hypochondriacal, infidelity/jealousy, erotomania/love, influence or control, possession of thoughts, thought insertion, thought withdrawal, and thought broadcasting.

---

### Q9: Enumerate the laws of attention and the external factors affecting attention.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Laws of attention: selectivity/attraction, sustainability, shifting, combination, fluctuation, and distraction. External stimulus factors: intensity, repetition, changeability, contrast, unfamiliarity, and combination of sensory stimuli.

---

### Q10: Classify internal factors affecting attention and list examples.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Internal factors are permanent or temporary. The source lists sensory fitness, lasting specific factors, intelligence, emotional state, physical state, and mental set.

---

### Q11: List factors responsible for shifting attention and factors that support sustained attention.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Factors responsible for shifting: spontaneity, satisfaction, exploration, fatigue, and monotony. Factors supporting sustainability: interest, curiosity, punishment, and praise.

---

### Q12: Describe methods of eliminating distraction, types of attention, and disorders of attention.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Methods to eliminate distraction: remove the distracting stimulus, exert extra effort, use negative adaptation, and combine the distracting stimulus with the main task. Types of attention: active and passive. Disorders: hyperprosexia, inattention, and distractibility.

---

### Q13: Distinguish symbols from signals and list the cortical sites of perception shown in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** A symbol stands for an object; a signal is received from an object. Sites listed: auditory perception, areas 41 and 42 in the temporal lobe; olfactory perception, area 28 in the temporal lobe; visual perception, areas 18 and 19 in the occipital lobe; somatosensory perception, areas 5 and 7 in the parietal lobe.

---

### Q14: List examples of extrasensory perception and the disorders of perception named in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Extrasensory perception examples: telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition, and psychokinesis. Disorders listed: camouflage, illusion, déjà vu/illusion of familiarity, hallucination, depersonalization, and derealization.

---

### Q15: Enumerate the listed methods of camouflage and factors affecting perception.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Methods of camouflage: addition, embedding, prolongation, avoiding contrast, and spread/proximity. Factors affecting perception are objective/figural and subjective/central. Objective factors: similarity, symmetry, proximity, closure, and good continuation. Subjective factors: mood/emotion, need, interest, mental set, and the “pregnant factor” as named in the source.

---

### Q16: List the effects of sensory deprivation and the factors affecting the occurrence of illusions.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Effects of sensory deprivation: fear, panic, depression, irritability, altered perception of time, hallucinations, reduced level of consciousness and attention, and somatic complaints such as weakness. Factors affecting illusions include physical causes (the source gives mirror illusion as an example), habit/familiarity, set/expectation, and an unanalyzed total impression. The source examples for the last three are partly unclear.

---

### Q17: Give the intelligence score ranges and categories listed in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Genius: above 140; very superior: 120–140; superior: 110–120; average: 90–110; dull average: 80–90; borderline: 70–80; mild mental retardation: 50–70; moderate mental retardation: 25–50; severe mental retardation: below 25. These labels and ranges are reproduced from the source.

---

### Q18: Enumerate Thurstone's factors of intelligence listed in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Verbal comprehension, word fluency, number, space, rote memory, perceptual ability, and reasoning.

---

### Q19: List the intelligence tests by age group and the Wechsler subscales.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Child tests: Stanford-Binet Scale, ages 2–18 years; Wechsler preschool intelligence scale, up to 5 years; Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children, ages 5–15 years. Adult test: Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale, ages 16–64 years. Wechsler verbal subscales: information, comprehension, arithmetic, digit span, and vocabulary. Performance subscales: digital symbol, picture completion, block design, picture arrangement, and object assembly.

---

### Q20: State the uses and criteria for intelligence tests listed in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Uses: educational purposes, vocational guidance, measuring intellectual deterioration, assessing social or intellectual harmony, checking intellectual improvement, and checking the effects of therapy. Criteria: reliability/consistency and validity (positive correlation of the test with its purpose).

---

### Q21: Enumerate the theories of emotion and categories or forms of emotion listed in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Theories: James–Lange, Cannon–Bard, Lindsley's activation theory, Papez–MacLean physiological theory, and Schachter's cognitive theory. Categories: pleasant, unpleasant, flat/inadequate, inappropriate/incongruous, and depersonalization. Pleasant forms: euphoria, elation (transcribed as “Flation” in the source), exaltation, and ecstasy. Unpleasant forms: grief, depression, and anxiety.

---

### Q22: List Piaget's cognitive-development stages, Erikson's psychosocial stages, and Freud's psychosexual stages.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Piaget: sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operations, and formal operations. Erikson: trust versus mistrust, autonomy versus shame and doubt, initiative versus guilt, industry versus inferiority, identity versus role confusion, intimacy versus isolation, generativity versus stagnation, and integrity versus despair. Freud: oral, anal, phallic, latency, and genital stages.

---

### Q23: Enumerate Kohlberg's levels and the stages of moral development listed under each level.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Level 1, preconventional: avoiding punishment and aiming at a reward. Level 2, conventional: good-boy/good-girl attitude and loyalty to law and order. Level 3, postconventional: justice and the spirit of the law, and universal ethical principles.

---

### Q24: List the prenatal and postnatal physical-development stages in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Prenatal: germinal, embryonic, and fetal stages. Postnatal: infancy (birth to 2 years), early childhood (2–6 years), later childhood (6 years to puberty), adolescence (puberty to 18 years), and adulthood (18 years to death).

---

### Q25: List the emotional-development milestones and their ages in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Social smiling: 4–6 weeks; anger, surprise, and sadness: 3–4 months; fear: 5–7 months; shame and shyness: 6–8 months; contempt and guilt: 24 months.

---

### Q26: Classify types of memory and list the processes of memory.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Types: sensory memory, short-term memory, and long-term memory. Long-term memory includes declarative and procedural memory. Processes: encoding, storage, and retrieval.

---

### Q27: List the steps for putting information into long-term memory and the types of encoding and rehearsal.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Steps: sensory memory, attention, short-term memory, and encoding. Encoding may be automatic or effortful. Effortful processing includes elaborative (deeper) rehearsal and maintenance (shallower) rehearsal. Forms of elaborative rehearsal: relating information to life and existing knowledge, meaning of information, organization, and imagery.

---

### Q28: List the biological basis of memory and the theories of forgetting in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Biological basis: visual, auditory, and other sensory systems; frontal lobes; amygdala; and hippocampus. Theories of forgetting: decay, interference, encoding failure, and retrieval failure.

---

### Q29: Classify memory dysfunction and enumerate the recall, recognition, and amnesia disorders listed.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Memory dysfunction is classified as disorders of recall and disorders of recognition. Recall disorders listed: organic or psychological amnesia, hypermnesia, and paramnesia/distorted memories. Recognition disorders: déjà vu and jamais vu. Amnesia types: retrograde and anterograde.

---

### Q30: Describe the physiological and psychological responses to stress and list the benefits of stress exposure.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Physiological responses include changes in heart rate, respiratory rate, blood sugar, and hormone levels. Psychological responses include fear, anxiety, panic, depression, loss of concentration, and amotivation. Benefits listed: increased vitality and energy, enthusiasm, optimism, increased mental alertness, improved memorization, increased productivity, and increased creativity.

---

### Q31: List the hazards of recurrent or intense stress, signs of stress, and common stressors.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Hazards: fatigue, irritability, pessimism and amotivation, reduced concentration, productivity, and creativity, decreased immunity and body healing, physical illness, anxiety, and depression. Signs may be physical, mental, or behavioral. Stressors listed: life changes, hassles, work and financial stress, home stress, acculturation and immigration, wars and disasters, marital stress, and developmental stress such as adolescence, midlife crisis, and retirement.

---

### Q32: Classify stress by duration, severity, and cause, and enumerate its causes and types of stressors.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Duration: acute or chronic. Severity: mild, moderate, or severe. By cause/type of stressor: environmental and social, psychological, or biological/body factors. The source separately lists environmental/social, psychological/mental, and body/biological factors as etiologic groups.

---

### Q33: List the factors that determine the impact of environmental stressors and events.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Magnitude of the event; whether its nature is positive or negative; timing and duration (acute or chronic); and the individual's vulnerability.

---

### Q34: Enumerate personal, environmental, and stressor-related factors that affect the impact of stress.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Personal factors: age, sex, IQ/cognitive appraisal, physical state, sleep and alertness, previous experience/prior information, and support system. Environmental factors: magnitude and duration of the problem, presence or absence of alternatives, “remolding factors” as worded in the source, and external support. Stressor factors: severity of the insult and duration of exposure.

---

### Q35: Describe the body's response to stress and list factors that modulate the response.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source distinguishes an acute/immediate response and a chronic/delayed response. Factors modulating the response: prior experience, preparatory information, individual differences and personality styles, ability to control the unpleasant stimulus, and social support.

---

### Q36: List the general management approaches for stress and the physical and psychiatric conditions associated with it.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Management: psychotherapeutic and pharmacological approaches. Physical-disease forms: psychosomatic and autoimmune diseases. Psychiatric disorders listed: anxiety and depression.

---

### Q37: Enumerate the biological, social, and prenatal factors affecting personality development.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Biological factors: sedative drugs, hypoglycemia, reduced diet and vitamin B, organic brain disease, and endocrine factors (thyroid, adrenals, gonads, pituitary, and pineal). Social factors: birth order and socioeconomic circumstances. Prenatal factors: maternal stress, testosterone, barbiturates and amphetamines, smoking, hypoxia, and low birth weight.

---

### Q38: List the approaches to personality development and the personality dimensions described in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Approaches: biological, psychoanalytical, social learning, humanistic, and cognitive. Hans Eysenck's two major dimensions: introversion versus extroversion and neuroticism versus emotional stability. The source's five dimensions are: extraversion versus introversion; neuroticism/emotionality/anxiety/instability versus stability; psychoticism/tough-mindedness/psychopathy/aggressiveness; conservatism versus radicalism; and general ability/intelligence.

---

### Q39: List the levels of consciousness, Freud's personality components, objective personality measures, and personality-disorder clusters described in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Levels of consciousness: conscious, preconscious, and unconscious. Freud's personality components: id, ego, and superego. Objective measures: MMPI, 16 PF, and NEO-PI. Personality-disorder clusters: A (odd and eccentric), B (dramatic and emotional), and C (anxious and fearful).

---

### Q40: Enumerate the types of learning and applications of classical conditioning in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Types of learning: trial and error, insight, imitation, observational learning, and classical conditioning. Applications of classical conditioning: acquisition of phobia, aversion therapy, and an enuresis alarm. The source also lists objects, places, persons, and times in relation to addiction, without a clear connection to the preceding heading.

---

### Q41: Classify factors affecting learning and list the personal and objective factors in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Factors are personal or objective. Personal factors: intelligence, previous learning, physical factors, emotional state, motivation, and general health. Objective factors: the learned object, method of learning, external factors, and the teacher's role.

---
