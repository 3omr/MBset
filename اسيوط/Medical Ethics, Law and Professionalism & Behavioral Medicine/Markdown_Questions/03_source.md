# Source 03 — Medical ethics and law review notes

Translated from the Arabic text layer. The source mixes explicit questions with short topic headings and answer notes; the QROC prompts below are normalized from those cues, and answers follow the source lists. Legal statements, penalties, and dates are reproduced as sourced and have not been independently checked or updated.

### Q1: Who may practice medicine in Egypt according to the source, and who is exempt from the nationality condition?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** A person must be an Egyptian citizen or a citizen of a country whose laws allow Egyptians to practice medicine there, and must be registered in the physicians' register. The source lists foreigners who enrolled at an Egyptian university before Law 42 of 1948 took effect as an exception to the nationality condition.

---

### Q2: Who may be entered in the public-health register?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** A person with a Bachelor of Medicine and Surgery from an Egyptian university, or with a foreign degree or diploma equivalent to it.

---

### Q3: In which cases may a physician be punished according to the source?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source lists causing a person's death through error, negligence, recklessness, lack of precaution, or violation of law; a gross breach by the physician; practicing while intoxicated; causing permanent disability through a gross breach; disclosing a secret; and performing an abortion with or without the pregnant woman's consent. It says the punishment is increased when the act causes the deaths of more than three people.

---

### Q4: List the forms of bribery, falsification, or other conduct involving physicians that are described in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source describes requesting or accepting a promise or gift for false testimony about pregnancy, illness, disability, or death; making a false disability certificate to avoid public service; issuing a false certificate or statement through favoritism; making false statements to authorities about a spouse's age for marriage; intentionally altering facts while acting as a court-appointed expert or translator; and handling narcotic substances for a purpose other than the one for which they were intended.

---

### Q5: How is a physician licensed to practice, and who is represented on the Medical Syndicate disciplinary body?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source says licensing follows a Bachelor of Medicine and Surgery and registration with the Ministry of Health and the Medical Syndicate. It lists a state judge, members of the Public Prosecution, and Medical Syndicate members as representatives on the disciplinary body.

---

### Q6: What disciplinary sanctions are listed, from less to more severe?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Notice, warning, reprimand, fine, temporary suspension from practice for up to one year, or removal of the physician's name from the register.

---

### Q7: For how long may heirs retain a medical facility license after its owner's death, and when may a physician whose name was removed from the register return to practice?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source says the heirs may retain the facility license for 20 years from the date of death. A physician whose name was removed may apply to the Syndicate for approval to practice again after at least two years.

---

### Q8: How may a physician appeal a disciplinary decision?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source says an in-person decision may be appealed before the appellate disciplinary body within 30 days of notice. An absentee decision may be appealed within 60 days.

---

### Q9: What qualifications or conditions does the source list for physicians working in a medical facility, including non-Egyptian physicians?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The physician should be Egyptian and registered with the Medical Syndicate. The source permits non-Egyptian physicians when the applicable professional law allows registration on a reciprocal basis and the competent authorities approve it. Foreign experts may be licensed for up to three months when their expertise is unavailable in Egypt.

---

### Q10: What does the source require before advertising a medical facility, and in which cases may its license be cancelled?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source lists Ministry of Health approval before advertising; it also mentions Syndicate approval and says an advertisement should include the diagnostic and treatment methods. Listed license-cancellation cases are: a request by the license holder; cessation of work for more than a year; moving the facility; using it for a different purpose; a judgment ordering closure or removal; repeated violations; or practice by people without a license.

---

### Q11: Describe the types of medical facilities and their characteristics as listed in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:**
- A private clinic is managed by a physician or dentist according to their profession; the source says it need not have beds.
- A specialist clinic may be used by one or more licensed physicians, receives and provides medical care to patients, has shared management when several physicians work there, and does not exceed five beds.
- A specialized medical center may be used by one or more physicians, receives patients, is limited to one specialty and its subspecialties, has shared management with one physician as the responsible technical director, does not exceed 25 beds, and may perform surgery.
- A private hospital is equipped according to applicable regulations and its specialties; the source lists at least 15 beds, two operating rooms, a recovery room, and an intensive-care room.

---

### Q12: Enumerate the physician's duties toward patients described in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Make every effort to relieve suffering; provide equal care; work within professional competence and seek more capable help when needed; avoid excessive fees; prescribe necessary medicines; explain the illness and its consequences unless humane reasons justify withholding information, subject to the patient's wishes and safety of others; provide a handover to a replacement physician; advise patients and families about prevention; obtain consent before examination or intervention; and provide written consent for surgery except when needed to save life. The source says the physician should do what is possible to save a minor, a person lacking capacity, or an unconscious patient when consent cannot be obtained.

---

### Q13: What additional professional and community duties does the source describe, and what conduct does it prohibit?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The physician should serve the community to the best of their ability in peace and war, act as a good example without exploitation, contribute to solving community health problems, and cooperate with state bodies on health policy and plans. Prohibited conduct listed includes using the physician's name to promote medicines, seeking a reward for referrals, consulting in commercial establishments or telecommunications companies, selling medicines or supplies for profit in the clinic, acting as an intermediary for another physician, using an untested diagnostic method, and self-advertising. The source allows announcements in newspapers up to three times when opening or relocating a clinic; for an absence longer than two weeks, it lists two announcements, one before leaving and one after returning.

---

### Q14: What should a physician do if a patient dies in a private medical facility or if a suspicious injury or assault occurs?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The physician should notify the competent authorities and report the death. The physician should report injuries or accidents suspected to involve a crime. If the physician is assaulted, the source says to notify the local Medical Syndicate branch as soon as possible. It also says physicians must provide the same healthcare to people deprived of liberty and others, and must not end a patient's life under a claim of compassion or mercy.

---

### Q15: What should a physician do when an epidemic disease is suspected?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Notify the competent health authorities.

---

### Q16: Who retains responsibility when physicians consult one another about a patient's diagnosis or treatment?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The physician who directly examines and treats the patient retains full responsibility, according to the source.

---

### Q17: What rules should a physician follow when discussing medical topics with the public through the media?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Do not state the workplace, contact details, or experience; state only the professional role and specialty; use simple language appropriate for the audience; and do not present uncertain scientific opinions as established facts.

---

### Q18: What duties does the source list toward colleagues and when several physicians share a patient's care?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Resolve professional disputes amicably, referring unresolved disputes to the local Syndicate branch and appeals to the General Syndicate. Do not disparage colleagues; professional criticism should go before an impartial scientific committee. A physician temporarily replacing a colleague should tell the patient before examination, avoid exploiting the situation, hand care back when the colleague returns, and report the care already provided. A physician should examine or treat a patient of a colleague only when invited by the colleague or hospital administration. The patient's family may invite another physician for consultation. If the treating physician declines to provide treatment recommended by consultants, the source says the physician may withdraw and leave care to one of the consultants.

---

### Q19: What are the types of consent and when does the source require written consent?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Implied consent may arise when a patient attends a physician seeking advice, especially when paying for the consultation and proposed intervention. The source lists verbal consent for taking samples or performing examinations such as vaginal, rectal, or blood sampling. Written consent is listed for surgery and interventions requiring general anesthesia, therapeutic abortion, external or internal examination in sexual-assault cases, and examination of victims or suspects in forensic cases or people referred by the prosecution.

---

### Q20: Who may consent to a medical examination, and when may examination occur without consent according to the source?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source names a competent adult patient aged 21 years or older, or the legal guardian for a minor, a person with intellectual disability, or an unconscious patient. It lists prisoners; people who handle food; people applying to universities or military colleges and employees referred to a medical committee; and cases in which intervention is necessary to save life as situations where examination may occur without the patient's consent.

---

### Q21: What should a physician do if an additional surgical intervention becomes necessary during an operation?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source says to proceed based on prior consent and inform the relatives waiting outside the operating room.

---

### Q22: What should be done if a competent, conscious patient refuses an operation that the patient's children insist on?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Do not perform the operation. The competent and aware patient's refusal prevails over the family's consent.

---

### Q23: When may urgent medical intervention proceed without the patient's or guardian's consent, and what conditions does the source list for emergency surgery?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source lists a patient arriving in deep unconsciousness, a dangerous health condition, and the absence of relatives with authority to consent. For emergency surgery, it lists approval of the treatment by competent health authorities; a specialist on a team of at least three physicians to approve; expected benefits exceeding harms; full explanation to the patient after waking; and free treatment or costs set by a competent authority.

---

### Q24: What reasons for refusing treatment does the source list, and when does it say a physician is not blameworthy for leaving a patient who refuses necessary treatment?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Reasons listed: fear of death or the unknown; giving up because of limited remaining life; poverty; ignorance or lack of awareness; distrust of the physician; frightening counselling about complications; and absence of severe symptoms. The source reports two scholarly views. It says the physician is not blameworthy after explaining the danger of refusing treatment to the patient and family; reporting to the guardian or authorities is required when feasible, while informing the patient and family is sufficient when reporting is difficult.

---

### Q25: What does the source say about a patient who refuses treatment and dies as a result?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source says a patient who refuses treatment is sinful and disobedient but is not considered to have killed themself; a person who gives up food and drink is considered to have killed themself; and refusing urgent surgery is described as casting oneself into destruction. This is reproduced from the source's religious discussion.

---

### Q26: Define medical error and classify the types and degrees listed in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Medical error is described as deviation from required conduct or action inconsistent with the caution expected in social life. Types: ordinary/nonprofessional/nontechnical/material error, which is unrelated to professional practice; and professional error, related to practice and failure to meet professional duties. Degrees: gross, which the source associates with conduct more negligent than that of most people; slight, which an ordinary person might commit; and trivial, which a careful and decisive person might commit. The source says a physician is asked about gross medical error.

---

### Q27: List the forms of medical error that the source associates with criminal liability.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Intentional assault, negligence or lack of precaution, recklessness, and breach of established professional standards. Examples of intentional conduct listed include prescribing poison, abandoning a patient in an emergency, mercy killing, killing people with infectious diseases, experiments for purposes other than treatment, procedures to evade military service, deliberate transmission of infectious disease, and legally prohibited procedures such as criminal abortion, female genital cutting, sex change, falsifying reports, and organ trafficking.

---

### Q28: Define negligence or lack of precaution and list the examples given in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source describes it as a passive failure to take the precaution expected when performing a positive duty. Examples: operating with unsterilized instruments that cause viral hepatitis; leaving surgical instruments or gauze in the abdomen; transfusing contaminated or incompatible blood; diagnostic or analytical error; proceeding without patient consent; disclosing a patient's secret; and inadequate anesthesia or failure to restore consciousness appropriately.

---

### Q29: Define recklessness and dereliction of duty and give the examples listed.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Recklessness is described as poor judgment, lack of skill, or ignorance of professional principles, including impulsive or incautious action during surgery. Examples include operating on the right thigh instead of the left and a patient sustaining skin burns because of an electrical short in operating-table wires, for which the source assigns responsibility to the hospital and physician. Dereliction includes violating laws, regulations, or decisions governing medical work and failing to report a death or birth.

---

### Q30: Enumerate the causes of medical errors listed in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Ignorance or lack of knowledge; limited experience; forgetfulness, negligence, and indifference; inaccurate diagnosis and failure to consult specialists; failure to refer the patient to the appropriate specialty; workplace problems; long work hours and unavailable equipment; weak oversight; and weak religious or ethical restraint.

---

### Q31: State the elements and assessment standard for medical liability described in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Elements: a physician's error, with the burden of proving it on the injured party; harm to the patient, whether moral or material; and a causal relationship between error and harm. Moral harm includes psychological suffering. Material harm includes loss of a chance for treatment or recovery and the patient's expenses. The standard for assessing physician conduct considers the conduct of another physician at the same level, the external circumstances surrounding the work, and consistency with professional traditions and established scientific principles.

---

### Q32: Classify the types of medical liability and the consequences or examples listed in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Moral/ethical liability concerns professional ethics, including preserving secrets and avoiding unnecessary surgery. Administrative disciplinary liability may include a notice, financial fine, or prohibition from practice. Syndicate disciplinary liability may include removing the physician's name from the licensed register. Civil liability requires compensation for material harm and may be contractual (breach of a valid physician-patient agreement to provide care, or achieve a result in some cases such as blood typing and transfusion) or tort-based (breach of a general duty or performing an operation outside what is permitted for a physician of that level). Criminal liability arises from violating a legal rule carrying imprisonment or another criminal penalty; examples listed include criminal abortion, female genital cutting, sex-change procedures, disclosure of professional secrets, refusal to provide assistance, dispensing dangerous drugs, falsifying medical reports, causing permanent disability, or causing a patient's death.

---

### Q33: How may an injured person obtain compensation in a tort-liability case, and what criminal penalty does the source list for falsifying medical reports?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source says compensation may be obtained through a civil lawsuit, which may be waived or settled. For knowingly issuing a false certificate or statement through favoritism, it lists imprisonment and a fine not exceeding 500 Egyptian pounds. It also says requesting or accepting a promise or gift for such conduct is punished under the bribery provisions, including punishment of the briber and intermediary. These penalties are reproduced from the source and are not current-law guidance.

---

### Q34: What grounds may exclude a physician's civil liability according to the source?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Prove that the harm resulted from force majeure or a sudden event, such as wars or earthquakes; prove that another person's error caused the harm, such as the patient going to another physician; or prove that the harm resulted from the patient's own error, such as deliberately ignoring medical instructions.

---

### Q35: How may a physician's medical error be proven according to the source?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** By physical evidence and verbal or other testimonial evidence.

---

### Q36: What are the elements of the crime of disclosing a professional secret, and what forms of disclosure does the source include?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Elements: an act of disclosure; the disclosed information is a secret; the act is committed by a physician or equivalent; and criminal intent. The source includes giving information even by denying it, disclosing part of a secret, naming a patient in cases involving secret diseases, abortion, or childbirth, and discussing a patient's case with a colleague without the patient's permission. Accidentally leaving papers is classified by the source as negligence rather than disclosure.

---

### Q37: In which situations may a physician disclose professional secrets according to the source?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** At the patient's request; when disclosure benefits the patient; when the physician defends against an accusation of negligence or error or seeks fees; and when reporting an infectious disease to a health office or preventive unit. The source says heirs do not acquire a right to the secret after the patient's death and separately mentions disclosure to relatives only.

---

### Q38: Which infectious diseases does the source list for reporting to health authorities?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Cholera, plague, typhus, yellow fever, meningitis, diphtheria, tetanus, rabies, tuberculosis, leprosy, and sexually transmitted diseases including syphilis, gonorrhea, and AIDS.

---

### Q39: What reporting duties does the source list for deaths, births, abnormal deaths, and occupational diseases?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Report deaths and births to the authorities; report suspected unnatural deaths; report occupational diseases to the competent administrative body, employer, health authorities, and Medical Syndicate. For a court expert's role, the source notes examination after patient consent and recording medical observations without identifying the patient.

---
