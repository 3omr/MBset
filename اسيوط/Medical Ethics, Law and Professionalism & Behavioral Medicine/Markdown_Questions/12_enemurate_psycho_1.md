# Source 12 — Psychology enumerate notes

This source is an English outline titled “Enumerate,” not a conventional question paper. The QROC prompts below are normalized from its section headings; model answers use the printed lists. Statements and classifications are preserved as sourced and have not been independently updated.

### Q1: List the domains of behavioral science and its importance to health and medicine.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Domains: psychology, sociology, and anthropology. Importance to health and medicine: understanding the etiology and presentation of illness, delivery of healthcare, and social and psychological treatment.

---

### Q2: State the listed benefits of studying psychology and the major perspectives of psychology.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Benefits: developing critical-thinking skills, improving communication skills, and understanding how biology, environment, and experience interact to shape behavior. Major perspectives: biological, psychodynamic, cognitive, behavioral, and humanistic.

---

### Q3: List the human life stages and the main domains of growth and development.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Infancy: birth to 1 year; early childhood: 1–6 years; late childhood: 6–12 years; adolescence: 12–18 years; early adulthood: 19–40 years; middle adulthood: 40–65 years; late adulthood: 65 years and older. Growth and development domains: physical, mental, emotional, and social.

---

### Q4: Name the major theories of development and their theorists.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Psychoanalytical theory: Sigmund Freud; psychosocial theory: Erik Erikson; cognitive theory: Jean Piaget; moral-development theory: Lawrence Kohlberg.

---

### Q5: List Freud's personality components and psychosexual stages with their age ranges.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Personality components: id, ego, and superego. Psychosexual stages: oral (birth to 18 months), anal (18 months to 3 years), phallic (3–6 years), latency (6 years to puberty), and genital (puberty onward).

---

### Q6: Enumerate Erikson's psychosocial stages.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Trust versus mistrust; autonomy versus shame and doubt; initiative versus guilt; industry versus inferiority; identity versus confusion; intimacy versus isolation; generativity versus stagnation; integrity versus despair.

---

### Q7: List the parenting styles named in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Authoritarian, permissive, authoritative, and uninvolved.

---

### Q8: Enumerate Piaget's cognitive-development stages and their age ranges.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Sensorimotor (0–2 years), preoperational (2–7 years), concrete operational (7–11 years), and formal operational (11 years and older).

---

### Q9: List Kohlberg's levels of moral development and the stages or orientations included in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Preconventional: obedience/punishment and individual interest. Conventional: interpersonal relationships and authority. Postconventional: social contract and universal ethics.

### Q12: List the personality theories named in the source.

### Q14: List Maslow's hierarchy of needs.

### Q15: What are the two types of self-concept in Carl Rogers' theory?

### Q16: List the three personality dimensions attributed to Hans and Sybil Eysenck in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -

### Q17: Enumerate the Big Five personality factors.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Openness to experience, extraversion, neuroticism, conscientiousness, and agreeableness.

---

### Q18: List the personality-assessment methods and examples of projective tests in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Self-report inventory: Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory (MMPI). Projective tests: Rorschach Inkblot Test, Thematic Apperception Test (TAT), Contemporized-Themes Concerning Blacks test, TEMAS (Tell-Me-A-Story), and Rotter Incomplete Sentence Blank (RISB).

---

### Q19: Classify personality disorders into the clusters and examples listed in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Cluster A, odd and eccentric: paranoid, schizoid, and schizotypal personality disorders. Cluster B, emotional and dramatic: antisocial, borderline, histrionic, and narcissistic personality disorders. Cluster C, anxious and fearful: avoidant, obsessive-compulsive, and dependent personality disorders.

---

### Q20: List the components of emotion and the two classification approaches named in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Components: subjective, cognitive, motor, and expressive/behavioral response. The source describes one classification based on subjective feeling and another emphasizing cognitive processes.

---

### Q21: Enumerate the theories of emotion listed in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** James–Lange (peripheralist) theory; Cannon–Bard theory; Sigmund Freud's theory; and cognitive theory involving physiological arousal and cognitive appraisal.

---

### Q22: List the physiological changes associated with emotion and the brain structures named in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Autonomic nervous-system manifestations, neuromuscular manifestations, and central nervous-system manifestations. Brain structures: hypothalamus, limbic system, and cerebral cortex (frontal lobe).

---

### Q23: State the psychiatric applications of autonomic manifestations of emotion listed in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Lie detector, psychophysiological measurements, and biofeedback training.

---

### Q24: List the effects of frontal-lobe lesions on emotion described in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Silly behavior; euphoria and emotional incontinence; excessive sexual demands; loss of fear and anxiety; and emotional reactions tending to be processed more in the right hemisphere.

---

### Q25: Classify emotions and list the examples given in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Pleasant emotions: euphoria, elation, exaltation, and ecstasy. Unpleasant emotions: grief, depression, and anxiety. Inadequate/flat emotions: emotional dulling, apathy, and indifference. The source also names incongruous/inappropriate emotions, depersonalization, and psychosomatic disorder.

---

### Q26: List the elements and functions of motivation described in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Need, drive, and incentives. Motivation may determine the origin, direction, maintenance, and cessation of behavior.

---

### Q27: Classify human motives and give the subtypes listed in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Inherited/innate/physiologically based motives: physiological, survival, emergency, and objective motives. Acquired/learned/psychologically based motives: general social, cultural social, and individual social motives.

---

### Q28: Enumerate the approaches or concepts of motivation and classify incentives and reinforcement as shown in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source lists instincts (including McDougall's propensities and psychoanalytical theory), needs and drives, incentives, balance/equilibrium or homeostasis, and social learning. Incentives may be positive or negative. Reinforcement in social learning may be external (direct or vicarious) or internal (self-regulatory process).

---

**EXP:** Food, sex, curiosity, fear, protection, submissiveness, anger, self-assertiveness, construction, appeal, acquisitiveness, laughter, comfort, rest, migratory behavior, sociability, digestion, changing/breathing/sneezing, and evacuation. The source labels this as 18 propensities but its list combines the final items; the wording is preserved.

### Q30: What two instincts are described in Freud's theory of motivation?

### Q31: List the types of learning and the approaches or theorists named in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Classical conditioning (Ivan Pavlov, Watson, and Mary Cover Jones); operant conditioning (Skinner and Thorndike); and cognitive-social approaches to learning.

---

### Q32: Define extinction and spontaneous recovery in classical conditioning and state the therapeutic importance listed for extinction.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Extinction is the basic idea used in systematic desensitization for phobias. Spontaneous recovery is the reappearance of an extinguished conditioned response after a rest period.

---

### Q33: List the cognitive concepts of learning in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Learning sets/learning to learn; latent learning and cognitive maps; social learning and modeling.

---

### Q34: List applications of learning theories in managing psychiatric disorders.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Aversion therapy for addiction and paraphilias; flooding for phobias; positive reconditioning for nocturnal enuresis; experimental deconditioning for tics; systematic desensitization for phobias; and modeling and reinforcement for people with intellectual disability and chronic psychotic patients. The source wording is retained.

---

### Q35: Enumerate the personal factors, learning-material factors, and learning types that affect learning.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Personal factors: intelligence, attention, motivation, physiological state, psychological state, and past experiences. Learned material/strategy factors: meaningful rather than meaningless material, mnemonic devices, and selecting, emphasizing, and summarizing material. Learning types: rote learning and content learning; the source states content learning is better than rote learning.

### Q50: Classify disorders of thinking and list the examples under each category.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Stream disorders: accelerated thinking with flight of ideas, retarded thinking, and thought blocking. Content disorders: overvalued ideas, obsessions, and delusions (reference, persecution, grandiosity, infidelity, love/erotomania, guilt, and hypochondriasis). Control disorders: thought withdrawal, insertion, and broadcasting. Form disorders: irrelevant answers, loosening of associations, tangentiality, derailment, incoherence, and word salad.
