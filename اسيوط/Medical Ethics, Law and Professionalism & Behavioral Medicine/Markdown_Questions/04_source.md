### Q1: List the domains of behavioral science, its importance to health and medicine, and the benefits of studying psychology.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Domains: psychology, sociology, and anthropology. Importance: understanding illness etiology and presentation, healthcare delivery, and social and psychological treatment. Genetically influenced characteristics listed: physical traits such as height, weight, and obesity; intellectual traits such as memory and intelligence; and emotional characteristics or disorders such as shyness.

### Q5: List the stages of grief according to Kübler-Ross and the major theories of development named in the source.

### Q6: Describe the id, ego, and superego as presented in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The id contains unconscious psychic energy, strives to satisfy basic sexual and aggressive drives, and operates on the pleasure principle with immediate gratification. The superego represents internalized ideals and social rules and provides standards for judgment and future aspirations. The ego is the executive part of personality, mediates between the id, superego, and reality, and follows the reality principle by satisfying the id's desires in ways that bring pleasure rather than pain.

### Q7: List the defense mechanisms described in the source and give the stated definitions of repression, regression, and displacement.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source discusses repression, regression, displacement, rationalization, sublimation, and reaction formation. Repression suppresses painful memories or thoughts and pushes unacceptable impulses into the unconscious. Regression returns to less mature coping strategies or behavior from an earlier stage. Displacement redirects an inappropriate urge or feeling from a threatening target toward a more acceptable or less threatening one.

### Q8: How does the source say the unconscious and personality can be assessed?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The unconscious may be assessed with projective tests that use ambiguous stimuli to elicit a person's inner dynamics; examples are the Rorschach and TAT. Personality assessment includes self-report inventories such as the MMPI and projective tests such as the Rorschach Inkblot Test, TAT, Contemporized-Themes Concerning Blacks test, TEMAS (“Tell Me a Story”), and RISB.

### Q9: List the components, classification approaches, and theories of emotion described in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Components: subjective, cognitive, motor, and expressive/behavioral response. Classification approaches: one based on subjective feeling and another emphasizing cognitive processes. Theories: James–Lange (physical response), Cannon–Bard (central brain system), Freud (conscious and unconscious repressed emotions), cognitive appraisal theory, and the Papez–MacLean circuit. The Papez–MacLean description says external events stimulate sensory pathways and the reticular activating system, then cortical psych sensory areas, with responses passing to the neuromuscular and limbic systems; limbic relay stations listed are the mammillary bodies, hippocampus, and hypothalamus.

### Q10: List the physiological changes associated with emotions and the emotional disturbances named in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Physiological manifestations: autonomic (gastrointestinal tract, smooth muscles, cardiovascular and renal systems, blood, endocrine system, and respiratory functions), neuromuscular, and central nervous system manifestations (hypothalamus, limbic system, and frontal cortex). Pleasant emotions: euphoria, elation, exaltation, and ecstasy. Unpleasant emotions: grief, depression, and anxiety. Other categories: flat/inadequate emotions, incongruous/inappropriate emotions, depersonalization, and psychosomatic disorders.

### Q11: Define motivation and list the components of motivation.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Motivation is the process that initiates, guides, and maintains goal-oriented behavior; it is also described as the force that arouses, directs, and maintains behavior and begins with a physiological or psychological deficiency or need. The source says the goal is called an incentive. Components: origin, direction, maintenance, and cessation of behavior.

### Q12: Classify motives and list the characteristics and examples of inherited and acquired motives.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Inherited/innate/physiological motives are universal, present from birth, and permanent; examples include physiological, survival, emergency, and objective motives. Acquired/learned/psychological motives arise through interaction with the environment; examples include general social, cultural social, and individual social motives.

### Q13: Summarize the theories of motivation and the types of incentives described in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Instinct theory describes a complex inborn behavior pattern occurring in response to certain stimuli; the source includes McDougall's propensities and psychoanalytical theory. Freud's theory includes the life instinct (Eros), associated with life, growth, libido, sex, and related activities, and the death instinct (Thanatos), directed toward destruction, inward as suicide, or outward as aggression. Humanistic theory says behavior is directed by needs (physiological, security, social, esteem, and self-actualization). Drive theory says people act to reduce internal tension, especially for behaviors with strong biological components. Incentive theory emphasizes external rewards; positive incentives encourage behavior leading to reinforcement, while negative incentives discourage behavior leading to punishment or failure. Balance/equilibrium includes homeostasis and arousal theory; social learning includes direct, vicarious, and internal self-regulatory reinforcement.

### Q16: List the two response types and the stimulus-response sequence in classical conditioning described in the source.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Responses: unconditioned (unlearned reflexes) and conditioned (learned). Meat powder is the unconditioned stimulus; salivation to meat powder is the unconditioned response; tone begins as a neutral stimulus and, after repeated pairing, becomes a conditioned stimulus; salivation to the tone becomes the conditioned response.

---

### Q17: Complete the learning statement about observing and imitating others, then list the stages of modeling.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** We learn by watching others and then imitating or modeling. Modeling steps: attention to what the model does; retention or remembering what was observed; reproduction of the behavior; and motivation to copy it, which depends on what happens to the model.
