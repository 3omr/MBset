## Enumerate

### Q1: List the substrates for gluconeogenesis.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Glucogenic amino acids, lactate, glycerol, pyruvate, and propionate (in ruminants).

---

### Q2: List the four rate-limiting enzymes of gluconeogenesis.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Glucose-6-phosphatase, fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase, pyruvate carboxylase, and phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase.

---

### Q3: What is the energy cost of synthesizing one glucose molecule from two pyruvate molecules?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Six high-energy phosphate bonds are consumed: 2 ATP for pyruvate to oxaloacetate, 2 GTP for oxaloacetate to phosphoenolpyruvate, and 2 ATP for 3-phosphoglycerate to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate.

---

## Define

### Q4: Define gluconeogenesis.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Gluconeogenesis is the formation of glucose from non-carbohydrate precursors.

- **A)** Glucagon
- **B)** Epinephrine

**EXP:** Insulin suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis, while glucagon and other counter-regulatory signals promote it.

### Q7: Which option lists the four rate-limiting enzymes of gluconeogenesis?

- **A)** Glucokinase, pyruvate carboxylase, phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase, and glucose-6-phosphatase
- **B)** Pyruvate carboxylase, phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase, fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase, and glucose-6-phosphatase
- **C)** Pyruvate kinase, pyruvate carboxylase, phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase, and glucose-6-phosphatase
- **D)** Phosphofructokinase, pyruvate carboxylase, phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase, and fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase

**Correct Answer:** B
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** These four enzymes bypass the irreversible steps of glycolysis during gluconeogenesis.

### Q8: Which of the following is not an enzyme of gluconeogenesis?

- **A)** Glucose-6-phosphatase
- **B)** Fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase
- **C)** Phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase
- **D)** Pyruvate kinase
- **E)** Pyruvate carboxylase

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Pyruvate kinase catalyzes an irreversible glycolytic step. Gluconeogenesis bypasses it with pyruvate carboxylase and phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase.

### Q10: Which glycolytic enzyme is also used in gluconeogenesis?

- **A)** Glucokinase
- **B)** Phosphofructokinase
- **C)** Pyruvate kinase
- **D)** Aldolase

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Aldolase catalyzes a reversible reaction and is used in both glycolysis and gluconeogenesis. The irreversible glycolytic enzymes are bypassed.

### Q14: How many ATP molecules are required to convert two pyruvate molecules into one glucose molecule?

- **A)** Two
- **B)** Three
- **C)** Six
- **D)** Eight

**Correct Answer:** C
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** The pathway consumes four ATP and two GTP, equivalent to six ATP molecules in high-energy phosphate bonds.

### Q15: Which of the following is not a substrate for gluconeogenesis?

- **A)** Palmitic acid
- **B)** Lactic acid
- **C)** Alanine
- **D)** Glycerol

**Correct Answer:** A
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Even-chain fatty acids such as palmitate cannot yield net glucose in humans. Lactate, glucogenic amino acids, and glycerol can supply gluconeogenic carbon.

---

### Q16: Which gluconeogenic enzyme is not located in the cytosol?

- **A)** Glucose-6-phosphatase
- **B)** Pyruvate carboxylase
- **C)** Phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase
- **D)** Aldolase A

**Correct Answer:** B
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Pyruvate carboxylase is located in the mitochondrial matrix and is the source-marked answer. Glucose-6-phosphatase is endoplasmic-reticulum-associated, so the strict phrase “not located in the cytosol” also makes option A arguable.

---

### Q17: Which of the following cannot act as a precursor for gluconeogenesis?

- **A)** Lactate
- **B)** Glycerol
- **C)** Alanine
- **D)** Acetyl-CoA

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Acetyl-CoA from even-chain fatty acids cannot produce net glucose in humans because the pyruvate dehydrogenase reaction is irreversible and acetyl-CoA carbons are lost as carbon dioxide in the citric acid cycle.
