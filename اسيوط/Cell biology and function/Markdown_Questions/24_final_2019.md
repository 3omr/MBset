### Q1: List four functions of essential fatty acids.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Essential fatty acids are components of membrane phospholipids; precursors of eicosanoids such as prostaglandins, thromboxanes, and leukotrienes; support normal skin integrity and growth; and contribute to normal reproductive and immune functions.

---

### Q2: List two mechanisms of ammonia intoxication.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Ammonia consumes α-ketoglutarate during glutamate formation, reducing its availability for the TCA cycle and impairing cerebral energy production. It also drives glutamine formation in astrocytes; intracellular glutamine accumulation causes osmotic swelling and contributes to cerebral edema.

---

### Q3: Mention the coenzymes required for oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex uses five coenzymes: thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP), lipoamide, coenzyme A (CoA), flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD), and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD⁺). The source asks for four, but the standard complete list contains five.

---

### Q4: Mention the name, coenzyme, cofactors, proton pumping, and inhibitors of Complex IV of the electron-transport chain.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Complex IV is cytochrome c oxidase. Its redox centers include heme a and heme a₃, with copper centers CuA and CuB. It transfers electrons from cytochrome c to oxygen, reducing oxygen to water, and pumps four protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane per molecule of oxygen reduced (four electrons). Major inhibitors include cyanide, carbon monoxide, azide, and hydrogen sulfide.

---

### Q5: Enumerate four functions of glutathione.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Glutathione (1) reduces hydrogen peroxide and lipid peroxides through glutathione peroxidase, (2) maintains protein and hemoglobin sulfhydryl groups in the reduced state, (3) conjugates and helps detoxify xenobiotics through glutathione transferases, and (4) participates in amino-acid transport through the γ-glutamyl cycle.

---

### Q6: List four reasons why lactose is suitable as the main sugar in an infant's feeding.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Lactose supplies glucose for energy and galactose for synthesis of brain glycolipids; its relatively low osmotic effect permits a substantial carbohydrate supply without excessive intestinal osmolarity; it promotes beneficial intestinal bacteria; and it supports calcium absorption.

---

### Q7: Describe the causes of the resting membrane potential.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The resting membrane potential results mainly from the membrane's greater permeability to K⁺ than to Na⁺, allowing K⁺ to diffuse out down its concentration gradient and leaving intracellular impermeant anions behind. A small inward Na⁺ leak partly offsets this negativity. The electrogenic Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase maintains the ion gradients and contributes directly by exporting three Na⁺ for every two K⁺ imported.

---

### Q8: Mention the mechanism of facilitated diffusion.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** A solute moves down its electrochemical or concentration gradient through a specific membrane carrier or channel, without direct ATP use. Carrier-mediated transport involves solute binding, a conformational change, and release on the other side. It is selective and, for carriers, saturable; transport may also show competition between similar solutes.

---

### Q9: Describe the types of autonomic ganglia.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Sympathetic ganglia include paravertebral ganglia in the sympathetic chains and prevertebral (collateral) ganglia near major abdominal arteries. Parasympathetic ganglia are terminal or intramural ganglia located close to, or within the wall of, their target organs.

---

### Q10: State the site of leptin release, how it crosses the blood-brain barrier, and three of its functions.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Leptin is secreted mainly by adipocytes. It reaches the brain through a saturable transport system across the blood-brain barrier and acts on hypothalamic leptin receptors. It suppresses appetite by reducing orexigenic signaling and promoting satiety; increases energy expenditure; and signals adequate energy stores to support reproductive and other neuroendocrine functions.

---

### Q11: Describe two causes of edema.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Edema may result from increased capillary hydrostatic pressure, which drives fluid into the interstitium, or reduced plasma colloid osmotic pressure, commonly due to hypoalbuminemia, which reduces fluid reabsorption. Increased capillary permeability, lymphatic obstruction, and renal sodium retention are other causes.

---

### Q12: Describe the mechanism of the Na⁺/K⁺ pump.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** On the cytoplasmic side, the pump binds three Na⁺ ions. ATP phosphorylates the pump, causing a conformational change that releases Na⁺ outside. Two extracellular K⁺ ions then bind; dephosphorylation returns the pump to its original conformation and releases K⁺ into the cytoplasm. Each ATP therefore exports three Na⁺ and imports two K⁺ against their gradients.

---

### Q13: Enumerate the sites where saltatory conduction occurs and mention its function.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Saltatory conduction occurs along myelinated nerve fibers in both the peripheral and central nervous systems. Action potentials are regenerated at the nodes of Ranvier, where voltage-gated Na⁺ channels are concentrated, and the impulse appears to jump from node to node. This increases conduction speed and reduces the energy required to restore ionic gradients.

---

### Q14: Mention the sites of muscarinic receptors.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Muscarinic receptors occur on parasympathetic effector cells, including cardiac muscle, smooth muscle, and exocrine glands. They are also present on eccrine sweat glands supplied by sympathetic cholinergic fibers, and in the central nervous system. Major subtypes include M₂ receptors in the heart and M₃ receptors in smooth muscle and glands.

---

### Q15: Discuss the effects of the sympathetic nervous system on the cardiopulmonary system.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Sympathetic stimulation increases heart rate, myocardial contractility, and conduction through the heart, mainly through β₁ receptors. In the airways, β₂-receptor activation relaxes bronchial smooth muscle and causes bronchodilation. It also redistributes blood flow through vascular effects to support increased cardiac output and tissue perfusion during activity or stress.

---

### Q16: Mention the effects of exposure to hot weather.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Heat exposure causes cutaneous vasodilation to increase heat transfer to the environment and stimulates sweating so that evaporation cools the body. Heart rate and skin blood flow rise; prolonged sweating can cause water and electrolyte loss, dehydration, and reduced circulating volume.

---

### Q17: Define specific dynamic action and mention its causes and factors affecting it.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Specific dynamic action (SDA), or the thermic effect of food, is the rise in energy expenditure above the resting level after food is consumed. It reflects digestion, absorption, transport, metabolism, and storage of nutrients. Protein produces the largest SDA, carbohydrate an intermediate effect, and fat the smallest; the amount and composition of the meal, nutritional state, and sympathetic activity also affect it.

---

### Q18: Enumerate factors affecting the rate of enzyme activity.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Enzyme activity is affected by enzyme concentration, substrate concentration, temperature, pH, product concentration, and the presence of cofactors or metal ions. Activators and inhibitors, ionic strength, and reaction time can also alter the measured rate.

---

### Q19: Mention three specific characteristics of glycolysis in red blood cells.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Mature red blood cells lack mitochondria, so glycolysis is their only source of ATP and proceeds anaerobically to lactate. The Rapoport-Luebering shunt produces 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate (2,3-BPG), which lowers hemoglobin's oxygen affinity and facilitates oxygen release to tissues; this shunt bypasses an ATP-generating glycolytic step.

---

### Q20: List the ketone bodies and enumerate causes of ketoacidosis.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The ketone bodies are acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, and acetone. Ketoacidosis can occur with uncontrolled diabetes mellitus, prolonged fasting or starvation, and alcoholic ketoacidosis; a very low-carbohydrate state may also promote excessive ketone production.

---

### Q21: Discuss the role of the liver in regulation of blood glucose.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** After a meal, the liver takes up glucose, phosphorylates it through glucokinase, and stores it as glycogen or uses it in metabolic pathways. Between meals it releases glucose by glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis from lactate, glycerol, and glucogenic amino acids. Hepatic glucose-6-phosphatase permits free glucose release into blood; insulin favors storage, whereas glucagon promotes glucose output.

---

### Q22: Enumerate enzymes involved in prokaryotic DNA replication.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Major enzymes include DNA helicase, primase, DNA polymerase III, DNA polymerase I, DNA ligase, and DNA gyrase (a topoisomerase). RNase H and other nucleases help remove RNA primers, while single-strand DNA-binding proteins stabilize the unwound templates but are not enzymes.

---

### Q23: List six characteristics of the genetic code.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The genetic code is triplet, degenerate, unambiguous, non-overlapping, and comma-less, and is nearly universal. It is read in the 5′ to 3′ direction; AUG is the usual start codon, while UAA, UAG, and UGA are stop codons.

---

### Q24: Mention three metabolic or diagnostic roles of transaminases.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Transaminases transfer amino groups between amino acids and α-keto acids, enabling synthesis of nonessential amino acids and channeling nitrogen toward glutamate and the urea cycle. They also link amino-acid carbon skeletons to energy metabolism and gluconeogenesis. Serum ALT and AST activities are measured as markers of hepatocellular injury; AST may also rise with cardiac or skeletal-muscle injury.

---

### Q25: Enumerate post-transcriptional modifications of eukaryotic mRNA.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The main processing steps are addition of a 7-methylguanosine cap to the 5′ end, cleavage and addition of a poly-A tail at the 3′ end, and removal of introns with joining of exons by RNA splicing. Some transcripts also undergo RNA editing or alternative splicing.
