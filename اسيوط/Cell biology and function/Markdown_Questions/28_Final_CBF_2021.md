### Q1: A poorly nourished man with prolonged alcohol use has high-output heart failure and leg edema. (a) Which water-soluble vitamin is deficient? (b) Name the disease.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) Thiamine (vitamin B₁). (b) Wet beriberi, the cardiovascular form of thiamine deficiency.

---

### Q2: A man with arthritis has dark pigmentation (ochronosis) of his cartilage. (a) Give the most probable diagnosis. (b) Name the defective enzyme.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) Alkaptonuria. (b) Homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase (homogentisic acid oxidase) deficiency in phenylalanine and tyrosine degradation.

---

### Q3: A child has severe polyuria, polydipsia, and urinary ketones. (a) Give the most probable diagnosis. (b) Mention two tests that support the diagnosis.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) Diabetes mellitus with ketoacidosis. (b) Demonstrate hyperglycemia and ketones, for example with plasma glucose and blood β-hydroxybutyrate or urine ketones; blood pH or bicarbonate can establish metabolic acidosis.

---

### Q4: Sickle-cell anemia is caused by a mutation in the β-globin gene. (a) Name the mutation type. (b) Name the two amino acids affected.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) A missense point mutation caused by a single-nucleotide substitution. (b) Glutamic acid is replaced by valine at position 6 of the β-globin chain.

---

### Q5: Name the deficient enzyme in each disease: (1) maple syrup urine disease, (2) homocystinuria, (3) albinism, and (4) phenylketonuria.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (1) Branched-chain α-ketoacid dehydrogenase complex. (2) Cystathionine β-synthase in classic homocystinuria. (3) Tyrosinase in the common oculocutaneous form. (4) Phenylalanine hydroxylase in classic phenylketonuria.

---

### Q6: Enumerate two of each: (1) fates of acetyl-CoA, (2) functions of glutathione, (3) pathways that maintain blood glucose during fasting, and (4) glucose transporters, one insulin-dependent and one insulin-independent.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (1) Acetyl-CoA can be oxidized in the TCA cycle or used for ketogenesis; it also supplies carbon for fatty-acid and cholesterol synthesis. (2) Glutathione reduces peroxides and maintains protein sulfhydryl groups; it also conjugates xenobiotics and participates in amino-acid transport. (3) Glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis maintain blood glucose during fasting. (4) GLUT4 is insulin-dependent; GLUT1 is insulin-independent. GLUT2 is another insulin-independent transporter in liver and pancreatic β cells.

---

### Q7: Define (a) plasma colloid-osmotic pressure and (b) edema.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) Plasma colloid-osmotic (oncotic) pressure is the osmotic force generated mainly by plasma proteins, especially albumin, that draws water into capillaries. (b) Edema is abnormal excess accumulation of fluid in the interstitial tissues or body cavities.

---

### Q8: Name two types of gated ion channels and state what opens or closes each type.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Voltage-gated channels open or close in response to changes in membrane potential. Ligand-gated channels respond to binding of a chemical messenger, such as a neurotransmitter. Mechanically gated channels respond to stretch, pressure, or other deformation and are another valid example.

---

### Q9: Mention the significance of the strength-duration curve.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The curve relates the minimum stimulus strength needed to excite a tissue to stimulus duration. Rheobase and chronaxie characterize excitability and help compare excitable tissues. Changes in curve shape or position can help assess denervation and nerve or muscle function.

---

### Q10: During stress and excitation, state the effect on gastrointestinal smooth muscle and sweat glands, including the relevant receptors.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Sympathetic activity generally reduces gastrointestinal motility and tone; β₂-receptor activation relaxes much of the gut wall, while α₁-receptor activation can contract sphincters. Sweating increases through muscarinic M₃ receptors on eccrine glands, which are supplied by sympathetic cholinergic fibers.

---

### Q11: An injury affects the first four sacral spinal segments. Name the injured nerve and state its physiological effects on the urinary bladder, rectum, and genitalia.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The relevant parasympathetic pathway is the pelvic splanchnic nerves (nervi erigentes), arising mainly from S2–S4. They contract the detrusor and promote relaxation of the bladder outlet for micturition; increase rectal motility and facilitate defecation; and mediate vasodilation involved in genital erection.

---

### Q12: List three gastrointestinal hormones that decrease food intake.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Cholecystokinin (CCK), glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), and peptide YY (PYY) promote satiety and reduce food intake.

---

### Q13: A woman has a respiratory quotient below one. Enumerate factors that decrease the RQ below unity.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** A respiratory quotient below 1 reflects predominant fat oxidation, whose RQ is about 0.7. It is associated with fasting or starvation, a low-carbohydrate or high-fat diet, and prolonged exercise when fat supplies a greater share of energy. Sauna-induced water loss does not itself reduce body fat or lower RQ.

---

### Q14: Which statement best describes removal of introns from mRNA precursors?

- **B)** It is controlled by RNA polymerase.
- **C)** It is regulated by RNA helicase.

### Q15: The template DNA strand is 5′-GATATCCATTAGTGAC-3′. Which RNA sequence is produced?

- **A)** 5′-CAGUGAUUACCUAUAG-3′
- **B)** 5′-CTATAGGTAATCACTG-3′
- **C)** 5′-GTCACTAATGGATATC-3′
- **D)** 5′-GUCACUAAUGGAUAUC-3′

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** derived
**EXP:** RNA polymerase reads the template 3′ to 5′ and synthesizes complementary RNA 5′ to 3′. Because the template is printed 5′ to 3′, reverse-complement it and substitute U for T; option D results.

### Q16: Which statement best describes the genetic code?

- **A)** There are 64 codons, all of which code for amino acids.
- **B)** The code is degenerate.
- **C)** Information is stored as sets of dinucleotide repeats called codons.
- **D)** Information is stored as sets of tetranucleotide repeats called codons.
