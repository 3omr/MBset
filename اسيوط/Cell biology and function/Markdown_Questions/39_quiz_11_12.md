## Quiz weeks 11–12

### Q1: Lipid-soluble molecules, such as alcohols, cannot diffuse through the plasma membrane.

- **A)** False
- **B)** True

**Correct Answer:** A
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** The source marks False. Lipid-soluble molecules can partition into the phospholipid bilayer and cross it by diffusion.

---

### Q2: How do fat-soluble molecules normally enter a cell?

- **A)** They pass through protein pores in the cell membrane.
- **B)** They never enter the cell.
- **C)** They are absorbed by phagocytosis.
- **D)** They dissolve in the lipid layers of the membrane and enter by diffusion.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Fat-soluble molecules can dissolve in the lipid bilayer and cross by simple diffusion.

---

### Q3: The rate of diffusion of a particle across a membrane will increase if what changes?

- **A)** The number of protein channels increases.
- **B)** The membrane becomes thicker.
- **C)** The particle becomes larger.
- **D)** The temperature decreases.

**Correct Answer:** A
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** More channels increase the available pathway for channel-mediated diffusion. Greater membrane thickness, larger particle size, or lower temperature generally slows diffusion.

---

### Q4: Which solution is hypertonic to red blood cells?

- **A)** 0.10% NaCl
- **B)** 0.9% NaCl
- **C)** 0.5% NaCl
- **D)** 1.0% NaCl

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** A 1.0% NaCl solution is more concentrated than the approximately isotonic 0.9% NaCl solution and is therefore hypertonic to red blood cells.
