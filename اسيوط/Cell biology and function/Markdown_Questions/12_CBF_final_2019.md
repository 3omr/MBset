### Q1: Describe two causes of edema.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Edema can result from increased capillary hydrostatic pressure, which pushes fluid into the interstitial space, or decreased plasma colloid osmotic pressure, commonly from hypoalbuminemia, which reduces fluid reabsorption. Other causes include increased capillary permeability, lymphatic obstruction, and renal sodium retention.

### Q2: Describe the mechanism of the Na⁺/K⁺ pump.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Three intracellular Na⁺ ions bind to the pump. ATP phosphorylates the pump, causing a conformational change that releases Na⁺ outside the cell. Two extracellular K⁺ ions then bind; dephosphorylation returns the pump to its original conformation and releases K⁺ inside. Each cycle uses one ATP to move three Na⁺ out and two K⁺ in against their gradients.
**Source Question:** 2
**Year:** 2019
**Tag:** Exams, Final 2019

### Q3: Enumerate the sites at which saltatory conduction can occur and mention its function.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Saltatory conduction occurs in myelinated nerve fibers of the peripheral and central nervous systems. Action potentials are regenerated at the nodes of Ranvier, where voltage-gated Na⁺ channels are concentrated, so the impulse appears to jump from node to node. This increases conduction velocity and reduces the energy needed to restore ion gradients.

### Q4: Mention the sites of muscarinic receptors.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Muscarinic receptors are found on parasympathetic effector cells, including cardiac muscle, smooth muscle, and glands. They are also present on eccrine sweat glands supplied by sympathetic cholinergic fibers and in the central nervous system.

### Q5: Discuss the effects of the sympathetic nervous system on the cardiopulmonary division.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** In the heart, sympathetic stimulation acting mainly through β₁ receptors increases heart rate, myocardial contractility, and conduction velocity, thereby increasing cardiac output. In the lungs, β₂-receptor activation relaxes bronchial smooth muscle and causes bronchodilation, improving airflow.

### Q6: Mention the effects of exposure to hot weather.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Heat exposure causes cutaneous vasodilation and increases sweating, promoting heat loss by radiation and evaporation. Heat production also falls through reduced muscle activity and other behavioral or physiological adjustments. Prolonged sweating may cause water and electrolyte loss.

### Q7: Define specific dynamic action and mention its causes and the factors affecting it.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Specific dynamic action (SDA), or the thermic effect of food, is the rise in energy expenditure above the resting level after food intake. It reflects the energy used for digestion, absorption, transport, metabolism, and storage of nutrients. Protein produces the greatest SDA, carbohydrate an intermediate effect, and fat the smallest. The amount and composition of the meal, nutritional state, and sympathetic activity also affect it.
**Source Question:** 7

### Q9: Mention three specific characteristics of glycolysis in red blood cells.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Mature red blood cells lack mitochondria, so glycolysis is their only source of ATP and proceeds anaerobically to lactate. The Rapoport–Luebering shunt produces 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate, which facilitates oxygen release from hemoglobin. Glycolytic NADH also supports reduction of methemoglobin to functional hemoglobin.

**EXP:** The ketone bodies are acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, and acetone. Ketoacidosis may occur in uncontrolled diabetes mellitus, prolonged fasting or starvation, and alcoholic ketoacidosis.

### Q11: Discuss the role of the liver in regulation of blood glucose level.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** After a meal, the liver takes up glucose and stores it as glycogen or uses it in metabolic pathways. Between meals, it maintains blood glucose by glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis from lactate, glycerol, and glucogenic amino acids. Hepatic glucose-6-phosphatase allows free glucose to be released into blood; insulin favors storage, whereas glucagon promotes hepatic glucose output.
**Source Question:** 11
**Year:** 2019
**Tag:** Exams, Final 2019

### Q12: Enumerate the enzymes involved in DNA replication in prokaryotes.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The major enzymes are DNA helicase, primase, DNA polymerase III, DNA polymerase I, DNA ligase, and DNA gyrase (a topoisomerase). RNase H and other nucleases can also participate in removal of RNA primers. Single-strand DNA-binding proteins stabilize unwound DNA but are not enzymes.

### Q13: List six characteristics of the genetic code.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The genetic code is triplet, degenerate, unambiguous, non-overlapping, comma-less, and nearly universal. It is read in the 5′ to 3′ direction; AUG is the usual start codon, and UAA, UAG, and UGA are stop codons.
**Source Question:** 13
**Year:** 2019

### Q14: Mention three metabolic or diagnostic roles of transaminases.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Transamination enables synthesis of nonessential amino acids and transfers amino nitrogen to α-ketoglutarate to form glutamate, helping channel nitrogen toward disposal. It also links amino-acid carbon skeletons with energy metabolism and gluconeogenesis. Serum ALT and AST activities are used as markers of tissue injury, especially hepatocellular injury; AST can also rise with cardiac or skeletal-muscle injury.
**Source Question:** 14
**Year:** 2019
**Tag:** Exams, Final 2019

### Q15: Enumerate the post-transcriptional modifications of mRNA.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Eukaryotic pre-mRNA is processed by addition of a 7-methylguanosine cap at the 5′ end, cleavage and addition of a poly-A tail at the 3′ end, and removal of introns with joining of exons by RNA splicing. Some transcripts also undergo RNA editing or alternative splicing.
