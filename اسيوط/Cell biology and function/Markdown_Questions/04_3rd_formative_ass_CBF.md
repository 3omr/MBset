### Q1: A 16-year-old female has xanthomas on her eyelids and a rare genetic deficiency of lipoprotein lipase, with type I hyperlipidemia and elevated chylomicrons. In which type of cell are triacylglycerols packaged into chylomicrons?

- **A)** Muscle cell
- **B)** Liver cell
- **C)** Heart cell
- **D)** Intestinal epithelial cell
- **E)** Adipose cell

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Question:** 1

### Q2: A professional cyclist consumes carbohydrates after workouts to support muscle glycogen storage. The activity of muscle glycogen synthase in resting muscle is increased by which of the following?

- **C)** Epinephrine
- **D)** Phosphorylation

**Answer Source:** derived

**EXP:** Insulin promotes glycogen synthesis by activating protein phosphatase activity, which dephosphorylates and activates glycogen synthase in skeletal muscle.

### Q3: Which fatty acid is not synthesized in the human body and must be supplied in the diet?

- **A)** Stearic acid
- **B)** Palmitic acid
- **C)** Linoleic acid
- **D)** Oleic acid

**Correct Answer:** C
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Question:** 3
**EXP:** Humans cannot introduce a double bond beyond carbon 9 of a fatty-acid chain. Linoleic acid is therefore essential and must come from the diet.

### Q4: A lesion in the cervical sympathetic chain causes which finding?

- **A)** Increased sweating
- **B)** Vasoconstriction of skin blood vessels
- **C)** Mydriasis (dilation of the pupil)
- **D)** Drooping of the upper eyelid

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Question:** 4
**EXP:** Cervical sympathetic denervation can cause Horner syndrome, which includes mild ptosis from loss of sympathetic input to the superior tarsal muscle, along with miosis and reduced facial sweating.

### Q5: Which statement about acetylcholine is true?

- **A)** It is broken down enzymatically by monoamine oxidase.
- **B)** It binds to alpha and beta receptors in the effector organ.
- **C)** It is the chemical transmitter released by all postganglionic nerve fibers.
- **D)** It makes reading difficult because of relaxation of the ciliary muscle.
- **E)** It is synthesized and released by some sympathetic postganglionic nerve fibers.

**Correct Answer:** E
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Question:** 5
**EXP:** Most sympathetic postganglionic fibers release norepinephrine, but cholinergic sympathetic postganglionic fibers innervate eccrine sweat glands. Acetylcholine is hydrolyzed by acetylcholinesterase and acts at cholinergic, not alpha or beta adrenergic, receptors.
**Tag:** Department, Formative, Week 3

### Q6: All of the following statements about the vagus nerve are true except:

- **A)** It increases intestinal peristaltic movement.
- **B)** It has no direct effect on ventricular contraction.
- **C)** It causes bronchoconstriction.
- **D)** It produces micturition.
- **E)** It reduces the contractility of the heart.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Question:** 6
**EXP:** Micturition is controlled primarily by sacral parasympathetic outflow through the pelvic nerves, not the vagus nerve. Vagal activity supports gastrointestinal motility, bronchoconstriction, and reduced cardiac rate and contractility.
**Tag:** Department, Formative, Week 3

### Q7: Which enzyme is used in both glycolysis and gluconeogenesis?

- **A)** Phosphofructokinase-1
- **B)** Hexokinase
- **C)** 3-phosphoglycerate kinase
- **D)** Glucose-6-phosphatase

**Correct Answer:** C
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Question:** 7
**EXP:** 3-Phosphoglycerate kinase catalyzes a reversible step used in both pathways. The irreversible glycolytic steps catalyzed by phosphofructokinase-1 and hexokinase are bypassed by different enzymes during gluconeogenesis.
**Tag:** Department, Formative, Week 3

### Q11: In humans, gluconeogenesis:

- **A)** Helps reduce blood glucose after a carbohydrate-rich meal
- **B)** Is activated by insulin
- **C)** Can result in conversion of protein into blood glucose
- **D)** Requires hexokinase

**Correct Answer:** C
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Question:** 11
**EXP:** Glucogenic amino acids from protein can supply carbon skeletons for hepatic gluconeogenesis. Insulin suppresses, rather than activates, this pathway; hexokinase is not used for the final glucose-release step.

### Q14: What is achieved by the Cori cycle, which involves lactate metabolism?

- **A)** Lactate is taken up by the liver for synthesis of glucose.
- **B)** Metabolic alkalosis is prevented by removing lactate from blood.
- **C)** Lactate is used by muscle for fuel.
- **D)** Lactate is removed from blood because it inhibits erythrocyte glycolysis.

**Correct Answer:** A
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Question:** 14
**EXP:** Lactate released by anaerobic tissues is transported to the liver and converted to glucose by gluconeogenesis. The glucose can then return to peripheral tissues, completing the Cori cycle.
**Tag:** Department, Formative, Week 3

### Q15: Which statement is true about type I diabetes mellitus?

- **A)** Pancreatic cells remain normal.
- **B)** It results from a person's cells becoming insulin-resistant.
- **C)** People with type I diabetes must take regular insulin injections.
- **D)** Untreated type I diabetes does not predispose to coma.

**Correct Answer:** C
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Question:** 15
**EXP:** Type I diabetes results from severe insulin deficiency, usually due to autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells. Lifelong exogenous insulin is required, and untreated disease can cause diabetic ketoacidosis and coma.
**Tag:** Department, Formative, Week 3
