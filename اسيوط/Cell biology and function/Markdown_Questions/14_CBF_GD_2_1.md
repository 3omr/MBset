## SDL 5 — Integration of metabolism

### Q1: A 30-year-old man has been fasting for several days. Which substance can the brain use as an alternate energy source, reducing its need for glucose?

- **A)** Fatty acid
- **B)** β-hydroxybutyrate
- **C)** Glycerol
- **D)** β-carotene
- **E)** Alanine

**Correct Answer:** B
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** During prolonged fasting, the liver converts fatty acids into ketone bodies. The brain can use β-hydroxybutyrate, which reduces but does not eliminate its glucose requirement.

---

### Q2: After 24 hours of fasting, which reaction contributes most to maintaining a healthy adult's blood glucose level?

- **A)** Glycogen → glucose-1-phosphate
- **B)** Acetyl-CoA → palmitate
- **C)** Oxaloacetate → phosphoenolpyruvate
- **D)** Fructose 6-phosphate → fructose 1,6-bisphosphate
- **E)** Acetoacetyl-CoA → 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA

**Correct Answer:** C
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** After a day of fasting, hepatic gluconeogenesis is an important source of blood glucose. Oxaloacetate is converted to phosphoenolpyruvate early in this pathway.

---

### Q3: Which type of metabolite can be used to generate glucose during severe starvation?

- **A)** Amino acids
- **B)** Fats
- **C)** Glycogen
- **D)** Starch

**Correct Answer:** A
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Glucogenic amino acids can supply carbon skeletons for gluconeogenesis. Even-chain fatty acids do not produce net glucose in humans.

---

### Q4: A 7-year-old girl has severe polyuria and polydipsia, and ketones are found in her urine. What is the most likely source of the ketones?

- **A)** Fatty acid breakdown
- **B)** Protein breakdown
- **C)** Glycogenolysis
- **D)** Gluconeogenesis
- **E)** The side chain of cholesterol

**Correct Answer:** A
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Increased fatty-acid β-oxidation supplies acetyl-CoA for ketogenesis, particularly when insulin is deficient or carbohydrate availability is low.

---

### Q5: Which statement about brain metabolism during starvation is true?

- **A)** The brain can use glucogenic amino acids for energy.
- **B)** The brain can use only glucose as fuel.
- **C)** Up to one quarter of the brain's energy requirement can come from fatty acids.
- **D)** Up to one half of the brain's energy requirement can come from ketone bodies.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** With prolonged starvation, the brain adapts to use ketone bodies for a substantial share of its energy. Long-chain fatty acids do not cross the blood–brain barrier in amounts that make them a major brain fuel.

---

### Q6: An individual on a long-term diet has reduced transcription of which enzyme?

- **A)** Malic enzyme
- **B)** Carnitine acyltransferase 1
- **C)** Carnitine acyltransferase 2
- **D)** Medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase
- **E)** Fatty acyl-CoA synthetase

**Correct Answer:** A
**Answer Source:** derived
**EXP:** Malic enzyme supports NADPH production for fatty-acid synthesis, a pathway downregulated during prolonged energy restriction. The source has no highlighted or printed answer for this item.

## SDL 5 — Free radicals (source numbering restarts at 1)

### Q8: A free radical has which defining property?

- **A)** Unpaired electrons
- **B)** Paired electrons
- **C)** No electrons
- **D)** An even number of electrons

**Correct Answer:** A
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** A free radical contains one or more unpaired electrons, which makes it chemically reactive.

---

### Q9: Which of the following is not an oxygen-derived free radical (ROS)?

- **A)** Superoxide anion
- **B)** Nitrogen dioxide
- **C)** Hydrogen peroxide
- **D)** Hydroxyl radical

**Correct Answer:** B
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** The source marks nitrogen dioxide, a reactive nitrogen species. The wording combines "free radical" and "ROS" imprecisely: hydrogen peroxide is an ROS but is not itself a free radical.

---

### Q10: Which is an endogenous source of free radicals?

- **A)** Cigarette smoke
- **B)** X-rays and gamma rays
- **C)** Xanthine oxidase
- **D)** Aniline dye

**Correct Answer:** C
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Xanthine oxidase is an endogenous enzyme that can generate reactive oxygen species during purine metabolism.

---

### Q11: Chronic granulomatous disease results from deficiency of which enzyme?

- **A)** Superoxide dismutase
- **B)** Myeloperoxidase
- **C)** Nitric oxide synthase
- **D)** NADPH oxidase

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** NADPH oxidase generates the superoxide used in the phagocyte respiratory burst. Its deficiency impairs microbial killing and causes chronic granulomatous disease.

---

### Q12: Which lipid is most affected by free-radical oxidation?

- **A)** Long-chain fatty acids
- **B)** Saturated fatty acids
- **C)** Monounsaturated fatty acids
- **D)** Polyunsaturated fatty acids

**Correct Answer:** D
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Polyunsaturated fatty acids are especially susceptible to lipid peroxidation because they contain multiple double bonds and readily abstractable hydrogen atoms.

---

### Q13: All of the following are preventive antioxidants except which one?

- **A)** Glutathione peroxidase
- **B)** Ferritin
- **C)** Vitamin E
- **D)** Ceruloplasmin

**Correct Answer:** C
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Vitamin E is a chain-breaking antioxidant that interrupts lipid-peroxidation chains. The other listed agents are commonly grouped among preventive antioxidants.

---

### Q14: Which is a tertiary antioxidant?

- **A)** Vitamin A
- **B)** Methionine sulfoxide reductase
- **C)** Catalase
- **D)** Transferrin

**Correct Answer:** B
**Answer Source:** marked
**EXP:** Methionine sulfoxide reductase repairs oxidized methionine residues in proteins, a form of tertiary antioxidant defense.
