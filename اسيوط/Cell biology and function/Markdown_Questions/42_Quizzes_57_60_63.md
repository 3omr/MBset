### Q1: Which statement best describes removal of introns from mRNA precursors?

- **A)** It requires no enzymes.
- **B)** It is regulated by RNA helicase.
- **C)** It is RNA-catalyzed in the absence of protein.
- **D)** It is controlled by RNA polymerase.
- **E)** It is carried out by spliceosomes.

**Correct Answer:** E
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** The source key identifies spliceosomes as the machinery that removes introns from pre-mRNA.

### Q2: Which statement best describes synthesis of mammalian (eukaryotic) mRNA?

- **A)** Mammalian mRNA undergoes minimal modification during maturation.
- **B)** Each mRNA often encodes several different proteins.
- **C)** The RNA sequence transcribed from a gene is virtually identical to the mRNA that exits the nucleus.
- **D)** It is produced from the same genes that produce rRNA and tRNA.
- **E)** The sequence of codons in mature mRNA corresponds linearly to the amino-acid sequence of its encoded protein.

**Correct Answer:** E
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** The source key identifies the colinear relationship between the coding sequence and its encoded amino-acid sequence. The stem's reference to the entire mature mRNA is imprecise because untranslated regions are also present.

### Q4: Which statement best describes the genetic code?

- **A)** The code is degenerate.
- **B)** There are 64 codons, all of which code for amino acids.
- **C)** Information is stored as sets of dinucleotide repeats called codons.
- **D)** Information is stored as sets of tetranucleotide repeats called codons.
- **E)** The sequence of codons in a gene has an exact linear correspondence to the amino-acid sequence of its translated protein.

**Correct Answer:** A
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** The genetic code is degenerate because most amino acids are encoded by more than one codon. Stop codons do not encode amino acids.

### Q5: How does erythromycin inhibit protein synthesis in susceptible bacteria?

- **A)** It inhibits initiation.
- **B)** It acts as an mRNA analogue.
- **C)** It mimics mRNA binding.
- **D)** It causes premature chain termination.
- **E)** It inhibits translocation by binding to the 50S ribosomal subunit.

**Correct Answer:** E
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Erythromycin binds the bacterial 50S ribosomal subunit and inhibits translocation during protein synthesis.

### Q6: Which statement best describes the so-called caps of RNA molecules?

- **A)** They are unique to eukaryotic mRNA.
- **B)** They allow correct translation of prokaryotic mRNA.
- **C)** They allow tRNA to be processed.
- **D)** They occur at the 3′ end of tRNA.
- **E)** They are composed of poly-A.

**Correct Answer:** A
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** The 5′ cap is a modified guanine nucleotide found on eukaryotic mRNA. A poly-A tail is added at the 3′ end and is not the cap.

### Q7: Which option gives the correct order of these protein-synthesis events: (1) peptide-bond formation; (2) loading of the small ribosomal subunit with initiation factors, mRNA, and initiator aminoacyl-tRNA; (3) ribosome translocation by three bases; (4) joining of the large ribosomal subunit; (5) delivery of aminoacyl-tRNA to the A site?

- **A)** 1, 5, 4, 2, 3
- **B)** 1, 5, 4, 3, 2
- **C)** 3, 1, 5, 4, 2
- **D)** 3, 2, 1, 5, 4
- **E)** 3, 4, 5, 2, 1

**Correct Answer:** C
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** The Moodle feedback marks C. However, the usual forward order from the listed events is 2 → 4 → 5 → 1 → 3, which is not among the options; the source-marked sequence is retained and flagged as inconsistent.

### Q8: How many high-energy phosphate-bond equivalents are used to activate an amino acid for protein synthesis?

- **A)** Three
- **B)** Two
- **C)** One
- **D)** Zero
- **E)** Four

**Correct Answer:** B
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase converts ATP to AMP and pyrophosphate, consuming two high-energy phosphate-bond equivalents.

### Q10: Sickle-cell anemia results from a glutamic-acid-to-valine substitution at position 6 of β-globin. What is the mutation mechanism?

- **A)** Crossing over
- **B)** Two-base insertion
- **C)** Single-base insertion
- **D)** Three-base deletion
- **E)** Single-base substitution

**Correct Answer:** E
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** The β-globin Glu6Val change is a missense point mutation caused by a single-nucleotide substitution.

### Q11: Sickle-cell anemia is a well-studied example of which mutation type?

- **A)** Frameshift
- **B)** Missense
- **C)** Insertion
- **D)** Nonsense
- **E)** Deletion

**Correct Answer:** B
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** The β-globin mutation changes one amino acid, making it a missense mutation.

### Q12: Which sequence describes the events of nucleotide-excision repair?

- **A)** Sealing, recognition, nicking, excision, replacement
- **B)** Nicking, excision, replacement, sealing, recognition
- **C)** Nicking, sealing, recognition, excision, replacement
- **D)** Nicking, recognition, excision, sealing, replacement
- **E)** Recognition, nicking, excision, replacement, sealing

**Correct Answer:** E
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Nucleotide-excision repair recognizes the lesion, cuts the damaged strand, removes the oligonucleotide, fills the gap, and seals the remaining nick.

### Q13: A child with extreme sun sensitivity and skin lesions is suspected to have xeroderma pigmentosum. Which repair pathway is defective?

- **A)** Transcription
- **B)** Translation
- **C)** DNA replication
- **D)** Base-excision repair
- **E)** Nucleotide-excision repair

**Correct Answer:** E
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Xeroderma pigmentosum is caused by defects in nucleotide-excision repair of ultraviolet-induced DNA lesions.

### Q14: Which spontaneous base deamination can cause a mutation if unrepaired?

- **A)** A → G
- **B)** G → A
- **C)** T → U
- **D)** U → C
- **E)** C → U

**Correct Answer:** E
**Answer Source:** key
**EXP:** Cytosine deamination produces uracil in DNA. If unrepaired, it can result in a C:G to T:A transition after replication.
