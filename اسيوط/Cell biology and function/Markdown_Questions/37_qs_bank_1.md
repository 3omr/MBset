### Q1: A 30-year-old man has been fasting for several days. Which substance can the brain use as an alternate energy source, reducing its need for glucose?

- **A)** Fatty acid
- **B)** β-hydroxybutyrate
- **C)** Glycerol
- **D)** β-carotene

**Correct Answer:** B
**Answer Source:** derived
**EXP:** During prolonged fasting, the liver produces ketone bodies. The brain can use β-hydroxybutyrate, reducing its glucose requirement.

## Essay questions

### Q41: A child needs surgical nutrition support. Define a high-biological-value protein and name two amino acids that become essential under specific conditions.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** A high-biological-value protein is highly digestible and supplies all essential amino acids in amounts and proportions adequate for protein synthesis. Arginine and glutamine can become conditionally essential during rapid growth, severe illness, or major stress.

---

### Q42: In a child with impaired tyrosine synthesis and hypopigmentation, name the enzyme that converts phenylalanine to tyrosine, two products synthesized from tyrosine, and the basis of the developmental delay.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Phenylalanine hydroxylase converts phenylalanine to tyrosine, using tetrahydrobiopterin and oxygen. Tyrosine is a precursor for catecholamines, thyroid hormones, and melanin; two examples are catecholamines and melanin. If the defect is phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency (phenylketonuria), phenylalanine and its metabolites accumulate and impair brain development; reduced tyrosine also lowers melanin synthesis.

---

### Q43: A patient with deep-vein thrombosis is treated with heparin. Classify heparin and list two functions.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Heparin is a highly sulfated acidic glycosaminoglycan (mucopolysaccharide). It acts as an anticoagulant by enhancing antithrombin activity and can release endothelial lipoprotein lipase into the circulation.

---

### Q44: A patient has severe hyperglycemia and metabolic acidosis. Give the diagnosis, one laboratory test used to monitor the condition, and the cause of the low blood pH.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The presentation is diabetic ketoacidosis. Serial blood glucose and blood β-hydroxybutyrate or urine/blood ketones monitor the acute episode; HbA1c assesses longer-term glycemic control. Increased hepatic ketogenesis produces acidic β-hydroxybutyrate and acetoacetate, lowering blood pH.

---

### Q45: For pyruvate dehydrogenase, state its intracellular location, one activator of pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase, and the products of its reaction.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex is in the mitochondrial matrix. ATP activates pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase. The complex catalyzes oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA and carbon dioxide, producing NADH.

---

### Q46: Which vitamin deficiency causes scurvy? List three functions of that vitamin.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Scurvy is caused by vitamin C deficiency. Vitamin C is required for hydroxylation of proline and lysine during collagen synthesis, supports antioxidant defense, and enhances non-heme iron absorption.

---

### Q47: Define competitive enzyme inhibition and list three factors that affect enzyme activity.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** A competitive inhibitor competes with the substrate for the enzyme's active site. Increasing substrate concentration can overcome the inhibition; apparent Km increases while Vmax is unchanged. Enzyme activity is affected by temperature, pH, substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, inhibitors, activators, and cofactor availability.

---

### Q48: Complete the statements about oncogenes and tumor-suppressor genes, with one example of each.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Oncogenes are activated or mutated forms of proto-oncogenes that promote cell growth or proliferation; examples include RAS and MYC. Tumor-suppressor genes normally restrain cell proliferation, maintain genomic integrity, or promote apoptosis; examples include TP53 and RB1.

---

### Q49: List two examples or functions for each: uncouplers of oxidative phosphorylation; glutathione; telomeres; post-transcriptional mRNA modifications; and glycogen-storage diseases.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** **Uncouplers:** 2,4-dinitrophenol and uncoupling protein 1 (thermogenin). **Glutathione:** reduces peroxides through glutathione peroxidase and helps maintain protein sulfhydryl groups in a reduced state. **Telomeres:** protect chromosome ends from degradation or end-to-end fusion and provide repeat DNA that buffers end-replication loss. **mRNA processing:** 5′ capping, intron splicing, and 3′ polyadenylation. **Glycogen-storage diseases:** von Gierke disease (type I) and Pompe disease (type II).

---

### Q50: Define positive feedback and give another example besides labor.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Positive feedback occurs when a response amplifies the initial stimulus, driving the process further in the same direction until an external event stops it. Blood coagulation is another example; activated clotting factors promote activation of additional factors.

---

### Q52: Why do myelinated axons require less energy than unmyelinated axons of the same diameter and length?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** In myelinated axons, action potentials propagate saltatorily between nodes of Ranvier. Ion movement across the membrane is concentrated at the nodes, so fewer ions cross overall and the Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase uses less ATP to restore the gradients.

---

### Q53: List factors that decrease cellular excitability.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Hyperpolarization, an increased threshold, reduced voltage-gated Na⁺-channel availability or blockade, and increased extracellular Ca²⁺ can reduce excitability.

---

### Q54: During stress and sympathetic excitation, describe the effects on the heart and spleen and name the receptors involved.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** In the heart, β₁-adrenergic receptor activation increases heart rate and contractility. In the spleen, α₁-adrenergic activation contracts the capsule and trabeculae, promoting splenic emptying.

---

### Q55: An injury to sacral spinal segments S1–S4 may affect which nerve pathway? Give one effect in each of three organs.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The pelvic splanchnic nerves arise mainly from S2–S4. Their parasympathetic effects include detrusor contraction and urination in the bladder, increased motility and defecation in the distal colon/rectum, and vasodilation supporting erection in erectile tissue.

---

### Q56: Explain how leptin regulates food intake.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Leptin is secreted by adipocytes in proportion to energy stores. It acts on hypothalamic circuits to reduce appetite, increase satiety, and support energy expenditure; leptin resistance can weaken these effects.

---

### Q57: Explain the mechanism of fever.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Microbial products and inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1, IL-6, and TNF stimulate prostaglandin E₂ production in the hypothalamus. PGE₂ raises the temperature set point, triggering vasoconstriction and shivering until body temperature reaches the new set point. When the set point falls, sweating and vasodilation dissipate heat.
