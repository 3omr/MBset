### Q1: A young child needs surgery after an automobile accident. The dietary plan includes high-biological-value protein and supplements an amino acid usually considered nonessential. (a) Define proteins of high biological value. (b) List two amino acids considered essential under specific needs.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) High-biological-value proteins contain all essential amino acids in adequate proportions and are efficiently digested and utilized; examples include egg and milk proteins. (b) Arginine and histidine may become conditionally essential during growth, prematurity, illness, or recovery when synthesis cannot meet increased requirements.

### Q2: A one-year-old child has delayed mental development and skin hypopigmentation due to a defect in tyrosine synthesis. (a) Name the enzyme required to synthesize tyrosine from phenylalanine. (b) Mention two biomolecules synthesized from tyrosine. (c) Explain the cause of mental retardation in this child.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) Phenylalanine hydroxylase converts phenylalanine to tyrosine; the reaction requires tetrahydrobiopterin and oxygen. (b) Tyrosine is a precursor for catecholamines and melanin; it also contributes to thyroid-hormone synthesis. (c) In phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency, phenylalanine and phenylketones accumulate and impair brain development; reduced tyrosine availability also limits melanin synthesis.

### Q3: A bedridden older adult develops leg swelling due to an obstructive deep-vein thrombosis and is started on heparin. (a) What type of molecule is heparin? (b) List two functions of heparin.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) Heparin is a highly sulfated glycosaminoglycan. (b) It acts as an anticoagulant by enhancing antithrombin inhibition of thrombin and factor Xa; it also releases lipoprotein lipase from the vascular endothelium into plasma.

### Q4: An obese man has polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, random blood glucose of 460 mg/dL, and blood pH of 7.13. (a) Give the diagnosis. (b) Mention one laboratory test for monitoring the condition. (c) Explain why the blood pH is decreased.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) Diabetes mellitus with diabetic ketoacidosis. (b) Glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) is used to assess longer-term glycemic control; blood glucose and ketones are also followed during acute treatment. (c) Severe insulin deficiency increases lipolysis and hepatic ketogenesis. Accumulated acetoacetate and β-hydroxybutyrate consume bicarbonate and lower blood pH.

### Q5: A woman with arsenic poisoning has inhibition of pyruvate dehydrogenase. (a) State the intracellular location of this enzyme complex. (b) Mention one activator of pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase. (c) Complete: Pyruvate dehydrogenase catalyzes the oxidative decarboxylation of ... forming ... .

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex is in the mitochondrial matrix. (b) ATP, NADH, or acetyl-CoA activates pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase, which phosphorylates and inhibits the complex. (c) It catalyzes the oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate, forming acetyl-CoA, carbon dioxide, and NADH.

### Q6: A four-year-old boy has weakness, anemia, gum disease, and delayed teething with skin hemorrhages and is diagnosed with scurvy. (a) Lack of which vitamin causes this disease? (b) List two functions of this vitamin.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) Vitamin C (ascorbic acid). (b) It is required for hydroxylation of proline and lysine during collagen synthesis and enhances intestinal absorption of non-heme iron. It also acts as a reducing antioxidant and participates in carnitine and catecholamine synthesis.

### Q7: A patient with colorectal cancer is treated with 5-fluorouracil, which inhibits thymidylate synthase competitively. (a) Define competitive enzyme inhibition. (b) List two factors that affect enzyme activity.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) In competitive inhibition, an inhibitor competes with the substrate for the enzyme's active site. Increasing substrate concentration can overcome the inhibition; the apparent Km increases while Vmax is unchanged. (b) Examples include temperature, pH, enzyme concentration, substrate concentration, cofactors, and inhibitors.

### Q8: Complete the statements: (a) Oncogenes are mutated versions of genes that promote ... such as ... . (b) Tumor-suppressor genes normally ... such as ... .

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) Oncogenes are mutated or overexpressed proto-oncogenes that promote cell growth or proliferation, such as RAS. (b) Tumor-suppressor genes normally restrain cell-cycle progression, repair DNA, or trigger apoptosis, such as TP53 or RB1.

### Q9: List two of each: (a) uncouplers of oxidative phosphorylation; (b) functions of glutathione; (c) functions of telomeres; (d) post-transcriptional modifications of mRNA; and (e) glycogen-storage diseases.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) 2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP) and FCCP; thermogenin is a physiological uncoupler. (b) Glutathione reduces peroxides and maintains protein sulfhydryl groups in the reduced state. (c) Telomeres protect chromosome ends from degradation and end-to-end fusion, and help maintain chromosome integrity during replication. (d) 5′ capping, 3′ polyadenylation, and intron removal by splicing. (e) Von Gierke disease (type I) and Pompe disease (type II); other examples include Cori, Andersen, and McArdle diseases.

### Q10: A pregnant woman in labor has severe lower abdominal pain. (a) Define positive feedback. (b) Give another example.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) Positive feedback occurs when a response amplifies the initial stimulus, driving the process further until a terminating event stops it. During labor, cervical stretch promotes oxytocin release, which strengthens uterine contractions and increases cervical stretch. (b) Other examples include blood clotting and the milk-ejection reflex.

### Q11: Mention factors that affect plasma-membrane permeability.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Permeability depends on solute size, lipid solubility, polarity and charge, and the concentration or electrochemical gradient. It is also affected by membrane lipid composition and fluidity, temperature, and the number and state of specific channels, carriers, or pores.

### Q12: Explain why conduction of an action potential in a myelinated axon requires less energy than in an unmyelinated axon of the same diameter and length.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Myelin insulates the internodal membrane, so depolarizing current travels farther and action potentials are regenerated mainly at the nodes of Ranvier. Fewer membrane regions exchange Na⁺ and K⁺, so the Na⁺/K⁺ pump uses less ATP to restore the ionic gradients.

### Q13: A drug is developed to decrease pain sensation. What factors decrease neuronal excitability?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Neuronal excitability decreases with membrane hyperpolarization, increased extracellular Ca²⁺, reduced Na⁺ entry or Na⁺-channel blockade, and conditions that raise the threshold for firing. Local anesthetics reduce excitability by blocking voltage-gated Na⁺ channels.

### Q14: During stress and excitation, describe the effects on the heart and spleen and name the relevant receptors.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Sympathetic stimulation acts mainly through β₁ receptors in the heart to increase heart rate and contractility. It contracts the splenic capsule mainly through α₁ adrenergic receptors, mobilizing stored blood cells into the circulation.

### Q15: A patient has an injury to the first four sacral segments of the spinal cord. (a) Name the injured nerve. (b) Enumerate three effects of this nerve in three different organs.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (a) The relevant sacral parasympathetic outflow travels in the pelvic splanchnic nerves (nervi erigentes), arising chiefly from S2–S4. (b) It contracts the detrusor and relaxes the internal urethral outlet to promote micturition; increases motility and secretion in the distal colon to support defecation; and mediates vasodilation involved in penile erection.

### Q16: Explain the role of leptin in regulating food intake in a person dieting to maintain an ideal weight.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Leptin is secreted by adipocytes in proportion to energy stores and signals the hypothalamus through transport across the blood-brain barrier. It suppresses appetite by inhibiting orexigenic NPY/AgRP neurons and stimulating anorexigenic POMC/CART pathways, while increasing energy expenditure. During weight loss, falling leptin can increase hunger and reduce energy expenditure.

### Q17: Explain the mechanism of fever during tonsillitis.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Microbial products stimulate immune cells to release endogenous pyrogens, including IL-1, IL-6, and TNF. These promote prostaglandin E₂ production in the hypothalamus, raising the temperature set point. The body then conserves and generates heat through cutaneous vasoconstriction and shivering until core temperature reaches the new set point.
