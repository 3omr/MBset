### Q1: A patient with progressive memory impairment has extracellular neuritic plaques and intraneuronal tangles. What is the probable diagnosis?

### Q3: A child with convulsions is suspected of GABA deficiency. How is GABA produced?

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** GABA is produced by decarboxylation of glutamate, catalyzed by glutamate decarboxylase with pyridoxal phosphate as coenzyme.

---

### Q4: Complete: Methionine is converted to homocysteine after the ... reaction.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The transmethylation reaction. S-adenosylmethionine donates a methyl group and is converted through S-adenosylhomocysteine to homocysteine.

---

### Q5: A patient with colon cancer receives methotrexate and hydroxyurea to decrease DNA synthesis. State the enzyme inhibited by each drug.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Methotrexate inhibits dihydrofolate reductase, reducing thymidylate synthesis. Hydroxyurea inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, reducing deoxyribonucleotide synthesis.

---

### Q6: Complete: Severe combined immunodeficiency can occur due to deficiency of the ... enzyme.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Adenosine deaminase (ADA). ADA deficiency causes toxic purine metabolites to accumulate and can impair lymphocyte development.

---

### Q7: Complete: The rate-limiting enzyme in fatty-acid synthesis is ... .

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Acetyl-CoA carboxylase.

---

### Q8: Complete: Glycerol kinase activates glycerol to glycerol 3-phosphate; this enzyme has absent or low activity in ... .

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Adipose tissue and skeletal muscle have little glycerol-kinase activity, so these tissues depend largely on glycolytic dihydroxyacetone phosphate to form glycerol 3-phosphate.

### Q9: In the well-fed state, list the main sources of fatty acids stored in adipose tissue.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Fatty acids are supplied by dietary triacylglycerol transported in chylomicrons, triacylglycerol in VLDL, and de novo lipogenesis from dietary carbohydrate.

### Q10: Complete: In glycogen-metabolism regulation, phosphorylation by cAMP-dependent protein kinase causes ... of glycogenesis and ... of glycogenolysis.

### Q12: A woman has megaloblastic anemia. State the red-cell size and color described in the source, and the vitamin deficiencies that can cause this anemia.

**Correct Answer:** -

### Q16: Name two levels or processes that can determine the cellular concentration of a protein.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Any two of the following: transcription; post-transcriptional mRNA processing; mRNA degradation; translation; post-translational protein modification; protein targeting and transport; or protein degradation.

### Q17: A child with phenylketonuria has seizures, pale skin, intellectual disability, and a musty urine odor. Name the deficient enzyme and explain the odor.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Classic phenylketonuria is due to phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency. The odor comes from urinary phenylketones, including phenylpyruvate, phenyllactate, phenylacetate, and phenylacetylglutamine.

---

### Q18: State two metabolic functions of transaminases.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Transaminases transfer amino groups from amino acids to α-ketoglutarate to form glutamate, which can release nitrogen for urea synthesis. They also synthesize nonessential amino acids by transferring amino groups to α-keto acids. The resulting carbon skeletons connect amino-acid metabolism with the TCA cycle, gluconeogenesis, and ketone-body formation.

---

### Q19: In the Krebs cycle, name the enzyme that produces GTP and the enzyme that produces FADH₂.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Succinyl-CoA synthetase (succinate thiokinase) produces GTP; succinate dehydrogenase produces FADH₂.

---

### Q20: Name plasma lipoprotein classes and state the principal function of each.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Chylomicrons transport dietary triacylglycerol from the intestine to peripheral tissues. VLDL transports liver-derived triacylglycerol to extrahepatic tissues. LDL delivers cholesterol to tissues. HDL participates in reverse cholesterol transport by carrying cholesterol from peripheral tissues toward the liver.

### Q23: Give one example of mRNA editing and explain how it changes the encoded protein.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** In intestinal apolipoprotein B (APOB) mRNA, an intestine-specific cytidine deaminase edits a CAA codon to UAA, creating a stop codon and producing the shorter ApoB-48 rather than hepatic ApoB-100. The source gives protein lengths of 4,536 and 2,152 amino-acid residues and labels them 100 and 48 kDa; the ApoB-100/ApoB-48 names correspond to approximate electrophoretic masses of about 500 and 250 kDa, so the source's kDa values are inaccurate.

### Q24: Define a frameshift mutation and state its effects.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Insertion or deletion of a number of nucleotides not divisible by three changes the reading frame. It alters downstream codons, can create a premature stop codon, and may produce a severely altered or truncated protein. The source also mentions three-nucleotide changes; additions or deletions in multiples of three are in-frame changes, not frameshift mutations.

---

### Q25: List three functions of telomeres.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Telomeres protect chromosome ends from degradation and inappropriate end-to-end fusion; help compensate for incomplete replication of chromosome ends and preserve coding DNA; and shorten with repeated cell division, serving as a marker of replicative history and cellular senescence. The source additionally lists chromosome-matrix interaction and aging-related roles.

---

### Q26: Compare the first and second promoter sequences in prokaryotes.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The −35 element has the consensus sequence 5′-TTGACA-3′ and lies about 35 bases upstream of the transcription start site; it is an important recognition/contact site for the RNA-polymerase holoenzyme. The −10 Pribnow element has the consensus 5′-TATAAT-3′ and lies about 10 bases upstream; it helps initiate DNA melting and open-complex formation.

---

### Q27: State a difference between eukaryotic and prokaryotic large ribosomal subunits.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The eukaryotic 60S subunit contains 28S, 5.8S, and 5S rRNAs with about 49 proteins. The prokaryotic 50S subunit contains 23S and 5S rRNAs with about 31 proteins.

---

### Q28: Give two differences between glucokinase and hexokinase.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Hexokinase occurs in most tissues, has a low Km (high glucose affinity), and is inhibited by glucose 6-phosphate. Glucokinase is found mainly in liver and pancreatic β cells, has a higher Km and greater capacity, and is not inhibited by glucose 6-phosphate. Insulin and feeding increase hepatic glucokinase expression, whereas fasting reduces it; glucokinase helps clear post-meal glucose and supports glucose sensing.

---

### Q29: State three differences between gene expression and DNA replication.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Gene expression produces RNA and, for protein-coding genes, proteins; replication duplicates the entire genome before cell division. Transcription copies selected gene regions into RNA, whereas replication copies both strands of all chromosomal DNA. Transcription occurs as needed throughout interphase; DNA replication occurs during S phase. In eukaryotes, transcription and DNA replication occur in the nucleus, while translation occurs in the cytoplasm.

---

### Q30: For lipolysis, give the rate-limiting enzyme and site of reaction.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source names hormone-sensitive lipase and the cytoplasm of adipose tissue. HSL hydrolyzes stored diacylglycerol; adipose triglyceride lipase initiates triacylglycerol hydrolysis.

---

### Q31: For β-oxidation, give the rate-limiting enzyme and site of reaction.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The source names acyl-CoA synthetase (thiokinase) and mitochondria in liver and muscle, excluding brain. Long-chain fatty acids require activation and the carnitine shuttle for mitochondrial entry; CPT-I is commonly described as the key regulated step in long-chain β-oxidation, so the source's rate-limiting-enzyme label is retained but flagged.

---

### Q32: For ketogenesis, give the rate-limiting enzyme and site of reaction.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Ketogenesis occurs in liver mitochondria. Mitochondrial HMG-CoA synthase is the key rate-limiting enzyme. The source lists HMG-CoA synthase or HMG-CoA lyase; HMG-CoA lyase catalyzes a later step and is not generally identified as the rate-limiting enzyme.

---

### Q33: State the forces that promote filtration across the capillary wall.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Filtration is promoted by capillary hydrostatic pressure and interstitial colloid-osmotic pressure.

### Q34: List two causes of edema.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Causes include increased capillary hydrostatic pressure, reduced plasma colloid-osmotic pressure (for example, hypoproteinemia), increased capillary permeability, or impaired lymphatic drainage. The source includes venous thrombosis, heart failure, undernutrition, severe burns, renal edema, hemorrhage, toxins, allergy, cancer, and filariasis as examples.

---

### Q35: Enumerate and explain the causes of the resting membrane potential.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The membrane is more permeable to K⁺ than to Na⁺, so net K⁺ leakage leaves the cell interior relatively negative. The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase maintains these gradients by moving three Na⁺ out for every two K⁺ moved in and is electrogenic. Large intracellular organic anions, including proteins and phosphates, cannot diffuse across the membrane and contribute to intracellular negativity.

---

### Q36: Explain the significance of the strength-duration curve.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The curve relates the stimulus strength required to excite tissue to the stimulus duration. A smooth curve with normal rheobase and chronaxie is consistent with intact innervation. Denervated muscle is less excitable to brief pulses and the curve shifts toward longer durations and greater stimulus requirements. Partial denervation can produce a kink or a composite curve because innervated and denervated fibers respond differently.

---

### Q37: A person takes cocaine, which blocks norepinephrine reuptake. Predict the effects on blood pressure and pupil size.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Increased sympathetic norepinephrine signaling raises blood pressure through vasoconstriction and causes pupillary dilation (mydriasis).

---

### Q38: Name the cholinergic receptor type on skeletal muscle and sweat glands.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Skeletal muscle neuromuscular junctions use nicotinic Nm receptors. Eccrine sweat glands use muscarinic M₃ receptors despite their sympathetic innervation, because the postganglionic fibers are cholinergic.

---

### Q39: State the effects of vagus-nerve stimulation on the heart, lungs, gastrointestinal tract, and associated glands.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** In the heart, vagal stimulation decreases heart rate and conduction and can modestly reduce contractility, lowering myocardial oxygen demand. In the lungs it causes bronchoconstriction and increases bronchial secretion. In the gastrointestinal tract it increases motility and secretion in the esophagus, stomach, and intestines while promoting sphincter relaxation. It stimulates gastric, hepatic, and pancreatic secretions; parasympathetic activity also promotes gallbladder contraction and relaxation of the sphincter of Oddi.

---

### Q40: Define the respiratory quotient (RQ).

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Respiratory quotient is the volume of carbon dioxide produced divided by the volume of oxygen consumed per unit time (VCO₂/VO₂).

---

### Q41: State two differences between hypertrophic and hyperplastic-hypertrophic obesity.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** In hypertrophic obesity, adipocytes enlarge mainly through increased stored fat, with little increase in cell number; it more often develops in adulthood and is generally easier to reverse. In hyperplastic-hypertrophic obesity, both adipocyte number and cell fat content increase; it often begins in childhood and is more difficult to reverse.

---

### Q42: Complete the mechanism of fever after bacterial endotoxin exposure.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Endotoxin stimulates immune cells to release pyrogenic cytokines, especially interleukin-1. These promote prostaglandin E₂ production, which acts on the hypothalamic thermoregulatory center and raises the temperature set point, producing fever.

---

### Q43: List two responses of the body to hot weather.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Cutaneous vasodilation increases heat loss by radiation, conduction, and convection; increased sweating promotes evaporative cooling. The source also lists inhibition of shivering and reduced thyroid-hormone secretion to decrease heat production.
