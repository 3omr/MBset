### Q6: List: (A) four effects of point mutations; (B) two types of chromatin remodeling; (C) three tryptophan derivatives; and (D) four eukaryotic mRNA post-transcriptional modifications.

### Q8: Name the types of membrane carriers and give an example of each.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Uniporters carry one solute in one direction, as in GLUT-mediated glucose transport. Symporters carry two or more solutes in the same direction, as in Na⁺-glucose cotransport. Antiporters exchange solutes in opposite directions, as in the Na⁺/Ca²⁺ or Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger. These examples match the source key.

---

### Q9: A child receives lidocaine for a dental procedure. Explain two factors that decrease neuronal excitability.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** Increased extracellular Ca²⁺ stabilizes the membrane and decreases Na⁺-channel opening, reducing excitability. Local anesthetics such as lidocaine block voltage-gated Na⁺ channels. Neuromuscular blockers such as curare can also prevent transmission at the motor end plate. The source lists these mechanisms.

### Q10: Injury affects the first four sacral spinal segments. (A) Name the injured autonomic division and nerve. (B) Name the postganglionic neurotransmitter and ganglion location. (C) Give the receptor type in the dilator pupillae, sympathetically supplied bronchial muscle, skeletal muscle, and sweat glands.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (A) Parasympathetic outflow through the pelvic splanchnic nerves (pelvic nerves), chiefly S2–S4. (B) Acetylcholine; terminal or intramural ganglia. (C) Dilator pupillae: α₁-adrenergic. Bronchial smooth muscle: β₂-adrenergic. Skeletal muscle at the neuromuscular junction: nicotinic Nm. Eccrine sweat gland: muscarinic M₃. These answers follow the printed source key.
