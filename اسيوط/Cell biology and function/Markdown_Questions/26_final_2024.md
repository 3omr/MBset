### Q1: A newborn develops lethargy, hypotonia, shallow breathing, and apnea with hyperammonemia and orotic aciduria type I. (A) Name the deficient enzyme. (B) Give its direct product. (C) Name the key regulatory enzyme of the disturbed cycle. (D) Give that enzyme's allosteric activator. (E) Name the two nitrogen sources incorporated into one urea molecule. (F) Name the enzyme defective in orotic aciduria type II and its affected pathway.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (A) Ornithine transcarbamylase (OTC). (B) Citrulline, formed from ornithine and carbamoyl phosphate. (C) Carbamoyl-phosphate synthetase I (CPS I). (D) N-acetylglutamate. (E) Free ammonia and aspartate. (F) UMP synthase deficiency impairs de novo pyrimidine synthesis. Orotic aciduria type I with hyperammonemia is characteristic of OTC deficiency; OTC catalyzes citrulline formation, while N-acetylglutamate activates CPS I.

### Q2: A man has urine that darkens on standing and black pigmentation of articular cartilage (ochronosis). Identify the diagnosis, the accumulated substance, the deficient enzyme, and the affected amino-acid pathway.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** This is alkaptonuria, caused by accumulation of homogentisic acid (homogentisate) due to deficiency of homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase. The defect impairs phenylalanine and tyrosine catabolism, specifically the tyrosine-degradation pathway.

### Q3: A one-year-old boy has developmental delay, self-biting, urate crystals in his diapers, and an impaired purine-salvage pathway. Give the diagnosis and deficient enzyme, then state one effect of hydroxyurea on nucleotide synthesis.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** The diagnosis is Lesch–Nyhan disease due to hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (HGPRT/HPRT) deficiency. Hydroxyurea inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, reducing deoxyribonucleotide production and thereby inhibiting DNA synthesis. The source describes this as an effect on purine synthesis; the direct pharmacologic target is ribonucleotide reductase, rather than the de novo purine-synthesis pathway.

### Q4: A child has fair skin, blond hair, a mousy urine odor, developmental delay, and increased phenylalanine. State the diagnosis, inheritance pattern, required coenzyme, cause of the urine odor, and amino acid restricted in treatment.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** This is phenylketonuria, usually due to phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency, and is inherited in an autosomal-recessive pattern. The hydroxylase reaction requires tetrahydrobiopterin (BH₄). The mousy odor is due chiefly to phenylacetate and related phenylketones. Dietary phenylalanine is restricted while enough is supplied for growth; tyrosine becomes conditionally essential.

### Q5: Enumerate the following: (A) the amino acid replacing glutamate in the β chain of hemoglobin in sickle-cell anemia; (B) the coenzyme central to simple decarboxylation; (C) an enzyme that introduces negative supercoils during DNA replication; (D) the rate-limiting enzyme of fatty-acid synthesis; (E) two oncogenes; (F) two causes of ketosis; and (G) two causes of negative nitrogen balance.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (A) Valine replaces glutamate at position 6 of the β-globin chain. (B) Thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP). (C) DNA gyrase (bacterial topoisomerase II). (D) Acetyl-CoA carboxylase. (E) Examples include RAS and MYC. (F) Prolonged fasting or starvation and uncontrolled diabetes mellitus; alcoholic ketoacidosis is another cause. (G) Inadequate protein or energy intake and increased protein catabolism during severe infection, burns, trauma, or major illness.

### Q6: List (A) three derivatives of tryptophan; (B) three types of RNA; (C) three characteristics of the genetic code; (D) four key gluconeogenic enzymes; and (E) four post-transcriptional modifications or processing steps of eukaryotic mRNA.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (A) Serotonin, melatonin, and niacin (the precursor for NAD⁺/NADP⁺). (B) Messenger RNA, transfer RNA, and ribosomal RNA. (C) The code is triplet, degenerate, and unambiguous; it is also nearly universal and non-overlapping. (D) Pyruvate carboxylase, phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase, fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase, and glucose-6-phosphatase. (E) 5′ capping, intron removal by splicing, 3′-end cleavage, and poly-A-tail addition; some transcripts also undergo RNA editing.

### Q7: Explain the biochemical cause of each fact: (A) β-oxidation does not occur in brain cells; (B) ketolysis does not occur in liver cells; (C) fetal hemoglobin has higher oxygen affinity than maternal hemoglobin; (D) pyruvate is converted to lactate during anaerobic conditions; and (E) DNA replication requires an RNA primer.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (A) Long-chain fatty acids are largely unavailable to the brain because they do not readily cross the blood-brain barrier; the brain instead uses glucose and, during prolonged fasting, ketone bodies. (B) The liver lacks succinyl-CoA:acetoacetate CoA transferase (thiophorase), which is required to activate ketone bodies for ketolysis. (C) Fetal hemoglobin binds 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate less strongly than adult hemoglobin, so its oxygen affinity is higher. (D) Lactate dehydrogenase reduces pyruvate to lactate while oxidizing NADH to NAD⁺, allowing glycolysis to continue without oxygen. (E) DNA polymerases cannot initiate a new strand de novo; they extend from the 3′-OH group supplied by a primer, which is synthesized as RNA by primase.

### Q8: (A) Complete the statements about childbirth feedback and the forces promoting absorption across capillary walls: The type of feedback in childbirth is ... feedback, in which the response ... the initiating stimulus; the two absorptive forces are ... and ... . (B) List and explain three factors affecting plasma-membrane permeability. (C) A woman with multiple sclerosis has delayed visual-evoked potentials and reduced nerve-conduction velocity. Name two sites where saltatory conduction occurs and explain its functions.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (A) Childbirth uses positive feedback: the response amplifies the initiating stimulus. The principal inward absorptive forces are plasma colloid-osmotic pressure and interstitial hydrostatic pressure. (B) Small molecules cross more readily than large molecules because they pass through membrane pathways more easily; lipid-soluble molecules diffuse through the bilayer more readily than polar solutes; and charged or highly polar substances cross poorly unless a specific channel or carrier is available. Permeability also depends on the abundance and state of membrane transport proteins. (C) Saltatory conduction occurs in myelinated nerve fibers of the central and peripheral nervous systems, at the nodes of Ranvier between myelin segments. The action potential is regenerated at successive nodes, increasing conduction velocity and limiting ion exchange to a small membrane area, which reduces the ATP needed to restore ion gradients.

### Q9: (A) For the ventricle, salivary glands, and internal anal sphincter in a relaxed person, state the effect and receptor. (B) During stress, state the effect and receptor in the bronchioles and sweat glands.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (A) Ventricle: parasympathetic activity modestly reduces heart rate and conduction through M₂ receptors. Salivary glands: parasympathetic stimulation increases watery secretion through M₃ receptors. Internal anal sphincter: it relaxes during defecation through pelvic parasympathetic activation of enteric inhibitory pathways, predominantly nitric oxide and VIP; a single direct muscarinic receptor does not adequately describe this sphincter relaxation. (B) Bronchioles: β₂-receptor activation relaxes bronchial smooth muscle and causes bronchodilation. Sweat glands: secretion increases through muscarinic M₃ receptors on eccrine glands supplied by sympathetic cholinergic fibers.

### Q10: (A) Complete the definitions: BMI = ... divided by ...; respiratory quotient (RQ) = ... / ... per unit time, and it rises above unity in ... and ... . (B) List the body's responses to cold weather.

**Correct Answer:** -
**EXP:** (A) Body mass index is body weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared (kg/m²). RQ is carbon dioxide produced divided by oxygen consumed per unit time (VCO₂/VO₂); it can rise above 1 during net lipogenesis after carbohydrate overfeeding and during strenuous, non-steady-state exercise. (B) Cold exposure causes cutaneous vasoconstriction, shivering, piloerection, and increased heat production through sympathetic and hormonal thermogenesis; sweating is reduced.
