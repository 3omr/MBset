## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q9: Discuss carbolic acid (phenol) regarding its clinical presentation and treatment.

**Model Answer:**
Clinical Presentation:
1. Local: Chemical corrosion with painless coagulative necrosis; produces a characteristic gray-white or brown eschar on lips/mouth; local sensory nerve terminal destruction prevents early pain.
2. Systemic: Rapidly absorbed across skin and mucosa; initial CNS stimulation (tremors, seizures) followed by medullary depression, deep coma, hypothermia, hypotension, and respiratory arrest.
3. Carboluria: Urine turns olive green or dark brown upon standing/exposure to air due to oxidation of hydroquinone and pyrocatechol metabolites.
Treatment:
1. Decontamination: For skin, immediately wash with polyethylene glycol (PEG) or copious water. If ingested, perform gentle gastric lavage with olive/castor oil or warm water and administer activated charcoal.
2. Airway & Resuscitation: Protect airway with endotracheal tube; correct metabolic acidosis with IV sodium bicarbonate; fluid resuscitation.
3. Supportive: Treat seizures with benzodiazepines, monitor renal function (risk of acute tubular necrosis).

### Q10: Describe the hematologic manifestations of plumbism (chronic lead poisoning).

**Model Answer:**
Hematologic manifestations of plumbism:
1. Anemia: Microcytic hypochromic anemia resulting from impaired heme biosynthesis (inhibition of delta-aminolevulinic acid dehydratase [ALAD] and ferrochelatase) and shortened erythrocyte lifespan.
2. Basophilic Stippling (Punctate Basophilia): Presence of fine, dark-blue aggregated ribosomal RNA granules within circulating red blood cells due to lead-induced inhibition of pyrimidine-5'-nucleotidase.
3. Biochemical Markers: Elevated blood lead level (BLL), elevated free erythrocyte protoporphyrin (FEP) / zinc protoporphyrin (ZPP), and increased urinary excretion of delta-ALA and coproporphyrin III.

---

### Q11: Outline the treatment of acute carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning.

**Model Answer:**
Treatment of acute carbon monoxide poisoning:
1. Immediate rescue: Remove patient immediately from the toxic environment into fresh air and ensure patent airway.
2. 100% Normobaric Oxygen: Administer high-flow 100% oxygen via a tight-fitting non-rebreather reservoir mask; reduces COHb elimination half-life from 320 minutes (room air) down to ~80 minutes.
3. Hyperbaric Oxygen (HBO): 100% O2 at 2.5 to 3.0 atmospheres absolute (ATA) in a hyperbaric chamber (reduces half-life to ~20 minutes). Indicated for: COHb > 25% (or > 15% in pregnant women), loss of consciousness/syncope, seizures, abnormal neuropsychiatric exam, or evidence of myocardial ischemia.
4. Supportive Care: Continuous cardiac monitoring, correction of metabolic acidosis, and treatment of cerebral edema.

### Q12: List the mechanisms by which aspirin toxicity causes metabolic acidosis.

**Model Answer:**
Mechanisms of metabolic acidosis in aspirin toxicity:
1. Uncoupling of Oxidative Phosphorylation: Salicylates uncouple oxidative phosphorylation in mitochondria, inhibiting ATP synthesis and accelerating glycolysis, leading to accumulation of lactic acid and pyruvic acid.
2. Impairment of Carbohydrate Metabolism: Inhibition of Krebs (citric acid) cycle dehydrogenases, resulting in accumulation of ketoacids (acetoacetic acid and beta-hydroxybutyric acid).
3. Increased Fatty Acid Metabolism: Stimulation of lipid catabolism with excessive ketone body production.
4. Exogenous Acid Load: Salicylic acid itself and its metabolites consume plasma bicarbonate buffers.
5. Renal Impairment: Decreased renal excretion of inorganic acids (sulfates, phosphates).

### Q13: Mention two toxins causing hyperthermia with different mechanisms.

**Model Answer:**
Two toxins causing hyperthermia with distinct mechanisms:
1. Salicylates (Aspirin):
 - Mechanism: Uncoupling of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, dissipating energy as heat instead of storing it as ATP, which dramatically increases metabolic rate and heat production.
2. Anticholinergic agents (e.g. Atropine, Datura stramonium):
 - Mechanism: Blockade of postganglionic muscarinic acetylcholine receptors on eccrine sweat glands, producing complete anhidrosis (cessation of sweating) and impairing cutaneous heat dissipation.

### Q15: Enumerate 2 toxins not removed by hemodialysis and mention their antidotes.

**Model Answer:**
Toxins poorly removed by hemodialysis and their specific antidotes:
1. Digoxin: Has a very large volume of distribution and high tissue binding. Antidote: Digoxin-specific Fab antibody fragments (DigiFab).
2. Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs - e.g. Amitriptyline): Highly lipophilic, large volume of distribution, high protein binding. Antidote / Management: Intravenous Sodium Bicarbonate.

### Q16: Enumerate 2 antidotes that must be given within a particular critical timeframe after poison exposure.

**Model Answer:**
Antidotes with critical administration timeframes:
1. N-Acetylcysteine (NAC) for Acetaminophen toxicity: Most effective when administered within 8 to 10 hours post-ingestion to completely prevent fatal hepatotoxicity.
2. Pralidoxime (2-PAM / Oximes) for Organophosphate poisoning: Must be administered before 'aging' of phosphorylated acetylcholinesterase occurs (usually within 24 to 48 hours).

### Q17: Enumerate 3 diagnostic or therapeutic uses of vitamins in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Uses of vitamins in clinical toxicology:
1. Vitamin K1 (Phytomenadione): Antidote for hypoprothrombinemia and bleeding caused by long-acting anticoagulant rodenticides (superwarfarins) or aspirin.
2. Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine): Specific antidote for seizures and metabolic acidosis in Isoniazid (INH) overdose, and adjuvant in ethylene glycol poisoning.
3. Vitamin B12a (Hydroxocobalamin): Specific first-line antidote for acute cyanide poisoning, binding cyanide to form nontoxic cyanocobalamin.

### Q18: What is the probable diagnosis and differential diagnosis of this envenomation case?

**Clinical Case:** A child 3 years old came to a toxicological unit with severe local pain, edema, salivation, vomiting, priapism, and respiratory distress after an accidental outdoor sting/bite.

**Model Answer:**
Probable Diagnosis: Scorpion sting (Scorpionism) or Neurotoxic Snake Envenomation (Elapidae / Cobra bite).
Differential Diagnosis:
1. Organophosphate or Carbamate insecticide poisoning (due to cholinergic storm / salivation).
2. Tetanus (muscle spasms).
3. Cytotoxic snake envenomation (Viperidae bite).

### Q20: Complete the full clinical picture of the scorpion / snake envenomation case.

**Clinical Case:** A child 3 years old came to a toxicological unit with severe local pain, edema, salivation, vomiting, priapism, and respiratory distress after an accidental outdoor sting/bite.

**Model Answer:**
Clinical Picture:
1. Local Signs: Puncture marks or sting site, local pain, erythema, localized edema, swollen tongue, and extremity swelling.
2. Autonomic / Systemic Manifestations: Autonomic storm with profuse salivation (drippling), vomiting, diaphoresis, hyperpyrexia, restlessness, priapism, and hypertension followed by hypotension.
3. Neuromuscular / Respiratory Manifestations: Dysphagia, ataxia, muscle fasciculations, cranial nerve palsies, and pulmonary edema leading to acute respiratory failure.

### Q21: How to treat this envenomated child?

**Clinical Case:** A child 3 years old came to a toxicological unit with severe local pain, edema, salivation, vomiting, priapism, and respiratory distress after an accidental outdoor sting/bite.

**Model Answer:**
Treatment Protocol:
1. Immediate First Aid: Reassure child, immobilize affected limb below heart level. Do NOT apply tourniquets, incisions, or suction.
2. Specific Antivenin: Administer IV Polyvalent Antivenin (Scorpion or Snake antivenom) diluted in saline; children receive the same or larger antivenin dose than adults due to higher venom-to-body weight ratio.
3. Airway & Respiratory Support: Suction secretions, maintain patent airway, oxygenation, and endotracheal intubation/mechanical ventilation if respiratory distress occurs.
4. Symptomatic Treatment: Atropine for excessive cholinergic salivation and pulmonary secretions; pain relief; tetanus toxoid booster.
