## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q9: Enumerate the basic principles in the treatment of opioid addiction.

**Model Answer:**
Basic principles in treatment of opioid addiction:
1. Detoxification: Gradual substitution and tapering using long-acting opioid agonists (Methadone) or partial agonists (Buprenorphine), supplemented with alpha-2 agonists (Clonidine) for autonomic withdrawal symptoms.
2. Maintenance Therapy: Long-term replacement therapy with Methadone or Buprenorphine/Naloxone (Suboxone) to suppress craving and prevent illicit drug use.
3. Relapse Prevention / Antagonist Therapy: Naltrexone (oral or depot injection) after full detoxification to block opioid receptors.
4. Psychological & Social Rehabilitation: Psychotherapy, behavioral modification, cognitive therapy, and community support groups (Narcotics Anonymous).

### Q10: What is the physiological antidote of methanol poisoning and its mechanism?

**Model Answer:**
Physiological Antidotes for Methanol Poisoning:
1. Ethanol: High affinity for alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH) (~20 times greater than methanol); competitively blocks the conversion of methanol into toxic formaldehyde and formic acid.
2. Fomepizole (4-Methylpyrazole): Potent competitive inhibitor of ADH without the intoxicating and sedative side effects of ethanol.
3. Folinic Acid (Leucovorin): Enhances tetrahydrofolate-dependent breakdown of toxic formic acid into nontoxic carbon dioxide and water.

### Q11: Outline the emergency treatment of acute carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning.

**Model Answer:**
Treatment of Acute Carbon Monoxide (CO) Poisoning:
1. Immediate Evacuation: Remove victim from contaminated environment immediately into fresh air and maintain patent airway.
2. 100% Normobaric Oxygen: Administer high-flow 100% O2 via tight-fitting non-rebreather mask (reduces COHb half-life from 320 minutes to 80 minutes).
3. Hyperbaric Oxygen (HBO): 100% O2 at 2.5-3.0 ATA (reduces COHb half-life to 20 minutes); indicated if COHb > 25% (or > 15% in pregnancy), coma, seizures, syncope, or abnormal ECG/myocardial ischemia.
4. Supportive Therapy: Correct metabolic acidosis, treat cerebral edema with head elevation and mannitol, and monitor cardiac rhythm continuously.

### Q12: Outline the treatment of acute snake bite envenomation.

**Model Answer:**
Treatment of Acute Snake Bite Envenomation:
1. First Aid: Reassure the victim, keep completely at rest, and immobilize the affected bitten limb in a neutral position below heart level using a splint and broad bandage. Absolutely avoid tourniquets, incisions, suction, or cryotherapy.
2. Specific Antivenom: Intravenous Polyvalent Snake Antivenom is the definitive antidote; administered diluted in IV infusion. Dose is guided by venom severity, and children receive the same or larger initial antivenom dose as adults.
3. Airway & Resuscitation: Provide supplemental oxygen, prepare for endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation for neurotoxic respiratory paralysis (Elapidae).
4. Supportive & Wound Care: Local wound cleaning with antiseptics, tetanus toxoid prophylaxis, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, and correction of coagulopathy (fresh frozen plasma/platelets for Viperidae bites).

### Q13: Define Idiosyncrasy in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Idiosyncrasy (Idiosyncratic Drug Reaction):
An abnormal, genetically determined individual qualitative response to a chemical or drug that differs significantly from the expected physiological response observed in the majority of individuals, not mediated by the immune system.
Example: Severe hemolytic anemia induced by primaquine or fava beans in individuals with Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency.

---

### Q14: Define Toxidrome in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Toxidrome (Toxic Syndrome):
A characteristic constellation of clinical signs, symptoms, and physical examination findings associated with exposure to a specific group or pharmacological class of toxic substances.
Classic examples include Cholinergic, Anticholinergic, Opioid, Sympathomimetic, and Sedative-Hypnotic toxidromes.

---

### Q15: Define Tardive Dyskinesia in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Tardive Dyskinesia:
A late-onset, potentially irreversible neurological disorder caused by chronic long-term exposure to dopamine receptor-blocking agents (such as first-generation antipsychotics/neuroleptics and metoclopramide).
It is clinically characterized by repetitive, involuntary, stereotypic purposeless movements, especially of the face, mouth, tongue (choreoathetoid movements, tongue protrusion, lip smacking), and occasionally choreiform movements of extremities.

---

### Q16: Define Cyanide Kit in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Cyanide Kit (Lilly / Taylor Cyanide Antidote Package):
A specific combination of three antidotal drugs administered sequentially to treat acute cyanide poisoning:
1. Amyl Nitrite Inhalant: Rapidly inhaled to produce initial methemoglobinemia (~5%).
2. Sodium Nitrite (3% IV): Converts hemoglobin into methemoglobin (~20-30%), which binds cyanide ions with high affinity to form non-toxic cyanmethemoglobin, freeing cytochrome oxidase.
3. Sodium Thiosulfate (25% IV): Acts as a sulfur donor for the hepatic enzyme rhodanese to convert cyanide into water-soluble, nontoxic thiocyanate excreted in urine.

### Q17: Differential diagnosis of coma with pin-point pupils (miosis).

**Model Answer:**
Differential diagnosis of coma with pin-point pupils (miosis):
1. Opioid overdose (Morphine, Heroin, Methadone, Fentanyl, Codeine).
2. Cholinergic toxicity (Organophosphate and Carbamate insecticides).
3. Pontine hemorrhage (damage to bilateral central descending sympathetic fibers).
4. Phenothiazines and other typical neuroleptics/antipsychotics.
5. Central alpha-2 agonists (Clonidine overdose).
6. Sedatives / Hypnotics (Barbiturate and Chloral hydrate overdose in deep stage).
