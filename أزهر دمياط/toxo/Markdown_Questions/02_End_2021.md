## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q5: State True or False: B.A.L. (Dimercaprol) is a physiological antidote for most metallic poisons.

**Model Answer:**
False. BAL is a chemical chelating agent that forms coordinate covalent bonds with heavy metals; it is not a physiological antagonist.

---

### Q6: State True or False: Never use salty water as an emetic in toxicological emergencies.

**Model Answer:**
True. Concentrated salt water is extremely dangerous and strictly contraindicated because it causes severe, rapidly fatal hypernatremia, cerebral edema, and seizures.

---

### Q7: State True or False: Polyvalent antisnake venom must be used for all snake venoms worldwide.

**Model Answer:**
False. Antisnake venom is species- and region-specific (formulated against venoms of indigenous snakes, e.g. Cerastes, Echis, Naja) and has no efficacy against non-targeted or exotic snake species.

### Q8: State True or False: All cases of botulism must be notified immediately to health authorities.

**Model Answer:**
True. Botulism is a designated class-A public health emergency; immediate statutory notification is mandatory to identify contaminated commercial food batches and protect the community.

### Q9: Describe the clinical picture of acute beta-blockers toxicity.

**Model Answer:**
Clinical Picture of Acute Beta-Blocker Toxicity:
1. Cardiovascular (Primary Hallmarks): Marked sinus bradycardia, conduction delays (prolonged PR interval, atrioventricular block), and profound hypotension / cardiogenic shock.
2. Respiratory: Severe bronchospasm and wheezing, especially in patients with reactive airway disease / asthma.
3. Central Nervous System: Delirium, stupor, coma, and generalized convulsions (prominent with lipophilic agents like Propranolol that cross blood-brain barrier and block sodium channels).
4. Metabolic: Hypoglycemia (impaired gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis) and hyperkalemia.

---

### Q10: Enumerate the immediate causes of death in acute alcohol (ethanol) poisoning.

**Model Answer:**
Immediate Causes of Death in Acute Alcohol Poisoning:
1. Central Respiratory Depression: Acute medullary respiratory center depression leading to fatal apnea and asphyxia.
2. Pulmonary Aspiration: Aspiration of acidic gastric contents into lungs during coma causing asphyxia or acute chemical pneumonitis (Mendelson syndrome).
3. Severe Hypothermia: Alcohol-induced peripheral vasodilation and depression of hypothalamic thermoregulation leading to fatal hypothermic cardiac arrest.
4. Trauma / Brain Damage: Subdural or epidural hematoma resulting from unwitnessed falls and head trauma while intoxicated.

### Q11: Explain the mechanism of action of Carbon Monoxide (CO) poisoning.

**Model Answer:**
Mechanism of Action of Carbon Monoxide (CO) Poisoning:
1. High Hemoglobin Affinity: CO binds hemoglobin with an affinity 210 to 250 times greater than oxygen, forming carboxyhemoglobin (COHb) and drastically reducing blood oxygen-carrying capacity.
2. Leftward Shift of Oxyhemoglobin Curve (Haldane Effect): CO alters hemoglobin conformation, locking remaining oxygen molecules and preventing oxygen release to peripheral tissues.
3. Cellular Cytochrome Inhibition: CO enters cells and binds mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV), halting oxidative phosphorylation and causing severe cellular hypoxia and lactic acidosis.
4. Myoglobin Binding: Binds cardiac and skeletal myoglobin with high affinity, leading to direct myocardial depression, arrhythmias, and rhabdomyolysis.

---

### Q12: Mention the therapeutic uses of Atropine as an antidote in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Therapeutic Uses of Atropine as an Antidote:
1. Organophosphate Insecticide Poisoning: Competitively blocks excessive acetylcholine at postganglionic muscarinic receptors to dry pulmonary secretions and reverse bronchospasm.
2. Carbamate Insecticide Poisoning: Drug of choice for muscarinic hyperstimulation.
3. Digitalis Cardiotoxicity: First-line emergency drug to treat digitalis-induced severe sinus bradycardia and high-degree AV nodal blocks.
4. Cholinergic Mushroom Poisoning: Antidote for Clitocybe and Inocybe species containing pure muscarine.

### Q13: Define Toxidrome in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Toxidrome (Toxic Syndrome):
A characteristic constellation of clinical signs, symptoms, and physical examination findings associated with exposure to a specific group or pharmacological class of toxic substances.
Classic examples include Cholinergic, Anticholinergic, Opioid, Sympathomimetic, and Sedative-Hypnotic toxidromes.

---

### Q14: Define Chelators in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Chelators (Chelating Agents):
Chemical substances containing active nucleophilic electron-donor ligand atoms (sulfhydryl -SH, amino -NH2, or carboxyl -COOH groups) that bind polyvalent heavy metal ions to form stable, non-toxic, heterocyclic water-soluble ring complexes that are readily excreted by the kidneys and eliminated in urine.

---

### Q15: Define Tolerance in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Tolerance:
A biological state of decreased responsiveness to a pharmacological agent resulting from its prior repeated administration, requiring increasingly larger doses of the drug to produce the same biological effect originally achieved with smaller doses.
Subtypes include Pharmacokinetic (metabolic) tolerance, Pharmacodynamic (cellular/receptor downregulation) tolerance, and Cross-tolerance.

---

### Q16: Define Food Poisoning in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Food Poisoning:
An acute illness typically caused by the ingestion of food or water contaminated with pathogenic microorganisms (bacteria, viruses, parasites), microbial preformed toxins (e.g. Staphylococcal enterotoxin, Botulinum toxin), or chemical poisons (e.g. heavy metals, ciguatera, mushroom toxins).
Manifests classically with acute gastrointestinal symptoms including nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, and diarrhea.

### Q18: Describe the full clinical picture of chronic lead poisoning in children.

**Clinical Case:** A child 4 years old came to a toxicological unit with mild ataxia, bilateral wrist drop, diminutions in vision and blue line on gums. Her mother stated that this child had been irritable with insomnia, attributing this behavior to noise from battery industry beside their residence.

**Model Answer:**
Clinical Picture of Pediatric Plumbism:
1. Gastrointestinal: Severe intermittent colicky abdominal pain (lead colic), persistent constipation, nausea, vomiting, and anorexia.
2. Hematological: Microcytic hypochromic anemia, pallor, and fatigue.
3. Neurological / Behavioral: Irritability, sleep disturbances (insomnia), loss of developmental milestones, ataxia, hyperactivity, encephalopathy (seizures, raised intracranial pressure, coma), and peripheral motor neuropathy (bilateral wrist drop / foot drop).
4. Oral / Mucosal: Blue-black lead line along gingival margins (Burtonian line).

### Q19: What are the investigations needed to prove your diagnosis in pediatric lead toxicity?

**Clinical Case:** A child 4 years old came to a toxicological unit with mild ataxia, bilateral wrist drop, diminutions in vision and blue line on gums. Her mother stated that this child had been irritable with insomnia, attributing this behavior to noise from battery industry beside their residence.

**Model Answer:**
Investigations Needed:
1. Blood Lead Level (BLL): Definitive diagnostic test measured by graphite furnace atomic absorption spectrometry (toxic level in children > 5 ug/dL; chelation indicated if >= 45 ug/dL).
2. Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Peripheral Blood Film: Shows microcytic hypochromic anemia with prominent basophilic stippling (punctate basophilia).
3. Plain Radiography of Long Bones (KUB and Knees): Characteristic dense metaphyseal bands of increased bone density ('lead lines') at distal femur and proximal tibia.
4. Erythrocyte Protoporphyrin (EP / FEP) and Zinc Protoporphyrin (ZPP): Elevated due to ferrochelatase inhibition.
5. 24-hour Urinary Lead Excretion and Delta-ALA levels.

### Q20: Enumerate the different lines of treatment for this lead-poisoned child.

**Clinical Case:** A child 4 years old came to a toxicological unit with mild ataxia, bilateral wrist drop, diminutions in vision and blue line on gums. Her mother stated that this child had been irritable with insomnia, attributing this behavior to noise from battery industry beside their residence.

**Model Answer:**
Lines of Treatment:
1. Environmental Source Removal: Immediately remove the child and family from proximity to the battery factory and prevent further environmental contamination.
2. Chelation Therapy:
 - For severe symptoms / BLL >= 70 ug/dL: Combined Dimercaprol (BAL) 4 mg/kg IM every 4 hours followed by CaNa2 EDTA (1000-1500 mg/m2/day IV infusion) to clear lead without precipitating acute encephalopathy.
 - For moderate toxicity (BLL 45-69 ug/dL): Oral Succimer (DMSA) 10 mg/kg TID for 5 days then BID for 14 days, or IV CaNa2 EDTA.
3. Nutritional Intervention: Ensure adequate dietary calcium, iron, and zinc to competitively reduce lead absorption from the gastrointestinal tract.
4. Supportive Care: Control seizures with IV benzodiazepines, manage cerebral edema with mannitol/head elevation, and physical therapy for wrist drop.
