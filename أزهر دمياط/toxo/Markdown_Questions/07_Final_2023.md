## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q9: State True or False: The early manifestation of acute digitalis toxicity is anorexia.

**Model Answer:**
True. Gastrointestinal symptoms, particularly anorexia, nausea, and vomiting, are the earliest clinical signs of digitalis intoxication.

---

### Q10: State True or False: The only antidote for acetaminophen poisoning is N-acetylcysteine.

**Model Answer:**
True. N-acetylcysteine (NAC) is the specific and sole approved antidote for acetaminophen-induced hepatotoxicity.

---

### Q11: State True or False: Cathartics are used in toxicology to increase the passage of toxic material through the bowel.

**Model Answer:**
True. Osmotic cathartics (e.g. Sorbitol, Magnesium citrate) accelerate transit through the GI tract, reducing absorption time.

---

### Q12: State True or False: Gastric lavage can be done safely with carbolic acid (phenol).

**Model Answer:**
True. Unlike other strong mineral corrosives where lavage is strictly contraindicated, gentle gastric lavage in phenol poisoning can be performed safely using a soft rubber tube and olive oil or water to remove the highly absorbed systemic toxin.

---

### Q13: Enumerate the basic principles in the treatment of opioid addiction.

**Model Answer:**
Basic principles in treatment of opioid addiction:
1. Detoxification: Gradual substitution and tapering using long-acting opioid agonists (Methadone) or partial agonists (Buprenorphine), supplemented with alpha-2 agonists (Clonidine) for autonomic withdrawal symptoms.
2. Maintenance Therapy: Long-term replacement therapy with Methadone or Buprenorphine/Naloxone (Suboxone) to suppress craving and prevent illicit drug use.
3. Relapse Prevention / Antagonist Therapy: Naltrexone (oral or depot injection) after full detoxification to block opioid receptors.
4. Psychological & Social Rehabilitation: Psychotherapy, behavioral modification, cognitive therapy, and community support groups (Narcotics Anonymous).

---

### Q14: Mention the physiological antidotes of methanol poisoning and their mechanism.

**Model Answer:**
Physiological Antidotes for Methanol Poisoning:
1. Ethanol: Competitively binds alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH) with 20 times higher affinity than methanol, preventing formation of formaldehyde and toxic formic acid.
2. Fomepizole (4-Methylpyrazole): Specific, potent competitive inhibitor of alcohol dehydrogenase without ethanol's inebriating side effects.
3. Folinic Acid (Leucovorin): Enhances breakdown of formic acid into carbon dioxide and water.

---

### Q15: Outline the treatment of acute morphine (opioid) poisoning.

**Model Answer:**
Treatment of Acute Morphine Poisoning:
1. Airway & Ventilation: Establish patent airway, assist ventilation with bag-valve-mask, and provide supplemental oxygen immediately.
2. Specific Antidote (Naloxone): Pure competitive mu-opioid receptor antagonist; administer 0.4 to 2 mg IV (or IM/subcutaneous/intranasal), repeated every 2-3 minutes until adequate spontaneous ventilation is restored.
3. Continuous Monitoring & Infusion: Because naloxone has a short half-life (30-90 minutes) while opioids often act much longer, monitor closely for resedation and initiate continuous IV naloxone infusion (two-thirds of the initial awakening dose per hour).
4. Decontamination: Single-dose activated charcoal if ingestion occurred within 1-2 hours and airway is secured.

---

### Q16: Enumerate the early complications of corrosive ingestion.

**Model Answer:**
Early complications of corrosive ingestion (< 24 to 48 hours):
1. Asphyxia and acute respiratory distress due to laryngeal edema or glottic spasm.
2. Severe hypovolemic and neurogenic shock from agonizing pain and massive fluid loss.
3. Acute gastrointestinal perforation (most commonly stomach or esophagus) leading to chemical peritonitis or mediastinitis.
4. Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC) and systemic metabolic acidosis.

---

### Q17: Define Plumbism (chronic lead poisoning).

**Model Answer:**
Plumbism (Chronic Lead Poisoning):
A chronic multisystem toxic syndrome resulting from repeated absorption of elemental, inorganic, or organic lead compounds.
Characterized by microcytic hypochromic anemia with basophilic stippling, lead colic (severe abdominal cramps), peripheral motor neuropathy (bilateral wrist/foot drop), Burtonian line on gingiva, and lead encephalopathy in children.

---

### Q18: Define Activated Charcoal in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Activated Charcoal:
An odorless, fine black powder prepared by pyrolysis of carbonaceous materials and activation with oxidizing gases, creating an immense internal porous surface area (1000-2000 m2/g).
Acts as a nonspecific physical adsorbent for most ingested drugs and toxins in the gastrointestinal lumen, preventing systemic absorption. Ineffective for heavy metals, corrosives, hydrocarbons, and simple alcohols.

---

### Q19: Define Cyanide Kit in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Cyanide Kit (Lilly / Taylor Cyanide Antidote Package):
A specific combination of three antidotal drugs administered sequentially to treat acute cyanide poisoning:
1. Amyl Nitrite Inhalant: Rapidly inhaled to produce initial methemoglobinemia (~5%).
2. Sodium Nitrite (3% IV): Converts hemoglobin into methemoglobin (~20-30%), which binds cyanide ions with high affinity to form non-toxic cyanmethemoglobin, freeing cytochrome oxidase.
3. Sodium Thiosulfate (25% IV): Acts as a sulfur donor for the hepatic enzyme rhodanese to convert cyanide into water-soluble, nontoxic thiocyanate excreted in urine.

---

### Q20: Define Idiosyncrasy in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Idiosyncrasy (Idiosyncratic Drug Reaction):
An abnormal, genetically determined individual qualitative response to a chemical or drug that differs significantly from the expected physiological response observed in the majority of individuals, not mediated by the immune system.
Example: Severe hemolytic anemia induced by primaquine or fava beans in individuals with Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency.

---

### Q21: Define Punctate Basophilia (Basophilic Stippling).

**Model Answer:**
Punctate Basophilia (Basophilic Stippling):
Presence of numerous fine, dark-blue granules of aggregated ribosomal RNA evenly dispersed throughout the cytoplasm of erythrocytes on blood film.
Classic diagnostic hallmark of plumbism (lead poisoning) caused by lead-induced inhibition of pyrimidine-5'-nucleotidase.

---

### Q22: Define Acrodynia (Pink Disease) in toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Acrodynia (Pink Disease):
A syndrome of chronic elemental or inorganic mercury poisoning occurring primarily in infants and young children, historically linked to teething powders containing calomel (mercurous chloride).
Characterized by painful, bright pink, desquamating, erythematous swelling of hands and feet, photophobia, profuse diaphoresis, irritability, hypotonia, and tachycardia.

---

### Q23: Define Antidote in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Antidote:
A specific therapeutic agent administered to counteract, neutralize, prevent, or reverse the physiological, biochemical, or toxicological effects of a specific poison or toxin.

---

### Q24: Define Food Poisoning in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Food Poisoning:
An acute illness typically caused by the ingestion of food or water contaminated with pathogenic microorganisms (bacteria, viruses, parasites), microbial preformed toxins (e.g. Staphylococcal enterotoxin, Botulinum toxin), or chemical poisons (e.g. heavy metals, ciguatera, mushroom toxins).
Manifests classically with acute gastrointestinal symptoms including nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, and diarrhea.

### Q25: What is the probable diagnosis of this envenomation case?

**Clinical Case:** A child 4 years old came to a toxicological unit with two small puncture wounds on the lower limb, then after 8 hours he was suffering from a picture resembling a drunken person with slow and shallow respiration.

**Model Answer:**
Probable Diagnosis:
Neurotoxic Snake Envenomation (Elapidae envenomation / Cobra bite).
Rationale: Presence of two small puncture fang marks, minimal local swelling, delayed onset (hours), prominent neurotoxic drunken-like presentation (ataxia, ptosis, slurred speech), and respiratory depression.

### Q26: Complete the full clinical picture of neurotoxic snake envenomation.

**Clinical Case:** A child 4 years old came to a toxicological unit with two small puncture wounds on the lower limb, then after 8 hours he was suffering from a picture resembling a drunken person with slow and shallow respiration.

**Model Answer:**
Clinical Picture of Neurotoxic Snake Bite (Elapidae):
1. Local Signs: Minimal local inflammation, one or two puncture fang marks, mild pain or local numbness/paresthesia.
2. Neuromuscular Manifestations: Curare-like post-synaptic acetylcholine receptor blockade causing progressive cranial nerve paralysis: bilateral ptosis, diplopia, blurred vision, dysphagia, dysarthria, and ataxia ('drunken appearance').
3. Respiratory: Progressive flaccid paralysis of intercostal muscles and diaphragm, shallow breathing, cyanosis, and fatal asphyxial respiratory arrest.
4. Autonomic/Systemic: Excessive salivation, drowsiness, coma, and circulatory collapse.

### Q27: What is the differential diagnosis of toxic agents causing a drunken-like presentation?

**Clinical Case:** A child 4 years old came to a toxicological unit with two small puncture wounds on the lower limb, then after 8 hours he was suffering from a picture resembling a drunken person with slow and shallow respiration.

**Model Answer:**
Differential diagnosis of toxic agents causing drunken-like presentation:
1. Alcohols: Ethanol, Methanol, Isopropanol, Ethylene glycol.
2. Sedative-Hypnotics: Benzodiazepines, Barbiturates, Chloral hydrate.
3. Neurotoxic snake envenomation (Elapidae / Cobra bite).
4. Anticonvulsants: Phenytoin, Carbamazepine toxicity.
5. Inhaled volatile hydrocarbons / solvents.

### Q28: What are the different lines of treatment for this snake-bitten child?

**Clinical Case:** A child 4 years old came to a toxicological unit with two small puncture wounds on the lower limb, then after 8 hours he was suffering from a picture resembling a drunken person with slow and shallow respiration.

**Model Answer:**
Treatment Protocol for Neurotoxic Snake Envenomation:
1. First Aid: Keep child calm, immobilize bitten limb with a splint below heart level, apply broad pressure immobilization bandage (PID). Avoid incisions, suction, or tourniquets.
2. Specific Antivenom: Intravenous Polyvalent Antisnake Venom diluted in normal saline; children receive the same or larger initial antivenom dose as adults because the dose neutralizes the injected venom load.
3. Airway & Respiratory Support: Endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation for impending respiratory muscle paralysis.
4. Anticholinesterase Trial: IV Neostigmine (with Atropine) may partially reverse post-synaptic neuromuscular blockade in certain neurotoxic venoms.
5. Supportive Care: Tetanus toxoid booster, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, and vital signs monitoring in PICU.
