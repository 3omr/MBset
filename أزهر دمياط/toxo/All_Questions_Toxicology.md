### Q25: Describe the clinical picture of acute beta-blockers toxicity.

### Q27: Explain the mechanism of action of Carbon Monoxide (CO) poisoning.

**Model Answer:**
Mechanism of Action of Carbon Monoxide (CO) Poisoning:
1. High Hemoglobin Affinity: CO binds hemoglobin with an affinity 210 to 250 times greater than oxygen, forming carboxyhemoglobin (COHb) and drastically reducing blood oxygen-carrying capacity.
2. Leftward Shift of Oxyhemoglobin Curve (Haldane Effect): CO alters hemoglobin conformation, locking remaining oxygen molecules and preventing oxygen release to peripheral tissues.
3. Cellular Cytochrome Inhibition: CO enters cells and binds mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV), halting oxidative phosphorylation and causing severe cellular hypoxia and lactic acidosis.
4. Myoglobin Binding: Binds cardiac and skeletal myoglobin with high affinity, leading to direct myocardial depression, arrhythmias, and rhabdomyolysis.

*Taxonomy: Exams, End 2021, Final 2021, Final 2022 | Year: 2022*

### Q35: What are the investigations needed to prove your diagnosis in pediatric lead toxicity?

**Model Answer:**

### Q36: Enumerate the different lines of treatment for this lead-poisoned child.

**Model Answer:**
Lines of Treatment:
1. Environmental Source Removal: Immediately remove the child and family from proximity to the battery factory and prevent further environmental contamination.
2. Chelation Therapy:
 - For severe symptoms / BLL >= 70 ug/dL: Combined Dimercaprol (BAL) 4 mg/kg IM every 4 hours followed by CaNa2 EDTA (1000-1500 mg/m2/day IV infusion) to clear lead without precipitating acute encephalopathy.

### Q51: Define Cyanide Kit in clinical toxicology.

**Model Answer:**
Cyanide Kit (Lilly / Taylor Cyanide Antidote Package):
A specific combination of three antidotal drugs administered sequentially to treat acute cyanide poisoning:
1. Amyl Nitrite Inhalant: Rapidly inhaled to produce initial methemoglobinemia (~5%).
2. Sodium Nitrite (3% IV): Converts hemoglobin into methemoglobin (~20-30%), which binds cyanide ions with high affinity to form non-toxic cyanmethemoglobin, freeing cytochrome oxidase.
3. Sodium Thiosulfate (25% IV): Acts as a sulfur donor for the hepatic enzyme rhodanese to convert cyanide into water-soluble, nontoxic thiocyanate excreted in urine.

### Q58: Complete the full clinical picture of acute iron poisoning.

**Model Answer:**
Clinical Picture of Acute Iron Poisoning (4 Stages):
1. Stage 1 (0.5-6 hours - Gastrointestinal Stage): Abdominal pain, vomiting, hematemesis, melena, diarrhea, and hypovolemic shock.
2. Stage 2 (6-24 hours - Latent / Relative Stability Stage): Transient clinical improvement; iron continues intracellular uptake.
3. Stage 3 (12-48 hours - Systemic Toxicity Stage): Profound shock, severe metabolic acidosis, jaundice, acute hepatic necrosis, hypoglycemia, and multiorgan failure.
4. Stage 4 (2-6 weeks - Delayed Sequelae): Gastric cicatrization, pyloric stenosis, and bowel strictures.

### Q59: What are the investigations needed to prove your diagnosis in acute iron toxicity?

**Model Answer:**
Investigations Needed:

### Q60: What are the different lines of treatment for acute iron toxicity?

**Model Answer:**
Lines of Treatment:

### Q101: What is the mechanism of action of kerosene toxicity?

**Model Answer:**
Mechanism of Action of Kerosene Toxicity:
1. Pulmonary Toxicity (Major Danger): Kerosene has low viscosity, low surface tension, and high volatility. Aspiration during ingestion or vomiting spreads liquid rapidly across the alveolar capillary bed, dissolving pulmonary surfactant, causing alveolar collapse, chemical pneumonitis, and severe non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema.

### Q212: Discuss carbolic acid (phenol) regarding its clinical presentation and treatment.

**Model Answer:**
Clinical Presentation:
1. Local: Chemical corrosion with painless coagulative necrosis; produces a characteristic gray-white or brown eschar on lips/mouth; local sensory nerve terminal destruction prevents early pain.
2. Systemic: Rapidly absorbed across skin and mucosa; initial CNS stimulation (tremors, seizures) followed by medullary depression, deep coma, hypothermia, hypotension, and respiratory arrest.
3. Carboluria: Urine turns olive green or dark brown upon standing/exposure to air due to oxidation of hydroquinone and pyrocatechol metabolites.
Treatment:
1. Decontamination: For skin, immediately wash with polyethylene glycol (PEG) or copious water. If ingested, perform gentle gastric lavage with olive/castor oil or warm water and administer activated charcoal.
2. Airway & Resuscitation: Protect airway with endotracheal tube; correct metabolic acidosis with IV sodium bicarbonate; fluid resuscitation.
3. Supportive: Treat seizures with benzodiazepines, monitor renal function (risk of acute tubular necrosis).

### Q213: Describe the hematologic manifestations of plumbism (chronic lead poisoning).

**Model Answer:**
Hematologic manifestations of plumbism:

### Q214: Outline the treatment of acute carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning.

**Model Answer:**
Treatment of acute carbon monoxide poisoning:
1. Immediate rescue: Remove patient immediately from the toxic environment into fresh air and ensure patent airway.
2. 100% Normobaric Oxygen: Administer high-flow 100% oxygen via a tight-fitting non-rebreather reservoir mask; reduces COHb elimination half-life from 320 minutes (room air) down to ~80 minutes.
3. Hyperbaric Oxygen (HBO): 100% O2 at 2.5 to 3.0 atmospheres absolute (ATA) in a hyperbaric chamber (reduces half-life to ~20 minutes). Indicated for: COHb > 25% (or > 15% in pregnant women), loss of consciousness/syncope, seizures, abnormal neuropsychiatric exam, or evidence of myocardial ischemia.
4. Supportive Care: Continuous cardiac monitoring, correction of metabolic acidosis, and treatment of cerebral edema.

### Q215: List the mechanisms by which aspirin toxicity causes metabolic acidosis.

**Model Answer:**
Mechanisms of metabolic acidosis in aspirin toxicity:
1. Uncoupling of Oxidative Phosphorylation: Salicylates uncouple oxidative phosphorylation in mitochondria, inhibiting ATP synthesis and accelerating glycolysis, leading to accumulation of lactic acid and pyruvic acid.
2. Impairment of Carbohydrate Metabolism: Inhibition of Krebs (citric acid) cycle dehydrogenases, resulting in accumulation of ketoacids (acetoacetic acid and beta-hydroxybutyric acid).
3. Increased Fatty Acid Metabolism: Stimulation of lipid catabolism with excessive ketone body production.
4. Exogenous Acid Load: Salicylic acid itself and its metabolites consume plasma bicarbonate buffers.
5. Renal Impairment: Decreased renal excretion of inorganic acids (sulfates, phosphates).
