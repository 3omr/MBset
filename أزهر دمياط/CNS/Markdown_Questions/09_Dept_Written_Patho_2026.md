### Q1: Define stroke.
**Model Answer:**
Stroke is an abrupt interruption of constant blood flow to the brain that causes acute loss of neurological function.

### Q11: Define cerebral aneurysm and mention its complications.
**Model Answer:**
Definition: An abnormal permanent localized dilatation of a cerebral arterial wall.
Complications: 1. Compression of surrounding brain parenchyma and cranial nerves. 2. Thrombosis inside the dilated sac. 3. Rupture leading to life-threatening subarachnoid hemorrhage.

**Tag:** `Department, Pathology 2026`

---

### Q12: Define brain abscess and mention its complications.
**Model Answer:**
Definition: A localized cavity containing pus due to acute suppurative inflammation caused by pyogenic bacteria.
Complications (Mnemonic GARS): Gangrene, Rupture (into ventricles or subarachnoid space), Amyloidosis (secondary), and Spread (direct or hematogenous).

**Tag:** `Department, Pathology 2026`

---

### Q14: Define Neurodegenerative diseases.
**Model Answer:**
A group of progressive disorders characterized by the selective loss of functional neurons, white matter changes, and abnormal protein accumulation.

**Tag:** `Department, Pathology 2026`

---

### Q15: How do neurodegenerative diseases differ anatomically?
**Model Answer:**
If the neuronal loss is primarily in the Hippocampus and cerebral cortex, it presents as dementia (Alzheimer’s disease); if the neuronal loss is in the Substantia Nigra (Basal Ganglia / Brainstem), it presents as a movement disorder (Parkinson’s disease).

**Tag:** `Department, Pathology 2026`

---

### Q17: What are the main clinical features of Alzheimer’s disease?
**Model Answer:**
Progressive dementia that impairs Behavior and Cognition. It characteristically begins with insidious loss of short-term memory while long-term memory is initially preserved.

**Tag:** `Department, Pathology 2026`

### Q18: Mention the types of abnormal proteins that accumulate in Alzheimer’s disease.
**Model Answer:**
1. Hyperphosphorylated Tau protein (forming intracellular neurofibrillary tangles).
2. Beta-amyloid (A-beta) peptide (forming extracellular senile/amyloid plaques).

**Tag:** `Department, Pathology 2026`

---

### Q19: Describe the gross pathology of the brain in Alzheimer’s disease.
**Model Answer:**
The brain undergoes marked symmetrical cortical atrophy (brain weight decreases), showing narrowing of the gyri, widening of the sulci (especially frontal and temporal lobes), and compensatory ventriculomegaly (hydrocephalus ex vacuo).

**Tag:** `Department, Pathology 2026`

---

### Q20: What is the primary pathological cause and anatomical location of Parkinson’s disease?
**Model Answer:**
Degeneration and loss of pigmented dopaminergic neurons in the Substantia Nigra pars compacta, resulting in a severe deficiency of dopamine in the striatum.

**Tag:** `Department, Pathology 2026`

### Q23: Enumerate the cardinal clinical features of Parkinson’s disease.
**Model Answer:**
Mnemonic (TRAP):
- T: Tremors (resting "pill-rolling" tremor).
- R: Rigidity (cogwheel or lead-pipe rigidity due to loss of dopamine).
- A: Akinesia / Bradykinesia (shuffling gait, loss of arm swing).
- P: Postural instability (stooped posture, tendency to fall).
(Also characteristic "mask-like" facial expression).

**Tag:** `Department, Pathology 2026`

### Q24: Define Demyelinating diseases.
**Model Answer:**
A group of diseases characterized by the destruction of myelin sheaths of nerve fibers while the underlying axons remain relatively preserved.

**Tag:** `Department, Pathology 2026`

**Model Answer:**
1. Nystagmus (abnormal involuntary eye movement).
2. Scanning speech (staccato, broken speech).
3. Intention tremor.

**Tag:** `Department, Pathology 2026`

### Q29: What is the cause and clinical result of Central Pontine Myelinolysis?
**Model Answer:**
Cause: Overly rapid intravenous correction of severe hyponatremia (sodium deficiency).
Clinical result: Non-inflammatory demyelination of the basis pontis leading to spastic quadriplegia and pseudobulbar palsy (locked-in syndrome).

**Tag:** `Department, Pathology 2026`
