### Q1: Discuss filum terminale: Definition, extension, and attachments.
**Model Answer:**
Definition: Prolongation of pia mater extending from apex of conus medullaris downward to back of coccyx (length ~20 cm).
Extension: From conus medullaris (lower border of L1 in adults) to back of first coccygeal vertebra (Co1).
Parts & Attachments:
1. Filum terminale internum (upper 15 cm): Lies inside dural sac and subarachnoid space surrounded by cauda equina, extending down to S2 vertebra.
2. Filum terminale externum (coccygeal ligament, lower 5 cm): Pierces dural sac at S2, acquires dura/arachnoid investment, and attaches to dorsal surface of Co1.

### Q2: Define dural venous sinuses and mention their characteristics.
**Model Answer:**
Definition: Venous channels between endosteal and meningeal layers of cranial dura mater.
Characteristics:
1. Devoid of muscular tissue in walls.
2. Lined by endothelium.
3. Valveless, permitting bidirectional flow depending on pressure gradients.
4. Rigidly held open by dura and cranial bones, preventing collapse when severed.

### Q6: Define Subthalamic Nucleus (body of Luys) and its clinical significance.
**Model Answer:**
Definition: Biconvex lens-shaped nucleus of diencephalon located ventral to thalamus, lateral to hypothalamus, and dorsal to substantia nigra.
Connections & Neurotransmitter: Excitatory glutamatergic nucleus in basal ganglia indirect pathway; receives GABAergic inhibition from GPe and projects excitatory glutamate to GPi and SNr.
Clinical significance: Lesion (usually lacunar stroke) causes hemiballismus (violent, uncontrollable, rapid flinging movements of contralateral extremities).

### Q7: What are the four types of glial cells in the CNS and their main functions?
**Model Answer:**
1. Astrocytes: Protoplasmic (gray matter) and fibrous (white matter). Maintain blood-brain barrier (BBB), regulate extracellular K+ and glutamate reuptake, form glial scar (gliosis).
2. Oligodendrocytes: Myelinate multiple CNS axons, enabling fast saltatory conduction.
3. Microglia: Resident macrophages derived from mesoderm (monocytes); perform phagocytosis and immune surveillance.
4. Ependymal cells: Ciliated cells lining brain ventricles and spinal central canal; participate in CSF circulation and form choroid plexus epithelium.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2021`

### Q8: Enumerate 3 extrapyramidal motor tracts and state their functions.
**Model Answer:**
1. Rubrospinal tract: Originates in red nucleus; facilitates contralateral limb flexor tone and inhibits extensors.
2. Vestibulospinal tract: Originates in lateral vestibular nucleus; facilitates ipsilateral extensor antigravity muscles to maintain balance and posture.
3. Reticulospinal tract: Pontine reticulospinal facilitates extensors; medullary reticulospinal inhibits voluntary movements and extensors.

### Q9: List the brainstem lemnisci, their sensory modalities, and their terminations.
**Model Answer:**
1. Medial lemniscus: Conscious proprioception, fine touch, vibration from gracile/cuneate nuclei to VPL nucleus of thalamus.
2. Spinal lemniscus (spinothalamic): Pain, temperature, crude touch from contralateral dorsal horn to VPL nucleus of thalamus.
3. Trigeminal lemniscus: Sensation from face and head (trigeminal sensory nuclei) to VPM nucleus of thalamus.
4. Lateral lemniscus: Auditory pathway from cochlear nuclei and superior olive to inferior colliculus.

### Q10: Enumerate the microscopic layers of the cerebellar cortex and their cellular components.
**Model Answer:**
1. Molecular layer (outermost): Stellate cells, basket cells, Purkinje cell dendrites, and parallel fibers of granule cells.
2. Purkinje cell layer (middle): Monolayer of large flask-shaped Purkinje cells; represents the sole inhibitory (GABAergic) output of cerebellar cortex.
3. Granular layer (innermost): Densely packed granule cells (glutamatergic) and Golgi type II cells, forming cerebellar glomeruli with mossy fibers.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2021`

### Q12: Demonstrate the mechanism of Babinski sign (extensor plantar response).
**Model Answer:**
Mechanism:
- Normal flexor response: S1 stimulation causes plantar flexion of toes because corticospinal tract tonically suppresses primitive spinal withdrawal reflex.
- Babinski sign: Corticospinal (pyramidal) lesion abolishes descending cortical inhibition, unmasking primitive multi-synaptic spinal flexion withdrawal reflex; lateral sole stimulation produces great toe dorsiflexion and fanning of remaining toes.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2021`

### Q13: Explain the effect of excessive stretch of the skeletal muscle (Inverse stretch reflex / Autogenic inhibition).
**Model Answer:**
Mechanism of Inverse Stretch Reflex (Golgi Tendon Reflex):
Excessive contraction or stretch produces extreme tension on Golgi tendon organs (GTOs). Ib afferent fibers carry impulses to spinal cord and stimulate inhibitory interneurons. Inhibitory interneurons release glycine/GABA, inhibiting alpha motor neurons supplying contracting muscle, causing sudden relaxation (clasp-knife reaction) and preventing avulsion/tendon rupture.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2021`

### Q14: Describe the functions and clinical significance of Wernicke's area.
**Model Answer:**
Functions: Sensory/receptive speech center in posterior part of superior temporal gyrus (area 22) in dominant hemisphere. Crucial for understanding, comprehension, and interpretation of spoken and written language.
Clinical significance (Wernicke's Aphasia): Fluent but meaningless speech ('word salad', paraphasias, neologisms), severely impaired auditory comprehension, inability to repeat phrases, with patient typically unaware of linguistic deficit.

### Q15: Describe the functions and anatomical location of the primary motor area (Area 4).

Functions: Initiates and executes discrete, voluntary, skilled motor movements of contralateral body, organized as an inverted motor homunculus; contributes ~30% of corticospinal (pyramidal) tract fibers and contains giant Betz cells in layer V.

### Q16: Compare between primary (short-term) memory and secondary (long-term) memory.
**Model Answer:**
1. Duration: Primary = seconds to minutes; Secondary = days, months, to years.
2. Mechanism: Primary = reverberating electrical circuits or presynaptic transient facilitation; Secondary = structural synaptic changes, LTP, new protein synthesis, increased AMPA receptors.
3. Capacity: Primary = limited (7 +/- 2 items); Secondary = virtually unlimited.
4. Vulnerability: Primary = easily disrupted by concussion, anesthesia; Secondary = stable and resistant to disruption.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2021`

### Q17: Why is Egypt subjected to have viral encephalitis like Dengue fever and West Nile virus?
**Model Answer:**
Factors:
1. Subtropical warm climate favoring mosquito breeding throughout most of the year.
2. Presence of vector mosquito species: Aedes aegypti (Dengue) and Culex pipiens (West Nile virus).
3. Stagnant water networks, irrigation canals, and agricultural drainage in rural Nile Delta.
4. Location along major bird migration flyways facilitating arboviral importation and transmission.

### Q18: Explain why persons bitten by a rabid animal can benefit from post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP).
**Model Answer:**
Reason: Rabies virus travels very slowly via retrograde axonal transport along peripheral nerves (8-20 mm/day), resulting in a long incubation period (typically 1-3 months). Prompt administration of active rabies vaccine and human rabies immunoglobulin (HRIG) allows host immune system to generate high titers of neutralizing antibodies that neutralize virus before it accesses the CNS.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2021`

### Q19: Compare between typical (first-generation) and atypical (second-generation) neuroleptics (antipsychotics).
**Model Answer:**
1. Mechanism: Typical = high-affinity D2 dopamine receptor antagonists; Atypical = dual 5-HT2A serotonin and D2 dopamine receptor antagonists.
2. Efficacy: Typical = controls only positive symptoms of schizophrenia; Atypical = treats both positive and negative symptoms (apathy, social withdrawal).
3. Side effects: Typical = frequent extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS, dystonia, parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia); Atypical = low EPS risk, but high metabolic side effects (weight gain, dyslipidemia, diabetes).

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2021`

### Q20: Define Alzheimer disease and discuss its main pathological hallmarks.
**Model Answer:**
Definition: Most common neurodegenerative cause of progressive dementia in elderly, presenting with insidious cognitive decline and memory impairment.
Pathological Hallmarks:
1. Extracellular Amyloid-Beta (Aβ42) senile/neuritic plaques from abnormal cleavage of APP by β- and γ-secretases.
2. Intracellular Neurofibrillary Tangles (NFTs) of hyperphosphorylated tau protein.
3. Marked cerebral cortical atrophy and ventricular enlargement (hydrocephalus ex vacuo), prominently in temporal/parietal lobes and hippocampus.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2021`

### Q21: Describe Epidural (Extradural) hemorrhage: Etiology, source of bleeding, neuroimaging, and clinical course.
**Model Answer:**
Etiology: Skull fracture across pterion injuring middle meningeal artery.
Neuroimaging: Hyperdense biconvex / lens-shaped hematoma limited by suture lines.
Clinical course: Brief initial loss of consciousness -> lucid interval of several hours -> rapid neurological deterioration, uncal herniation (blown pupil, hemiparesis), and coma requiring emergency surgical evacuation.

### Q22: Describe Subdural hemorrhage: Etiology, source of bleeding, and neuroimaging appearance.
**Model Answer:**
Etiology: Tearing of bridging veins traversing subdural space into superior sagittal sinus from blunt acceleration-deceleration trauma (elderly, infants).
Neuroimaging: Crescent-shaped / concave hematoma crossing cranial suture lines along cerebral convexity.
Types: Acute (hyperdense), Subacute (isodense), Chronic (hypodense).

### Q23: Describe Subarachnoid hemorrhage: Etiology, hallmark symptoms, and neuroimaging.
**Model Answer:**
Etiology: Rupture of berry / saccular aneurysm in circle of Willis or arteriovenous malformation.
Hallmark symptoms: Sudden catastrophic 'worst headache of my life' (thunderclap headache), vomiting, meningismus (neck stiffness, photophobia).
Neuroimaging: Non-contrast CT shows blood in basal cisterns, sulci, and sylvian fissures. Lumbar puncture demonstrates xanthochromia.

### Q26: Explain the clinical differences in Brown-Sequard syndrome at T10 level.
**Model Answer:**
Brown-Sequard syndrome (T10 hemisection):
1. Ipsilateral below lesion: UMN spastic paralysis (corticospinal tract); loss of conscious proprioception, vibration, two-point discrimination (dorsal column).
2. Contralateral below lesion (1-2 segments below): Loss of pain and temperature (spinothalamic tract).
3. At lesion level (T10): Complete flaccid LMN paralysis and band of total anesthesia in T10 dermatome.

### Q27: Compare between Conus Medullaris and Cauda Equina syndromes.
**Model Answer:**
1. Location: Conus = terminal spinal cord (L1-L2); Cauda Equina = lumbosacral nerve roots (below L2).
2. Onset: Conus = sudden, symmetric; Cauda Equina = gradual, asymmetric.
3. Motor: Conus = mild symmetric paresis; Cauda Equina = marked asymmetric flaccid paraplegia with absent tendon jerks.
4. Sensory: Conus = symmetric saddle anesthesia (S3-S5); Cauda Equina = asymmetric dermatomal sensory loss.
5. Sphincters: Conus = early and severe urinary/fecal incontinence and impotence; Cauda Equina = late.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2021`

### Q28: Describe the blood-brain barrier: Structural components and physiological importance.
**Model Answer:**
Components: 1. Continuous non-fenestrated capillary endothelium with tight junctions (zonulae occludentes). 2. Continuous endothelial basement membrane. 3. Astrocyte perivascular end-feet (podocytes) covering 99% of capillary surface.
Importance: Protects neural tissue from systemic toxins, fluctuating ions, and pathogens; maintains exact ionic microenvironment for neuronal transmission; allows selective passage of glucose (GLUT1) and essential nutrients.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2021`

### Q29: Describe the course and termination of the corticospinal (pyramidal) tract.
**Model Answer:**
Origin: Area 4 (30%), Area 6 & SMA (30%), Areas 3, 1, 2 (40%).
Course: Corona radiata -> posterior limb of internal capsule -> middle 3/5 of crus cerebri in midbrain -> basilar pons -> anterior medulla (pyramids).
Decussation & Termination: Lower medulla pyramidal decussation: 85-90% cross to form lateral corticospinal tract in lateral column, synapsing on alpha/gamma motor neurons in anterior horn; 10-15% uncrossed anterior corticospinal tract cross at segmental level.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2021`
