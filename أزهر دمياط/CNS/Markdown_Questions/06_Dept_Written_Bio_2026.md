### Q1: Mention the difference of action and function between L-Dopa & Alpha-methyl dopa.
**Model Answer:**
1. L-dopa: Used as a drug in Parkinsonism; since dopamine does not cross the blood-brain barrier, L-dopa is administered to cross the BBB and increase dopamine levels in the brain.
2. Alpha-methyl dopa: Antihypertensive agent that inhibits dopa decarboxylase, reducing dopamine and noradrenaline synthesis, and acts on central alpha-2 receptors to lower sympathetic tone.

### Q5: Explain the mechanisms of botulinum toxin and curare at the neuromuscular junction.
**Model Answer:**

### Q10: Mention the essential criteria that a chemical messenger must meet to be classified as a neurotransmitter.
**Model Answer:**
1. Synthesized within the presynaptic neuron.
2. Stored in presynaptic vesicles and released upon depolarization in Ca2+-dependent manner.
3. Exerts a specific physiological effect on postsynaptic receptors matching exogenous application.
4. Possesses a specific termination mechanism (enzymatic inactivation, reuptake, or diffusion).

**Tag:** `Department, Biochemistry 2026`

### Q11: Enumerate the primary mechanisms for neurotransmitter inactivation at synapses.
**Model Answer:**
1. Reuptake into presynaptic terminals or uptake into astrocytes.
2. Enzymatic degradation within the cleft (e.g. acetylcholinesterase, COMT).
3. Passive diffusion away from the synaptic cleft.

### Q17: Define Prions and name their associated disease mechanism.
**Model Answer:**
Prions are proteinaceous infectious particles devoid of nucleic acids. They propagate by inducing conformational transition of normal cellular prion protein (PrPC) into the abnormal, beta-sheet-rich, protease-resistant scrapie isoform (PrPSc).

**Tag:** `Department, Biochemistry 2026`

### Q18: Explain the normal versus abnormal pathways of Amyloid Precursor Protein (APP) cleavage.
**Model Answer:**
1. Non-amyloidogenic (Normal) pathway: APP is sequentially cleaved by alpha-secretase and gamma-secretase, producing soluble neuroprotective sAPP-alpha and p3 peptide.
2. Amyloidogenic (Pathogenic) pathway: APP is cleaved by beta-secretase (BACE1) followed by gamma-secretase, generating neurotoxic Amyloid-beta peptides (Aβ40 and Aβ42).

**Tag:** `Department, Biochemistry 2026`

### Q19: Differentiate between Amyloid Plaques and Neurofibrillary Tangles.
**Model Answer:**
Amyloid Plaques: Extracellular deposits composed of aggregated Amyloid-beta (Aβ) peptides derived from abnormal cleavage of APP by beta and gamma secretases.
Neurofibrillary Tangles: Intracellular structures composed of hyperphosphorylated tau protein that detaches from microtubules and aggregates into paired helical filaments.

**Tag:** `Department, Biochemistry 2026`

### Q21: What are the physiological energy substrates utilized by the adult brain under fed and fasting conditions?
**Model Answer:**
Under normal fed conditions: Glucose is the exclusive fuel. During prolonged fasting or starvation: Ketone bodies (acetoacetate and beta-hydroxybutyrate) cross the blood-brain barrier and can provide up to 60-70% of cerebral energy needs.

**Tag:** `Department, Biochemistry 2026`

### Q22: Why are fatty acids not used as a primary energy source by the human brain?
**Model Answer:**
1. Fatty acids bound to albumin cross the blood-brain barrier very slowly.
2. Mitochondrial beta-oxidation in neurons requires high oxygen consumption, risking hypoxia and generating excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS).
3. Neurons have low levels of key beta-oxidation enzymes.

**Tag:** `Department, Biochemistry 2026`

### Q23: What are the main roles of lipids in the brain?
**Model Answer:**
Brain lipids serve primarily structural roles: forming the multilayered myelin sheath for saltatory nerve conduction, establishing cellular membranes, facilitating vesicle trafficking, and contributing to signal transduction rather than energy production.

**Tag:** `Department, Biochemistry 2026`

### Q24: Describe transport mechanisms across the Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB).
**Model Answer:**
1. Simple lipid diffusion: Highly lipophilic non-polar molecules (e.g. O2, CO2, alcohol, steroid hormones, lipid-soluble drugs).
2. Facilitated transport: Specific carrier-mediated systems (e.g. GLUT1 for D-glucose, LAT1 for essential neutral amino acids).
3. Receptor-mediated transcytosis: Transferrin, insulin, LDL.

**Tag:** `Department, Biochemistry 2026`

### Q25: Why does the human brain consume approximately 20% of resting whole-body energy?
**Model Answer:**
The brain requires continuous ATP to: 1. Maintain ionic gradients across neuronal membranes via Na+/K+-ATPase pumps. 2. Drive neurotransmitter synthesis, vesicular packaging, and synaptic transmission. 3. Support axonal transport, membrane remodeling, and cellular biosynthesis.

**Tag:** `Department, Biochemistry 2026`

### Q26: Explain the cerebral metabolic adaptations during feeding versus fasting states.
**Model Answer:**
Feeding State: Brain relies entirely on glucose; insulin promotes glycolysis and lipid synthesis.
Fasting/Starvation State: Liver glycogen is depleted; liver converts fatty acids to ketone bodies (acetoacetate and beta-hydroxybutyrate), which the brain oxidizes to acetyl-CoA to spare muscle protein from gluconeogenesis.

**Tag:** `Department, Biochemistry 2026`

### Q27: What is the importance of the Pentose Phosphate Pathway (PPP) in cerebral tissue?
**Model Answer:**
1. Generates NADPH required for reductive biosynthesis (fatty acids, myelin) and regeneration of reduced glutathione (antioxidant defense).
2. Produces ribose-5-phosphate for RNA, DNA, and nucleotide coenzyme synthesis.

**Tag:** `Department, Biochemistry 2026`

### Q28: Enumerate the essential biochemical roles of NADPH in brain metabolism.
