### Q10: Enumerate effects of point mutation on protein expression

**Model Answer:**
1. Silent mutation: Same amino acid is incorporated due to codon degeneracy; no change in protein function.
2. Missense mutation: A different amino acid is incorporated, which may alter protein structure or activity (e.g., Sickle cell anemia).
3. Nonsense mutation: Premature stop codon (UAA, UAG, UGA) is created, producing a truncated, non-functional protein.
4. Splice-site mutation: Mutation at an intron-exon junction leading to aberrant splicing and non-functional protein product.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2020`

### Q11: Explain: In liver cells, the Apo B gene produces Apo B-100, but in intestinal cells produces Apo B-48

**Model Answer:**
This occurs due to RNA editing (post-transcriptional base modification). In intestinal cells, the enzyme cytidine deaminase converts a specific cytosine (C) to uracil (U) in the Apo B mRNA, changing the codon CAA (encoding glutamine) into the premature stop codon UAA. Translation terminates early, producing the shorter Apo B-48 protein. In liver cells, this editing does not occur, and the full-length Apo B-100 protein is synthesized.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2020`

### Q12: Explain: Why the genetic code is degenerate (redundant)

**Model Answer:**
Because most amino acids are specified by more than one triplet codon (61 codons code for 20 standard amino acids). For example, leucine, serine, and arginine are each coded by 6 different codons. This provides a vital protective buffer against deleterious consequences of point mutations.

**Tag:** `Exams, Final 2020`

### Q13: Explain the structure of a chromosome and the significance of the centromere

**Model Answer:**
• Structure: Each chromosome is formed by two sister chromatids joined together at a constriction point called the centromere. The centromere divides each chromatid into two arms: a short arm (p arm) and a long arm (q arm).
• Significance of Centromere: The centromere gives each chromosome its characteristic morphological shape and contains kinetochores where spindle microtubules attach during cell division, ensuring accurate chromosome segregation to daughter cells.

### Q14: Discuss the difference between genotype and phenotype with examples

• Phenotype: The observable physical, biochemical, or physiological expression of that genotype as influenced by environmental factors (e.g., brown eyes vs blue eyes, normal vs sickle cell hemoglobin).

### Q15: Describe the process of karyotyping and its importance in medical genetics

**Model Answer:**
• Process:
  1. Peripheral blood is collected in an anticoagulant tube (heparin).
  2. Mononuclear lymphocytes are separated and cultured in growth medium with phytohemagglutinin (PHA) to stimulate mitotic division.
  3. After 48-72 hours, colchicine is added to arrest dividing cells in metaphase by disrupting spindle microtubules.
  4. Cells are treated with a hypotonic solution to swell nuclei, fixed, spread on slides, and stained (e.g., Giemsa banding).
  5. Metaphase spreads are photographed and arranged in homologous pairs.
