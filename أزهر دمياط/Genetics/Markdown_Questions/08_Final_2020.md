## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q29: Define: The genetic code

**Model Answer:**
The set of rules by which information encoded in genetic material (DNA or RNA sequences) is translated into proteins by living cells. It consists of 64 triplet codons, degenerate, unambiguous, non-overlapping, and comma-less.

---

### Q30: Define: Splicing

**Model Answer:**
The post-transcriptional modification process in which non-coding sequences (introns) are excised from the primary RNA transcript (pre-mRNA) and coding sequences (exons) are joined together by the spliceosome to produce mature mRNA.

---

### Q31: Define: Cell cycle

**Model Answer:**
The ordered sequence of events that a cell goes through resulting in cell growth and division into two daughter cells. It consists of Interphase (G1, S, and G2 phases) and the M phase (Mitosis and Cytokinesis).

---

### Q32: Define: Gene

**Model Answer:**
The fundamental physical and functional unit of heredity; a specific sequence of nucleotides in DNA (or RNA in some viruses) that encodes the synthesis of a functional gene product, either RNA or protein.

---

### Q33: Enumerate stages of Prophase I of the First Meiotic Division

**Model Answer:**
1. Leptotene (condensation of chromatin into visible chromosomes).
2. Zygotene (synapsis of homologous chromosomes forming bivalents).
3. Pachytene (crossing over and genetic recombination).
4. Diplotene (dissolution of synaptonemal complex, chiasmata become visible).
5. Diakinesis (terminalization of chiasmata and nuclear envelope breakdown).

---

### Q34: Enumerate effects of point mutation on protein expression

**Model Answer:**
1. Silent mutation: Same amino acid is incorporated due to codon degeneracy; no change in protein function.
2. Missense mutation: A different amino acid is incorporated, which may alter protein structure or activity (e.g., Sickle cell anemia).
3. Nonsense mutation: Premature stop codon (UAA, UAG, UGA) is created, producing a truncated, non-functional protein.
4. Splice-site mutation: Mutation at an intron-exon junction leading to aberrant splicing and non-functional protein product.

---

### Q35: Explain: In liver cells, the Apo B gene produces Apo B-100, but in intestinal cells produces Apo B-48

**Model Answer:**
This occurs due to RNA editing (post-transcriptional base modification). In intestinal cells, the enzyme cytidine deaminase converts a specific cytosine (C) to uracil (U) in the Apo B mRNA, changing the codon CAA (encoding glutamine) into the premature stop codon UAA. Translation terminates early, producing the shorter Apo B-48 protein. In liver cells, this editing does not occur, and the full-length Apo B-100 protein is synthesized.

---

### Q36: Explain: Why the genetic code is degenerate (redundant)

**Model Answer:**
Because most amino acids are specified by more than one triplet codon (61 codons code for 20 standard amino acids). For example, leucine, serine, and arginine are each coded by 6 different codons. This provides a vital protective buffer against deleterious consequences of point mutations.
