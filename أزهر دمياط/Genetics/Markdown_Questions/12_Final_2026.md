# Final 2026 Exam Questions (Written)

### Q1: Define: The genetic code

**Model Answer:**
The set of rules by which information encoded in genetic material (DNA or RNA sequences) is translated into proteins by living cells. It consists of 64 triplet codons, degenerate, unambiguous, non-overlapping, and comma-less.

---

### Q2: Define: Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA)

**Model Answer:**
Circular, double-stranded DNA found in mitochondria, maternally inherited, lacking histones, with a higher mutation rate than nuclear DNA. It encodes 13 respiratory chain proteins, 22 tRNAs, and 2 rRNAs.

---

### Q3: Define: Karyotyping

**Model Answer:**
The process of pairing and ordering all the chromosomes of an organism, providing a genome-wide snapshot of an individual's chromosomes to detect numerical or structural abnormalities.

---

### Q4: Define: Apoptosis

**Model Answer:**
Programmed, energy-dependent cell death characterized by cell shrinkage, chromatin condensation, nuclear fragmentation, plasma membrane blebbing, and formation of apoptotic bodies without inducing an inflammatory response.

---

### Q5: Enumerate components of PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction)

**Model Answer:**
1. DNA template.
2. Forward and reverse DNA primers.
3. Deoxynucleoside triphosphates (dNTPs).
4. Thermostable DNA polymerase (Taq polymerase).
5. Buffer solution containing magnesium ions (Mg2+).

---

### Q6: Enumerate translation initiation complex components

**Model Answer:**
1. Small ribosomal subunit (40S in eukaryotes / 30S in prokaryotes).
2. mRNA.
3. Initiator tRNA carrying methionine (Met-tRNA).
4. Eukaryotic initiation factors (eIFs).
5. GTP as energy source.
6. Large ribosomal subunit (60S/50S).

---

### Q7: Enumerate stages of Prophase I of the First Meiotic Division

**Model Answer:**
1. Leptotene (condensation of chromatin into visible chromosomes).
2. Zygotene (synapsis of homologous chromosomes forming bivalents).
3. Pachytene (crossing over and genetic recombination).
4. Diplotene (dissolution of synaptonemal complex, chiasmata become visible).
5. Diakinesis (terminalization of chiasmata and nuclear envelope breakdown).

---

### Q8: Discuss Interphase of the cell cycle

**Model Answer:**
The non-dividing phase of the cell cycle where the cell grows and replicates its DNA in preparation for cell division. Divided into three successive phases:
1. G1 phase (Gap 1): Intensive cell growth, RNA, and protein synthesis.
2. S phase (Synthesis): Semi-conservative DNA replication and duplication of centrosomes.
3. G2 phase (Gap 2): Synthesis of proteins required for spindle apparatus and final preparation for mitosis.
* Quiescent cells can exit G1 and enter G0 phase.

---

### Q9: Give reason: DNA replication is semi-conservative

**Model Answer:**
Because during DNA replication, each of the two newly synthesized DNA molecules retains one intact parental (conserved) strand and one newly synthesized complementary daughter strand.

---

### Q10: Explain the end replication problem (The TG strand at 3'-end is longer than its complement strand at 5'-end, leaving ssDNA shorter in length)

**Model Answer:**
DNA polymerase can only synthesize DNA in the 5' to 3' direction and requires an RNA primer. When the terminal RNA primer is degraded from the lagging strand, DNA polymerase cannot synthesize across the gap because there is no upstream 3'-OH group, leaving a single-stranded 3' overhang and causing progressive shortening of linear chromosomes (resolved by telomerase).

---

### Q11: Explain the molecular defect in Xeroderma Pigmentosum

**Model Answer:**
It is caused by deficiency or inherited mutation in UV-specific endonuclease (excinuclease), impairing the Nucleotide Excision Repair (NER) pathway. As a result, the cell cannot repair UV-induced thymine-thymine dimers, leading to DNA damage accumulation, severe photosensitivity, and early development of skin cancers.

---

### Q12: Explain: Sickle cell anemia as an example of genetic mutation

**Model Answer:**
It is caused by a point mutation (single base transversion of A to T) in codon 6 of the beta-globin gene (GAG to GTG), which substitutes hydrophilic glutamic acid with hydrophobic valine. This causes hemoglobin S (HbS) to polymerize under low oxygen tension, resulting in sickle-shaped erythrocytes and hemolytic anemia.

---

### Q13: Differentiate between template strand (antisense strand) and coding strand (sense strand)

**Model Answer:**
- Template Strand (Antisense / Non-coding strand): The 3' to 5' DNA strand that serves as the physical template for RNA polymerase during transcription; its sequence is complementary to the synthesized mRNA.
- Coding Strand (Sense / Non-template strand): The 5' to 3' DNA strand whose nucleotide sequence is identical to the synthesized mRNA (with thymine T in DNA instead of uracil U in RNA).
