### Q1: Regulation of Cholesterol Synthesis

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
Key regulatory enzyme is HMG-CoA reductase. Regulated by: 1) Feedback inhibition by intracellular cholesterol; 2) Hormonal regulation: Insulin and thyroid hormones activate (dephosphorylation) while glucagon and epinephrine inactivate (phosphorylation by AMP-activated protein kinase); 3) Transcriptional regulation via SREBP-2 (Sterol Regulatory Element-Binding Protein); 4) Competitive inhibition by statins.

### Q4: The metabolism of chylomicrons

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
1) Synthesized in intestinal mucosal cells from dietary lipids (TAG, cholesterol, Apo B-48); 2) Secreted into lymphatic system and enter blood via thoracic duct; 3) Mature by acquiring Apo C-II and Apo E from HDL; 4) Apo C-II activates lipoprotein lipase (LPL) on capillary endothelium of adipose tissue and muscle to hydrolyze TAG into glycerol and FFAs; 5) Chylomicron remnants depleted of TAG are taken up by the liver via Apo E recognition by LDL-receptor-related protein (LRP).

### Q5: Apoproteins functions

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
1) Apo B-48: Structural protein for chylomicrons, required for secretion; 2) Apo B-100: Structural protein for VLDL and LDL, ligand for LDL receptor; 3) Apo C-II: Activator of lipoprotein lipase (LPL); 4) Apo E: Recognition ligand for hepatic clearance of remnants and IDL; 5) Apo A-I: Major structural apoprotein of HDL, activates LCAT (lecithin-cholesterol acyltransferase).

### Q6: Role of Lipoprotein (a) in heart disease

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
Lipoprotein(a) consists of an LDL-like particle containing Apo B-100 covalently linked to apolipoprotein(a). Apo(a) shares high structural homology with plasminogen, competing with plasminogen for binding to endothelial cells and fibrin, thereby inhibiting fibrinolysis and promoting thrombosis. It also accelerates atherogenesis by promoting endothelial dysfunction and foam cell formation, serving as an independent risk factor for CAD and myocardial infarction.

### Q7: Importance of HDL

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
1) Mediates reverse cholesterol transport, removing excess cholesterol from peripheral tissues and transporting it to the liver for biliary excretion; 2) Contains Apo A-I which activates LCAT to esterify cholesterol; 3) Acts as a circulating reservoir for Apo C-II and Apo E for chylomicrons and VLDL; 4) Has anti-inflammatory, antioxidant (via paraoxonase), and anti-thrombotic properties.

### Q8: Atherosclerosis and LDL (mechanism)

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
1) Excess circulating LDL penetrates endothelial barrier into arterial intima; 2) LDL undergoes oxidative modification by free radicals; 3) Oxidized LDL induces endothelial activation, expressing adhesion molecules (VCAM-1) and chemoattractants (MCP-1) that recruit monocytes; 4) Monocytes differentiate into macrophages; 5) Macrophages ingest oxidized LDL via scavenger receptors (SR-A, CD36) forming lipid-laden foam cells; 6) Foam cells release cytokines promoting smooth muscle migration and fibrous plaque formation.

### Q10: Prevention of atherosclerosis

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
1) Lifestyle modifications: Healthy diet low in saturated fats and trans fats, high in soluble fiber and omega-3 fatty acids; 2) Regular aerobic exercise and weight management; 3) Smoking cessation; 4) Strict glycemic control in diabetics and BP control (<130/80 mmHg); 5) Pharmacological therapy: Statins for dyslipidemia, low-dose aspirin in high-risk patients.
