### Q35: Explain the effect of increased venous return on the heart rate (Bainbridge reflex).

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
An increase in venous return increases the volume of blood entering the right atrium, stretching low-pressure stretch receptors (type B atrial receptors) located at the veno-atrial junctions. Afferent impulses travel via the vagus nerve to the medulla (nucleus tractus solitarius), which reflexively increases sympathetic efferent discharge to the sinoatrial (SA) node and decreases parasympathetic tone. This results in reflex tachycardia (Bainbridge reflex), preventing venous pooling and matching cardiac output to venous return.

### Q36: Discuss the mechanism of blood pressure regulation by the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS).

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
1) Trigger: Decreased renal perfusion pressure (sensed by renal baroreceptors in afferent arterioles), decreased Na+ delivery to macula densa, or increased renal sympathetic activity stimulates juxtaglomerular (JG) cells to secrete renin into the bloodstream; 2) Enzymatic Cascade: Renin cleaves circulating angiotensinogen (from liver) to form angiotensin I (decapeptide). Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), predominantly on pulmonary endothelial cells, cleaves Ang I to form Angiotensin II (octapeptide); 3) Actions of Angiotensin II: Potent direct arteriolar vasoconstriction (via AT1 receptors) increasing Total Peripheral Resistance (TPR); stimulates adrenal cortex (zona glomerulosa) to secrete aldosterone, promoting renal tubular Na+ and water reabsorption and K+ excretion; stimulates pituitary secretion of vasopressin (ADH); stimulates thirst center in hypothalamus; increases sympathetic outflow.

### Q37: Explain on a biochemical basis how vegetable oils and Polyunsaturated Fatty Acids (PUFA) protect against atherosclerosis.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
Polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs, such as linoleic acid and omega-3 alpha-linolenic, EPA, and DHA) protect against atherosclerosis through multiple biochemical mechanisms: 1) Upregulation of hepatic LDL receptors: PUFAs increase LDL receptor gene expression, enhancing the clearance of atherogenic LDL from circulation and lowering plasma LDL-cholesterol; 2) Promotion of cholesterol excretion: PUFAs enhance hepatic 7-alpha-hydroxylase activity, accelerating the conversion of cholesterol into bile acids for fecal elimination; 3) Reduction of hepatic VLDL secretion: Omega-3 PUFAs reduce hepatic synthesis and secretion of triacylglycerol and VLDL by inhibiting diacylglycerol acyltransferase (DGAT) and promoting fatty acid beta-oxidation; 4) Anti-inflammatory and antithrombotic effects: Omega-3 PUFAs compete with arachidonic acid, shifting eicosanoid synthesis toward less inflammatory series-3 prostaglandins and series-5 leukotrienes, and anti-aggregatory thromboxane A3, suppressing platelet aggregation and endothelial inflammation.
