### Q33: What is the surgical importance of the transverse pericardial sinus?

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
The transverse sinus lies posterior to the ascending aorta and pulmonary trunk, and anterior to the SVC and left atrium. During cardiac surgery (e.g. coronary artery bypass or aortic repair), a ligature or surgical clamp can be passed through the transverse sinus to temporarily occlude both the aorta and pulmonary trunk, halting blood flow while establishing cardiopulmonary bypass.

### Q41: Explain why Lipoprotein(a) has a significant role in coronary heart disease.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
Lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] consists of an LDL particle linked to apolipoprotein(a). Apo(a) has structural homology to plasminogen but lacks fibrinolytic activity. It competitively inhibits plasminogen binding to fibrin and endothelial surfaces, impairing plasmin formation and fibrinolysis, thereby promoting atherothrombosis. ### Q44: Enumerate three cardiovascular reflexes affecting heart rate.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
1) Arterial baroreceptor reflex: Carotid sinus and aortic arch baroreceptors respond to increased arterial pressure by increasing vagal parasympathetic output to SA node, slowing HR (bradycardia), and decreasing sympathetic activity; 2) Bainbridge (atrial) reflex: Stretch of right atrial wall by increased venous return stimulates low-pressure stretch receptors, reflexively increasing HR via sympathetic efferents to prevent atrial blood pooling; 3) Peripheral chemoreceptor reflex: Hypoxia/hypercapnia stimulates carotid and aortic bodies, producing reflex tachycardia and increased vasoconstriction.

### Q45: Explain the physiological importance of the long absolute refractory period (ARP) in cardiac muscle.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
Cardiac muscle action potential exhibits a prolonged plateau phase (phase 2) mediated by inward L-type Ca2+ current, giving an ARP that lasts almost as long as the entire mechanical contraction (~250-300 ms). Importance: 1) Prevents summation and tetanic contractions, ensuring the heart alternates rhythmically between contraction (systole) and relaxation (diastole) for ventricular filling; 2) Prevents premature re-excitation and re-entrant tachyarrhythmias, maintaining unidirectional electrical wave propagation.
