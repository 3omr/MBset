### Q11: Enumerate the causes of the pacemaker potential (prepotential) in the SA node.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
The pacemaker potential (prepotential) in the SA node is caused by three ionic mechanisms: 1) Decreasing potassium efflux: progressive inactivation of delayed rectifier K+ channels decreases outward hyperpolarizing K+ current; 2) Funny current (If): inward sodium current through hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated (HCN) channels activated at negative potentials below -50 mV; 3) Calcium influx: transient opening of low-voltage-activated T-type Ca2+ channels in the late phase of prepotential, followed by L-type Ca2+ channel opening at threshold (-40 mV) to generate the upstroke.

### Q12: Explain the mechanisms by which moderate hypoxia increases heart rate.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
Moderate hypoxia increases heart rate through: 1) Peripheral chemoreceptor stimulation: carotid and aortic body chemoreceptors sense reduced arterial PO2, stimulating the medullary cardiac center to increase sympathetic efferent discharge to the SA node; 2) Increased circulating catecholamines: hypoxia stimulates the adrenal medulla to release epinephrine and norepinephrine; 3) Pulmonary stretch receptor reflex: hypoxia induces hyperventilation, and lung inflation stimulates vagal pulmonary stretch receptors which reflexively inhibit the cardiac vagal center, causing tachycardia.

### Q13: Enumerate the limits and components of cardiac reserve.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
Cardiac reserve is the difference between the maximum cardiac output an individual can achieve and the cardiac output at rest. Factors/limits determining cardiac reserve include: 1) Heart rate reserve: maximal HR (approx. 220 - age) versus resting HR (60-80 bpm); 2) Stroke volume reserve: ability to increase SV from resting (~70 mL) to maximal (~120-150 mL in athletes) via increased end-diastolic volume (Frank-Starling) and increased myocardial contractility (inotropic reserve); 3) Coronary flow reserve: maximal increase in coronary blood flow during maximal exertion (normally 4-5 fold above baseline).

### Q15: Describe the light microscopic (LM) structure of cardiac muscle fibers.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
1) Involuntary striated muscle consisting of cylindrical branching and anastomosing cells; 2) Each cardiomyocyte has a single centrally located oval euchromatic nucleus (occasionally binucleated); 3) Sarcoplasm contains cross-striations with A and I bands, rich in mitochondria and glycogen; 4) Cardiomyocytes are joined end-to-end by intercalated discs with transverse steps (fascia adherens and desmosomes for mechanical anchoring) and longitudinal segments (gap junctions for electrical coupling); 5) Endomysium surrounding each fiber contains a rich capillary plexus.

### Q17: Define the following: 1) Rheumatic fever; 2) Angina pectoris; 3) Cardiomyopathy; 4) Myocarditis.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
1) Rheumatic fever: An acute, immunologically mediated, multi-system inflammatory disease occurring 2-3 weeks after Group A beta-hemolytic streptococcal (Streptococcus pyogenes) pharyngeal infection, characterized by cross-reactive antibodies attacking host cardiac and other tissues. 2) Angina pectoris: A clinical syndrome characterized by paroxysmal, transient substernal chest discomfort or pressure, radiating to left shoulder/arm or jaw, precipitated by myocardial ischemia due to imbalance between myocardial oxygen supply and demand, without causing myocyte necrosis. 3) Cardiomyopathy: A heterogeneous group of myocardial diseases associated with mechanical and/or electrical dysfunction, typically presenting with inappropriate ventricular hypertrophy or dilatation, not secondary to CAD, hypertension, or valvular disease. 4) Myocarditis: Inflammatory disease of the myocardium diagnosed by histological, immunological, and immunohistochemical criteria, most commonly caused by viral infection (Coxsackievirus B, adenovirus, parvovirus B19).
