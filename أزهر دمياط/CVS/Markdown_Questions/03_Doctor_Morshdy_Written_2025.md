### Q22: Mention preventive measures for Rheumatic fever.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
1. Primary prevention: Prompt and complete treatment of Streptococcus pyogenes pharyngitis with penicillin for a full 10-day course.
2. Secondary prevention: Long-acting benzathine penicillin G monthly injections to prevent recurrence in patients with prior rheumatic fever.
3. Antibiotic prophylaxis before dental procedures for patients with established rheumatic heart disease.

**Correct Answer:** -

### Q23: Mention preventive measures for Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis (SBE).

2. Good oral and dental hygiene.
3. Secondary antibiotic prophylaxis in patients with pre-existing valvular disease.

### Q24: What is the most reliable method for prevention and control of plague?

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
Vector and reservoir control measures: Application of insecticides (anti-flea measures) followed by rodent control (anti-rat measures), combined with quarantine of pneumonic cases and chemoprophylaxis with doxycycline for close contacts.

### Q26: Give reason: Antibiotic prophylaxis is required before oral surgery in patients with a history of rheumatic fever.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
Dental manipulations produce transient bacteremia with oral viridans streptococci (Streptococcus mutans, mitis). In patients with rheumatic valve damage, these bacteria readily adhere to the abnormal endothelial surface and fibrin deposits, causing Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis (SBE).

**Correct Answer:** -

### Q27: Give reason: Blood culture has no role in the diagnosis of rheumatic fever.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
Rheumatic fever is not a direct bacterial infection of the heart, but a sterile post-streptococcal autoimmune disease caused by cross-reactivity (molecular mimicry) between streptococcal M protein and host cardiac tissues. The bacterium has usually been cleared by the time rheumatic symptoms appear.

**Correct Answer:** -

---

### Q28: Give reason: Rat and flea control measures are critical in plague prevention.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
Because wild and domestic rodents (rats) are the natural animal reservoir and rat fleas (Xenopsylla cheopis) are the primary transmitting vector of Yersinia pestis to humans. Insecticides must precede rodenticides to prevent starved fleas from abandoning dead rats and biting humans.

**Correct Answer:** -

### Q30: Classify bacterial endocarditis regarding the causative organism and clinical course.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
1. Acute Bacterial Endocarditis (ABE): Rapid, fulminant destruction of normal or abnormal valves, caused predominantly by virulent bacteria like Staphylococcus aureus and Streptococcus pyogenes.
2. Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis (SBE): Indolent, protracted course affecting previously damaged or abnormal valves, caused predominantly by low-virulence organisms like Streptococcus viridans and Enterococcus faecalis.

### Q33: Compare between Streptococcus pyogenes and Streptococcus viridans regarding carditis.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
1. Streptococcus pyogenes: Causes acute pancarditis (endocarditis, myocarditis with Aschoff bodies, pericarditis) as a post-infectious sterile autoimmune sequela (rheumatic fever); blood cultures are negative for the organism in the heart.
2. Streptococcus viridans: Directly colonizes damaged endocardium/valves forming infective bacterial vegetations (Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis); blood cultures are positive and viable bacteria are present on the valve.

### Q42: Why do AIDS patients commonly suffer from repeated opportunistic fungal infections?

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
Fungi are opportunistic pathogens held in check by cellular immunity. Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) selectively infects and destroys CD4+ T-helper lymphocytes, severely depleting cell-mediated immunity and leaving the patient highly susceptible to fungal infections like Candida albicans, Cryptococcus neoformans, and Pneumocystis jirovecii.

### Q44: Give reason: Rheumatic fever occurs 2 to 3 weeks after Streptococcus pyogenes pharyngitis.

**Type:** QROC

**Model Answer:**
A latent period of 2 to 3 weeks is required for the patient's immune system to undergo sensitization, clonal expansion of B cells, and synthesis of high titers of antibodies against streptococcal antigens (such as M protein) that cross-react with human myocardial and valvular glycoproteins (molecular mimicry).
