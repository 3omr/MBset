### Question 1

Describe the structure of the cell membrane and explain the significance of its lipid bilayer?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: * **Structure:** It is approximately 7.5 nm thick. In Electron Microscopy (EM), it appears trilaminar (unit membrane) with two electron-dense layers separated by an electron-lucent zone.
 * **Significance:** The lipid bilayer is formed mainly of phospholipids and cholesterol. The hydrophilic heads face the surface, while the hydrophobic tails face each other in the interior. This structure maintains the structural integrity and creates a semi-permeable barrier.

---

### Question 2

Explain the roles of proteins in the cell membrane. Differentiate between intrinsic and extrinsic proteins?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: * **Roles:** Proteins act as ion channels, carrier proteins for transport, and cell receptors for drugs and hormones.
 * **Intrinsic (Integral) Proteins:** Embedded within the membrane thickness; some span the entire thickness (transmembrane proteins).
 * **Extrinsic (Peripheral) Proteins:** Located on the cytoplasmic or extracellular surfaces and do not enter the lipid bilayer.

---

### Question 3

What is the glycocalyx, and what functions does it serve in the cell membrane?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: * **Definition:** A carbohydrate layer (glycoproteins and glycolipids) on the external surface of the plasma membrane.
 * **Functions:** * Cell-to-cell adhesion.
 * Contains antigens like Major Histocompatibility Complexes (MHC).
 * Repels adjoining cells due to its negative charge.

---

### Question 4

Discuss the structural differences between Rough and Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum and their respective functions?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: * **Rough ER (RER):** Consists of parallel flattened sacs (cisternae) studded with ribosomes. Function: Protein synthesis (secretory, lysosomal, and membrane proteins).
 * **Smooth ER (SER):** System of interconnecting tubules without ribosomes. Function: Synthesis of steroid hormones, lipids, and glycogen; detoxification; and calcium storage in muscles.

---

### Question 5

What is the structural significance of the double membrane structure of mitochondria?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: * The outer membrane contains porins for permeability. The inner membrane forms **cristae** to increase surface area for respiratory enzymes. It is rich in cardiolipin, making it impermeable to ions.

---

### Question 6

Compare and contrast lysosomes and peroxisomes in terms of structure and function?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: * **Lysosomes:** Contain hydrolytic enzymes (pH 5.0) for intracellular digestion. Formed from the Golgi complex.
 * **Peroxisomes:** Contain oxidative enzymes (peroxidase, catalase) for fatty acid oxidation and neutralizing free radicals. They are self-replicating and not formed in Golgi.

### Question 30

Discribe the general character of epithelial tissue?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: * Single or multiple layers of close-packed cells with very little extracellular material.
 * Held together by intercellular junctions.
 * Lying on the basement membrane.
 * Avascular, nourished by diffusion from the underlying connective tissue layer.
 * Rich in sensory nerve endings.
 * Has the capacity to regenerate.
 * Derived from all three primordial germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm).
 * Covers the body surface, lines cavities/organs, and constitutes most glands.

### Question 48

Explain the differences between keratinized and non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: * **Keratinized:** Apical layers are dead cells, lack nuclei, and are packed with keratin protein. Found in epidermis (palms/soles). Protects against abrasion, water loss, and pathogens.
 * **Non-keratinized:** Lacks dead cell layers at the surface. Found in oral cavity, esophagus, vagina, and anal canal. Protects from mechanical abrasions during eating, swallowing, and defecation.

### Question 54

Explain the differences between collagen, reticular, and elastic fibers?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: * **Collagen Fibers:** Most abundant, thick, wavy, non-branched, and white in color. They are very strong with high tensile strength.
 * **Reticular Fibers:** Thinner than collagen, branched, and form delicate networks (reticulum). They are made of collagen type III and are argyrophilic (stained black with silver).
 * **Elastic Fibers:** Thinner than collagen, straight, branched, and highly deformable (can stretch and recoil).

---

### Question 55

What are glycosaminoglycans (GAGs), and what role do they play in connective tissue?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: * **Definition:** Polysaccharides composed of repeating non-branching disaccharides.
 * **Role:** They are highly negatively charged and hydrophilic, attracting sodium ions and water. This creates a hydrated, gel-like matrix that bears mechanical forces and regulates the passage of molecules.

---

### Question 56

Discuss the structural characteristics of elastic fibers and their functional significance?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: * **Structure:** Formed of an elastin core surrounded by microfibrils of fibrillin-I and elastin.
 * **Significance:** They provide elasticity to tissues, allowing them to stretch (up to 150%) and recoil. They are found in areas like the aorta and elastic arteries.

### Question 57
