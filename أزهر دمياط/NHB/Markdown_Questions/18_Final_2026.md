### Question 9

Steps of fertilization:

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Phase 1: Penetration of the corona radiata by capacitated spermatozoa using acrosomal hyaluronidase.
Phase 2: Penetration of the zona pellucida mediated by acrosin; triggers cortical and zona reaction to block polyspermy.
Phase 3: Fusion of oocyte and sperm cell membranes.
Phase 4: Oocyte completes its second meiotic division forming mature ovum and second polar body.
Phase 5: Formation and fusion of male and female pronuclei restoring diploid number (46) to form the zygote.

**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Decidua basalis: Lies deep to conceptus at implantation site; forms the maternal component of the definitive placenta.

### Question 21

The importance of Feed-forward mechanisms:

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Feed-forward mechanisms anticipate changes in regulated physiological variables before they occur, triggering adaptive homeostatic responses in advance to minimize delays inherent in negative feedback loops.
Examples:
1. Shivering and cutaneous vasoconstriction upon entering a cold environment before core temperature drops.
2. Cephalic phase of gastric and salivary secretion triggered by the sight or smell of food before ingestion.
3. Anticipatory rise in heart rate and respiratory rate prior to commencing vigorous exercise.

### Question 25

Total body water is lower in females than in males (Explain why):

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Females naturally possess a higher percentage of adipose tissue (body fat) and a lower skeletal muscle mass compared to males. Adipose tissue is relatively anhydrous (contains only ~10% water), whereas muscle tissue contains ~75% water. Therefore, total body water accounts for ~50% of total body weight in females compared to ~60% in adult males.

### Question 26

Why are parasympathetic actions more localized than sympathetic?:

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Restricted divergence: Parasympathetic preganglionic fibers synapse with very few postganglionic neurons (ratio ~1:2), whereas sympathetic fibers diverge widely (ratio 1:20 or more).
2. Location of ganglia: Parasympathetic ganglia are terminal (located in or immediately adjacent to target organs), confining responses to individual viscera.
3. Rapid transmitter degradation: Acetylcholine is rapidly hydrolyzed by local acetylcholinesterase, whereas sympathetic hormones (epinephrine/norepinephrine) enter systemic circulation and persist longer.

### Question 30

Why does anterior hypothalamus stimulation decrease heart rate?:

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: The anterior hypothalamus (preoptic area) contains the heat-loss center and acts as a major parasympathetic center. Stimulation sends efferent descending projections to the dorsal motor nucleus of vagus and nucleus ambiguus in the medulla, increasing vagal cardiac efferent activity which releases acetylcholine at SA/AV nodes, opening K+ channels (ACh-sensitive) and causing hyperpolarization and bradycardia.

### Question 39

What are the structural differences between cilia and microvilli?:

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Cytoskeletal core: Cilia contain an axoneme composed of microtubules in a '9+2' doublet arrangement; Microvilli contain a bundle of actin microfilaments linked by villin and fimbrin.
2. Basal structure: Cilia arise from basal bodies (centriole-derived 9 triplets); Microvilli insert into the terminal web of actin filaments in apical cytoplasm.
3. Function and Motility: Cilia are actively motile (ciliary beat for fluid transport); Microvilli are non-motile (increase apical absorptive surface area).
4. Dimensions: Cilia are long (5-10 μm) and thick (0.2 μm); Microvilli are short (1-2 μm) and thin (0.1 μm).

### Question 40

Write the Electron Microscopic (E.M) Structures of the non-dividing nucleus?:

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Nuclear Envelope: Double membrane with outer membrane studded with ribosomes and inner membrane lined by nuclear lamina, enclosing perinuclear space and perforated by nuclear pore complexes.
2. Chromatin: Heterochromatin (condensed, electron-dense, inactive) and Euchromatin (dispersed, electron-lucent, active).
3. Nucleolus: Dense non-membranous body composed of fibrillar centers, dense fibrillar component, and granular component for rRNA synthesis and ribosome subunit assembly.
4. Nuclear Matrix (Nucleoplasm): Semi-fluid proteinaceous ground substance supporting nuclear contents.

### Question 48

Enumerate three functions of lecithin:

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Main constituent of pulmonary surfactant (dipalmitoyl lecithin / DPPC) that lowers alveolar surface tension and prevents atelectasis.
2. Major structural phospholipid of cellular membranes and circulating plasma lipoproteins (HDL, VLDL, chylomicrons).
3. Serves as essential fatty acid donor for LCAT (Lecithin-Cholesterol Acyltransferase) in HDL, promoting cholesterol esterification and reverse cholesterol transport to prevent atherosclerosis.

### Question 49

Enumerate three Functions of glutathione?:

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Master intracellular antioxidant: Detoxifies hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and lipid hydroperoxides via glutathione peroxidase.
2. Protects and maintains essential protein and enzyme sulfhydryl (-SH) groups in the reduced state, preventing erythrocyte hemolysis.
3. Phase II xenobiotic detoxification: Conjugates with electrophilic toxins, drugs, and carcinogens via glutathione S-transferase for urinary excretion (mercapturic acids).

### Question 50

Enumerate three benefits of Free Radicals:

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Microbicidal defense: Phagocytic respiratory burst uses superoxide and hydroxyl radicals produced by NADPH oxidase to kill phagocytosed bacteria.
2. Physiological signaling: Nitric oxide (NO•) serves as an essential vasodilator, neurotransmitter, and regulator of vascular tone.
3. Antitumor surveillance: ROS induce apoptosis and programmed destruction of damaged, infected, or malignant cells, and mediate actions of cancer chemotherapy.
