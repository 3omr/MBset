### Question 82

**Correct Answer**: -

### Question 83

Describe: The types of synovial joints according to axes of movements

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Uniaxial joints (move in one axis/plane):
   - Hinge joint (flexion/extension, e.g., elbow, interphalangeal joints).
   - Pivot joint (rotation, e.g., atlantoaxial, superior radio-ulnar joint).
2. Biaxial joints (move in two axes at right angles):
   - Ellipsoid / Condyloid joint (e.g., wrist joint).
   - Saddle joint (e.g., 1st carpometacarpal joint of thumb).
3. Polyaxial / Multiaxial joints (move in three axes):
   - Ball-and-socket joint (flexion/extension, abduction/adduction, rotation, e.g., shoulder, hip joints).
4. Non-axial (Plane) joints (gliding movement, e.g., intercarpal, acromioclavicular joints).

### Question 84

Explain: The effect of parasympathetic stimulation on constrictor pupillae muscles, spleen, urinary bladder, and stomach

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Constrictor pupillae muscle: Causes pupilloconstriction (miosis) and accommodation for near vision.
2. Spleen: Parasympathetic has NO innervation or effect on the spleen capsule (spleen is exclusively innervated by sympathetic).

### Question 85

Explain: Lecithin has a role in prevention of atherosclerosis

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Lecithin (phosphatidylcholine) provides the fatty acid needed by the enzyme LCAT (Lecithin-Cholesterol Acyltransferase) to esterify free cholesterol in HDL particles (forming lysolecithin and cholesteryl esters). This esterification is crucial for reverse cholesterol transport, removing cholesterol from peripheral tissues and blood vessels and delivering it to the liver for excretion in bile, thereby preventing atheroma and plaque formation in blood vessels.

### Question 86

Explain: Covalent modification can regulate enzyme activity (with example)

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Enzyme activity is altered by reversible covalent attachment or removal of a chemical group (most commonly phosphorylation and dephosphorylation catalyzed by protein kinases and protein phosphatases).
Example: Glycogen phosphorylase is activated by phosphorylation (phosphorylase b converted to active phosphorylase a), whereas Glycogen synthase is inactivated by phosphorylation and activated by dephosphorylation.

### Question 87

Explain: Regulation of lipolysis during fasting

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: During fasting, blood glucose drops, leading to decreased insulin secretion and increased glucagon and epinephrine secretion.
1. Glucagon and epinephrine bind to G-protein coupled receptors on adipocytes, activating adenylate cyclase.
2. Adenylate cyclase increases cAMP levels, which activates Protein Kinase A (PKA).
3. PKA phosphorylates and activates Hormone-Sensitive Lipase (HSL) and perilipin.
4. Active HSL hydrolyzes stored triacylglycerols into free fatty acids (FFAs) and glycerol, which are released into the circulation to supply energy to peripheral tissues and liver.
