### Question 31

Describe the general characteristics of epithelial tissue?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Cells are closely aggregated with very little intercellular substance (scant ECM).
2. Avascular; receives nutrition and oxygen by diffusion from underlying connective tissue capillaries.
3. Rests on an underlying basement membrane that separates it from connective tissue.
4. Cells exhibit polarity (apical, lateral, and basal domains).
5. High regenerative capacity (frequent mitotic division).
6. Rich in sensory nerve endings.

### Question 36

Explain why changes in pH can affect the catalytic activity of the enzyme?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Changes in pH alter the ionization (charge) state of the acidic and basic amino acid side chains at the enzyme's active site and on the substrate. Extreme pH changes can disrupt ionic and hydrogen bonds maintaining the tertiary structure, causing denaturation and loss of catalytic function. Each enzyme operates at an optimum pH.

### Question 37

Biochemical basis of Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS)?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (hyaline membrane disease) is caused by a deficiency of lung surfactant (primarily dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine / lecithin), synthesized by Type II alveolar cells. Deficiency causes high alveolar surface tension, leading to alveolar collapse (atelectasis) during expiration and severe hypoxemia.

### Question 38

Define Zymogens with example?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Zymogens (proenzymes) are inactive precursors of enzymes that require specific biochemical cleavage of one or more peptide bonds to convert into their catalytically active forms. Example: Pepsinogen (cleaved by HCl into active pepsin) or Trypsinogen (cleaved by enterokinase into active trypsin).

### Question 42

### Question 43

Explain the effect of sympathetic stimulation on the liver and bronchi & bronchioles?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Liver: Stimulates glycogenolysis (breakdown of glycogen to glucose) and gluconeogenesis via beta-2 and alpha-1 adrenergic receptors, resulting in increased blood glucose level (hyperglycemia) to supply energy for stress/emergency.
2. Bronchi & bronchioles: Causes bronchodilatation (relaxation of bronchial smooth muscles via beta-2 adrenergic receptors) and reduces bronchial secretions, facilitating airflow during the fight-or-flight response.

### Question 44

Explain the effect of inhibition of Na-K pump?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Inhibition of the Na+-K+ ATPase pump (e.g., by cardiac glycosides like digitalis / ouabain or hypoxia/ATP depletion) causes:
1. Intracellular accumulation of Na+ and loss of intracellular K+.
2. Decreased Na+ concentration gradient across the membrane, impairing secondary active transport mechanisms (e.g., Na+/Ca2+ antiporter, leading to increased intracellular Ca2+).
3. Gradual depolarization of the resting membrane potential (loss of electronegativity), leading to decreased cell excitability.
4. Cellular swelling due to osmotic influx of water following retained Na+.

### Question 45

Compare between passive and active transport according to: (Concentration gradient, Energy, Carriers, Examples)?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Concentration gradient: Passive transport occurs along (down) electrochemical gradient (from high to low); Active transport occurs against (uphill) electrochemical gradient (from low to high).
2. Energy: Passive transport requires no cellular metabolic energy (no ATP); Active transport requires metabolic energy (ATP directly in primary active transport or ion electrochemical gradient in secondary active transport).
3. Carriers: Passive transport may not require carrier (simple diffusion) or requires carrier (facilitated diffusion); Active transport always requires specific carrier protein (pump or transporter).
4. Examples: Passive: Simple diffusion of O2/CO2, Facilitated diffusion of glucose via GLUT-4; Active: Na+-K+ ATPase pump (primary), Na+-glucose cotransport / SGLT-1 (secondary).

### Question 46

Compare between types of autonomic ganglia (Lateral, Collateral, Terminal) according to Site and Relay for?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Lateral (Paravertebral) ganglia:
   - Site: Arranged in two chains on both sides of the vertebral column.
   - Relay for: Sympathetic preganglionic fibers destined for head, neck, thoracic viscera, body wall, and limbs.
2. Collateral (Prevertebral) ganglia:
   - Site: In front of the vertebral column, around origin of main abdominal branches of the aorta (celiac, superior mesenteric, inferior mesenteric ganglia).
   - Relay for: Sympathetic preganglionic fibers (splanchnic nerves) destined for abdominal and pelvic viscera.
3. Terminal (Peripheral) ganglia:
   - Site: Close to or inside the wall of the effector organs (e.g., intramural ganglia of Meissner and Auerbach in GIT).
   - Relay for: Parasympathetic preganglionic fibers (cranial and sacral outflow).

### Question 47

Compare between primary and secondary cartilaginous joints as regarding to: (Type of cartilage, Ossification)?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Type of cartilage:
   - Primary cartilaginous (synchondrosis): Bones are joined by hyaline cartilage (e.g., epiphyseal plate, spheno-occipital joint).
   - Secondary cartilaginous (symphysis): Articular surfaces covered by hyaline cartilage and connected by a plate of fibrocartilage (e.g., symphysis pubis, intervertebral discs).
2. Ossification:
   - Primary cartilaginous: Temporary joints that completely ossify with age (synostosis) after bone growth ceases.
   - Secondary cartilaginous: Permanent joints that do NOT ossify throughout life (except pathological ankylosis).

### Question 48

Explain: The trachea has multiple C cartilaginous rings?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: The trachea is supported by 16-20 C-shaped hyaline cartilaginous rings. ### Question 52

Describe different phases of fertilization?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: Phase 1: Penetration of the corona radiata by capacitated spermatozoa using hyaluronidase.
Phase 2: Penetration of the zona pellucida mediated by acrosomal enzymes (acrosin); triggers cortical/zona reaction to prevent polyspermy.
Phase 3: Fusion of the oocyte and sperm cell membranes; oocyte completes 2nd meiotic division forming female pronucleus; sperm head forms male pronucleus; pronuclei fuse to form diploid zygote.

### Question 53

Classify the bones according to their shapes and give one example for each type?

**Type**: QROC
**Correct Answer**: -
**Explanation**: 1. Long bones: Length exceeds width, consist of shaft and two ends (e.g., Humerus, Femur).
2. Short bones: Cuboidal shape (e.g., Carpal bones, Tarsal bones).
3. Flat bones: Two plates of compact bone enclosing spongy bone / diploë (e.g., Scapula, Sternum, Parietal bone of skull).

5. Pneumatic bones: Contain air-filled cavities/sinuses (e.g., Maxilla, Frontal, Sphenoid bones).
6. Sesamoid bones: Embedded within tendons (e.g., Patella).
