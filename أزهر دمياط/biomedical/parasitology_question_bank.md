## 1. Definitions (التعاريف)

### **A. Reservoir Host**
* **Definition:** An animal that harbors the same species and stage of parasite as humans, serving as a continuous source of infection to humans (e.g., pigs for *Balantidium coli*).

### **B. Infective Stage**
* **Definition:** The specific developmental stage in a parasite's life cycle that is capable of initiating an infection when introduced into a susceptible host.

### **C. Ectoparasites**
* **Definition:** Parasites that live on the external surface/skin of the host, causing an **infestation** (e.g., lice, fleas, ticks).

### **D. Pathogenic Stage**
* **Definition:** The stage of the parasite (usually larval or adult) that causes trauma, tissue destruction, obstruction, or disease manifestations in the host.

### **E. Saprozoonosis**
* **Definition:** A zoonotic disease where the parasite requires a non-animal environmental stage (such as soil or water) outside the host to complete its development before becoming infective.

### **F. Endodyogeny**
* **Definition:** A form of asexual reproduction involving internal budding where two daughter cells are formed within the parent cell (characteristic of *Toxoplasma gondii* tachyzoites).

### **G. Cyclodevelopmental Transmission**
* **Definition:** Biological vector transmission where the parasite undergoes essential developmental changes (metamorphosis/stage change) inside the vector **without** multiplying in number (e.g., *Wuchereria bancrofti* in mosquitoes).

### **H. Incomplete Metamorphosis (Hemimetabolous)**
* **Definition:** A type of insect development consisting of three stages: **Egg $\to$ Nymph $\to$ Adult**, where the nymph morphologically resembles the adult (e.g., lice, bugs).

### **I. Mosquitoes Bionomics**
* **Definition:** The comprehensive study of the biology, ecology, behavior, breeding habits, feeding patterns, and vector roles of mosquitoes.

### **J. Metazoonosis**
* **Definition:** A zoonotic disease whose transmission cycle requires both vertebrate and invertebrate host species (e.g., Arboviruses, Leishmaniasis).

## 4. Detailed Parasite Accounts & Disease Profiles (موضوعات تفصيلية)

### **A. Comprehensive Profile: Balantidiasis (*Balantidium coli*)**

* **Geographical Distribution:** Cosmopolitan (worldwide), particularly common in warm, humid tropical and subtropical regions.
* **Definitive Host:** Humans.
* **Reservoir Host:** **Pigs** (the most critical reservoir), monkeys, and rats.
* **Habitat:** Large intestine (cecum and colon).
* **Pathogenesis:** Mechanical trauma and enzymatic tissue destruction via **lytic enzymes** (hyaluronidase) causing flask-shaped mucosal ulcers.
* **Clinical Picture:**
  * *Asymptomatic:* Common in carrier state.
  * *Acute Balantidiasis:* Severe dysentery with frequent bloody/mucoid stools, abdominal pain, and tenesmus.
  * *Chronic Balantidiasis:* Recurrent diarrhea alternating with constipation, abdominal tenderness, and anemia.
* **Complications:**
  1. Intestinal perforation leading to fatal peritonitis.
  2. Extra-intestinal invasion (rare): liver abscesses, pulmonary involvement.
* **Laboratory Diagnosis:**
  1. **Microscopic Stool Examination:** Detection of motile ciliated **trophozoites** in diarrheic stools or spherical **cysts** in formed stools.
  2. **Sigmoidoscopy:** Direct visualization of intestinal ulcers and biopsy examination.
* **Treatment:** **Tetracycline** (Drug of choice), Metronidazole, or Iodoquinol.
* **Prevention & Control:** Sanitary disposal of human and pig feces, protection of food/water from contamination, personal hygiene, and proper management of pig farms.

---

### **B. Management & Control of *Hymenolepis nana***
* **Drug of Choice:** **Praziquantel**.
* **Critical Measures:** High attention to preventing **autoinfection** (internal & external), strict personal hygiene, handwashing, and mass treatment.

---

### **C. Outcome of Accidental Ingestion of Specific Eggs**
1. **Ingestion of *Fasciola* Eggs:**
   * **Outcome:** **No infection occurs.** *Fasciola* eggs passed in stool are unembryonated and non-infective to humans; infection requires ingestion of encysted metacercariae on watercress.
2. **Ingestion of *Heterophyes heterophyes* Eggs:**
   * **Outcome:** **No infection occurs.** The infective stage for humans is the encysted metacercaria inside fish tissue, not the egg.

---

### **D. Clinical Value of Thiabendazole**
* Broad-spectrum antihelminthic agent.
* **Drug of choice** for *Strongyloides stercoralis* infection.
* Highly effective in treating Cutaneous Larva Migrans (*Ancylostoma braziliense*).

---

### **E. Diagnostic Stages & Transmission of *Fasciola hepatica***
1. **Diagnostic Stage:** Unembryonated operculated egg passed in human stool.
2. **Mode of Transmission:** Ingestion of encysted metacercariae on aquatic plants/vegetables (e.g., watercress).
3. **Intermediate Host:** Freshwater snails of the genus ***Lymnaea***.

## 5. Comparisons (المقارنات)

### **Cyclodevelopmental vs. Propagative Biological Vector Transmission**

| Feature | Cyclodevelopmental Transmission | Propagative Transmission |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Parasite undergoes essential developmental changes inside vector **without multiplication**. | Parasite **multiplies numerically** inside vector **without morphological development**. |
| **Numerical Change** | **No** (number of organisms remains constant or decreases). | **Yes** (number of organisms increases significantly). |
| **Cyclical Change** | **Yes** (changes forms/stages). | **No** (form remains identical). |
| **Examples** | Filarial worms (*Wuchereria bancrofti*) in *Culex*; *Dracunculus* in *Cyclops*. | *Yersinia pestis* (Plague) in fleas; Arboviruses (Yellow fever, Dengue) in mosquitoes. |

## 6. Give Reasons & Scientific Explanations (علل والتفسيرات العلمية)

### **1. Why do allergic manifestations occur in Ascariasis (*Ascaris lumbricoides*)?**
* **Larval Lung Migration (Löffler's Syndrome):** Transiting larvae cause direct alveolar destruction, inducing fever, cough, wheezing, and transient pulmonary infiltrates.
* **Th2 Immune Response Activation:** Strong induction of $Th2$ immune pathways leading to elevated IgE levels, hyper-eosinophilia, and mast cell degranulation.
* **Parasitic Antigens:** Worm excretory-secretory products (e.g., tropomyosin) act as potent allergens, triggering urticaria, asthma, and skin rashes.
* **Cross-Reactivity:** *Ascaris* proteins cross-react with environmental allergens, exacerbating underlying allergic airway conditions.

---

### **2. Why can Salpingitis occur in Enterobiasis (*Enterobius vermicularis*)?**
* **Erratic Migration:** Gravid adult female pinworms exit the anal sphincter at night to lay eggs and occasionally migrate erratically across the perineum into the vulva and vagina.
* **Ascending Invasion:** Female worms ascend through the vagina, uterine cavity, and into the Fallopian tubes.
* **Inflammatory Response:** Presence of worms or trapped eggs induces chronic granulomatous inflammation and **salpingitis**.
* **Secondary Bacterial Transport:** Worms carry enteric bacteria into the upper genital tract, potentially resulting in pelvic inflammatory disease (PID).

## 7. Clinical Case Scenarios (الحالات الكلينيكية)

### **Case 1: Pulmonary & Intestinal Helminthiasis**
* **Presentation:** A 10-year-old child presents with fever, dyspnea, and cough. Sputum examination reveals blood streaks and live larvae. Blood count shows marked eosinophilia. Symptoms resolve in 2 weeks, followed by abdominal colic, nausea, and passage of a $20\text{ cm}$ cylindrical worm in stool.
  * **A. Causative Parasite:** ***Ascaris lumbricoides***.
  * **B. Infective Stage:** **Embryonated egg** (containing $L_2/L_3$ larva).
  * **C. Mode of Infection:** Ingestion of food or water contaminated with embryonated *Ascaris* eggs.
  * **D. Drug of Choice:** **Albendazole** or **Mebendazole**.
  * **E. Two Complications:** Intestinal obstruction (worm bolus), Löffler's syndrome / Biliary ascariasis.

---

### **Case 2: Dysenteric Protozoan Infection**
* **Presentation:** A young worker from Manshiyat Naser (garbage collector) complains of painful, frequent evacuation of small stool quantities mixed with blood and mucus. Microscopy confirms protozoan infection.
  * **A. Causative Protozoan:** ***Entamoeba histolytica***.
  * **B. Pathogenic Stage:** **Trophozoite** (invasive form).
  * **C. Primary Habitat:** **Large intestine** (cecum and colon).

---

### **Case 3: Nocturnal Perianal Pruritus in Pediatrics**
* **Presentation:** A mother brings her 4-year-old daughter due to intense perianal itching for 2 weeks, occurring predominantly at night. Examination shows localized erythema around the anus without systemic symptoms.
  * **A. Probable Parasite:** ***Enterobius vermicularis*** (Pinworm / Threadworm).
  * **B. Confirmatory Diagnostic Test:** **Scotch tape (perianal cellulose tape) swab** performed early in the morning to detect characteristic asymmetric eggs.

---

### **Case 4: Raw Vegetable Contamination**
* **Presentation:** A 6-year-old child presents with abdominal colic, vomiting, and diarrhea after consuming unwashed raw vegetables. Stool microscopy demonstrates brownish, oval eggs ($60 \times 45\ \mu\text{m}$) with a thick, mammillated outer coat.
  * **A. Provisional Diagnosis:** ***Ascaris lumbricoides***.
  * **B. Main Complications:** Löffler's syndrome, intestinal lumen obstruction, malnutrition, and vitamin deficiencies.

## 8. Medical Entomology & Vector Control (الحشرات والتحكم في النواقل)

### **A. Medical Importance of Mosquitoes & Flies**

#### **1. *Culex* Mosquitoes:**
* Vector for **West Nile Virus**.
* Vector for **Lymphatic Filariasis** (*Wuchereria bancrofti*).

#### **2. Sandflies (*Phlebotomus* spp.):**
1. **Sandfly Fever** (Phlebotomus fever / Pappataci fever virus).
2. **Carrion's Disease** / Bartonellosis (*Bartonella bacilliformis*).
3. **Leishmaniasis** (Visceral, Cutaneous, Mucocutaneous).
4. **Toscana Virus**.
5. Irritative allergic dermatitis.

---

### **B. Vector Control Measures**

#### **1. Control Measures Against Aquatic Mosquito Stages:**
* **Chemical Larviciding:**
  * Insect Growth Regulators (IGRs) like **Methoprene**.
  * **Paris Green** (copper acetoarsenite).
  * Surface oils (Petroleum oils / Mineral oils) to deny larval respiration.

#### **2. Control of Sandflies (*Phlebotomus*):**
* **Chemical Control:**
  * Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS) using **DDT** or synthetic **Pyrethroids**.
  * Insecticide-Treated Bed Nets (ITNs) with fine mesh.
* **Environmental Management:**
  * Sanitation, filling breeding cracks/crevices in walls and soil, housing improvements, and clearing surrounding vegetation.
