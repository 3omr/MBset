## 1. Definitions (التعاريف)

### **a. Drug Resistance**
* **Definition:** Unresponsiveness of an organism to a drug, such that the organism doesn't respond and the drug can no longer affect it.

### **b. Defective Virus Particles / Defective Interfering Particles**
* **Definition:** Virus particles containing a defective genome that cannot multiply by themselves; they can only replicate in cells simultaneously infected with a helper virus that supplies the missing function. When they interfere with the entrance/replication of another virus, they are called *defective interfering particles*.

### **c. Opportunistic Pathogen / Opportunistic Fungi**
* **Definition:** Organisms (or members of the normal flora/microbiota) that are harmless under normal conditions but become pathogenic and cause disease when host immunity is compromised or under specific altered conditions.

### **d. Pseudovirions**
* **Definition:** Virus-like particles that contain host cell DNA instead of viral DNA inside the viral capsid. They occur during viral assembly when host DNA fragments accidentally get packaged. They can infect cells but cannot replicate.

### **e. Conjugation**
* **Definition:** Direct gene transfer from one bacterial cell (donor/male) to another (recipient/female) via cell-to-cell contact mediated by a sex pilus forming a cytoplasmic bridge through which DNA passes.

### **f. Dimorphic Fungi**
* **Definition:** Fungi capable of existing in two distinct morphological forms depending on environmental conditions (temperature): yeast form at body temperature ($37^\circ\text{C}$) and filamentous/mold form at room temperature ($22^\circ\text{C}$ to $25^\circ\text{C}$).

### **g. Incubation Period**
* **Definition:** The time interval between the initial infection (entry of the pathogen) and the first appearance of signs or symptoms of the disease.

### **h. Prions**
* **Definition:** Atypical infectious agents composed solely of misfolded proteins without any nucleic acids (DNA/RNA). They cause slow, progressive neurodegenerative diseases.

### **i. Carrier**
* **Definition:** An apparently healthy individual who harbors a pathogenic microorganism in their body and sheds/secretes it to the outside without exhibiting clinical signs or symptoms. Carriers can be transient (incubatory) or chronic/permanent (e.g., Hepatitis B).

### **j. Specialized Transduction**
* **Definition:** Transfer of specific restricted parts of host bacterial chromosome along with temperate bacteriophage DNA to another bacterial cell due to an error during lysogenic excision.

### **k. Generalized Transduction**
* **Definition:** Transfer of any random fragment of bacterial host DNA to another bacterium via a bacteriophage capsid due to an error during lytic packaging.

### **l. L-Forms**
* **Definition:** Bacteria that have lost their cell wall either artificially or induced by environmental conditions/antibiotics (such as penicillin), which can revert back and synthesize their cell wall upon removal of the inducing agent.

### **m. Nosocomial Infection (Hospital-Acquired Infection - HAI)**
* **Definition:** Infections acquired by a patient during hospital stay/admission with no evidence that the infection was present or incubating upon entry into the healthcare facility (usually appearing $\ge 48$ hours after admission).

### **n. Probiotics**
* **Definition:** Live non-pathogenic, non-toxic microorganisms which, when administered in adequate amounts, confer a health benefit on the host by restoring normal flora balance and improving digestion/immunity.

### **o. Lysogenic Phage (Temperate Phage)**
* **Definition:** A bacteriophage that integrates its genome into the host bacterial chromosome (as a prophage) and replicates passively alongside host multiplication without destroying the bacterial cell immediately.

### **p. Mutation**
* **Definition:** A sudden, stable, and inheritable change in the nucleotide sequence of the genetic material (DNA or RNA).

### **q. Parasites**
* **Definition:** Organisms that live on or inside another host organism, deriving nutrients at the host's expense.

### **r. Myiasis**
* **Definition:** Parasitic infestation of live human or animal tissue by dipterous fly larvae (maggots).

## 2. Give Reasons (علل / اذكر السبب)

### **a. Antibiotics affect only bacterial cell wall / ribosomes, not human cells.**
* **Cell wall reason:** Bacterial cells contain a rigid cell wall composed of peptidoglycan, a structure completely absent in human cells.
* **Ribosome reason:** Bacteria possess 70S ribosomes ($50\text{S} + 30\text{S}$ subunits), whereas human cells possess 80S ribosomes ($60\text{S} + 40\text{S}$ subunits); thus, targets are biochemically distinct.

### **b. It is difficult to find an antiviral or antifungal drug that selectively inhibits the pathogen without affecting the host cell.**
* **Antiviral reason:** Viruses are obligate intracellular parasites that rely completely on host cellular machinery, enzymes, and metabolic pathways for replication.
* **Antifungal reason:** Fungi are eukaryotic organisms similar in structure and metabolic pathways to human host cells, presenting few unique target sites.

### **c. Bacterial spores are highly resistant to destruction.**
* **Reasons:**
  1. Thick, tough outer layers (core, cortex, and spore coat).
  2. High concentrations of dipicolinic acid and calcium ions in the core.
  3. Extremely low metabolic activity and low water content.

### **d. Silent mutation (Samesense mutation) has no effect on gene function.**
* **Reason:** Due to degeneracy of the genetic code, the mutated codon still codes for the exact same amino acid; hence, no alteration in protein structure or function occurs.

### **e. L-forms can regain their cell wall, whereas Mycoplasma cannot.**
* **Reason:** Mycoplasma naturally lacks the genetic capability to synthesize a cell wall (genotypic trait), whereas L-forms retain the genetic blueprint and lose cell wall structures only phenotype-wise due to external factors.

### **f. Culture and sensitivity testing is much more vital in hospital-acquired infections (HAI) than community-acquired infections.**
* **Reason:** Hospital pathogens (e.g., MRSA, VRSA, *Pseudomonas*) are frequently multi-drug resistant, highly virulent, and infect vulnerable/immunocompromised patients requiring rapid targeted therapy.

### **g. The mutation rate in viruses (especially RNA viruses) is significantly higher than in bacteria.**
* **Reason:** RNA viruses replicate using viral RNA polymerases or reverse transcriptase, which lack proofreading (3'-to-5' exonuclease) activity. Bacteria have stable DNA genomes and high-fidelity DNA polymerases with proofreading capabilities.

### **h. Prophylactic antibiotics are indicated before oral or intestinal surgeries.**
* **Reason:** To prevent transient bacteremia caused by normal flora (e.g., *Streptococcus viridans*) entering the bloodstream, which could lead to subacute bacterial endocarditis (SABE) or post-operative surgical wound infections.

### **i. Disease carriers are more dangerous to the community than clinically overt patients.**
* **Reason:** Carriers are apparently healthy, show no clinical signs, move freely in society, and are difficult to detect, diagnose, or isolate compared to symptomatic patients.

### **j. Bacteria are ideal host cells for recombinant DNA technology and genetic studies.**
* **Reason:** Rapid rate of multiplication, simple genome, ease of cultivation on inexpensive artificial media, and controllable growth conditions.

### **k. Capsular Neisseria is more virulent than non-capsular Neisseria.**
* **Reason:** The capsule is anti-phagocytic, protective against host defenses, and aids in adherence to host surfaces.

## 3. Mention & Enumerate (اذكر وعدد)

### **a. Mechanisms of Action of Antiviral Drugs**
1. Interference with viral adsorption/attachment to host cell receptors.
2. Inhibition of viral uncoating (e.g., Amantadine).
3. Inhibition of viral nucleic acid synthesis, transcription, or replication (e.g., Acyclovir, Zidovudine).
4. Prevention of viral assembly, maturation, or release/budding (e.g., Neuraminidase inhibitors).
5. Induction of cellular antiviral state (Interferons).

### **b. Mechanisms of Action of Antifungal Drugs**
1. Binding to ergosterol in fungal cell membrane causing pore formation (e.g., Amphotericin B).
2. Inhibition of ergosterol synthesis (e.g., Azoles like Fluconazole).
3. Inhibition of fungal cell wall chitin/glucan synthesis (e.g., Caspofungin).
4. Inhibition of fungal nucleic acid synthesis (e.g., Flucytosine).

### **c. Practical Applications of Bacteriophages**
1. Research tools in molecular genetics and recombinant technology.
2. Cloning vectors in genetic engineering.
3. Phage typing for epidemiological identification of bacterial strains.
4. Phage therapy as an alternative treatment for antibiotic-resistant bacterial infections.

### **d. Functions of Plasmids**
1. **Conjugation:** Carrying fertility (F) factors mediating bacterial mating.
2. **Resistance (R factor):** Conferring resistance to antibiotics, heavy metals, and UV radiation.
3. **Bacteriocin & Toxin Production:** Coding for toxins like enterotoxins, exotoxins, and bacteriocins.
4. **Biochemical activities:** Sugar fermentation (e.g., lactose/galactose fermentation in *E. coli*).
5. Vectors in genetic engineering and recombinant DNA technology.

### **e. Functions of Mesosomes**
1. **Septal Mesosomes:** Attached to bacterial chromosomes and involved in cell division and septum formation.
2. **Lateral Mesosomes:** Increase total membrane surface area for respiratory enzymes, active transport, and protein secretion.

### **f. Applications of Recombinant DNA Technology**
1. Production of recombinant vaccines (e.g., Hepatitis B vaccine).
2. Production of human therapeutic proteins (e.g., insulin, growth hormones, interferons).
3. Production of endorphins (analgesics) and antibiotics.
4. Production of nitrogen-fixation proteins for agricultural enhancement.
5. Synthesis of DNA probes for molecular diagnostic assays.
6. Gene therapy.

### **g. Characteristics of Effective Probiotics**
1. Ability to survive passage through acidic gastric juice and bile.
2. Ability to adhere to and colonize intestinal epithelial cells.
3. Non-pathogenic, non-toxic, and stable during storage.
4. Ability to utilize normal dietary substrates and produce beneficial metabolites.

### **h. Indications for Antibiotic Prophylaxis**
1. Surgical prophylaxis before high-risk procedures (e.g., bowel surgery, cardiac surgery, dental procedures in rheumatic heart disease).
2. Post-exposure prophylaxis for close contacts of infectious cases (e.g., *Neisseria meningitidis* exposure).
3. Preventing recurrent infections in immunocompromised or susceptible individuals.

### **i. Parts / Links in the Chain of Infection**
1. Infectious Agent / Pathogen
2. Reservoir / Source of infection
3. Portal of Exit
4. Mode of Transmission
5. Portal of Entry
6. Susceptible Host

## 4. Classifications (التصنيفات)

### **a. Classification of Viruses**
* **By Nucleic Acid:** DNA viruses vs. RNA viruses (single-stranded vs. double-stranded).
* **By Symmetry:** Icosahedral, Helical, or Complex.
* **By Envelope:** Enveloped vs. Non-enveloped (Naked) viruses.

### **b. Classification of Fungi**
* **By Morphology:**
  1. Yeasts (e.g., *Candida*)
  2. Yeast-like fungi
  3. Filamentous / Molds (e.g., *Aspergillus*)
  4. Dimorphic fungi (e.g., *Histoplasma*)
* **By Clinical Presentation / Mycoses:**
  1. Superficial Mycoses (stratified to stratum corneum, e.g., *Pityriasis versicolor*)
  2. Cutaneous Mycoses (skin, hair, nails, e.g., Dermatophytosis)
  3. Subcutaneous Mycoses (dermis/subcutaneous tissue, e.g., Mycetoma)
  4. Systemic / Deep Mycoses (internal organs, e.g., *Histoplasmosis*)
  5. Opportunistic Mycoses (in immunocompromised hosts, e.g., Candidiasis, Aspergillosis)

### **c. Classification of Bacteria by Oxygen Requirements**
1. **Obligate Aerobes:** Require oxygen for growth.
2. **Obligate Anaerobes:** Oxygen is toxic; grow only in absence of oxygen.
3. **Facultative Anaerobes:** Grow with or without oxygen (prefer oxygen).
4. **Microaerophiles:** Require low concentrations of oxygen ($2\text{--}10\%$).
5. **Aerotolerant Anaerobes:** Do not use oxygen but tolerate its presence.

## 5. Comparisons (المقارنات)

### **a. Gram-Positive vs. Gram-Negative Cell Wall**

| Feature | Gram-Positive Bacteria | Gram-Negative Bacteria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Peptidoglycan Layer** | Thick ($40\text{--}50\%$ of dry weight, multi-layered) | Thin ($5\text{--}10\%$ of dry weight, single-layered) |
| **Teichoic Acids** | Present | Absent |
| **Outer Membrane / LPS** | Absent | Present (contains Lipopolysaccharide / Endotoxin) |
| **Lysozyme/Penicillin Sensitivity** | Highly susceptible (forms **Protoplast**) | Less susceptible (forms **Spheroplast**) |

---

### **b. Exotoxins vs. Endotoxins**

| Property | Exotoxins | Endotoxins |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Source** | Secreted by both Gram-positive & Gram-negative bacteria | Integral component of Gram-negative cell wall (LPS) |
| **Chemical Nature** | Proteins | Lipopolysaccharides (Lipid A) |
| **Heat Stability** | Heat-labile (destroyed at $>60^\circ\text{C}$) | Heat-stable (withstands autoclaving) |
| **Toxicity & Antigenicity** | High toxicity, highly antigenic | Low toxicity, weakly antigenic |
| **Toxoid Conversion** | Convertible to toxoids by formaldehyde | Cannot be converted to toxoids |
| **Release Mechanism** | Secreted actively by living cells | Released upon cell lysis / disintegration |

---

### **c. Flagella vs. Pili (Fimbriae)**

| Feature | Flagella | Pili (Fimbriae) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Structure & Subunits** | Long, helical appendages made of **Flagellin** | Short, straight hair-like projections made of **Pillin** |
| **Origin** | Embedded in cytoplasmic membrane via basal body | Arise from outer membrane / cell surface |
| **Primary Function** | Bacterial motility & locomotion | Adhesion/Colonization (Ordinary Pili) & Conjugation (Sex Pili) |

---

### **d. Chronic vs. Latent Viral Infection**

| Feature | Chronic Viral Infection | Latent Viral Infection |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Viral Release & Detection** | Continuous release and detection of virus over long periods | Intermittent or absent viral release; virus un-detectable during latency |
| **Example** | Hepatitis B Virus (HBV), Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) | Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV), Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV) |

---

### **e. Lytic vs. Lysogenic Bacteriophage Cycle**

| Feature | Lytic Cycle | Lysogenic Cycle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phage Integration** | Phage DNA remains separate from host chromosome | Phage DNA integrates into host chromosome (**Prophage**) |
| **Host Cell Outcome** | Host cell undergoes lysis and destruction | Host cell survives and replicates carrying phage genome |
| **Progeny Production** | Immediate synthesis of phage particles | Delayed; viral genes quiet until induction |

## 6. Clinical Cases & Clinical Questions (الحالات الكلينيكية)

### **Case 1: Tapeworm Infestation**
* **Presentation:** A 31-year-old male fond of eating undercooked beef suffers from abdominal pain, nausea, diarrhea, weight loss, and passage of active white segments without defecation. Stool examination reveals round eggs ($40\ \mu\text{m}$ in diameter) with a radially striated shell.
* **Provisional Diagnosis:** *Taenia saginata* infection (Beef Tapeworm).
* **Infective Stage:** *Cysticercus bovis* (larval stage in beef muscle).

## 8. Professor Comments & Exam Tips (ملاحظات وتوجيهات أستاذ المادة)

> [!NOTE]
> **ملاحظات وتوجيهات الدكتور المكتوبة بخط اليد والمذكورة في أوراق الامتحانات:**
> 1. **الكلمات المفتاحية (Keywords):** في أسئلة التعاريف، يركز المصحح على الألفاظ العلمية المحددة (مثلاً في تعريف الـ Prions يجب ذكر: `Infectious particle`, `protein only`, `causes slow disease`).
> 2. **الإجابات المباشرة والدقيقة:** تجنب كتابة حشو أو كلام خارج عن موضوع السؤال لأن المصحح يقرأ الورقة بالكامل ويعطي انطباعاً سلبياً عن الإجابات غير المرتبطة بالطلب.
> 3. **المقارنات:** يجب دائماً كتابة أوجّه المقارنة (`Points of comparison`) بشكل واضح ومحدد بين الطرفين.
> 4. **أسئلة الـ MCQs:** اقرأ رأس السؤال جيداً قبل النظر إلى الخيارات، وحدد الإجابة الصحيحة منطقياً ثم تأكد من استبعاد باقي الخيارات الخاطئة.
