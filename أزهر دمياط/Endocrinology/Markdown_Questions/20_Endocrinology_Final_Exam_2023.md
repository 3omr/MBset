### Q1: Indications of insulin.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Insulin is indicated for all patients with type 1 diabetes; for diabetic ketoacidosis or hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state; for marked symptomatic hyperglycemia or catabolism; when glycemic targets are not reached with non-insulin therapy; and in pregnancy when needed for safe glycemic control.

### Q3: Compare between 1ry & 2ry hypoadrenalism ( causes, clinical picture, investigations & treatment 2.5 marks ).

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Primary adrenal insufficiency results from adrenal cortical destruction, most often autoimmune, but also from tuberculosis, hemorrhage, infection, metastases, or genetic disease. Cortisol, aldosterone, and adrenal androgens may be deficient. Patients may have fatigue, weight loss, hypotension, salt craving, hyperpigmentation, hyponatremia, and hyperkalemia. Tests show low cortisol, high ACTH, an inadequate cortisol response to cosyntropin, high renin, and low aldosterone. Treat with glucocorticoid replacement plus fludrocortisone, education about stress dosing, and treatment of the cause. Secondary adrenal insufficiency results from pituitary or hypothalamic disease, surgery, irradiation, or prolonged glucocorticoid suppression. ACTH and cortisol are low or inappropriately normal; aldosterone is usually preserved. Hyperpigmentation and hyperkalemia are generally absent, and other pituitary deficits may coexist. Assess ACTH and other pituitary hormones, perform cosyntropin testing as appropriate, and evaluate the pituitary when indicated. Treat with glucocorticoid replacement and address the pituitary cause; fludrocortisone is generally unnecessary.

### Q4: Enumerate risk factors of diabetic nephropathy & how to screen & treat.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Risk factors include long diabetes duration, poor glycemic control, hypertension, albuminuria, smoking, dyslipidemia, obesity, and family or genetic susceptibility. Screen with a spot urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and serum creatinine with estimated GFR: at diagnosis and annually in type 2 diabetes, and beginning five years after diagnosis and annually in type 1 diabetes. Confirm persistent albuminuria with repeat testing. Treatment includes improved glycemic and blood-pressure control, an ACE inhibitor or ARB when hypertension and albuminuria are present, an SGLT2 inhibitor for eligible patients with type 2 diabetes and CKD, lipid management, smoking cessation, and nephrology referral for advanced or progressive disease.

### Q5: Define precocious puberty, subclinical hypothyroidism & metabolic syndrome.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Precocious puberty is the appearance of secondary sexual characteristics before age 8 years in girls or 9 years in boys. Subclinical hypothyroidism is an elevated serum TSH with a normal free T4 concentration. Metabolic syndrome is a cluster of cardiometabolic risk factors, commonly diagnosed when at least three are present: central obesity, elevated triglycerides, low HDL cholesterol, elevated blood pressure, and elevated fasting glucose.

### Q6: Mention one clinical benefit of SGLT inhibitors, carbimazole, GLP-1 analogues & pioglitazone.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** SGLT2 inhibitors reduce heart-failure hospitalization and slow progression of diabetic kidney disease in appropriate patients. Carbimazole controls hyperthyroidism by inhibiting thyroid hormone synthesis. GLP-1 receptor agonists improve glycemic control and commonly promote weight loss, with cardiovascular benefit for selected agents. Pioglitazone improves insulin sensitivity and lowers blood glucose in type 2 diabetes.

### Q8: Enumerate types & clinical manifestations of malignant thyroid.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** The main types are papillary, follicular, medullary, and anaplastic thyroid carcinoma. Presentation may include a painless thyroid nodule or goiter, cervical lymphadenopathy, hoarseness, dysphagia, dyspnea, or local invasion. Papillary carcinoma commonly spreads to cervical nodes; follicular carcinoma more often spreads hematogenously to bone or lung; medullary carcinoma may be associated with MEN2 and can cause diarrhea or flushing; anaplastic carcinoma typically causes a rapidly enlarging neck mass with compressive symptoms and a poor prognosis.
