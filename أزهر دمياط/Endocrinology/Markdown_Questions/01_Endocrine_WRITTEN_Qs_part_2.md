### Q1: Radiological finding in thyrotoxicosis?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 7
**EXP:** Radioiodine uptake is increased in thyrotoxicosis caused by increased hormone synthesis and decreased in thyroiditis. Thyroid scintigraphy distinguishes the pattern: diffuse uptake suggests Graves disease, a solitary hot nodule suggests a toxic adenoma, and multiple areas of uptake suggest toxic multinodular goiter.

### Q2: Pituitary diseases: causes and clinical types of hypopituitarism.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 11
**EXP:** Presentation
- Hormone excess.
- Hormone deficit.
- Mass effect.

Causes of hypopituitarism
- Pituitary causes: infarction, inflammation, autoimmune, iatrogenic, injury, irradiation, idiopathic, infiltration, inherited.
- Extra-pituitary causes: compression of the stalk; hypothalamic disease (weight loss, heavy exercise, chronic illness, steroid use).

Patterns of hypopituitarism
- Panhypopituitarism: Sheehan syndrome (no lactation, 2ry amenorrhea, weakness/fatigue, hair loss).
- Selective deficiency: GH deficiency; hypogonadism.
  - In female: amenorrhea, vaginal dryness, loss of 2ry sex characteristics, loss of libido.
  - In male: testes atrophy, erectile dysfunction, loss of 2ry sex characteristics, gynecomastia, loss of libido.

Diagnosis
- Suspicion: hypothyroid manifestation except no weight gain, alabaster skin, fine wrinkling hair, hypotension.
- Screening: TSH, FSH, LH, ACTH, GH and prolactin.
- Confirmation: insulin stimulation test and synACTH, TRH and GnRH (clomid).
- Identify cause and complications.

Treatment
- Cortisone.
- Thyroxin.
- Gonads hormone (estrogen or testosterone).

### Q3: Manifest tetany.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 10
**EXP:** - Muscle spasm: facial (risus sardonicus), jaw (trismus), larynx (laryngismus stridulus), diaphragm (hiccough), wall of urinary bladder (enuresis), sphincter (retention), back (opisthotonus).
- Carpopedal spasm, twitches, convulsions.
- Nails → loss of luster, brittle.
- Skin → desquamation, atrophy, alopecia.
- Hair → premature loss.
- Lens → cataract.
- Teeth → hypoplastic, transverse furrows, punctate holes.

Treatment
- In acute attacks → calcium gluconate 10 ml slowly over 10 min.
- Treat the cause:
  - Hypocalcemia → oral calcium lactate 2 gm TDS; active vitamin D (1-25 D3); thiazide diuretics to decrease calcium excretion in urine.
  - Hypomagnesemia → oral Mg.
  - In alkalosis → treat the cause.

### Q4: Hypocalcemia: causes, clinical picture and provocative tests.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 9
**EXP:** Causes
1. Decreased calcium intake: starvation, dysphagia.
2. Decreased calcium absorption: hypoparathyroidism, decreased vitamin D, myxedema, panhypopituitarism, gastrectomy, malabsorption.
3. Increased loss in urine: loop diuretics, renal rickets, CRF.
4. Calcium precipitation in tissue: acute pancreatitis, hungry bone, overtreatment with calcitonin, phosphate.

Clinical picture
In latent cases: Ca 7–9 mg%.

Provocative tests
1. Chvostek: tapping of facial nerve → facial muscle contraction.
2. Trousseau: inflation of sphygmomanometer above systolic pressure for 5 min → carpal spasm.
3. Erb's: current <4 mA → muscle contraction.

### Q5: Hypoglycemia: causes, clinical picture and treatment.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 8
**EXP:** Cause
Glucose <50 mg/dL due to overdose of insulin or some oral antidiabetic (OAD) drugs, or a missed meal.

Clinical picture
1. Adrenergic manifestations (sympathetic overactivity): rapid onset of irritability, tremors, shaking, pallor, sweating and palpitation. Signs: dilated pupils, pallor, coldness, moist skin, strong rapid pulse and increased BP.
2. Neuroglycopenic manifestations (decreased brain energy): dizziness, lack of concentration, cognitive impairment, inappropriate behavior; convulsions, coma, brain damage → death.

Treatment
- Mild (conscious) → oral glucose.
- Severe (unconscious) → I.V. glucose, I.M. glucagon.

### Q6: Approaches to diagnosis of solitary thyroid nodule.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 7
**EXP:** 1. Suspicion: neuropsychiatric irritability (insomnia, anxiety and tremors) in a patient with cardiac manifestations (dyspnea and palpitation), especially if metabolic symptoms are present. Note: thyroid function test is mandatory in atrial fibrillation (A.F.).
2. Screening: mainly suppressed TSH.
3. Confirmatory: elevated free T4 and/or T3.
4. Identify cause:
   - Graves: TSH receptor antibodies.
   - Thyrotoxicosis with hyperthyroidism: increased radioactive iodine uptake (RIU) (does not confirm the site of uptake).
   - Thyroiditis: decreased RIU.
   - Isotope scanning: confirms the site of uptake.

### Q7: Prediabetes: definition, diagnostic criteria, importance, prognosis, included groups and management.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 6
**EXP:** Definition
A condition in which blood glucose is higher than normal but not high enough to be classified as DM. This condition is sometimes called impaired glucose tolerance (IGT) or impaired fasting glucose (IFG), depending on the test used to diagnose it.

Criteria for diagnosis
1. Fasting blood glucose: 100–125 mg%.
2. Postprandial blood glucose: 140–199 mg%.
3. Hb-A1c: 5.7–6.4.

Importance (manifestation)
1. Increased risk for macrovascular complications due to insulin resistance (I.R.) → cardiovascular system (CVS) diseases.
2. Predictor for subsequent type 2 DM.
3. Stressful conditions (pregnancy) → blood glucose reaches the diabetic range.

Prognosis
Good prognosis and may return to normal glucose level.
- IGT leads to type 2 DM in about 34% of cases over 5 years.
- IGT and IFG together lead to type 2 DM in 65% of cases.
- Prediabetics are at high risk for cardiovascular complications.

This group includes
1. Positive family history.
2. Obesity.
3. Renal glucosuria.
4. Female with bad obstetric history → macrosomia.

Management
1. Lifestyle modifications: increase exercise, decrease weight.
2. Diet: decrease fat and simple carbohydrate; increase fibers and fruits.
3. Drugs: insulin sensitizer (metformin), statins, ACE/ARB (for HTN).

   - Acute from the start: meningococcal septicemia.

   - Hemorrhage in the gland.

### Q11: Primary hyperaldosteronism: investigations. What kind of patients will you investigate?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 4
**EXP:** Investigations
1. Plasma aldosterone concentration (PAC) ↑ (normal 3–15 ng%).
2. Plasma renin concentration (PRC) ↓.
3. PRA:PAC ratio >20 → suspect the case.
4. Confirmatory tests (aldosterone suppression):
   - Saline infusion → positive if >10 ng/dl.
   - Fludrocortisone suppression test.
   - Captopril suppression test.
5. CT, MRI of adrenal gland.

Patients to investigate
1. Hypertension and hypokalemia.
2. Moderate or severe hypertension: systolic >160 mmHg, diastolic >100 mmHg.
3. Drug-resistant hypertension.
4. Hypertension with adrenal incidentaloma.
5. Family history of Conn's syndrome, or early hypertension/cerebrovascular accident at a young age <40 years.

### Q12: Subacute thyroiditis: clinical picture, investigations and treatment.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 3
**EXP:** Clinical picture
- Thyroid pain (the most common cause).
- Painful swallowing, sore throat.
- Fever, fatigue, malaise.
- Usually self-limited and resolves in 6–8 weeks, with only 1% having permanent hypothyroidism.
- May be transient hypothyroidism for 2–8 weeks.

Investigations
- Diminished radioiodine uptake.
- Elevated ESR.
- High T3/T4.

Treatment
- Mild cases → none or aspirin.
- Severe cases → prednisone.
- Thyrotoxicosis → propranolol.

### Q14: Management of diabetic ketoacidosis.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 2
**EXP:** 1. Hospitalization in an intensive care unit (ICU).
2. Correct hyperglycemia with short-acting insulin: intravenous (I.V.) during ketosis; subcutaneous (S.C.) after ketosis.
3. Correct dehydration with I.V. fluids: first normal saline, then 5% glucose.
4. Correct acidosis with I.V. sodium bicarbonate infusion.
5. Correct hypokalemia → potassium (K).
6. Treat the cause → antibiotics for infection.
7. Monitor the patient: clinically, level of consciousness, dehydration and blood pressure (BP); in the laboratory, glucose, pH, HCO3 and K; urine ketones and glucose.

### Q15: Diabetic ketoacidosis: causes, clinical picture and diagnostic criteria.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Causes
1. Absolute insulin deficiency: missed insulin.
2. Relative insulin deficiency: psychological distress; pregnancy and labour; stroke; acute myocardial infarction (AMI); surgery; trauma; infections (pneumonia, urinary tract infection); drugs (corticosteroids, thiazides).

Clinical picture
1. Hyperglycemia → severe polyuria and polydipsia.
2. Dehydration → dry skin, sunken eyes, dry tongue, weak rapid pulse.
3. Acidosis → gastrointestinal symptoms (acute abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting) and respiratory signs (acetone odour, Kussmaul breathing).
4. Disturbed consciousness: mild disorientation → frank coma.
5. Other findings: clinical picture of the cause (e.g. infection) and complications (e.g. atrial fibrillation).

Diagnostic criteria
1. Plasma glucose (PG) >300 mg/dl with glucosuria.
2. Ketonemia with ketonuria.
3. Metabolic acidosis: HCO3 <15 mEq/L, pH <7.3.
4. Anion gap >12 mEq/L.
