**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 3
**EXP:** The most likely diagnosis is Graves disease causing thyrotoxicosis, given palpitations, weight loss, anxiety, tremor, stare and goitre. Other features include heat intolerance, sweating, increased appetite, frequent stools, proximal myopathy, lid retraction or lag, diffuse vascular goitre and possible thyroid bruit. ### Q4: A 23-year old female patient presented with history of 2 months dyspnea, palpitation, anxiety, insomnia, and weight loss. **Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 3, 4
**EXP:** The most likely diagnosis is Graves disease (autoimmune thyrotoxicosis), supported by weight loss, dyspnoea, palpitations, anxiety, insomnia and a family history. The likely etiology is Graves disease; other causes include toxic multinodular goitre, toxic adenoma, thyroiditis, iodine or amiodarone exposure, and exogenous thyroid hormone. ### Q6: An 18 years old girl Presented with unintentional weight loss, nocturia, Polyuria and high blood glucose. On examination, there was abdominal pain, dehydration, Hypotension and mental confusion. [1st 2015] A- Most likely diagnosis B- Investigations C- treatment plan

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 4
**EXP:** This is diabetic ketoacidosis, likely new-onset type 1 diabetes. Check bedside and laboratory glucose, venous blood gas, serum bicarbonate and anion gap, blood or urine ketones, electrolytes (especially potassium), urea/creatinine, osmolality, ECG, urinalysis, and cultures or other infection studies when indicated. Begin isotonic IV crystalloid, assess and replace potassium before insulin if potassium is low, then give IV regular insulin with frequent glucose, potassium and ketone monitoring. **Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 4
**EXP:** The likely diagnosis is primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison disease), suggested by postural hypotension, vomiting and mucocutaneous hyperpigmentation. Confirm with morning cortisol and ACTH, electrolytes, glucose, plasma renin and aldosterone, and a short Synacthen stimulation test when the patient is stable; ACTH is high and cortisol response is inadequate. ### Q11: A 15 years old boy diabetic on insulin therapy presented coma & profuse Sweating. [2012] a. what is the most likely diagnosis. How can you confirm. b. Give possible explanation for this presentation and other possible clinical features c. Enumerate 5 different causes for hypoglycemia. d. Suggest the acceptable therapeutic strategy.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 4
**EXP:** a. Most likely diagnosis: hypoglycaemic coma (insulin-induced hypoglycaemia). Confirmed by capillary (bedside) and laboratory blood glucose below 70 mg/dl (often below 50 mg/dl) and prompt recovery after glucose (Whipple's triad).
b. Explanation: excess insulin relative to glucose intake lowers blood glucose; neuroglycopenia causes impaired consciousness and coma, and the counter-regulatory sympathetic (adrenaline) discharge causes profuse sweating. Other possible clinical features: tachycardia, palpitation, tremor, pallor, hunger, anxiety, headache, confusion, abnormal behaviour, slurred speech, convulsions, focal neurological signs; the skin is moist and breathing is normal (no dehydration or acidotic breathing, unlike DKA).
c. Causes of hypoglycaemia: insulin overdose or error in dose/timing; missed or delayed meals, vomiting; unplanned or strenuous exercise; alcohol; renal failure (reduced insulin clearance); hypoadrenalism or hypopituitarism; malabsorption (coeliac disease); (in non-diabetics: insulinoma, sulfonylurea intake).
d. Therapeutic strategy: immediately IV dextrose (0.5-1 g/kg; e.g. 2-5 ml/kg of 10% dextrose, or 50 ml of 50% in older children) or glucagon 0.5-1 mg IM/SC if IV access is not available; recheck glucose after 15 minutes and repeat if needed; maintain with dextrose infusion then oral complex carbohydrate once conscious; identify the cause and adjust the insulin dose, meal plan and exercise; education of the patient and family about symptoms, carrying sugar, home glucose monitoring, glucagon kit and diabetic identification card.

**Answer Source:** derived

**EXP:** a. Most probable 3 D.D.: diabetic ketoacidosis; hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state; hypoglycaemic coma (continuing insulin with loss of appetite) - all precipitated by the febrile infection (also consider sepsis/meningitis and stroke).
b.
