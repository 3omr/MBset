### Q1: Incretin based therapy in TTT of DM

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Incretin-based therapy includes GLP-1 receptor agonists (for example, liraglutide, semaglutide, or dulaglutide) and DPP-4 inhibitors (for example, sitagliptin or linagliptin). GLP-1 agonists enhance glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppress glucagon, slow gastric emptying, and reduce appetite and weight. DPP-4 inhibitors prolong endogenous GLP-1 and GIP activity, increasing glucose-dependent insulin secretion and reducing glucagon, with generally weight-neutral effects. Hypoglycemia is uncommon unless these drugs are combined with insulin or a sulfonylurea. GLP-1 agents commonly cause nausea and other gastrointestinal symptoms; pancreatitis and gallbladder disease are uncommon concerns. Select an agent according to glycemic needs, cardiovascular and kidney disease, weight goals, contraindications, and patient preference.

### Q2: Clinical pictutre of Addison disease.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Primary adrenal insufficiency causes chronic fatigue, weakness, weight loss, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, salt craving, and postural dizziness. Examination may show hypotension or volume depletion, diffuse hyperpigmentation of skin and mucosa, and sometimes vitiligo. Acute adrenal crisis can cause severe hypotension or shock, vomiting, abdominal pain, fever, confusion, and hypoglycemia. Typical laboratory findings include hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, low glucose, and sometimes mild hypercalcemia; primary disease has low cortisol with high ACTH and often high plasma renin with low aldosterone.

### Q3: Indications of insulin.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Insulin is essential in type 1 diabetes and is used urgently for diabetic ketoacidosis and hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state. It is indicated in type 2 diabetes with marked or symptomatic hyperglycemia, ketosis or catabolism, inadequate control despite appropriate non-insulin therapy, or when other agents are contraindicated or unsuitable. It is also used for diabetes in pregnancy when medication is required, for gestational diabetes not controlled with nutrition and activity, and during severe acute illness, surgery, or critical care when needed for safe glucose control.

### Q4: Case of hypothyroidism and approach to diagnosis.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Suspect hypothyroidism with fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, dry skin, hoarse voice, bradycardia, delayed relaxation of reflexes, menstrual disturbance, or goiter, and assess relevant history such as thyroid surgery, radioiodine, neck irradiation, autoimmune disease, and lithium or amiodarone use. Screen with serum TSH in a stable patient with an intact pituitary; an elevated TSH prompts free T4 measurement. Confirm primary overt hypothyroidism with high TSH and low free T4; high TSH with normal free T4 indicates subclinical disease. Low free T4 with low or inappropriately normal TSH suggests central hypothyroidism and requires evaluation of other pituitary axes. Identify the cause with thyroid peroxidase antibodies, medication and treatment history, examination, and, when central disease is suspected, pituitary hormone testing and pituitary MRI. Assess for adrenal insufficiency before starting levothyroxine when pituitary disease is possible.

### Q5: Drug causes Hyperprolactinemia?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Drugs that can raise prolactin include dopamine D2 antagonists, especially antipsychotics such as risperidone, paliperidone, amisulpride, and haloperidol; prokinetics such as metoclopramide and domperidone; some antidepressants; opioids; verapamil; methyldopa; estrogens; and, less commonly, H2 blockers such as cimetidine. Review the medication list and timing before diagnosing a prolactinoma.

### Q6: Pituitary causes hypogonadism?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Pituitary causes include prolactinoma or other hyperprolactinemia suppressing hypothalamic GnRH; a nonfunctioning pituitary macroadenoma compressing gonadotrophs or the stalk; hypopituitarism from pituitary tumors, apoplexy, Sheehan syndrome, trauma, surgery, radiotherapy, infiltrative disease, or hypophysitis; and congenital or genetic pituitary hormone deficiencies. The resulting low LH and FSH cause secondary hypogonadism.

### Q7: Clinical picture of GH deficiency

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Image:** Images/21_Q11.png
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** In children, growth hormone deficiency causes reduced height velocity and proportionate short stature, delayed bone age and dentition, a relatively immature facial appearance, and increased central adiposity; intelligence is usually normal. Neonates may have hypoglycemia and boys may have micropenis. In adults, features include reduced lean mass and exercise capacity, increased visceral fat, reduced bone density, adverse lipid changes, fatigue, and impaired quality of life; these findings are nonspecific and require biochemical confirmation.

### Q8: Enumerate functional adrenal neoplasms.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Functional adrenal neoplasms include cortisol-secreting cortical adenomas or carcinomas causing Cushing syndrome; aldosterone-producing adenomas causing primary aldosteronism; androgen- or estrogen-secreting cortical tumors causing virilization or feminization; and catecholamine-secreting adrenal medullary pheochromocytomas. Some cortical tumors can secrete more than one steroid class.

### Q9: Causes of panhypopituitarism.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Causes include pituitary macroadenoma or other sellar tumors; surgery or radiotherapy; pituitary apoplexy; postpartum ischemic necrosis (Sheehan syndrome); traumatic brain injury; infiltrative or inflammatory disease such as sarcoidosis, tuberculosis, hemochromatosis, or hypophysitis; metastasis or infection; and congenital or genetic defects affecting the pituitary or hypothalamus. A lesion affecting the gland or stalk can impair multiple anterior pituitary axes; posterior pituitary involvement may also cause diabetes insipidus.
