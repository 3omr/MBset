# Pathology Department Book Questions 2026 (Written)

### Q1: Homeostasis

**Model Answer:**
The normal ability of cells to maintain stable physiologic functions.

---

### Q2: Cell injury

**Model Answer:**
Variety of changes (structural and functional) of the stressed cell due to external as well as internal environmental changes (altered homeostasis).

---

### Q3: Cellular adaptation

**Model Answer:**
Modification of cell structure and function in response to stress to preserve the vitality of cells. It is reversible.

---

### Q4: Atrophy

**Model Answer:**
Decrease in size of mature organ due to decrease in cell size and/or number.

---

### Q5: Hypertrophy

**Model Answer:**
Increase in size of mature organ due to increase in cell size.

---

### Q6: Hyperplasia

**Model Answer:**
Increase in number of cells leading to increase in the size of affected tissue or organ.

---

### Q7: Metaplasia

**Model Answer:**
Change of one type of tissue to another type of same category "epithelium to epithelium or connective tissue to connective tissue".

---

### Q8: Cloudy Swelling and Hydropic Degeneration

**Model Answer:**
It is a form of reversible cell injury characterized by intracellular water accumulation.

---

### Q9: Necrosis

**Model Answer:**
Death of group of cells within the living body.

---

### Q10: Apoptosis

**Model Answer:**
Programmed single cell death (cell suicide).

---

### Q11: Inflammation

**Model Answer:**
Local response of living vascularized tissue to an irritant aiming to eliminate necrotic tissues and irritant, and initiate healing (repair).

---

### Q12: Tuberculosis (TB)

**Model Answer:**
Chronic infective granuloma caused by TB bacilli.

---

### Q13: Bilharziasis

**Model Answer:**
Chronic infective granuloma.

---

### Q14: Tissue Repair

**Model Answer:**
Replacement of a damaged tissue by a new healthy one.

---

### Q15: Regeneration

**Model Answer:**
Replacement of the damaged tissue by tissue of the same type.

---

### Q16: Intracellular accumulation of fat (Steatosis)

**Model Answer:**
Intracellular accumulation of fat.

---

### Q17: Hyaline change (Hyalinosis)

**Model Answer:**
Intracellular accumulation or extracellular depositions of protein (microscopic change).

---

### Q18: Anthracosis

**Model Answer:**
Black pigmentation of lung due to carbon inhalation (intracellular accumulation of carbon).

---

### Q19: Hyperpigmentation

**Model Answer:**
Intracellular accumulation of melanin.

---

### Q20: Hypopigmentation

**Model Answer:**
Decrease in melanin.

---

### Q21: Hemosiderosis

**Model Answer:**
Excessive deposition of hemosiderin.

---

### Q22: Hemosiderin

**Model Answer:**
Brownish iron-containing pigment.

---

### Q23: Pathological calcification

**Model Answer:**
Deposition of calcium salts in sites other than bone and teeth.

---

### Q24: Amyloidosis

**Model Answer:**
Extracellular deposition of abnormal protein.

---

### Q25: Hemorrhage

**Model Answer:**
Escape of blood outside the blood vessels or heart.

---

### Q26: Hemothorax

**Model Answer:**
Accumulation of blood in pleura.

---

### Q27: Hemopericardium

**Model Answer:**
Accumulation of blood in Pericardium.

---

### Q28: Hemoperitoneum

**Model Answer:**
Accumulation of blood in Peritoneum.

---

### Q29: Hemarthrosis

**Model Answer:**
Accumulation of blood in Joint space.

---

### Q30: Hyperemia

**Model Answer:**
Increased amount of blood in an organ due to active dilatation of its arteries.

---

### Q31: Congestion

**Model Answer:**
Increased amount of blood in an organ due to obstruction of its venous outflow.

---

### Q32: Oedema

**Model Answer:**
Abnormal increase of fluid in the intercellular tissue spaces or body cavities.

---

### Q33: Anasarca

**Model Answer:**
Extensive generalized edema all over the body including body cavities.

---

### Q34: Infarction

**Model Answer:**
Area of ischemic necrosis due to sudden ischemia.

---

### Q35: Gangrene

**Model Answer:**
Massive tissue necrosis followed by putrefaction.

---

### Q36: NET

**Model Answer:**
Neoplasia Tumor.

---

### Q37: Neoplasia

**Model Answer:**
New proliferation (growth) forming an abnormal mass (uncontrolled, with no useful function).

---

### Q38: Cancer

**Model Answer:**
Genetic disease initiated by non-lethal DNA damage.

---

### Q39: Carcinogenesis

**Model Answer:**
A multistep process: Initiation, Promotion, Progression.

---

### Q40: Initiation in Carcinogenesis

**Model Answer:**
Irreversible mutation of target gene.

---

### Q41: Promotion in Carcinogenesis

**Model Answer:**
Single initiated cell promoted into a benign tumor (reversible stimulation).

---

### Q42: Progression in Carcinogenesis

**Model Answer:**
Benign neoplasm transformed into malignant cells, showing invasion and metastasis.

---

### Q43: Hamartoma

**Model Answer:**
Mass of normal tissue of a certain organ arranged haphazardly (tumor-like mass).

---

### Q44: Dysplasia

**Model Answer:**
Non-neoplastic disordered proliferation (growth) of cells.
--

### Q45: Causes of cell injury

**Model Answer:**
Hypoxia (low tissue oxygen)
Infectious agents (Viruses, Bacteria, Fungi, parasites)
Immunologic reactions (autoimmune diseases)
Physical agents (trauma, heat, cold, radiation)
Chemical agents (concentrated acids, alkalies, poisons, drugs)
Nutritional disturbance (excess as in obesity, deficiency as in protein and vitamins deficiency)

---

### Q46: Cell injury effects

**Model Answer:**
Cellular adaptation
Reversible cell injury
Irreversible cell injury
Intracellular and extracellular accumulations

---

### Q47: Types of cellular adaptation

**Model Answer:**
Atrophy
Hypertrophy
Hyperplasia
Metaplasia

---

### Q48: Examples of Atrophy

**Model Answer:**
*Physiological:* Atrophy of thymus after puberty, atrophy of uterus after labor, aging.
*Pathological:* Disuse atrophy (after bone fracture), ischemic atrophy, neurogenic atrophy.

---

### Q49: Examples of Hypertrophy

**Model Answer:**
*Physiological:* Hypertrophy of uterus during pregnancy, hypertrophy of skeletal muscle during exercise.
*Pathological:* Hypertrophy of cardiac muscles in systemic hypertension, compensatory hypertrophy.

---

### Q50: Examples of Hyperplasia

**Model Answer:**
*Physiological:* Hyperplasia of female breast during puberty, pregnancy, lactation.
*Pathological:* Skin around wound, liver cells after partial destruction, bone marrow after hemorrhage, breast/prostate due to hormonal stimulation, endometrium.

---

### Q51: Examples of Metaplasia (always pathological and reversible)

**Model Answer:**
Squamous metaplasia of respiratory epithelium in smokers.
Squamous metaplasia of urinary bladder in bilharziasis.
Intestinal metaplasia of esophagus in reflux esophagitis (Barrett's esophagus).

---

### Q52: Examples of Reversible cell injury

**Model Answer:**
Cloudy swelling
Hydropic degeneration
Fatty Change (Degeneration)

---

### Q53: Examples of Irreversible cell injury

**Model Answer:**
Necrosis
Apoptosis

---

### Q54: Causes of Cloudy Swelling and Hydropic Degeneration

**Model Answer:**
Mild injury, short duration.

---

### Q55: Features of Gross picture of Cloudy Swelling / Hydropic Degeneration

**Model Answer:**
1. Size: enlarged.
2. Shape: preserved.
3. Surface: smooth.
4. Color: pale (due to compression of capillaries by swollen cells).
5. Capsule: stretched.
6. Consistency: soft.
7. Cut surface: cloudy and opaque.

---

### Q56: Types of Necrosis

**Model Answer:**
Coagulative, Caseation, Liquefactive, Fat, and Fibrinoid necrosis.

---

### Q57: Causes of Coagulative necrosis

**Model Answer:**
Acute ischemia of all organs except CNS.

---

### Q58: Most common sites of Coagulative necrosis

**Model Answer:**
Heart, spleen, and kidney.

---

### Q59: Causes of Liquefactive necrosis

**Model Answer:**
Brain infarction (high lipid and large fluid contents of nervous tissue)
Pyogenic abscess (by proteolytic enzymes)

---

### Q60: Examples of Liquefactive necrosis

**Model Answer:**
Brain infarction, pyogenic abscess.

---

### Q61: Causes of Traumatic fat necrosis

**Model Answer:**
Trauma to the adipose tissue of the breast and subcutaneous fat.

---

### Q62: Causes of Enzymatic fat necrosis

**Model Answer:**
Acute pancreatitis.

---

### Q63: Causes of Fibrinoid necrosis

**Model Answer:**
Autoimmune collagen diseases (e.g., Rheumatic fever).

---

### Q64: Causes of Physiologic apoptosis

**Model Answer:**
Embryogenesis, hormone-dependent tissue breakdown (e.g., endometrial breakdown during menstrual cycle).

---

### Q65: Causes of Pathologic apoptosis

**Model Answer:**
Liver cells in viral hepatitis (Councilman bodies)
Malignant tumor cells
Some cases of atrophy
Organ rejection

---

### Q66: Features of Microscopic picture of Necrosis

**Model Answer:**
Cell membrane: disappears.
Cytoplasm: Swollen, deeply eosinophilic (due to loss of normal basophilia of RNA and presence of denatured protein).

---

### Q67: Nuclear changes in Necrosis

**Model Answer:**
Pyknosis (nucleus becomes small and dark)
Karyorrhexis (nuclear fragmentation)
Karyolysis (faint dissolved nucleus)

---

### Q68: Morphological features of Apoptosis

**Model Answer:**
Cell shrinkage.
Condensation and fragmentation of nuclear chromatin.
Formation of membrane blebs followed by separation into apoptotic bodies (dark nuclear fragments surrounded by eosinophilic cytoplasm).
Engulfment of apoptotic bodies by phagocytes.
Lack of inflammation in surrounding tissue.

---
