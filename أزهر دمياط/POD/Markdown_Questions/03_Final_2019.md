## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q61: Enumerate 2 types of receptors and example for each.

**Model Answer:**
1. G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs): e.g., Adrenergic receptors (alpha and beta receptors), Muscarinic receptors.
2. Ligand-gated ion channels (Ionotropic receptors): e.g., Nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, GABA-A receptors.
(Also: Tyrosine kinase receptors: e.g., Insulin receptors; Intracellular receptors: e.g., Steroid receptors).

---

### Q62: Mention 2 side effects of erythromycin.

**Model Answer:**
1. Gastrointestinal disturbance (nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, diarrhea) due to stimulation of motilin receptors.
2. Cholestatic jaundice (hepatotoxicity), especially with erythromycin estolate formulation.

---

### Q63: Mention 2 antifungal drugs and mechanism of each.

**Model Answer:**
1. Amphotericin B (or Nystatin): Binds to fungal membrane ergosterol, creating pores that cause leakage of intracellular ions and fungal cell death.
2. Fluconazole (or Ketoconazole): Inhibits fungal cytochrome P450 enzyme 14-alpha-demethylase, blocking ergosterol synthesis and disrupting fungal cell membrane structure.

---

### Q64: Give reasons: Propranolol is contraindicated in asthmatic patients.

**Model Answer:**
Propranolol is a non-selective beta-blocker that blocks beta-2 adrenergic receptors in bronchial smooth muscle, leading to unopposed bronchoconstriction and potentially severe, life-threatening bronchospasm in patients with bronchial asthma.

---

### Q65: Give reasons: Chloramphenicol is contraindicated in patients with G6PD enzyme deficiency.

**Model Answer:**
Chloramphenicol is an oxidant medication that causes oxidative stress in erythrocytes. In patients with G6PD deficiency, inadequate NADPH and glutathione production impairs protection against oxidative damage, precipitating acute hemolytic anemia.

---

### Q66: Define: metaplasia, thrombosis, ulcer, resolution, melanoma, and leukocytosis.

**Model Answer:**
- Metaplasia: Reversible transformation of one adult differentiated cell/tissue type into another differentiated cell type of the same category (e.g., columnar to squamous epithelium in smokers).
- Thrombosis: Formation of a solid mass (thrombus) composed of blood constituents within the intact cardiovascular system during life.
- Ulcer: A localized excavation or discontinuity of the surface of an organ or tissue produced by sloughing of inflammatory necrotic tissue.
- Resolution: Complete structural and functional restoration of the inflamed tissue to normal following acute inflammation.
- Melanoma: Malignant neoplasm originating from melanocytes, typically occurring in the skin.
- Leukocytosis: An abnormal elevation of total white blood cell count in the peripheral blood (> 11,000/mm³), usually reactive to infection or inflammation.

---

### Q67: Compare between metastatic calcification and dystrophic calcification.

**Model Answer:**
- Dystrophic Calcification: Occurs in dying, necrotic, or damaged tissues (e.g., caseous necrosis of TB, atheromatous plaques, damaged heart valves). Serum calcium and phosphate levels are NORMAL; calcium metabolism is normal.
- Metastatic Calcification: Occurs in previously normal, vital tissues (especially lungs, kidneys, and stomach walls). Serum calcium level is ELEVATED (hypercalcemia) due to primary hyperparathyroidism, bone metastases/myeloma, or vitamin D intoxication.

---

### Q68: Compare between benign and malignant tumors.

**Model Answer:**
- Differentiation & Anaplasia: Benign tumors are well-differentiated (closely resemble normal tissue of origin); malignant tumors show poor differentiation or anaplasia.
- Rate of Growth: Benign tumors grow slowly; malignant tumors grow rapidly.
- Local Invasiveness: Benign tumors are circumscribed, cohesive, and usually encapsulated without invading surrounding tissues; malignant tumors are non-encapsulated, locally invasive, and infiltrate surrounding parenchyma.
- Metastasis: Benign tumors NEVER metastasize; malignant tumors have the capacity to spread to distant organs via lymphatics, blood vessels, or transcoelomic seeding.
