# Final 2022 Exam Questions

## Section 1: Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

### Q1: Post-Mortem Lividity (Hypostasis):
- **A)** Begins immediately after death as soon as the circulation stops
- **B)** Begins 2 hours after the circulation stops
- **C)** Begins 4 hours after the circulation stops
- **D)** Begins 1 hour after the circulation stops

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Physiologically, hypostatic settling of blood in dependent capillaries starts immediately upon cessation of cardiac pumping and arrest of circulation, becoming clinically visible within 1 to 2 hours.

---

### Q2: Which of the following are true regarding valid consent in alleged rape?
- **A)** Age must be above 18 years old
- **B)** The female must be mentally sane
- **C)** She must be conscious and not under threats or fear
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Legal consent is valid only when given freely by a conscious, sane adult (age >= 18) without physical coercion, threats, or fraud.

---

### Q3: The most characteristic diagnostic feature of a stab wound is that:
- **A)** Edges are regular
- **B)** Tissue bridges are present
- **C)** It has two acute angles
- **D)** Its depth into tissues is greater than its surface length

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** A stab wound is defined by depth of penetration exceeding surface cutaneous length, distinguishing it from an incised cut wound.

---

### Q4: The following are characters of a firearm wound inlet (entrance) EXCEPT:
- **A)** Loss of skin substance
- **B)** Regular, rounded or oval shape
- **C)** Presence of abrasion collar and soiling ring
- **D)** External beveling of bone

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** An entrance wound produces internal beveling (coning inward) of the cranial inner table. External beveling occurs at the exit wound.

---

### Q5: The number of deciduous (milk) teeth in humans is:
- **A)** 16 in number
- **B)** 18 in number
- **C)** 20 in number
- **D)** 32 in number

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** There are exactly 20 milk (deciduous) teeth (4 central incisors, 4 lateral incisors, 4 canines, and 8 deciduous molars).

### Q6: The legal age of getting a motor vehicle driving license (18 years) is identified radiologically by X-ray on:
- **A)** The hip joint
- **B)** The knee joint
- **C)** The wrist joint
- **D)** The ankle joint

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** At 18 years, radiological union of the lower epiphyses of the tibia and fibula with their shafts occurs at the ankle joint.

---

### Q7: All of the following are recognized fundamental patterns of fingerprints EXCEPT:
- **A)** Arches
- **B)** Loops
- **C)** Whorls
- **D)** Rectangular

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The Galton-Henry system recognizes Arches, Loops, Whorls, and Composites; rectangular is not a pattern.

### Q8: A septate hymen is characterized by:
- **A)** A number of small openings
- **B)** A number of fimbriae
- **C)** A partition either longitudinal or transverse dividing the orifice
- **D)** A centrally placed circular opening

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** A septate hymen has a central tissue band or septum that divides the vaginal entrance into two separate apertures.

---

### Q9: Characteristics of acute pontine hemorrhage include all of the following EXCEPT:
- **A)** Deep coma
- **B)** Fixed dilated pupil
- **C)** Severe hyperthermia
- **D)** Bilateral quadriplegia

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Pontine hemorrhage produces pinpoint pupils (due to sympathetic tract interruption), deep coma, quadriplegia, and hyperpyrexia.

---

### Q10: In close and near-range gunshot wounds, the firing distance can be accurately estimated by:
- **A)** Site of the inlet
- **B)** Gunpowder marks (smudging, tattooing, and burning)
- **C)** Site of the exit
- **D)** Direction of firing

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Gunpowder residues indicate firing range: flame burning (<10-15 cm), soot smudging (<30 cm), and unburnt powder tattooing (<100 cm).

## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q11: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of lucid interval:

**Model Answer:**
1. Civil Capacity: Validating wills, contracts, deeds, and marriage during a lucid interval in individuals with episodic mental illness.
2. Criminal Responsibility: Determining if a crime was committed during a lucid period (culpable) vs active psychosis/insanity (not guilty by reason of insanity).
3. Traumatic Brain Injury (Extradural Hemorrhage): A classic diagnostic hallmark of extradural (epidural) hematoma, where a patient regains consciousness before secondary deterioration due to expanding hematoma, signaling emergency neurosurgical intervention.
4. Witness Competence: Assessing legal validity of testimony given during a lucid period.

---

### Q12: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of DNA finger prints:

**Model Answer:**
1. Criminal Investigation: Positive identification and individualization of suspects linking biological evidence (blood, semen, saliva, hair roots, touch DNA) to crime scenes.
2. Disputed Parentage: Definitive resolution of disputed paternity, maternity, and baby-switching disputes with >99.9% statistical certainty.
3. Mass Disaster Victim Identification (DVI): Identifying decomposed, charred, skeletonized, or mutilated human remains.
4. Sexual Assault Cases: Isolating and profiling offender DNA from mixed vaginal/rectal swabs using differential extraction and STR/Y-STR typing.

---

### Q13: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of contusions:

**Model Answer:**
1. Proof of Ante-Mortem Trauma: Differentiating vital bruising (extravasation and tissue infiltration) from postmortem hypostasis.
2. Age of Injury Estimation: Chronological color changes (red-dark blue -> greenish-blue -> brownish-yellow -> normal) due to hemoglobin breakdown.
3. Patterned Contusions: Reconstructing the causative weapon or instrument (intradermal bruise pattern matching weapon shapes: whip, rod, cane, tire treads, ligature mark, fingertip bruises in manual strangulation).
4. Manner of Death: Multiple bruises on the neck in throttling, or inner thighs/arms in sexual assault and defensive struggles.

---

### Q14: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of brain stem death:

**Model Answer:**
1. Legal Definition of Death: Establishes unequivocal somatic death despite mechanical ventilation and maintained somatic circulation.
2. Organ Transplantation: Legal and ethical authorization for harvesting viable vital organs (heart, liver, kidneys, lungs) before organ autolysis.
3. Discontinuation of Artificial Life Support: Provides legal immunity to physicians to withdraw futile mechanical cardiopulmonary support.
4. Civil and Criminal Timelines: Determining precise legal time of death for inheritance, insurance claims, and homicidal culpability.

---

### Q15: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of virginity:

**Model Answer:**
1. Investigation of Alleged Rape / Sexual Assault: Demonstrating recent traumatic rupture, bruising, or laceration of the hymen.
2. Civil and Matrimonial Disputes: Accusations of non-virginity on the wedding night (elastic dilatable hymen), impotence of the husband as grounds for divorce, and defamation cases.
3. Differentiating Recent from Old Ruptures: Establishing whether defloration was fresh (<7-10 days with active inflammation, tenderness, and clots) or chronic.
4. Evaluating Congenital Anomalies: Diagnosing imperforate, cribriform, or microperforate hymen requiring medical hymenotomy.

---

### Q16: Define an abrasion.

**Model Answer:**
A superficial cutaneous injury characterized by the destruction, denudation, or removal of the epidermal layers of the skin (or mucous membranes) produced by friction or compression from a blunt object, without penetration through the full thickness of the dermis.

---

### Q17: Define cerebral compression.

**Model Answer:**
A serious, progressive pathological syndrome caused by a space-occupying lesion inside the rigid cranial cavity (such as an expanding extradural, subdural, or intracerebral hematoma, depressed skull fracture, or cerebral edema), leading to critical elevation of intracranial pressure, brain distortion, brain herniation, and compression of vital brainstem centers.

---

### Q18: Define shaken baby syndrome (abusive head trauma).

**Model Answer:**
A severe form of non-accidental abusive head trauma occurring in infants and young children caused by violent, repetitive rotational and angular shaking acceleration-deceleration forces, typically characterized by the classic diagnostic triad: subdural hematoma, diffuse encephalopathy/cerebral edema, and bilateral extensive retinal hemorrhages.

---

### Q19: Define molecular (cellular) life and death.

**Model Answer:**
Molecular (cellular) life refers to the continued metabolic, biochemical, and enzymatic viability of individual cells and tissues (muscles, cornea, sperm, ciliated epithelium) for several hours after somatic death. Molecular death occurs when individual cells undergo irreversible autolysis and loss of vital excitability.

---

### Q20: Define rape.

**Model Answer:**
The unlawful, intentional carnal knowledge (penile penetration, however slight, of the female vagina) of a female committed without her valid, free, and informed consent, obtained through physical force, threats of grievous bodily harm, duress, or while she is mentally incompetent, unconscious, intoxicated, or below the statutory age of consent (under 18 years).

---

### Q21: True or False: The female sternum is shorter and wider than the male sternum to provide more abdominal space for the gravid uterus during pregnancy.

**Model Answer:**
True. In females, the body of the sternum is less than twice the length of the manubrium (manubrium/body ratio), making the sternum shorter and broader to accommodate thoracic changes and the gravid uterus.

---

### Q22: True or False: Classical ABO blood grouping is a positive test in disputed paternity, meaning it can definitively prove paternity.

**Model Answer:**
False. Classical blood grouping is an exclusionary (negative) test: it can definitively exclude an falsely accused man from paternity, but can never conclusively prove paternity (only DNA profiling can prove paternity).

---

### Q23: True or False: Subdural hemorrhage occurs typically due to traumatic laceration of the middle meningeal artery.

**Model Answer:**
False. Laceration of the middle meningeal artery produces EXTRADURAL hemorrhage; subdural hemorrhage is typically caused by tearing of parasagittal bridging cortical veins crossing the subdural space.

---

### Q24: True or False: Subconjunctival hemorrhage remains bright red throughout its course until it is completely absorbed.

**Model Answer:**
True. Because atmospheric oxygen diffuses directly through the thin, transparent conjunctiva, extravasated hemoglobin remains oxygenated, so subconjunctival hemorrhage does not undergo the typical color changes of cutaneous contusions and remains bright red until absorbed.

---

### Q25: True or False: The main system responsible for sudden natural death in adults is the respiratory system, most commonly from acute pneumonia.

**Model Answer:**
False. The cardiovascular system accounts for the overwhelming majority (approx. 50-70%) of all sudden natural deaths, predominantly due to ischemic coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, and acute dysrhythmias.

### Q26: Case Question 1: What are the possible causes of death in this female victim?

**Clinical Case:** A 30-year-old female was found dead in her flat, in a prone position. Medico-legal examination revealed 17 wounds distributed over the front and back of her trunk.

**Model Answer:**
1. Severe hemorrhagic shock (exsanguination) from major thoracic/abdominal vascular or visceral transection.
2. Acute bilateral hemothorax / pneumothorax and pulmonary collapse.
3. Cardiac tamponade from penetrating wounds into the pericardium and heart.
4. Secondary air embolism or asphyxia due to pulmonary aspiration of blood.

---

### Q27: Case Question 2: What was the intention of the assailant, and why?

**Clinical Case:** A 30-year-old female was found dead in her flat, in a prone position. Medico-legal examination revealed 17 wounds distributed over the front and back of her trunk.

**Model Answer:**
The intention was willful, premeditated homicide (murder), evidenced by the multiple stab wounds (17 wounds) distributed across critical target regions (chest and back), inflicted while the victim was incapacitated or fleeing.

### Q28: Case Question 3: Can the medico-legal expert differentiate between ante-mortem and post-mortem wounds? Explain.

**Clinical Case:** A 30-year-old female was found dead in her flat, in a prone position. Medico-legal examination revealed 17 wounds distributed over the front and back of her trunk.

**Model Answer:**
Yes. Ante-mortem wounds exhibit active arterial bleeding, firmly adherent fibrin blood clots, retracted gaped edges due to vital skin/muscle tone, and vital leukocytic infiltration and enzymatic changes at wound margins.

---

### Q29: Case Question 4: Can the medico-legal expert estimate the time since death (date of crime)? Explain.

**Clinical Case:** A 30-year-old female was found dead in her flat, in a prone position. Medico-legal examination revealed 17 wounds distributed over the front and back of her trunk.

**Model Answer:**
Yes, by evaluating the chronological progression of postmortem changes:
1. Algor mortis (rate of core body cooling).
2. Rigor mortis (progression and extent of muscle stiffening).
3. Postmortem hypostasis (distribution, color, and fixation).
4. Decomposition changes (marbling, green discoloration, putrefaction).
5. Gastric content digestion and eye changes.

---

### Q30: Case Question 5: How can you differentiate between a cut wound and a stab wound?

**Clinical Case:** A 30-year-old female was found dead in her flat, in a prone position. Medico-legal examination revealed 17 wounds distributed over the front and back of her trunk.

**Model Answer:**
| Feature | Cut (Incised) Wound | Stab (Penetrating) Wound |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dimensional Rule** | Surface length exceeds tissue depth | Tissue depth exceeds surface length |
| **Mechanism** | Gliding/drawing of a sharp cutting edge | Inward thrusting of a pointed instrument |
| **Margins** | Clean, linear, non-abraded | Clean, linear or slit-like |
| **Internal Injury** | Superficial (skin, fat, subcutaneous vessels) | Deep visceral and cavitary penetration |
