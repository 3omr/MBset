# Final 2026 Exam Questions

## Section 1: Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

### Q1: Which site of hemorrhage is most dangerous for a small amount of blood?
- **A)** Pleural cavity
- **B)** Pericardial sac
- **C)** Muscle tissue
- **D)** Cranial cavity

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Because the skull is a rigid, unyielding bony box, even a small hemorrhage (e.g., 30-50 mL) inside the cranial cavity causes dramatic elevation of intracranial pressure, brain herniation, and rapid death.

### Q2: Post-Mortem pictures of death from burns due to dry heat include the following EXCEPT:
- **A)** The body shows ante-mortem burns of various degrees with boxer's attitude
- **B)** Generalized redness of the body
- **C)** No CO hemoglobin in the blood
- **D)** Particles of soot in the air passages
- **E)** Hemoconcentration

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** In vital burns from dry heat/fires, inhalation of smoke produces high levels of carboxyhemoglobin (CO-Hb) in the blood (typically >20-60%). Absence of CO-Hb indicates postmortem burning.

### Q3: An age of 18 years is estimated by doing an X-ray on:
- **A)** Mandible joint
- **B)** Knee joint
- **C)** Ankle joint
- **D)** Sternum

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Fusion of the distal epiphyses of the tibia and fibula at the ankle joint occurs at approximately 18 years of age.

### Q4: Certain conditions must be ascertained for identification by flash of light on firearm wounds EXCEPT:
- **A)** The victim must have a perfect vision
- **B)** The victim must be facing the assailant
- **C)** The assailant must be previously known by the victim
- **D)** The distance of firing must not exceed 15 meters

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Identification by firearm flash is only possible if the firing distance is very close (not exceeding 2 to 3 meters, never 15 meters).

## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q5: Mention the sure signs of drowning.

**Model Answer:**
1. Fine, white, persistent froth around the mouth and nostrils (pumice lung) and in the respiratory air passages.
2. Cadaveric spasm in drowning victims grasping weeds, mud, sand, or gravel.
3. Water, sand, and fine mud present deep in the stomach, duodenum, and terminal bronchioles.
4. Diatoms / planktons demonstrated in closed organs (bone marrow, liver, brain, kidneys).

---

### Q6: Explain the medico-legal importance (M.L.I) of powder marks in firearm wounds.

**Model Answer:**
1. Distinguishing inlet from exit wounds: Powder marks (tattooing and smudging) are present around the entrance wound only.
2. Estimating distance of fire: Smudging indicates close range (<30 cm), tattooing indicates near range (<100 cm).
3. Determining angle of fire: Circular pattern indicates perpendicular shot; oval/eccentric pattern indicates oblique fire.
4. Type of gunpowder: Black powder causes dense burning/tattooing; smokeless powder causes minimal/microscopic tattooing.

---

### Q7: Enumerate the immediate signs of death (somatic death).

**Model Answer:**
1. Complete and irreversible cessation of respiration (apnea).
2. Complete and irreversible cessation of blood circulation and heart beats.
3. Complete loss of consciousness and voluntary movements.
4. Generalized primary flaccidity of all muscles and loss of all vital reflexes (pupillary, corneal, deep reflexes).
5. Characteristic changes in the eyes: loss of corneal reflex, haziness/drying of cornea, segmentation of retinal blood columns (railroad/cattle-truck appearance), and dilated fixed pupils.

---

### Q8: Discuss the medico-legal importance (M.L.I) of cadaveric spasm.

**Model Answer:**
1. Proof of Ante-Mortem Nature: Indicates that the object was grasped firmly at the exact moment of death.
2. Proof of Manner of Death (Suicide vs Homicide): Grasping of weapon (gun, knife, poison cup) proves suicide, while grasping assailant's hair, cloth, or button indicates homicidal struggle.
3. Proof of Drowning: Grasping weeds, mud, or water plants proves that death was due to ante-mortem drowning.

---

### Q9: Differentiate between contused wounds and cut wounds.

**Model Answer:**
| Feature | Contused Wound | Cut (Incised) Wound |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Causative Instrument** | Heavy blunt instrument | Sharp-edged cutting instrument |
| **Edges** | Irregular, jagged, swollen, abraded, and contused | Regular, clean-cut, sharp, no abrasions/contusions |
| **Tissue Bridges** | Present (intact vessels, nerves, fibers across floor) | Absent (all structures cleanly divided) |
| **Angles** | Blunt or irregular | Acute and sharp |
| **Hair** | Crushed or intact with roots | Cleanly cut across shafts |
| **Hemorrhage** | Usually mild to moderate (vessels crushed/retracted) | Profuse and continuous (cleanly cut open vessels) |
| **Foreign Material** | Commonly present (dirt, grit, clothing fibers) | Usually clean |
| **Healing** | Delayed, by secondary intention, leaving ragged scar | Rapid, by primary intention, leaving fine linear scar |

### Q10: Define suspended animation (apparent death).

**Model Answer:**
A clinical state in which vital functions of the body (respiration, circulation, brain functions) are depressed to the minimum compatible with life, undetectable by routine clinical examination, simulating death. Seen in drowning, hypothermia, electric shock, newborn asphyxia, and severe sedative intoxication.

---

### Q11: Define strangulation.

**Model Answer:**
A form of violent mechanical asphyxia caused by constriction of the neck by a ligature (ligature strangulation) or hands/fingers (manual strangulation / throttling) without suspension of the body.

---

### Q12: Define heat stiffness (heat rigor).

**Model Answer:**
A physical condition simulating rigor mortis caused by exposure of the body to extreme temperatures (>65°C), leading to thermal coagulation and denaturation of muscle proteins and permanent muscle contraction, classically producing the 'boxer attitude' (pugilistic attitude).

---

### Q13: Define Battered Baby Syndrome (child abuse / shaken baby syndrome).

**Model Answer:**
A non-accidental physical trauma inflicted on infants or young children by parents or caregivers, characterized by repeated unexplained skeletal fractures at different stages of healing, cutaneous bruises of varying ages, and severe intracranial trauma (acute subdural hematoma, retinal hemorrhages) without corresponding external impact.

---

### Q14: Case Question 1: How can you verify the age of both the assailant (21 years) and the victim (40 years) using X-ray examination (union of epiphysis)?

**Clinical Case:** A 21-year-old man hit a 40-year-old man with a stone on his head. Some hairs were found on the stone. The victim fainted for a few minutes then he was transmitted to the hospital in a drowsy state. X-ray of the head revealed a linear fracture and extradural hemorrhage. Few hours later, the patient became comatose again.

**Model Answer:**
1. For the Assailant (21 years old): X-ray of the medial end of the clavicle, iliac crest, and ischial tuberosity (union occurs between 20-21 years). Complete union of all long bone epiphyses with complete fusion confirms age >= 21 years.
2. For the Victim (40 years old): Epiphyseal union is complete; age is verified by fusion of skull sutures (coronal suture begins at 30-40, sagittal suture closes at 40-45), osteoarthritic changes, and teeth attrition / root dentin translucency (Gustafson method).

### Q15: Case Question 2: Can you determine the site of skull impact?

**Clinical Case:** A 21-year-old man hit a 40-year-old man with a stone on his head. Some hairs were found on the stone. The victim fainted for a few minutes then he was transmitted to the hospital in a drowsy state. X-ray of the head revealed a linear fracture and extradural hemorrhage. Few hours later, the patient became comatose again.

**Model Answer:**
Yes. The site of impact corresponds directly to the location of the localized scalp contused laceration, subgaleal hematoma, and the starting point / hub of the linear fissure fracture, overlying the torn middle meningeal artery branches in the temporo-parietal region.

### Q16: Case Question 3: How could you explain loss of consciousness twice by the same trauma?

**Clinical Case:** A 21-year-old man hit a 40-year-old man with a stone on his head. Some hairs were found on the stone. The victim fainted for a few minutes then he was transmitted to the hospital in a drowsy state. X-ray of the head revealed a linear fracture and extradural hemorrhage. Few hours later, the patient became comatose again.

**Model Answer:**
1. First coma (immediate, transient): Due to cerebral concussion from widespread functional neuronal disturbance and mechanical shear stress at the moment of impact.
2. Lucid interval: Temporary recovery of consciousness as concussion resolves while arterial bleeding begins.
3. Second coma (delayed, progressive): Due to cerebral compression resulting from expanding extradural hematoma peeling the dura and causing increased intracranial pressure.

---

### Q17: Case Question 4: What are the eye manifestations during the 2nd coma?

**Clinical Case:** A 21-year-old man hit a 40-year-old man with a stone on his head. Some hairs were found on the stone. The victim fainted for a few minutes then he was transmitted to the hospital in a drowsy state. X-ray of the head revealed a linear fracture and extradural hemorrhage. Few hours later, the patient became comatose again.

**Model Answer:**
1. Ipsilateral pupil: First constricted due to early oculomotor nerve irritation, then becomes dilated and fixed due to oculomotor nerve paralysis from uncal herniation.
2. Contralateral pupil: Remains normal initially, then dilates and fixes as compression becomes bilateral.
3. Fundoscopy: Papilledema, retinal venous engorgement, and retinal hemorrhages due to acute intracranial hypertension.

### Q18: Case Question 5: Discuss the medico-legal importance (M.L.I) of the period between the two periods of coma (Lucid Interval).

**Clinical Case:** A 21-year-old man hit a 40-year-old man with a stone on his head. Some hairs were found on the stone. The victim fainted for a few minutes then he was transmitted to the hospital in a drowsy state. X-ray of the head revealed a linear fracture and extradural hemorrhage. Few hours later, the patient became comatose again.

**Model Answer:**
1. Diagnostic importance: Strongest diagnostic indicator of extradural hematoma requiring urgent surgical burr hole evacuation.
2. Civil and testamentary capacity: During the lucid interval, the victim is rational and conscious, legally capable of making a valid will, giving a dying declaration, or conducting financial transactions.
3. Criminal responsibility: Proves the trauma was the proximate cause of delayed death, establishing the causal link in homicide.

### Q19: Case Question 6: What is the medico-legal importance (M.L.I) of the hair examination found on the stone?

**Clinical Case:** A 21-year-old man hit a 40-year-old man with a stone on his head. Some hairs were found on the stone. The victim fainted for a few minutes then he was transmitted to the hospital in a drowsy state. X-ray of the head revealed a linear fracture and extradural hemorrhage. Few hours later, the patient became comatose again.

**Model Answer:**
1. Confirms the stone is the causative weapon.
2. Verifies whether the hair is human or animal (medullary index <0.3 in humans).
3. Determines somatic origin (scalp hair).
4. Confirms if hair was pulled out or crushed by blunt trauma.
5. Comparison with victim's hair (color, length, thickness, DNA from follicle roots) definitively links the weapon to the victim.
