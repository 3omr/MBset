# Final 2023 Exam Questions

## Section 1: Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

### Q1: One of the following is true regarding ante-mortem abrasion:
- **A)** Bright red in color
- **B)** Exudation of serum is prominent
- **C)** Intra-vital reaction is seen
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Ante-mortem abrasions display vital signs including bright red color due to vascular congestion, active exudation of serum and blood forming a crust/scab, and vital inflammatory reaction microscopically.

---

### Q2: In a completely charred body at autopsy, a medical officer can determine that burns were ante-mortem if they find:
- **A)** Soot in respiratory passages
- **B)** Cyanosis of finger nails
- **C)** Congestion of the kidney
- **D)** Pale internal organs

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Inhalation of soot particles deep into the trachea, bronchi, and alveoli along with elevated carboxyhemoglobin (CO-Hb >10%) proves vital respiration during the fire.

---

### Q3: All of the following are complications of head injury EXCEPT:
- **A)** Retrograde amnesia
- **B)** Post-traumatic automatism
- **C)** Post-traumatic hypothyroidism
- **D)** Post-traumatic diabetes insipidus

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Traumatic head injury sequelae include retrograde/anterograde amnesia, post-concussional syndrome/automatism, and hypothalamic-pituitary diabetes insipidus. Hypothyroidism is not a recognized trauma complication.

---

### Q4: Maceration occurs only due to:
- **A)** Intra-uterine foetal death
- **B)** Extra-uterine foetal death
- **C)** Death due to firearm injuries
- **D)** Death due to violent asphyxia (drowning)

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Maceration is an aseptic autolysis of the dead fetus occurring strictly in utero within intact, sterile amniotic fluid.

---

### Q5: The number of deciduous (milk) teeth in humans is:
- **A)** 16 in number
- **B)** 18 in number
- **C)** 20 in number
- **D)** 32 in number

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** There are 20 deciduous teeth (8 incisors, 4 canines, 8 molars).

---

### Q6: A septate hymen has:
- **A)** A number of small openings
- **B)** A number of fimbriae
- **C)** A central tissue partition dividing the opening longitudinally or transversely
- **D)** A centrally placed opening

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** A septate hymen is characterized by a central vertical or horizontal fibrous band dividing the vaginal orifice into two separate apertures.

---

### Q7: Characters of pontine hemorrhage include the following EXCEPT:
- **A)** Coma
- **B)** Fixed dilated pupil
- **C)** Hyperthermia
- **D)** Quadriplegia

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Pontine hemorrhage causes pinpoint pupils due to disruption of sympathetic pathways, hyperthermia, bilateral flaccid paralysis (quadriplegia), and deep coma.

---

### Q8: Brain anemia (cerebral ischemia) is the main cause of death in:
- **A)** Drowning
- **B)** Hanging
- **C)** Strangulation
- **D)** Smothering

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** In hanging, suspension of the body produces immediate compression of the carotid arteries and jugular veins, causing acute cerebral anemia and immediate loss of consciousness.

## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q9: True or False: The type of instrument used in a crime can be determined from the vital reaction of the wound.

**Model Answer:**
False. Vital reaction determines whether the wound was inflicted ante-mortem or post-mortem; the type of instrument is determined from the physical morphology, edges, angles, and dimensions of the wound.

---

### Q10: True or False: An abrasion collar may be present around the exit of firearm wounds in shored exit wounds.

**Model Answer:**
True. In shored exit wounds, firm support against the exit skin (e.g. tight belt, wall, or floor) produces an abrasion margin around the exit defect simulating an entrance collar.

---

### Q11: True or False: Curling's ulcer occurs due to rupture of heart muscle after burns.

**Model Answer:**
False. Curling's stress ulcer is an acute gastro-duodenal mucosal erosion/ulceration resulting from severe hypovolemia, mucosal ischemia, and systemic shock following severe cutaneous burns.

---

### Q12: True or False: In electrocution, it is necessary to continue artificial respiration until rigor mortis sets in.

**Model Answer:**
True. In electrocution, prolonged paralysis of the respiratory center can simulate apparent death (suspended animation); cardiopulmonary resuscitation must be maintained persistently until clear irreversible signs of death (rigor mortis) appear.

---

### Q13: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of finger prints:

**Model Answer:**
1. Absolute Personal Identification: No two individuals have identical ridge patterns (chance < 1 in 64 billion), including monozygotic twins.
2. Permanent and Invariable: Formed at the 4th fetal month and persist unchanged until postmortem putrefaction.
3. Scene of Crime Evidence: Latent prints link criminals to weapons, vehicles, and burglary scenes.
4. Civil Registration: Passports, national identity cards, contracts, biometric verification.
5. Mass Disasters: Rapid identification of decomposed, mutilated, or burned victims.

---

### Q14: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of abrasions:

**Model Answer:**
1. Evidence of Direct Trauma: Proves physical impact, friction, or resistance.
2. Patterned Abrasions: Reconstruct the causative object (e.g. radiator grill, tire tread, fingernail crescent scratches in manual throttling, ligature furrow pattern in hanging).
3. Direction of Impact: Peeling epidermal tags point towards the direction of the dragging force.
4. Age of Injury: Color changes of scab (bright red -> reddish-brown -> dark brown -> detachment in 7-10 days).
5. Differentiating Ante-Mortem from Post-Mortem: Presence of bright red vital crust, vital exudate, and microscopic polymorphonuclear infiltration.

---

### Q15: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of brain stem death:

**Model Answer:**
1. Legal Certification of Death: Defines somatic death in intensive care settings, authorizing discontinuation of mechanical ventilation and artificial life-support systems.
2. Organ Transplantation: Allows retrieval of viable, warm-perfused donor organs (kidneys, liver, heart, lungs) before cellular/molecular death ensues.
3. Ethical and Civil Implications: Prevents futile medical over-treatment and allows legal distribution of estates and inheritance.

---

### Q16: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of the hymen:

**Model Answer:**
1. Proof of Virginity: An intact hymen provides unequivocal medical proof of virginity in living females.
2. Sexual Assault & Rape Cases: Detecting fresh tears, lacerations, ecchymosis, or bleeding confirms recent penile or foreign-body penetration.
3. Age of Defloration Tears: Distinguishes recent tears (red, bleeding, sharp edges, tender) from old tears (pale, cicatricial, rounded, healed margins after 7-10 days).
4. Civil Disputes: Resolves allegations of impotence, false claims of defloration, divorce annulments, and slander.

---

### Q17: Define defense wounds.

**Model Answer:**
Wounds (abrasions, contusions, incised wounds) inflicted on the victim's body (most commonly on the palmar aspect of the fingers/hands, ulnar borders of forearms, or shins) when the conscious victim attempts to fend off an assault, grasp a sharp weapon, or shield vital head and neck structures.

---

### Q18: Define cerebral concussion.

**Model Answer:**
An immediate, widespread, and transient impairment of neural function caused by mechanical head trauma, manifested clinically by immediate loss of consciousness followed by spontaneous, rapid, and complete recovery without macroscopic structural brain lesions.

---

### Q19: Define violent mechanical asphyxia.

**Model Answer:**
A pathological state of acute oxygen deprivation in tissues combined with carbon dioxide retention caused by mechanical interference with the respiratory process (e.g. hanging, strangulation, suffocation, drowning, traumatic crush asphyxia).

---

### Q20: Define death from a forensic medicine perspective.

**Model Answer:**
The complete and irreversible cessation of the integrated functioning of the organism as a whole, characterized by permanent arrest of circulation, respiration, and whole brain activity (somatic death), followed by progressive cellular necrobiosis (molecular death).

---

### Q21: Case Question 1: What are the possible causes of death after 2 days with explanation?

**Clinical Case:** A 25-year-old male patient was stabbed by a sharp instrument (dagger) into the chest wall and died after 2 days.

**Model Answer:**
1. Secondary Hemorrhage and Hypovolemic Shock: Delayed slippage of a vascular ligature or erosion of intercostal/pulmonary vessels causing massive secondary hemothorax.
2. Acute Sepsis and Septic Shock: Intrathoracic bacterial contamination introduced by the unsterile blade leading to acute purulent empyema, purulent pericarditis, or mediastinitis.
3. Acute Respiratory Failure / ARDS: From traumatic lung laceration, progressive hemopneumothorax, or shock lung.
4. Thromboembolism: Massive pulmonary embolism arising from pelvic or deep vein thrombosis secondary to post-traumatic bed rest.

---

### Q22: Case Question 2: What are the characters of this stab wound?

**Clinical Case:** A 25-year-old male patient was stabbed by a sharp instrument (dagger) into the chest wall and died after 2 days.

**Model Answer:**
1. Depth: Internal depth significantly exceeds surface cutaneous length.
2. Edges: Regular, sharp, clean-cut margins without bruising or abrasion collar.
3. Angles: If caused by a double-edged dagger, both angles are acute; if single-edged, one angle is sharp and the opposite is blunt.
4. Tissue Bridging: Complete absence of bridging vessels, nerves, or connective tissue across the wound gap.

---

### Q23: Case Question 3: How can you differentiate between a cut (incised) wound and a stab wound?

**Clinical Case:** A 25-year-old male patient was stabbed by a sharp instrument (dagger) into the chest wall and died after 2 days.

**Model Answer:**
| Feature | Cut (Incised) Wound | Stab (Punctured) Wound |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Length vs Depth** | Surface length is greater than internal depth | Internal depth is greater than surface length |
| **Mechanism** | Sliding/drawing of sharp cutting blade | Thrusting/penetrating motion of pointed tip |
| **Internal Tract** | Superficial, confined to skin and subcutaneous tissues | Deep internal tract penetrating body cavities/organs |
| **Primary Threat** | Acute external blood loss | Fatal internal hemorrhage, pneumothorax, visceral perforation |

---

### Q24: Case Question 4: Can the medico-legal expert differentiate between ante-mortem and post-mortem wounds? Explain the criteria.

**Clinical Case:** A 25-year-old male patient was stabbed by a sharp instrument (dagger) into the chest wall and died after 2 days.

**Model Answer:**
Yes. Ante-mortem status is confirmed by:
1. Profuse active bleeding with spurting and marked internal hemothorax/hemopericardium.
2. Firmly adherent, dark-red laminated blood clots that resist washing with water.
3. Gaping of margins due to intact living tissue elastic fiber recoil.
4. Vital reaction: Microscopically, active cellular inflammation (polymorphonuclear leukocytes), capillary congestion, and fibrin deposition within the wound edges.

---
