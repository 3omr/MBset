# Final 2021 Exam Questions

## Section 1: Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

### Q1: Which of the following is TRUE regarding an ante-mortem abrasion?
- **A)** Bright red in color
- **B)** Exudation of serum is abundant
- **C)** Intra-vital inflammatory reaction is seen histologically
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Ante-mortem abrasions demonstrate bright red vital coloration, crust/scab formation from serum exudation, and histological leukocytic infiltration at the margins.

---

### Q2: In a completely charred body at autopsy, the medical officer can confirm the burns were ante-mortem if there is:
- **A)** Soot particles in the respiratory passages
- **B)** Cyanosis of the finger nails
- **C)** Congestion of the kidneys
- **D)** Pale internal organs

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The presence of fine soot particles deep inside the larynx, trachea, and bronchi proves that the victim was breathing during the fire.

---

### Q3: Maceration of a fetus occurs exclusively due to:
- **A)** Intra-uterine foetal death
- **B)** Extra-uterine foetal death
- **C)** Death due to firearm injuries
- **D)** Death due to violent asphyxia (Drowning)

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Maceration is an aseptic autolysis occurring only when a dead fetus is retained inside the intact, closed amniotic fluid environment in utero for >= 24 hours.

---

### Q4: The following are characters of a firearm wound inlet (entrance) EXCEPT:
- **A)** More loss of skin substance
- **B)** More regular rounded contour
- **C)** Presence of abrasion collar and soiling ring
- **D)** External beveling of skull bone

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** An entrance wound produces internal beveling (coning of the inner table) in the skull vault. External beveling is a characteristic of exit wounds.

---

### Q5: All of the following are recognized complications of head trauma EXCEPT:
- **A)** Retrograde amnesia
- **B)** Post-traumatic automatism
- **C)** Post-traumatic hypothyroidism
- **D)** Post-traumatic diabetes insipidus

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Head trauma can injure the pituitary stalk producing diabetes insipidus, memory impairment, or automatism, but post-traumatic hypothyroidism is not a recognized direct consequence.

---

### Q6: All of the following are conditions required for valid legal consent in alleged rape EXCEPT:
- **A)** Age of the female above 18 years old
- **B)** She must be mentally sane
- **C)** She must be awake and fully conscious
- **D)** Consent obtained under threats or fear

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Consent obtained under duress, fear, threats of violence, or intimidation is legally invalid and legally constitutes rape.

---

### Q7: The only sure physical sign of virginity is an:
- **A)** Intact hymen
- **B)** Intact posterior commissure and fourchette
- **C)** Narrow vagina with prominent rugae
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** An intact, unruptured hymenal membrane is the sole definitive physical confirmation of virginity.

---

### Q8: Which of the following is NOT a recognized anatomical type of stab wound?
- **A)** Patterned wound
- **B)** Transfixing wound
- **C)** Puncture wound
- **D)** Penetrating wound

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Patterned wounds are typical of contusions, abrasions, and electric contact marks. Stab wounds are classified into puncture, penetrating, perforating, and transfixing wounds.

## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q9: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of blood stains:

**Model Answer:**
1. Confirming presence of blood: Chemical preliminary tests (Kastle-Meyer, benzidine, luminol) and confirmatory microscopic/microchemical crystal tests (Teichmann's hemin, Takayama's hemochromogen).
2. Species determination: Precipitin test and antihuman globulin inhibition determine whether blood is human or animal.
3. Individualization: DNA profiling and STR typing match blood to a specific victim or suspect.
4. Blood Spatter Analysis: Geometry and directionality of blood spatters reconstruct weapon trajectory, victim position, and sequence of events at the crime scene.
5. Source of Blood: Distinguishing menstrual, systemic, or nasal blood.

---

### Q10: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of pregnancy:

**Model Answer:**
1. Execution of Capital Punishment: Delaying the death penalty for pregnant convicted women until delivery and weaning.
2. Civil and Inheritance Rights: Verification of legitimacy, paternity, posthumous inheritance, and alimony claims.
3. Criminal Offenses: Investigating criminal abortion, infanticide, concealed birth, and statutory rape.
4. Aggravating Circumstance: Assault resulting in termination of pregnancy increases criminal penalties.

---

### Q11: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of abrasions:

**Model Answer:**
1. Proof of Ante-Mortem Trauma: Bleeding, exudation, and scab formation.
2. Patterned Imprints: Reconstructs the striking weapon or medium (fingernail scratches in manual strangulation, ligature marks, vehicle bumper imprints, weapon muzzles).
3. Direction of Force: Epidermal tags indicate the direction of movement.
4. Age of Injury: Chronological scab changes and healing.
5. Manner of Death: Multiple abrasions on inner thighs/genitalia indicate sexual assault.

---

### Q12: Define fingerprints (dactyloscopy).

**Model Answer:**
The unique, permanent morphological impression formed by the papillary friction ridges and furrows located on the volar surfaces of the human fingertips, exhibiting high individuality, immutability throughout life, and permanence from intra-uterine life until post-mortem decomposition.

---

### Q13: Define cerebral concussion.

**Model Answer:**
A widespread, immediate, transient, and reversible impairment of neurological function and consciousness resulting from mechanical trauma to the head, typically devoid of visible macroscopic or permanent anatomical structural brain lesions, followed by amnesia and complete spontaneous recovery.

---

### Q14: Define secondary rifling marks on a fired bullet.

**Model Answer:**
The fine, microscopic striations (scratches) engraved on the cylindrical surface of a bullet produced by microscopic imperfections, burrs, tool marks, and erosions along the bore of a specific weapon's barrel, providing unique individual ballistics identification.

---

### Q15: Define molecular (cellular) life and death.

**Model Answer:**
Molecular (cellular) life refers to the continued survival and metabolic, biochemical, and electrical excitability of individual tissues and cells (muscles, cilia, spermatozoa) for hours following somatic death. Molecular death occurs when all cells lose their vitality and undergo irreversible autolytic decomposition.

---

### Q16: Define drowning (wet drowning).

**Model Answer:**
A form of violent mechanical asphyxia resulting from submersion or immersion of the respiratory orifices (mouth and nose) in a liquid medium (usually water), leading to inhalation of fluid into the pulmonary air passages, mechanical airway obstruction, alveolar-capillary block, and systemic hypoxia.

---

### Q17: Define legal (therapeutic) abortion.

**Model Answer:**
The lawful termination of pregnancy before fetal viability performed by a licensed medical practitioner in an authorized hospital setting to preserve the pregnant woman's life or physical/mental health, after written consultation with another specialist and informed written consent from the woman and her husband.

---

### Q18: True or False: 'Manner of death' refers to the physiological derangement produced by the cause of death that precipitates fatal cessation of life.

**Model Answer:**
False. The physiological derangement (e.g., cardiac tamponade, ventricular fibrillation, septic shock) is the MECHANISM of death. The MANNER of death is the legal circumstance explaining how the death came about (natural, accidental, suicidal, homicidal).

---

### Q19: True or False: Brain stem death is of paramount importance legally, ethically, socially, and in vital organ transplantation.

**Model Answer:**
True. Brain stem death provides the legal definition of death, allowing discontinuation of artificial life support and ethical organ procurement while tissues remain viable.

---

### Q20: True or False: The main cause of sudden natural death in adults is diseases of the respiratory system such as lobar pneumonia.

**Model Answer:**
False. Cardiovascular diseases (ischemic coronary disease, acute myocardial infarction) account for 50-70% of all sudden natural deaths.

### Q21: Case Question 1: What is the probable diagnosis?

**Clinical Case:** A 20-year-old male was brought to the emergency department with multiple fractures of the pelvis and femoral bones from a motorcycle accident. He was suffering from severe dyspnea, cyanosis, petechial rash, and dark urine. Chest X-ray showed diffuse bilateral lung congestion and edema ('snowstorm' appearance).

**Model Answer:**
Fat Embolism Syndrome (FES) complicating multiple pelvic and long bone fractures.

---

### Q22: Case Question 2: What are the possible causes and mechanisms of death in this patient?

**Clinical Case:** A 20-year-old male was brought to the emergency department with multiple fractures of the pelvis and femoral bones from a motorcycle accident. He was suffering from severe dyspnea, cyanosis, petechial rash, and dark urine. Chest X-ray showed diffuse bilateral lung congestion and edema ('snowstorm' appearance).

**Model Answer:**
1. Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) and fulminant pulmonary edema due to microvascular fat emboli obstructing pulmonary capillaries and free fatty acid endothelial toxicity.
2. Acute Cerebral Fat Embolism causing diffuse micro-infarctions, cerebral edema, coma, and herniation.
3. Acute right ventricular failure (acute cor pulmonale).
4. Acute renal tubular necrosis from myoglobinuria and lipid glomerular plugging.

---

### Q23: Case Question 3: What are the clinical and laboratory investigations needed to prove this diagnosis?

**Clinical Case:** A 20-year-old male was brought to the emergency department with multiple fractures of the pelvis and femoral bones from a motorcycle accident. He was suffering from severe dyspnea, cyanosis, petechial rash, and dark urine. Chest X-ray showed diffuse bilateral lung congestion and edema ('snowstorm' appearance).

**Model Answer:**
1. Urine Analysis: Examination for free fat globules in urine (floating fat globules stained with Sudan III or Osmic acid).
2. Blood Examination: Hypoxemia on arterial blood gas (PaO2 < 60 mmHg), sudden drop in hemoglobin, and thrombocytopenia.
3. Chest Imaging: High-resolution CT or Chest X-ray demonstrating bilateral patchy alveolar infiltrates ('snowstorm appearance').
4. Fundoscopy: Detection of fat emboli or petechial retinal hemorrhages (Gurd's criteria).
5. Histopathology (post-mortem): Frozen sections of lung, kidney, and brain stained with Oil Red O, Sudan III, or Osmic acid demonstrating intravascular lipid droplets.
