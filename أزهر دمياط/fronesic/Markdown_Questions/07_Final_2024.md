# Final 2024 Exam Questions

## Section 1: Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

### Q1: The most common type of fingerprint pattern in humans is:
- **A)** Arches
- **B)** Ulnar loops
- **C)** Radial loops
- **D)** Whorls

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Loops constitute roughly 65-68% of all fingerprint patterns, with ulnar loops (opening towards the ulna/little finger) representing the vast majority (approx. 60%).

### Q2: In voluntary skeletal muscles, rigor mortis appears first in:
- **A)** Small muscles of the hand
- **B)** Sternocleidomastoid muscle
- **C)** Orbicularis oculi muscle
- **D)** Rectus abdominis muscle

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** According to Nysten's law, rigor mortis manifests first in the orbicularis oculi (eyelids) within 1-2 hours after death before progressing downward to neck, chest, upper limbs, and lower limbs.

---

### Q3: An early physical change of the body indicating its position after death is:
- **A)** Secondary flaccidity
- **B)** Rigor mortis
- **C)** Putrefaction
- **D)** Hypostasis (Postmortem lividity)

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Hypostasis (postmortem lividity) settles in the dependent parts of the body under the influence of gravity, indicating whether the body was lying supine, prone, or suspended, and reveals if the body was moved after death.

---

### Q4: Defense wounds are characterized by all of the following EXCEPT / denote:
- **A)** Present on the limbs of the victim (palms, forearms)
- **B)** Denote a mobile victim
- **C)** Denote a conscious victim attempting self-defense
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Defense wounds result from spontaneous instinctual defensive efforts against a sharp or blunt weapon, conclusively indicating that the victim was conscious, mobile, and actively resisting assault.

---

### Q5: The medico-legal importance (M.L.I) of the age of 21 years old includes:
- **A)** Age of full civil (legal) rights
- **B)** Complete criminal responsibility without juvenile leniency
- **C)** Eligibility for certain state employment and appointments
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Age 21 represents the age of full legal majority in Egyptian civil and penal jurisdictions, removing all parental or guardian guardianship.

---

### Q6: Which of the following statements is TRUE regarding venous air embolism?
- **A)** It may occur on top of artificial pneumothorax or open surgical neck wounds
- **B)** Few cubic centimeters (1-2 mL) are always fatal
- **C)** Air is typically trapped in the left ventricle causing systemic ischemia
- **D)** Cerebral vessels are always beaded in postmortem examination

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Venous air embolism commonly occurs during surgical or traumatic transection of non-collapsible neck veins (subclavian, jugular), criminal abortion, or therapeutic pneumothorax procedures, typically requiring 70-100 mL of air to produce a fatal right ventricular air lock.

---

### Q7: The clinical picture of acute pontine hemorrhage includes the following EXCEPT:
- **A)** Deep coma
- **B)** Fixed dilated pupil
- **C)** Severe hyperthermia
- **D)** Bilateral quadriplegia

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Pontine hemorrhage characteristically produces pinpoint reactive or fixed pupils (due to sympathetic tract damage), along with rapid onset of deep coma, bilateral flaccid/spastic quadriplegia, and extreme central hyperthermia.

---

### Q8: A ring fracture around the foramen magnum of the skull is a specialized form of:
- **A)** Fissure fracture
- **B)** Depressed fracture
- **C)** Comminuted fracture
- **D)** Cut fracture

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A ring fracture of the skull base is an indirect circular fissure fracture encircling the foramen magnum, produced by force transmitted along the vertebral column (fall on feet/buttocks).

## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q9: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of DNA finger prints:

**Model Answer:**
1. Criminal Investigation: Conclusively links biological trace evidence (blood, semen, saliva, hair, bone) to a specific perpetrator or excludes innocent suspects.
2. Disputed Parentage: Resolves paternity and maternity disputes with over 99.9% statistical certainty.
3. Mass Disaster Victim Identification: Identifies severely mutilated, decomposed, or skeletonized remains (using STR and mtDNA analysis).
4. Exoneration: Provides definitive scientific evidence to exonerate wrongfully convicted individuals.

---

### Q10: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of contusions:

**Model Answer:**
1. Proof of Ante-Mortem Nature: Demonstrates vital extravasation of blood into subcutaneous tissues.
2. Patterned Bruising: Reflects the shape of the striking weapon (whip marks, tire tread marks, ligature marks, finger marks in manual strangulation, bite marks).
3. Aging of Injuries: Color transitions (red -> dark purple -> green -> yellow) estimate the time elapsed since injury.
4. Manner of Death: Multiple bruises of varying ages indicate child abuse (battered child) or domestic violence; neck bruises indicate strangulation.
5. Degree of Force: Extent reflects intensity of violence, vascularity, and tissue laxity.

---

### Q11: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of hypostasis:

**Model Answer:**
1. Sure Sign of Death: Confirms somatic death and cessation of circulation.
2. Estimation of Time Since Death: Appears in 1-2 hours, fully developed in 4-6 hours, and fixes after 8-12 hours.
3. Position of Body at Death: Settles in dependent parts; reveals if the body was moved or repositioned after death.
4. Cause of Death: Characteristic coloration indicates specific toxic/pathological states (cherry-red in CO, chocolate-brown in methemoglobinemia, bright pink in cyanide, dark purple in violent asphyxia).

---

### Q12: Enumerate 4 of types and causes of asphyxia in burns:

**Model Answer:**
1. Carbon Monoxide Poisoning: High concentration of CO in smoke binds avidly to hemoglobin forming carboxyhemoglobin (CO-Hb), blocking oxygen transport (anemic/histotoxic anoxia).
2. Carbon Dioxide Asphyxia: Depletion of ambient oxygen and toxic accumulation of CO2 in enclosed burning compartments.
3. Smoke / Particulate Inhalation: Dense soot particles in tracheobronchial tree trigger acute bronchospasm, mucosal destruction, and acute pulmonary edema.
4. Acute Laryngeal / Glottic Edema: Inhalation of superheated hot air and steam causes acute inflammatory swelling of the vocal cords and glottis, obstructing upper airways.

---

### Q13: Enumerate 4 characters/shapes of the inlet in close range wounds:

**Model Answer:**
In close-range firearm discharge (within contact to a few centimeters):
1. Contact Wound over Flat Bone (e.g. Skull): Stellate (cruciform or star-shaped) due to expansion of subcutaneous gases rebounding off bone, with muzzle imprint (circular impression of the weapon's barrel) stamped onto the skin.
2. Close-Range over Soft Tissue: Circular or rounded with inverted edges, central defect with loss of skin substance.
3. Collars and Zones: Concentric abrasion collar, grease collar (soiling ring), surrounding circular zone of thermal burning/singeing of hair, dense black smoke smudging, and extensive powder tattooing.

---

### Q14: Define strangulation.

**Model Answer:**
A form of violent mechanical asphyxia caused by constriction of the neck by an encircling ligature (ligature strangulation) or hands and fingers (manual strangulation/throttling) without suspension of the body.

---

### Q15: Define a stab wound.

**Model Answer:**
A penetrating or perforating trauma produced by the thrusting or forceful plunge of a pointed, sharp-edged, or tapering instrument, wherein the depth of penetration within the body tissues exceeds the surface length of the cutaneous opening.

---

### Q16: Define beveling (coning) in cranial gunshot wounds.

**Model Answer:**
The oblique, cone-shaped shelving or excavation of skull bone produced as a firearm projectile passes through the cranial vault, wherein the diameter of the defect progressively widens in the direction of the bullet's travel (internal beveling at entrance, external beveling at exit).

---

### Q17: Define violent mechanical asphyxia.

**Model Answer:**
A pathological state of acute oxygen deprivation in tissues combined with carbon dioxide retention caused by external mechanical interference with the normal process of respiration, pulmonary ventilation, or blood flow to the brain (e.g., hanging, strangulation, suffocation, drowning).

---

### Q18: Define sudden death.

**Model Answer:**
Death that is unexpected, non-traumatic, non-violent, and occurring instantaneously or within 24 hours of the appearance of acute clinical symptoms in an apparently healthy person or someone with latent unrecognized disease.

---

### Q19: Case Question 1: Can you determine the site of skull impact?

**Clinical Case:** A 23-year-old man hits a 40-year-old man with a stone on his head. The victim fainted for a few minutes, then was transmitted to the hospital in a drowsy state. CT of the head revealed a linear skull fracture and an extradural hemorrhage. Then the patient developed coma again after a few hours.

**Model Answer:**
Yes. The point of impact is marked by localized scalp contusion/laceration and the epicenter of the linear fissure fracture in the temporo-parietal region overlying the grooved bone of the middle meningeal artery branches.

### Q20: Case Question 2: Explain the loss of consciousness twice by the same trauma.

**Clinical Case:** A 23-year-old man hits a 40-year-old man with a stone on his head. The victim fainted for a few minutes, then was transmitted to the hospital in a drowsy state. CT of the head revealed a linear skull fracture and an extradural hemorrhage. Then the patient developed coma again after a few hours.

**Model Answer:**
1. First coma (immediate, temporary): Resulted from acute cerebral concussion (transient diffuse neuronal paralysis and brainstem shear stress at impact).
2. Lucid interval: Temporary recovery of consciousness as concussion resolves while arterial extradural bleeding slowly accumulates.
3. Second coma (delayed, progressive): Resulted from acute cerebral compression caused by the expanding biconvex extradural hematoma peeling the dura off the skull and raising intracranial pressure.

---

### Q21: Case Question 3: What is the clinical picture of the patient in the 2nd coma?

**Clinical Case:** A 23-year-old man hits a 40-year-old man with a stone on his head. The victim fainted for a few minutes, then was transmitted to the hospital in a drowsy state. CT of the head revealed a linear skull fracture and an extradural hemorrhage. Then the patient developed coma again after a few hours.

**Model Answer:**
1. Neurological: Deepening coma, decerebrate/decorticate posturing, contralateral hemiplegia/hemiparesis, positive Babinski sign.
2. Pupillary signs (Hutchinson pupil): Ipsilateral pupil first constricts (irritation), then becomes widely dilated and fixed (uncal herniation compressing 3rd cranial nerve); eventually bilateral dilated fixed pupils.
3. Vital Signs (Cushing's triad): Hypertension, bradycardia, and irregular/Cheyne-Stokes respirations.
4. Fundoscopy: Bilateral papilledema and engorged tortuous retinal veins.

---

### Q22: Case Question 4: What are the primary vascular causes of extradural hemorrhage (EDH)?

**Clinical Case:** A 23-year-old man hits a 40-year-old man with a stone on his head. The victim fainted for a few minutes, then was transmitted to the hospital in a drowsy state. CT of the head revealed a linear skull fracture and an extradural hemorrhage. Then the patient developed coma again after a few hours.

**Model Answer:**
1. Arterial (most common, 85%): Laceration or rupture of the anterior or posterior branches of the middle meningeal artery (MMA) running in grooves on the inner table of the temporal/parietal bone beneath the pterion.
2. Venous (15%): Tearing of the middle meningeal companion veins, dural venous sinuses (superior sagittal sinus, transverse sinus), or diploic veins crossing the fracture line.

---

### Q23: Case Question 5: What are the major complications of head injuries?

**Clinical Case:** A 23-year-old man hits a 40-year-old man with a stone on his head. The victim fainted for a few minutes, then was transmitted to the hospital in a drowsy state. CT of the head revealed a linear skull fracture and an extradural hemorrhage. Then the patient developed coma again after a few hours.

**Model Answer:**
1. Intracranial Hemorrhages: Extradural, subdural, subarachnoid, and intracerebral hemorrhages.
2. Cerebral Herniations: Uncal (transtentorial), subfalcine, and tonsillar (foramen magnum / coning) herniations.
3. Infections: Post-traumatic meningitis, subdural empyema, and brain abscess (especially with open compound or base-of-skull fractures causing CSF rhinorrhea/otorrhea).
4. Late Neuropsychiatric Sequelae: Post-concussion syndrome, post-traumatic epilepsy, retrograde/anterograde amnesia, chronic encephalopathy, and focal neurological deficits.

---

### Q24: Case Question 6: What are the possible causes of death if this condition is left untreated?

**Clinical Case:** A 23-year-old man hits a 40-year-old man with a stone on his head. The victim fainted for a few minutes, then was transmitted to the hospital in a drowsy state. CT of the head revealed a linear skull fracture and an extradural hemorrhage. Then the patient developed coma again after a few hours.

**Model Answer:**
1. Brainstem tonsillar herniation ('coning') compressing vital respiratory and cardiac regulatory centers in the medulla oblongata, leading to fatal apnea and cardiac arrest.
2. Global cerebral ischemia and irreversible brain death due to intracranial pressure exceeding cerebral perfusion pressure.
3. Acute neurogenic pulmonary edema.
4. Secondary terminal complications (aspiration pneumonia, hyperpyrexia, systemic sepsis).
