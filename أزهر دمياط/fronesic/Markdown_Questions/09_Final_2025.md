# Final 2025 Exam Questions

## Section 1: Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

### Q1: Maceration of a dead fetus is seen exclusively in:
- **A)** Intra-uterine foetal death
- **B)** Extra-uterine foetal death
- **C)** Death due to firearm injuries
- **D)** Death due to violent asphyxia

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Maceration is an aseptic autolytic softening of fetal tissues occurring only when a fetus dies inside the closed, sterile amniotic fluid environment in utero and remains retained for at least 24 hours.

---

### Q2: The only sure sign of virginity is:
- **A)** Intact hymen
- **B)** Intact posterior commissure and fourchette
- **C)** Narrow vagina
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** An intact hymen is the sole definitive proof of virginity. Other signs (narrow vagina, intact fourchette, rugose vaginal walls) are presumptive and supportive only.

---

### Q3: Deciduous (milk) teeth in humans are:
- **A)** 16 in number
- **B)** 18 in number
- **C)** 20 in number
- **D)** 32 in number

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The temporary (deciduous/milk) dentition consists of exactly 20 teeth (4 central incisors, 4 lateral incisors, 4 canines, and 8 molars).

---

### Q4: Rigor mortis occurs first in which voluntary muscle?
- **A)** Small muscles of the hand
- **B)** Muscles of the eyelids (Orbicularis oculi)
- **C)** Muscles of the neck
- **D)** Muscles of the legs

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** According to Nysten's law of rigor mortis progression, stiffening in voluntary skeletal muscles begins in the orbicularis oculi (eyelids) within 1-2 hours after death.

---

### Q5: The medico-legal importance (M.L.I) of the age of 21 years old includes:
- **A)** Attaining full civil and legal rights
- **B)** Criminal responsibility
- **C)** Employment in government
- **D)** Consent of rape in female

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Age 21 represents the age of full civil majority and civil rights under Egyptian law, ending parental/guardian tutelage and granting unrestricted legal contracting authority.

---

### Q6: Which of the following is NOT true about fresh water drowning?
- **A)** Hyperkalemia
- **B)** Hypovolemia
- **C)** Ventricular fibrillation
- **D)** Hemolysis

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Fresh water is hypotonic relative to blood; rapid alveolar absorption into the pulmonary circulation causes massive HYPERvolemia (dilutional hypervolemia), hemodilution, hemolysis, hyperkalemia, and fatal ventricular fibrillation.

---

### Q7: Characters of pontine hemorrhage include all of the following EXCEPT:
- **A)** Coma
- **B)** Fixed dilated pupil
- **C)** Hyperthermia
- **D)** Quadriplegia

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Pontine hemorrhage classically presents with pinpoint pupils (due to disruption of descending sympathetic pathways and unopposed parasympathetic tone), hyperthermia, bilateral flaccid quadriplegia, and deep coma.

---

### Q8: Rapid causes of death in burns include all of the following EXCEPT:
- **A)** Acute edema of the glottis
- **B)** Acute adrenal insufficiency
- **C)** Injury of vital organs
- **D)** Neurogenic shock

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Rapid/immediate causes of death in burns include neurogenic shock, acute glottic edema, smoke/CO inhalation, and trauma to vital organs. Adrenal insufficiency and sepsis are delayed complications occurring days to weeks later.

## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q9: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of teeth examination:

**Model Answer:**
1. Age Estimation: Counting erupted deciduous teeth (up to 2 years), mixed dentition (6-12 years), permanent teeth, third molar eruption (17-21 years), and Gustafson's microscopic criteria in adults (attrition, periodontitis, secondary dentin, cementum apposition, root translucency, root resorption).
2. Identification of Unknown Bodies: Forensic odontogram charting, comparison with ante-mortem dental records, dental restorations, fillings, dentures, and prosthetics.
3. Sex Determination: Dental dimensions (canine tooth index), Barr bodies in dental pulp.
4. Bite Mark Analysis: Matching bite marks found on victims of rape, homicide, or battered children to suspect dentition.
5. Source of DNA: Dental pulp is highly protected from heat and decomposition, providing pristine DNA samples.

---

### Q10: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of the lucid interval:

**Model Answer:**
1. Pathognomonic diagnostic feature: Indicative of extradural hemorrhage (EDH) from middle meningeal artery injury, requiring emergency neurosurgical burr-hole evacuation.
2. Civil Capacity: During the lucid interval, the individual possesses full testamentary capacity to write a valid will, execute contracts, or issue a dying declaration.
3. Criminal Liability: Proves that the traumatic head injury caused by the assailant was the direct proximate cause of delayed fatal intracranial compression.

---

### Q11: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of distinction between somatic and cellular deaths:

**Model Answer:**
1. Organ Transplantation: Organs must be harvested during the period between somatic death (brain stem death) and molecular/cellular tissue necrosis.
2. Resuscitation: Immediate emergency measures (CPR, defibrillation) can successfully reverse somatic death before irreversible cellular destruction occurs.
3. Medicolegal Certification: Medical death certificates require confirmation of somatic death.
4. Supravital Reactions: Assessing postmortem excitability of muscles to electrical/chemical stimuli during the early postmortem period helps estimate the precise postmortem interval (PMI).

---

### Q12: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of powder marks in firearm injuries:

**Model Answer:**
1. Differentiation of Inlet from Outlet: Powder marks (tattooing and smudging) occur exclusively around the entrance wound.
2. Estimation of Firing Distance: Close range (<30 cm) causes smudging (smoke soot deposition); near range (<100 cm) causes tattooing (unburnt/partially burnt powder granules embedded in skin).
3. Direction and Angle of Fire: Circular symmetrical distribution indicates perpendicular firing; eccentric or oval distribution indicates an oblique trajectory.
4. Weapon Type: Distribution and density reflect weapon barrel length and powder composition (black vs smokeless powder).

---

### Q13: Enumerate 4 of M.L.I of pregnancy:

**Model Answer:**
1. Suspension of Capital Punishment: Under Article 476 of the Egyptian Criminal Procedure Code, execution of a pregnant woman sentenced to death is postponed until two months after delivery.
2. Civil and Inheritance Rights: Entitles the posthumous unborn child to legal inheritance, paternity rights, and claims for maternal alimony/maintenance.
3. Mitigation or Element in Crimes: In alleged infanticide, concealed birth, or disputed legitimacy/paternity.
4. Assessment in Criminal Abortion: Proving the existence of pregnancy is an essential element in charges of induced abortion.

---

### Q14: Define sudden natural death.

**Model Answer:**
Death that is unexpected, not known to have been caused by trauma or poisoning, occurring instantaneously or within 24 hours of the onset of acute symptoms in an apparently healthy individual or someone with mild asymptomatic natural disease.

---

### Q15: Define pulmonary embolism.

**Model Answer:**
The occlusion of the pulmonary arterial trunk or its branches by an embolus, most commonly a detached thromboembolus originating from deep venous thrombosis (DVT) of the lower extremities or pelvic veins following trauma, immobilization, surgery, or burns.

---

### Q16: Define drowning from a forensic perspective.

**Model Answer:**
A form of violent mechanical asphyxia caused by submersion or partial immersion of the external respiratory orifices (nose and mouth) in a liquid medium, resulting in fluid aspiration into the air passages.

---

### Q17: Define criminal abortion.

**Model Answer:**
The intentional, unlawful termination of pregnancy and expulsion or destruction of the product of conception at any stage of gestation before full term, performed without legitimate medical indication and justification.

---

### Q18: Define cadaveric spasm (instantaneous rigor).

**Model Answer:**
A rare form of abnormal postmortem stiffening that occurs instantaneously at the exact moment of death without an intervening phase of primary muscular flaccidity, crystallizing the terminal muscular activity and posture of the victim, seen in deaths involving severe emotional tension, violent struggle, exhaustion, or sudden injury to the brain stem.

---

### Q19: Case Question 1: How could you estimate the age of the victim?

**Clinical Case:** A 60-year-old male farmer was found dead in his farm. On examination, there were multiple penetrating wounds on the chest with one angle sharp while the other was rounded (2 cm in length and 5 cm in depth) and there was a deep-seated transverse cut wound on the front of the neck.

**Model Answer:**
1. Skull Suture Closure: Obliteration of coronal, sagittal, and lambdoid sutures (sagittal closes 40-45, coronal closes 40-50, lambdoid closes 45-55 years).
2. Gustafson Dental Method: Microscopic changes in teeth (marked attrition, secondary dentin deposition, gingival recession/periodontitis, root transparency, cementum apposition).
3. Skeletal Degeneration: Osteoarthritis of spine/joints, ossification of laryngeal and costal cartilages, arcus senilis in eyes, thinning of skull bone.

### Q20: Case Question 2: Suggest the possible causes of death in this case.

**Clinical Case:** A 60-year-old male farmer was found dead in his farm. On examination, there were multiple penetrating wounds on the chest with one angle sharp while the other was rounded (2 cm in length and 5 cm in depth) and there was a deep-seated transverse cut wound on the front of the neck.

**Model Answer:**
1. Exsanguination and acute hemorrhagic shock: Profuse external and internal hemorrhage from severed major neck vessels (carotid arteries, jugular veins) and penetrating chest wounds injuring the heart, lungs, or internal thoracic vessels.
2. Air Embolism: Venous air embolism due to negative intrathoracic pressure sucking atmospheric air into slashed jugular veins.
3. Asphyxia: Aspiration of blood into the severed larynx/trachea causing terminal airway drowning in blood (choking).

### Q21: Case Question 3: Could you determine whether these wounds are ante-mortem or post-mortem? Explain the criteria.

**Clinical Case:** A 60-year-old male farmer was found dead in his farm. On examination, there were multiple penetrating wounds on the chest with one angle sharp while the other was rounded (2 cm in length and 5 cm in depth) and there was a deep-seated transverse cut wound on the front of the neck.

**Model Answer:**
Yes. Wounds are confirmed ANTE-MORTEM by:
1. Profuse bleeding with spurting and significant bloodstains around the scene.
2. Firmly adherent, arterial dark-red blood clots in the wound margins and tissue crevices (cannot be washed away with water).
3. Gaping of wound edges due to vital elastic retraction of living skin and muscle fibers.
4. Vital reaction: Macroscopic swelling, redness, and microscopic infiltration of polymorphonuclear leukocytes and fibrin meshwork within wound edges.

### Q22: Case Question 4: How can you identify the causative instruments in this case?

**Clinical Case:** A 60-year-old male farmer was found dead in his farm. On examination, there were multiple penetrating wounds on the chest with one angle sharp while the other was rounded (2 cm in length and 5 cm in depth) and there was a deep-seated transverse cut wound on the front of the neck.

**Model Answer:**
1. Chest penetrating wounds: Produced by a SINGLE-EDGED cutting/stabbing instrument (e.g. knife or dagger with one sharp edge and one blunt spine), indicated by one acute angle and one blunt/rounded angle, blade width approximately 2 cm, blade minimum length 5 cm.
2. Neck cut wound: Produced by a sharp cutting instrument (e.g. razor blade, sharp butcher knife, or scythe/sickle) with clean-cut regular edges, sharp acute angles, absence of tissue bridging, and greater length than depth.

### Q23: Case Question 5: Suggest the manner of death (circumstances of the incident) and justify your reasoning.

**Clinical Case:** A 60-year-old male farmer was found dead in his farm. On examination, there were multiple penetrating wounds on the chest with one angle sharp while the other was rounded (2 cm in length and 5 cm in depth) and there was a deep-seated transverse cut wound on the front of the neck.

**Model Answer:**
The manner of death is HOMICIDAL. Justification:
1. Coexistence of multiple deep penetrating stab wounds to the chest along with a deep-seated cut throat.
2. Inaccessibility and severity: Penetrating deep chest trauma combined with severe transverse cut throat would rapidly incapacitate the victim, making self-infliction physically impossible.
3. Location: Remote farm scene; absence of suicidal hesitation (tentative) marks on neck or wrists; presence of multiple scattered fatal puncture wounds.

---

### Q24: Case Question 6: How can you differentiate between suicidal and homicidal cut throat wounds?

**Clinical Case:** A 60-year-old male farmer was found dead in his farm. On examination, there were multiple penetrating wounds on the chest with one angle sharp while the other was rounded (2 cm in length and 5 cm in depth) and there was a deep-seated transverse cut wound on the front of the neck.

**Model Answer:**
| Feature | Suicidal Cut Throat | Homicidal Cut Throat |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hesitation Marks** | Present (tentative superficial cuts at origin) | Absent |
| **Direction** | Starts high on left, slopes down across neck (right-handed) | Usually horizontal or irregular |
| **Level** | Above thyroid cartilage | Below or across thyroid cartilage |
| **Depth** | Deepest at beginning, tails off at end | Uniformly deep throughout |
| **Edges** | Even, clean-cut, regular | Uneven, jagged, multiple incisions |
| **Defense Wounds** | Absent on hands and forearms | Commonly present on palms, fingers, ulnar borders |
| **Weapon** | Found at scene, often clutched in hand (cadaveric spasm) | Absent from scene (removed by assailant) |
| **Scene** | Inside locked room, in front of mirror, clothes arranged | Signs of struggle, disturbance, clothes torn |
