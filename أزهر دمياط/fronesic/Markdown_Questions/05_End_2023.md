# End 2023 Exam Questions

### Q1: The anterior fontanel starts to close at the age of:
- **A)** 12 months
- **B)** 18 months
- **C)** 6 months
- **D)** None of the above

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The anterior fontanel begins physiological closure at approximately 12 months of age and completes bony obliteration between 18 to 24 months.

---

### Q2: Fingerprints appear during intrauterine life at the age of:
- **A)** 16th week
- **B)** 20th week
- **C)** 24th week
- **D)** None of the above

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Epidermal friction ridges on the fingers and palms develop during early intrauterine life between the 12th and 16th weeks of gestation and remain unchanged throughout life.

---

### Q3: In disputed paternity, both sets of parents and the disputed child are examined carefully for:
- **A)** Facial features
- **B)** Transmitted hereditary abnormalities
- **C)** Types of fingerprints
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Morphological examination in disputed paternity includes facial resemblance, dermatoglyphics, anthropometric traits, and hereditary physical abnormalities alongside serological/DNA tests.

---

### Q4: The commonest type of scalp wound is a:
- **A)** Firearm wound
- **B)** Abrasion
- **C)** Cut wound
- **D)** Contused wound

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Because the scalp is tightly stretched over unyielding skull bones, blunt impacts crush and split the scalp, making contused lacerated wounds the most frequent scalp injury.

---

### Q5: The most common cause of basal skull fractures is:
- **A)** Direct violence
- **B)** Indirect violence
- **C)** All of the above
- **D)** None of the above

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Fractures of the base of the skull most commonly result from indirect violence (e.g. falls on feet/buttocks transmitting force up the vertebral column, or blows to the mandible driving the condyles upward).

---

### Q6: Extradural hematoma is due to vascular injury that is:
- **A)** Arterial only
- **B)** Venous only
- **C)** Arterial or venous
- **D)** Pathological

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Extradural hematomas arise most commonly from arterial laceration of the middle meningeal artery (MMA, ~85%), but can also originate from venous tears in middle meningeal veins or dural venous sinuses (~15%).

---

### Q7: Types of stab wounds include:
- **A)** Puncture and penetrating wounds
- **B)** Puncture and perforating wounds
- **C)** Puncture and transfixing wounds
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Stab wounds encompass puncture, penetrating (entering closed body cavities), perforating (piercing through viscera), and transfixing wounds.

---

### Q8: All of the following are considered manners of death EXCEPT:
- **A)** Natural
- **B)** Accidental
- **C)** Homicidal and suicidal
- **D)** Brain stem death

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The recognized legal manners of death are: Natural, Accidental, Suicidal, Homicidal, and Undetermined. Brain stem death is a clinical/physiological mechanism/state of death, not a legal manner.

---

### Q9: Certain conditions must be ascertained for identification of an assailant by the flash of firearm discharge, including:
- **A)** The victim must have perfect vision
- **B)** The victim must be facing the assailant
- **C)** The distance of firing must be very close (not exceeding 2-3 meters)
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Identification by gunshot muzzle flash requires optimal darkness, facing each other, clear visual acuity, and firing distance within a few feet (<2-3 meters).

---

### Q10: General characters of firearm entrance wounds include all of the following EXCEPT:
- **A)** Loss of skin substance
- **B)** Both entrance and exit wounds must always be present
- **C)** Internal beveling of cranial bone
- **D)** Presence of an abrasion collar and wound track

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** An exit wound is not mandatory; in low-velocity impacts or deep penetration, the projectile frequently lodges within tissues without an exit wound.

---

### Q11: The following are immediate complications of wounds EXCEPT:
- **A)** Shock
- **B)** Hemorrhage
- **C)** Embolism
- **D)** Infection

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Infection (sepsis, tetanus, gas gangrene) requires bacterial incubation time, developing as a delayed or remote complication (after 24-48 hours), whereas shock, hemorrhage, and air embolism can occur immediately.

---

### Q12: In traumatic rupture of internal solid organs (e.g. spleen, liver):
- **A)** Unless treated by prompt surgical operation, death is inevitable
- **B)** The patient suffers from symptoms of shock and internal hemorrhage
- **C)** Death is always instantaneous
- **D)** Both A and B are correct

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Internal parenchymal rupture of solid abdominal organs produces acute internal bleeding (hemoperitoneum) and hypovolemic shock, requiring urgent surgical exploration to prevent death.

---

### Q13: Types of fat embolism in trauma include:
- **A)** Pulmonary (Venous) fat embolism
- **B)** Systemic (Arterial) fat embolism
- **C)** Both A and B
- **D)** None of the above

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Fat globules entering torn venules in fractured bones travel to pulmonary capillary beds (pulmonary/venous fat embolism) or cross patent foramen ovale/capillaries into systemic circulation (systemic/arterial fat embolism to brain, kidneys, skin).

---

### Q14: The clinical severity and prognosis of burn injuries depend on:
- **A)** Degree and depth of burn
- **B)** Total percentage of body surface area involved
- **C)** Age and general health of the victim
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Burn prognosis depends on percentage of body surface area involved (Rule of Nines), burn depth (degree), age of the patient, and location of burns.

---

### Q15: Scald burns caused by hot liquids typically spread:
- **A)** From above downwards following gravity in streaks
- **B)** Exclusively from below upwards
- **C)** In circular concentric rings
- **D)** None of the above

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Liquid scalds run down the body under the influence of gravity, producing tapering streaks and gutters pointing downwards.

---

### Q16: Postmortem hypostasis (lividity) becomes fixed after:
- **A)** 4 hours
- **B)** 2 hours
- **C)** 8 hours
- **D)** Within one hour

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Postmortem hypostasis becomes fully fixed due to hemolysis and perivascular tissue staining after approximately 8-12 hours.

---

### Q17: Sure post-mortem signs of drowning include:
- **A)** Fine persistent froth around mouth and nose and in airways
- **B)** Cadaveric spasm grasping weeds or sand
- **C)** Presence of diatoms (planktons) in closed organs
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Froth at the respiratory orifices, cadaveric spasm clutching water weeds, and diatoms in bone marrow/internal organs are conclusive ante-mortem signs of drowning.

---

### Q18: Valid legal consent in alleged rape requires:
- **A)** Age of the female above 18 years
- **B)** The female must be mentally sane
- **C)** The female must be fully conscious, awake, and not under duress or threats
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Under penal law, consent is vitiated and legally void if obtained from a minor (<18), an insane person, or through fear, coercion, intoxicants, or fraud.

---

### Q19: The only sure physical sign of virginity is:
- **A)** Intact hymen
- **B)** Intact posterior commissure
- **C)** Intact fourchette
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** An intact, normal hymenal membrane is the sole anatomical confirmation of virginity.

---

### Q20: In Child Abuse Syndrome (battered child), abuse may manifest as:
- **A)** Physical abuse
- **B)** Emotional / mental abuse
- **C)** Sexual abuse
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Child maltreatment encompasses physical trauma, severe neglect, psychological/emotional abuse, and sexual abuse.
