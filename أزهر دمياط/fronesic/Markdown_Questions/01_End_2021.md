# End 2021 Exam Questions

## Section 1: Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

### Q1: The most characteristic feature of a stab wound is:
- **A)** Regular edges
- **B)** Tissue bridges
- **C)** Two acute angles
- **D)** Length is less than depth
- **E)** Width is more than depth

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** A stab (punctured) wound is defined primarily by its depth exceeding its surface length (depth > length), caused by a pointed weapon penetrating perpendicularly or obliquely.

---

### Q2: In a firearm injury, there must always be:
- **A)** Two wounds: inlet and exit
- **B)** Loss of substance
- **C)** Soiling ring around the exit
- **D)** Everted edges of the inlet wound
- **E)** Muzzle imprint around the inlet wound

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The entrance wound in a firearm injury always displays actual loss of tissue/substance (punched-out defect) created by bullet passage, whereas an exit wound or muzzle imprint is not universally present.

---

### Q3: A comminuted skull fracture is characterized by all of the following EXCEPT:
- **A)** Occurs from falling from a height
- **B)** Results from direct blow by a heavy blunt wide surface object
- **C)** Radiated fissures in all directions may occur
- **D)** Shape of the injury suggests the position of the assailant
- **E)** Fragments of bone may be driven inwards

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Patterned/signature fractures reproduce weapon shape or assailant orientation. Comminuted fractures (splintered/crushed into multiple fragments) result from high-energy broad impact and do not indicate assailant position.

---

### Q4: Maceration is seen only in cases of:
- **A)** Intra-uterine foetal death
- **B)** Extra-uterine foetal death
- **C)** Death due to firearm injuries
- **D)** Death due to violent asphyxia (drowning)

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Maceration is aseptic autolysis of a dead fetus retained in the sterile amniotic fluid inside the uterus for at least 24-48 hours.

---

### Q5: Cerebral ischemia is the main cause of death in:
- **A)** Drowning
- **B)** Hanging
- **C)** Strangulation
- **D)** Smothering

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** In judicial and complete suspension hanging, bilateral compression of carotid and vertebral vessels rapidly produces fatal cerebral ischemia/anemia within seconds.

---

### Q6: The age of eligibility for a motor vehicle driving license in females (18 years) is identified by X-ray of:
- **A)** The hip joint
- **B)** The knee joint
- **C)** The wrist joint
- **D)** The ankle joint

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Fusion of the lower epiphysis of the radius and ulna (wrist joint) occurs at 18-19 years in females, confirming the statutory age of 18.

---

### Q7: Post-mortem hypostasis (lividity):
- **A)** Begins immediately after death when circulation ceases
- **B)** Begins 2 hours after circulation stops
- **C)** Begins 4 hours after circulation stops
- **D)** Begins 1 hour after circulation stops

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Hypostasis begins immediately upon circulatory arrest as blood sinks gravitationally to dependent vessels, although it becomes macroscopically visible as patches within 1-2 hours.

---

### Q8: Acute cardiovascular failure (syncope) is manifested by the following EXCEPT:
- **A)** Fainting and blurring of vision
- **B)** Marked pallor of the skin and mucous membranes
- **C)** Subnormal body temperature & cold clammy sweating
- **D)** Cyanosis
- **E)** Weak, rapid, imperceptible pulse and low blood pressure

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Cyanosis is the classic hallmark of asphyxia/respiratory failure. Syncope/circulatory shock presents with marked peripheral pallor, collapse, and imperceptible pulse.

---

### Q9: Cadaveric spasm occurs in the following circumstances of death EXCEPT:
- **A)** Suicidal deaths
- **B)** Homicidal struggles
- **C)** Violent drowning
- **D)** Death due to chronic pathological diseases

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Cadaveric spasm requires sudden, violent death associated with intense emotional stress, physical exhaustion, or acute cerebral injury (drowning, mountain falls, firearm suicide). It does not occur in gradual deaths from chronic disease.

---

### Q10: The total number of primary (deciduous / milk) teeth is:
- **A)** 16 in number
- **B)** 18 in number
- **C)** 20 in number
- **D)** 32 in number

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The temporary (deciduous) dentition consists of exactly 20 teeth (4 central incisors, 4 lateral incisors, 4 canines, and 8 molars).

---

### Q11: At the age of 18 years, all of the following are true EXCEPT:
- **A)** Legal marriage age for males and females
- **B)** Age of valid legal consent for sexual intercourse
- **C)** Age of full adult criminal execution responsibility
- **D)** Age of mandatory civil retirement

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** At 18 years, an individual attains the age of legal marriage, driving license eligibility, and full adult criminal sanctions, while retirement occurs decades later.

---

### Q12: Regarding skull fissure fractures, all of the following are true EXCEPT:
- **A)** They may radiate along lines of bony weakness
- **B)** Caused by a heavy blunt object with a narrow striking surface with moderate forces
- **C)** They may be polar or bipolar fractures
- **D)** They may be linear or multiple radiating fractures

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A narrow striking surface with moderate-to-heavy force causes a DEPRESSED or punched-out fracture. Fissure fractures result from diffuse blunt impacts with a broad striking surface.

---

### Q13: Contused wounds are classified into all of the following EXCEPT:
- **A)** Split contused wound
- **B)** Crushed contused wound
- **C)** Fabricated contused wound
- **D)** Flap avulsion contused wound

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Contused wounds are morphologically split, crushed, stretch, or avulsion/flap lacerations. 'Fabricated' is a motivational/manner classification (self-inflicted, usually superficial cuts/abrasions), not a morphological type.

---

### Q14: All of the following are recognized medico-legal importance (M.L.I) of pregnancy EXCEPT:
- **A)** To delay execution of capital punishment in convicted pregnant females
- **B)** To produce an heir later on for inheritance claims
- **C)** To claim alimony and financial maintenance
- **D)** To aggravate the punishment of the accused as in a quarrel resulting in abortion

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The forensic medical importance encompasses staying capital execution (Article 476 Egyptian Code of Criminal Procedure), verifying pregnancy in alimony claims, and legal prosecution for assault resulting in abortion (Article 260 Penal Code). 'Producing an heir later on' is a personal/familial motive rather than a formal medico-legal application.

## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q15: Enumerate the phases of death:

**Model Answer:**
1. Somatic (Clinical) Death: Complete, irreversible cessation of the vital tripod of life (functions of brain, heart, and lungs).
2. Cellular (Molecular) Death: The progressive, irreversible death and cessation of metabolic, enzymatic, and biochemical activity of individual tissues and cells occurring hours after somatic death.

---

### Q16: Enumerate 4 of postmortem changes:

**Model Answer:**
1. Algor Mortis: Progressive cooling of the body until it reaches ambient environmental temperature.
2. Postmortem Hypostasis (Lividity): Gravitational pooling of blood in dependent microvessels producing purple-red discoloration.
3. Rigor Mortis: Progressive stiffening and rigidity of voluntary and involuntary muscles due to ATP depletion.
4. Putrefaction / Decomposition: Aseptic autolysis and bacterial breakdown of tissues producing green abdominal discoloration, marbling, bloating, and liquefaction.

---

### Q17: Enumerate 4 of types and causes of asphyxia resulting from burn injuries:

**Model Answer:**
1. Carbon Monoxide Anoxia: Inhalation of CO in smoke forming toxic carboxyhemoglobin (CO-Hb > 50%), causing histotoxic and anemic anoxia.
2. Inhalation of Smoke & Particulates: Causes reflex laryngospasm, severe chemical tracheobronchitis, and acute pulmonary edema.
3. Carbon Dioxide Suffocation: Oxygen depletion and CO2 accumulation in enclosed fire spaces.
4. Acute Edema of the Glottis: Inhalation of superheated air or flame burns of the neck compressing the larynx.

---

### Q18: Enumerate 4 of anatomical types of stab wounds:

**Model Answer:**
1. Puncture Wound: Deep penetrating wound into skin and muscle where depth > length.
2. Penetrating Stab Wound: The blade breaches a closed serous body cavity (pleural, pericardial, or peritoneal cavity).
3. Perforating Stab Wound: The blade enters and passes completely through an internal organ (e.g. liver, lung, heart).
4. Transfixing Stab Wound: The weapon enters one body surface and emerges completely through the opposite anatomical surface (e.g., through a limb or neck).

---

### Q19: Define shock from a forensic medicine perspective.

**Model Answer:**
A state of acute systemic circulatory collapse resulting in profound, generalized reduction in tissue and organ perfusion, leading to cellular hypoxia, metabolic failure, and irreversible organ damage if uncorrected.

---

### Q20: Define embolism.

**Model Answer:**
The sudden impaction and obstruction of a blood vessel by an intravascular mass (solid, liquid, or gaseous) carried from its site of origin through the bloodstream to a distant site.

---

### Q21: Define black spatter (powder tattooing) in firearm injuries.

**Model Answer:**
The involuntary mechanical tattooing of the skin caused by unburnt and partially burnt gunpowder grains propelled at high velocity from the weapon muzzle, becoming permanently embedded within the epidermis and dermis around the entrance wound.

---

### Q22: Define primary rifling marks on a fired bullet.

**Model Answer:**
The class characteristic parallel helical grooves and lands engraved onto the cylindrical surface of a bullet as it travels along the spiraled rifled barrel, reflecting the weapon's caliber, number of grooves, direction of twist (right or left), and pitch of rifling.

---

### Q23: True or False: Non-rifled weapons (shotguns) are designed to fire a single solid aerodynamic missile only.

**Model Answer:**
False. Non-rifled weapons (smooth-bore shotguns) typically fire a charge consisting of hundreds of small lead pellets (shots) contained in a cartridge, rather than a single bullet (unless specialized single slugs are used).

---

### Q24: True or False: Classical ABO Group O individuals are designated as universal blood donors.

**Model Answer:**
True. Group O red blood cells lack both A and B surface agglutinogens, allowing emergency transfusion of packed red blood cells to recipients of any ABO blood group.

---

### Q25: True or False: The main cause of death in strangulation is acute cerebral anemia.

**Model Answer:**
False. While hanging causes death primarily via cerebral anemia from carotid compression, strangulation (ligature or manual) causes death primarily via mechanical airway closure (asphyxia) and reflex vagal cardiac inhibition from carotid sinus stimulation.

---

### Q26: True or False: Legal consent in sexual intercourse is valid if the female is conscious even if her age is below 18 years.

**Model Answer:**
False. Under penal law, intercourse with a female under 18 years of age is legally defined as statutory rape; consent is legally invalid regardless of willingness.

---

### Q27: Case Question 1: How did the medico-legal examiner reach the conclusion that this firearm injury was homicidal and NOT accidental?

**Clinical Case:** A 50-year-old male was transmitted to the hospital with two firearm injuries in the chest. He died shortly after arrival. The accompanying persons stated that the injury was accidental while the victim was cleaning his weapon. Autopsy examination determined that the case was homicidal firearm injury.

**Model Answer:**
1. Multiplicity of shots: The presence of two distinct fatal gunshot wounds to the chest makes an accidental cleaning discharge practically impossible.
2. Firing distance: The wounds lacked close-contact signs (no muzzle impression, no subcutaneous gas pocketing), indicating they were fired from a distance beyond the victim's arm reach.
3. Direction and Trajectory: The bullet tracks followed downward, backward, or oblique angles inconsistent with self-inflicted handling while cleaning.
4. Absence of cleaning materials: Lack of cleaning rods, patches, or solvent residues on the hands of the deceased.
5. Clothing: Shots were fired through intact clothing rather than exposed skin.

---

### Q28: Case Question 2: How can you differentiate the firearm inlet (entrance) from the exit wound?

**Clinical Case:** A 50-year-old male was transmitted to the hospital with two firearm injuries in the chest. He died shortly after arrival. The accompanying persons stated that the injury was accidental while the victim was cleaning his weapon. Autopsy examination determined that the case was homicidal firearm injury.

**Model Answer:**
| Feature | Entrance Wound (Inlet) | Exit Wound (Outlet) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Size** | Matches or smaller than projectile caliber | Typically larger than inlet |
| **Shape** | Rounded or oval | Irregular, torn, slit-like, or stellate |
| **Loss of Substance** | Present (circular hole punched out) | Absent (edges approximate cleanly) |
| **Margins** | Inverted, clean-cut | Everted, ragged, shredded |
| **Abrasion Collar** | Present (abrasion rim around defect) | Absent (unless shored exit) |
| **Soiling Ring** | Present (grease/dirt collar from bullet) | Absent |
| **Powder Residues** | Present in close/near range discharges | Absent |
