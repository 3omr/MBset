# End 2025 Exam Questions

## Section 1: Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

### Q1: All of the following are probable signs of death in drowning EXCEPT:
- **A)** Hypothermia of the body
- **B)** Goose skin (Cutis anserina)
- **C)** Mushroom of froth around mouth and nose
- **D)** Paleness of the skin

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Persistent fine white mushroom froth at the mouth and nostrils (emanating from the respiratory tract) is a SURE (diagnostic) sign of ante-mortem drowning, whereas cutis anserina, bleached sodden skin (washerwoman hands), and paleness are probable signs.

---

### Q2: The main cause of death in hanging is:
- **A)** Cerebral anemia
- **B)** Mechanical asphyxia
- **C)** Reflex cardiac inhibition
- **D)** Hypovolemic shock

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Bilateral compression of the carotid arteries and internal jugular veins causes rapid cerebral ischemia (cerebral anemia) and immediate loss of consciousness, representing the most common cause of death in hanging.

---

### Q3: The type of instrument used in a crime can be recognized from:
- **A)** The characters of the wounds
- **B)** The process of healing
- **C)** The site of the wounds
- **D)** The nature of the crime

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The morphology and physical characteristics of a wound (regular vs irregular edges, presence of tissue bridging, shape, dimensions, depth) definitively identify the causative weapon.

---

### Q4: Types of stab wounds include:
- **A)** Puncture wounds
- **B)** Perforating wounds
- **C)** Transfixing wounds
- **D)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Stab wounds include puncture wounds (depth > length, into skin/muscle), penetrating wounds (entering body cavities), perforating wounds (passing through an organ/body part), and transfixing wounds (entering one surface and emerging from the opposite side).

---

### Q5: The postmortem picture of death due to hemorrhage includes the following EXCEPT:
- **A)** Pale body and mucous membranes
- **B)** Faint hypostasis
- **C)** Contracted spleen with corrugated capsule
- **D)** Congested heart and large blood vessels

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** In death from hemorrhage, internal organs are ischemic and bloodless; the heart cavities and large blood vessels are collapsed and empty (not congested), and subendocardial hemorrhages of the left ventricle are seen.

---

### Q6: The medico-legal importance (M.L.I) of the age of 18 years is:
- **A)** Age of civil (legal) right
- **B)** Legal marriage age for males and females
- **C)** Age of membership of national assembly
- **D)** Age of full adult criminal execution

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Under Egyptian law, 18 years is the minimum legal age for marriage for both males and females, obtaining a driver's license, and validity of legal consent in sexual offenses.

---

### Q7: Postmortem hypostasis (lividity) becomes fixed after:
- **A)** 1 hour
- **B)** 2 hours
- **C)** 8 hours
- **D)** 2 days

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Hypostasis starts within 1-2 hours after death, becomes fully formed in 4-6 hours, and becomes completely fixed (due to hemolysis and tissue staining) after 8-12 hours.

## Section 2: Written Questions

### Q8: Discuss the medico-legal importance (M.L.I) of the lucid interval.

**Model Answer:**
1. Diagnostic importance: It is the clinical hallmark of an expanding extradural (epidural) hematoma caused by arterial bleeding (middle meningeal artery), mandating urgent emergency neurosurgical intervention (burr-hole evacuation).
2. Civil and testamentary capacity: During the lucid interval, the individual is fully conscious, lucid, and sound of mind, legally capable of making a valid will (testament), managing property, signing contracts, or giving a valid dying declaration.
3. Criminal responsibility: It proves that the head trauma was the direct proximate cause of the second progressive coma and eventual death, establishing the causal link in criminal homicide.

---

### Q9: Discuss the causes of death in burns.

**Model Answer:**
1. Immediate Causes (within hours):
 - Primary neurogenic shock (intense pain and emotional shock).
 - Suffocation and asphyxia due to inhalation of smoke, carbon monoxide, and carbon dioxide.
 - Acute edema of the glottis from inhalation of hot fumes or flame burns of the neck.
2. Delayed Causes (after 24-48 hours):
 - Secondary hypovolemic shock (massive loss of plasma from blistered and weeping surfaces).
 - Acute renal failure (due to shock, hypovolemia, and hemoglobinuria).
 - Acute toxemia and Curling's stress ulcer (acute gastroduodenal ulceration).
3. Late Causes (after days/weeks):
 - Sepsis and septic shock (Pseudomonas, Staph aureus wound infection).
 - Pulmonary complications (hypostatic pneumonia, ARDS).
 - Exhaustion, cachexia, and amyloidosis.

---

### Q10: Enumerate the criteria for diagnosis of brain stem death.

**Model Answer:**
1. Deep comatose state of established irreversible etiology (e.g. massive trauma, ICH).
2. Complete absence of all brainstem reflexes:
 - Pupillary light reflex absent.
 - Corneal reflex absent.
 - Oculo-vestibular (caloric) reflex absent.
 - Oculo-cephalic (doll's head) reflex absent.
 - Gag and cough reflexes absent.
3. Apnea Test: Complete absence of spontaneous respiratory efforts after disconnecting ventilator and allowing arterial PaCO2 to rise >= 60 mmHg.
4. Persistence of these criteria for a designated observation period (e.g. 6-24 hours) by an independent panel of specialists.

---

### Q11: Discuss the medico-legal importance (M.L.I) of the hymen.

**Model Answer:**
1. Proof of virginity: An intact, normal hymen is the sole conclusive physical proof of female virginity.
2. Examination in alleged rape / sexual assault: Detects fresh tears, lacerations, bleeding, or bruising of the hymen.
3. Differentiating recent from old tears: Recent tears show red, swollen, tender, bleeding edges with clotted blood; old tears show healed, pale, retracted cicatricial margins.
4. Medicolegal disputes: In divorce cases, paternity disputes, defamation claims, and false accusations of defloration.

---

### Q12: Define cerebral concussion.

**Model Answer:**
A widespread, immediate, and transient physiological disruption of neural function following mechanical head trauma, manifested by transient loss of consciousness followed by spontaneous, complete recovery, without identifiable macroscopic structural brain lesions.

---

### Q13: Define Baby Shaking Syndrome.

**Model Answer:**
A form of non-accidental inflicted head trauma in infants caused by violent back-and-forth shaking, producing rotational angular acceleration-deceleration forces that tear bridging cerebral veins, resulting in acute subdural hematoma, severe brain edema, and diffuse retinal hemorrhages without external signs of skull trauma.

---

### Q14: Define suspended animation (apparent death).

**Model Answer:**
A state in which the vital signs of life (circulation, respiration, and brain activity) are reduced to such an extreme minimum that they cannot be detected by routine clinical methods, falsely simulating death, while molecular life persists and recovery is possible with prompt resuscitation. Commonly encountered in cases of near-drowning, hypothermia, electric shock, newborn asphyxia, and severe barbiturate or narcotic intoxication.

---

### Q15: Define rape from a forensic perspective.

**Model Answer:**
The unlawful sexual intercourse (penetration of the vulva/vagina by the penis, however slight, without requiring ejaculation or hymenal rupture) with a female without her valid, free, and conscious consent, or by force, threats, fraud, or when the victim is incapacitated (minor, insane, unconscious, or intoxicated).

---

### Q16: Differentiate between entrance (inlet) and exit firearm wounds.

**Model Answer:**
| Feature | Firearm Entrance Wound (Inlet) | Firearm Exit Wound (Outlet) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Size** | Smaller than or equal to projectile caliber | Usually larger and more irregular than inlet |
| **Shape** | Rounded (perpendicular) or oval (oblique) | Irregular, stellate, slit-like, or crescentic |
| **Margins / Edges** | Inverted, clean-cut, sharp | Everted, ragged, torn |
| **Loss of Substance** | Definite loss of skin substance | No actual loss of substance (edges approximate) |
| **Abrasion Collar** | Present (concentric or eccentric) | Absent (except in shored exit wounds) |
| **Grease / Dirt Collar** | Present (soiling ring from projectile lubricant) | Absent |
| **Burning, Smudging, Tattooing** | Present in close/near range discharges | Absent (unless shored or tandem shot) |
| **Bone Beveling** | Internal beveling of the inner skull table | External beveling of the outer skull table |
