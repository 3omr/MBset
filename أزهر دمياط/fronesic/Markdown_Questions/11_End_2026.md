# End 2026 Exam Questions

### Q1: Dribbling of saliva is a feature of:
- **A)** Ante-mortem hanging
- **B)** Strangulation
- **C)** Postmortem hanging
- **D)** Drowning

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Dribbling of saliva is a vital ante-mortem sign of hanging caused by irritation of salivary glands by the ligature.

---

### Q2: Which of the following is NOT true about fresh water drowning?
- **A)** Hyperkalemia
- **B)** Hypovolemia
- **C)** Ventricular fibrillation
- **D)** Hemolysis

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Fresh water drowning causes hemodilution and hypervolemia (not hypovolemia), along with massive hemolysis and fatal ventricular fibrillation due to hyperkalemia.

---

### Q3: The mechanism of death in Venous Air Embolism involves:
- **A)** Blockage of coronary arteries
- **B)** An air trap obstructing pulmonary circulation
- **C)** Systemic hypertension
- **D)** Cerebral infarction

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** In venous air embolism, air bubbles form an air lock / froth trap in the right ventricle and pulmonary outflow tract, mechanically obstructing pulmonary blood flow.

---

### Q4: If a victim dies from complications of a necessary surgical operation, the assailant is:
- **A)** Not responsible
- **B)** Only responsible for the initial wound
- **C)** Responsible for the result of the operation
- **D)** Charged with a lesser offense

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Under medicolegal rules of surgical intervention, if the operation is bona fide and necessary to save life, the assailant remains legally responsible for fatal operative complications.

---

### Q5: What is a common intracranial finding in Baby Shaking Syndrome?
- **A)** Chronic subdural hematoma
- **B)** Acute subdural hematoma
- **C)** Epidural hematoma
- **D)** No abnormalities

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Violent rotational acceleration-deceleration shearing forces tear parasagittal bridging veins, producing acute subdural hematoma and retinal hemorrhages.

---

### Q6: A cut wound looks like a contused wound in:
- **A)** Scalp
- **B)** Over the shin of the tibia
- **C)** Axilla
- **D)** Forehead

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** In loose skin areas like the axilla, cut wounds may retract irregularly and appear ragged or contused.

---

### Q7: Which of the following is a primary mechanism for Rigor Mortis?
- **A)** Depletion of ATP
- **B)** Loss of muscle tone
- **C)** Loss of reflexes
- **D)** Severe nervous stress

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Rigor mortis occurs due to postmortem depletion of ATP, preventing detachment of actin-myosin cross-bridges and causing persistent actin-myosin complex rigor.

---

### Q8: A contusion becomes yellow due to:
- **A)** Biliverdin on the 5th day
- **B)** Biliverdin on the 7th day
- **C)** Reduced hemoglobin on the 3rd day
- **D)** Bilirubin on the 5th day

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Degradation of extravasated blood pigment: red (day 1) -> dark blue/purple (day 2-3) -> greenish from biliverdin (day 4) -> yellow from bilirubin/hematoidin (day 5-7).

---

### Q9: Cadaveric spasm is essential in the diagnosis of:
- **A)** Firearm injuries
- **B)** Criminal death
- **C)** Suicide
- **D)** Stab injuries

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Cadaveric spasm instantly preserves the last voluntary act at the moment of death (e.g., grasping a firearm, knife, or poison cup), proving suicide.

---

### Q10: Rigor Mortis appears first in:
- **A)** Muscles of hand
- **B)** Muscles of neck
- **C)** Eyelids
- **D)** Muscles of lower limbs

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** According to Nysten's law, rigor mortis appears first in the involuntary muscles (myocardium), then in voluntary muscles starting with the orbicularis oculi (eyelids) within 1-2 hours.

---

### Q11: A man was hit by the blunt head of an axe; this will most likely result in:
- **A)** Fissure fracture
- **B)** Depressed fracture
- **C)** Comminuted fracture
- **D)** Cut fracture

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A heavy blunt object with a localized striking surface (like the head/butt of an axe) produces a localized depressed fracture matching the striking object.

---

### Q12: A skull fracture that results from falling from a height onto the buttocks is a:
- **A)** Comminuted fracture
- **B)** Indirect fracture
- **C)** Polar fracture
- **D)** Ring fracture

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** A fall from height landing on feet or buttocks transmits upward force through the spine, driving the cervical spine into the base of the skull and creating a ring fracture around the foramen magnum.

---

### Q13: The clinical picture of a lucid interval is commonly seen in which type of intracranial hemorrhage?
- **A)** Subdural hemorrhage
- **B)** Extradural hemorrhage
- **C)** Intracerebral hemorrhage
- **D)** Subarachnoid hemorrhage
- **E)** All of the above

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The classic lucid interval (initial concussion coma -> temporary recovery of consciousness -> secondary relapse into coma) is pathognomonic of extradural hemorrhage from middle meningeal artery rupture.

---

### Q14: The most valuable test to identify the owner of a seminal stain is:
- **A)** Physical appearance only
- **B)** Acid phosphatase level only
- **C)** DNA fingerprinting
- **D)** Fluorescence under UV only

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** DNA fingerprinting is definitive and specific for identifying the exact individual source of seminal stains.

---

### Q15: All of the following are causes of death from burns within 6 hours EXCEPT:
- **A)** Primary neurogenic shock
- **B)** Inhalation of CO and CO2 leading to carboxyhemoglobin and asphyxia
- **C)** Inhalation of smoke leading to pulmonary edema and asphyxia
- **D)** Acute edema of the glottis in burns of the neck

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Primary shock, carbon monoxide poisoning, and acute asphyxia occur within hours. Massive acute inflammatory edema of the glottis and septic complications develop as delayed causes (over 24-48 hours).

---

### Q16: An age of 2 years is detected by:
- **A)** Closure of the anterior fontanel
- **B)** Appearance of the ossific center in the lower end of the radius
- **C)** Eruption of all milk teeth
- **D)** All of the above
- **E)** None of the above

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** At age 2 years: anterior fontanel is completely closed (18-24 months), ossific center for the lower end of radius appears, and all 20 deciduous teeth are fully erupted.

---

### Q17: In fingerprint patterns (Dactylography), the most common pattern is:
- **A)** Arches (16-17%)
- **B)** Loops (68%)
- **C)** Whorls (35%)
- **D)** Composite (10%)

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Loops constitute roughly 65-68% of all fingerprint patterns, making them the most prevalent pattern in humans.

---

### Q18: All of the following are characters of scalds EXCEPT:
- **A)** Caused by moist heat (hot liquids or steam)
- **B)** Spread from above downwards (except in case of steam)
- **C)** They are of 1st or 2nd degrees only, appearing in streaks of vesicles
- **D)** Hair is singed

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Scalds are caused by moist heat, therefore hairs are NEVER singed (singeing of hair only occurs in dry heat / flame burns).

---

### Q19: The delayed cause of death in criminal abortion is mainly:
- **A)** Air embolism
- **B)** Sepsis
- **C)** Reflex vagal inhibition
- **D)** Hemorrhage

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Immediate causes include reflex vagal inhibition, hemorrhage, and air embolism. The principal delayed cause of death is sepsis (generalized peritonitis, septicemia, septic shock).

---

### Q20: The main cause of death in typical hanging is:
- **A)** Reflex cardiac inhibition
- **B)** Asphyxia
- **C)** Cerebral anemia
- **D)** Tearing of the pons

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Bilateral compression of the carotid arteries and jugular veins causes instantaneous acute cerebral ischemia / cerebral anemia and rapid unconsciousness within seconds.
