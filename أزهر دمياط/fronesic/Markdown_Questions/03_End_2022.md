# End 2022 Exam Questions

### Q1: The objective in identification study:-
- **A)** Is to give the origin of collection of bones.
- **B)** Is to give points of identification of body remains.
- **C)** Is to write a correct birth certificate Age of criminal responsibility.
- **D)** All of the above.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Forensic identification establishes individual identity in the living and dead, determining species, sex, race, age, stature, and distinctive physical markers.

---

### Q2: Diagnosis of poisons from colour of lines at their root are true except:-
- **A)** Gray line: in mercury poisoning.
- **B)** Green line: in copper poisoning.
- **C)** Black line: in lead poisoning.
- **D)** Yellow line: in cadmium poisoning.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Cadmium produces yellow rings at the necks of the teeth (cadmium ring), not a gum line. Lead produces a bluish-black Burtonian line, copper a greenish line, and mercury a purplish-gray line.

---

### Q3: Factors affecting type of skull fracture except:-
- **A)** Momentum of the force.
- **B)** Size of striking surface.
- **C)** The mobility of the skull at the time of the blow.
- **D)** Elasticity of the brain.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Skull fractures are governed by the physical properties of the skull bone (thickness, curvature, buttresses) and mechanics of impact (momentum, striking surface, head fixation). Brain elasticity affects parenchymal contusion, not skull bone fracture.

---

### Q4: Brain injuries includes:-
- **A)** Cerebral concussion.
- **B)** Intracranial haemorrhage.
- **C)** Cerebral Compression.
- **D)** All of above.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Mechanical head injuries encompass functional disruption (concussion), parenchymal contusion/laceration, secondary hemorrhages, and elevated intracranial pressure (cerebral compression).

---

### Q5: Legal Classification of wounds except:-
- **A)** Simple wound.
- **B)** Mortal wound.
- **C)** Dangerous wound.
- **D)** Defense wound.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Under penal legal classification, wounds are classified by clinical outcome into Simple wounds (healing <20 days without infirmity), Dangerous wounds (permanent infirmity or hospitalization >20 days), and Mortal wounds (inevitably fatal). 'Defense wound' is a medicolegal anatomical classification, not a legal category.

---

### Q6: Extradural haematoma is mostly:-
- **A)** Arterial.
- **B)** Venous.
- **C)** Capillary.
- **D)** Lymphatic's.

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Extradural hematomas are arterial in origin in roughly 85% of cases, resulting from laceration of the middle meningeal artery branches beneath a temporal/parietal fracture line.

---

### Q7: Type of contused wound:-
- **A)** Puncture.
- **B)** Penetrating.
- **C)** Transfixing.
- **D)** None of the above.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Puncture, penetrating, and transfixing wounds are classifications of stab wounds. Contused wounds are classified into split contusions, stretch contusions, crushing contusions, and avulsion/tear contusions.

---

### Q8: Certain condition must be ascertained for identification by flash of light:
- **A)** The victim must have a perfect vision.
- **B)** The victim must be facing the assailant.
- **C)** The distance of firing must not exceed 10 meters.
- **D)** All of the above.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Identification by firearm flash requires that the victim has perfect vision, directly faces the assailant, and the distance is close enough.

---

### Q9: Postmortem hypostasis become fixed after:-
- **A)** 4 hours.
- **B)** 2 hours.
- **C)** 8 hours.
- **D)** None of the above.

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Postmortem hypostasis becomes fully fixed and unblanchable by digital pressure after approximately 8-12 hours due to postmortem hemolysis and diffusion of hemoglobin into surrounding perivascular tissues.

---

### Q10: Character of Firearm Wounds except:-
- **A)** Loss of substance.
- **B)** The inlet & exit wounds must be present.
- **C)** Bevelling.
- **D)** Presence of track.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A bullet can lodge within tissues or bone, meaning an exit wound is frequently absent in gunshot trauma.

---

### Q11: Immediate wounds complication except:-
- **A)** Shock.
- **B)** Haemorrhage.
- **C)** Embolism.
- **D)** None of the above.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Shock, hemorrhage, and embolism are all recognized immediate complications that can cause death within minutes to hours of injury.

---

### Q12: Type of solid (Detached Thrombi) are:-
- **A)** Thrombophlebitis.
- **B)** Phlebothrombosis.
- **C)** A and b.
- **D)** None of the above.

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Venous thrombi that detach to cause pulmonary embolism can originate either from inflamed veins (thrombophlebitis) or non-inflamed veins with stasis (phlebothrombosis).

---

### Q13: Types of fat embolism:-
- **A)** Venous.
- **B)** Arterial.
- **C)** A and b.
- **D)** None of the above.

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Traumatic fat embolism from long bone fractures can lodge in the pulmonary vascular bed (venous/pulmonary fat embolism) or pass into the systemic arterial circulation affecting brain, kidneys, and skin (arterial/systemic fat embolism).

---

### Q14: Wallace classification of burn depend on:-
- **A)** Degree.
- **B)** Body surface area.
- **C)** Clinical prognosis.
- **D)** All of the above.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Wallace's Rule of Nines is used to estimate the percentage of total body surface area (BSA) involved in burns.

---

### Q15: Mode of death due to burn:-
- **A)** Most commonly accidental.
- **B)** Most commonly suicidal.
- **C)** Most commonly homicidal.
- **D)** None of the above.

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Accidental burning (domestic, industrial, scalding) is by far the most frequent manner/mode of death in burns.

---

### Q16: All of the following are condition simulating rigor mortis except:-
- **A)** Cadaveric spasm.
- **B)** Hot stiffness.
- **C)** Cold stiffness.
- **D)** None of the above.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Cadaveric spasm, heat stiffness (heat rigor due to protein coagulation), and cold stiffness (freezing of body fluids) are all recognized conditions simulating true postmortem rigor mortis.

---

### Q17: Sure signs of drowning are:-
- **A)** Presence of froth.
- **B)** Presence of cadaveric spasm.
- **C)** All of the above.
- **D)** None of the above.

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Persistent fine white froth at mouth/nostrils and cadaveric spasm clutching water-bed debris are definitive ante-mortem signs confirming death occurred by drowning.

---

### Q18: Consent should be valid in rape:-
- **A)** If the age above 18.
- **B)** If she is sane.
- **C)** If she is Awake.
- **D)** All of the above.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Valid legal consent requires that the female is of legal age (>=18), mentally competent (sane), and conscious/awake without fear, coercion, or intoxication.

---

### Q19: Sure signs of virginity are :-
- **A)** Intact hymen.
- **B)** Intact posterior commisure.
- **C)** Intact fourchette.
- **D)** All of the above.

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** An intact, normal hymenal membrane with regular margins is the sole definitive physical proof of female virginity.

---

### Q20: In child abuse syndrome may:-
- **A)** Physical.
- **B)** Mental.
- **C)** Sexual.
- **D)** All of the above.

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Child abuse syndrome (battered child syndrome) may manifest as physical trauma, emotional/mental deprivation, and sexual abuse.
