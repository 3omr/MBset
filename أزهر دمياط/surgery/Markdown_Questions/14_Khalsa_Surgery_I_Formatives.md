### Q1: Which of the following is true regarding the fibroblastic phase of wound healing:

- **A)** Early during wound healing, the predominant composition of the matrix is fibronectin and type II collagen
- **B)** After complete replacement of the scar with type III collagen, the mechanical strength will equal that of uninjured tissue approximately 6 to 12 months post injury

**Correct Answer:** C
**Type:** QCS
**Answer Source:** derived

- **B)** Calcium
- **C)** Sodium
- **D)** Potassium

**Correct Answer:** D
**Type:** QCS
**Answer Source:** derived
**Year:** None
**Tag:** `External, Khalsa`
**Explanation:** Potassium (K+) is the principal intracellular cation (concentration ~140-150 mEq/L), whereas sodium (Na+) is the primary extracellular cation.

### Q54: Regarding hypokalaemia, all the following statements are true, except:

- **A)** Serum potassium of 2. 5 mmol/L is hypokalaemia
- **B)** Hypokalaemia causes acidosis
- **C)** Severe diarrhoea may cause hypokalaemia
- **D)** Hypokalaemia causes muscle weakness
- **E)** Hypokalaemia causes paralytic ileus

**Correct Answer:** B
**Type:** QCS
**Answer Source:** derived
**Year:** None
**Tag:** `External, Khalsa`
**Explanation:** Hypokalemia causes metabolic alkalosis (not acidosis), as hydrogen ions shift intracellularly in exchange for potassium ions, and renal tubules excrete H+ to conserve K+.

### Q56: A hard mass in the testes in a 30-year-old male is best diagnosed by:

- **A)** Core needle biopsy
- **B)** Fine needle aspiration cytology
- **C)** Orchiectomy
- **D)** Open biopsy and frozen section

**Correct Answer:** D
**Type:** QCS
**Answer Source:** derived
**Year:** None
**Tag:** `External, Khalsa`
**Explanation:** Suspected testicular tumors must NEVER be biopsied transscrotally due to tumor spillage risks into inguinal lymphatics; definitive management and histological diagnosis is via radical inguinal orchiectomy (or high inguinal exposure with clamping and frozen section).
