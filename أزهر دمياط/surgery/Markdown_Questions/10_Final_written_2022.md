### Q1: Factors affecting wound healing

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Factors affecting wound healing include general and local factors. General factors are older age, malnutrition (including deficiencies of vitamins A and C, protein, or zinc), chronic disease such as diabetes or cancer, corticosteroid therapy, and irradiation. Local factors include reduced vascularity, excessive wound tension, immobilization, foreign bodies or necrotic tissue, infection, and poor tissue apposition or adherence to an underlying bony surface.

### Q2: Summarize ttt of hypovolemic shock

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Treat hypovolemic shock with immediate ABC resuscitation: secure the airway, support breathing and give oxygen, and control external bleeding while restoring circulation. Establish large-bore IV access, send blood for investigations and cross-match, and give warmed isotonic crystalloid while arranging early blood products for ongoing major hemorrhage. Identify and control the cause, including urgent exploration and repair of bleeding when indicated; immobilize associated fractures and keep the patient warm. Monitor pulse, blood pressure, temperature, mental state, urine output, blood gases and, when indicated, central venous pressure. Avoid fluid overload and use inotropes only when appropriate after adequate volume resuscitation.

### Q3: General complications of burn

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Major burns can cause hypovolemic shock and multisystem complications. Respiratory complications include inhalational injury and acute respiratory distress syndrome; renal complications include acute kidney injury; gastrointestinal complications include stress ulceration and paralytic ileus. Cardiovascular depression may reduce cardiac output. Extensive burns can cause anemia and hypoproteinemia, while the endocrine stress response increases catecholamines, cortisol and antidiuretic hormone. Infection and sepsis are important later complications.

### Q4: Complications of blood transfusion

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Transfusion complications may affect the donor or recipient. Donor complications include vasovagal or neurogenic symptoms, anemia after excessive donation, and local thrombophlebitis. Immediate recipient complications include circulatory overload, acute hemolytic reaction, febrile or pyrogenic reaction, allergic reaction, thrombophlebitis and air embolism. Massive transfusion may cause hypothermia, arrhythmias, citrate-related hypocalcemia, metabolic acidosis, coagulopathy, hyperkalemia, pulmonary microembolism and reduced oxygen-carrying capacity. Delayed complications include transfusion-transmitted infections such as hepatitis B or C, HIV, syphilis, brucellosis and malaria.

### Q5: Compare between subcutaneous lipoma and sebaceous cyst

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** A subcutaneous lipoma is a benign adipose tumor, usually a soft, lobulated, mobile, painless mass with a slippery edge; it may be attached to the skin at multiple points and is generally superficial to muscle. Treatment is observation or enucleation/excision when symptomatic, enlarging or diagnostically uncertain. A sebaceous (epidermoid) cyst is a small, well-defined cystic swelling attached to the skin at a punctum, often containing keratin. It may become infected and suppurate. Definitive treatment is complete excision of the cyst wall when inflammation has settled; drain an acute abscess if present.

### Q6: Compare between acute lactitional mastitis & mastitis carcinomatosis

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Acute lactational mastitis has an acute onset and rapidly progressive course, usually with high fever, fiery red overlying skin, marked tenderness and firm tender axillary nodes. It generally improves with appropriate antibiotics and supportive care. Mastitis carcinomatosa has a gradual onset and slowly progressive course, often with low-grade fever, dusky red skin, mild tenderness and hard, non-tender axillary nodes. It does not respond to antibiotics and requires urgent assessment and treatment as inflammatory breast cancer.

### Q7: DD of midline neck swellings

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Differential diagnoses of a midline neck swelling include solid and cystic lesions. Solid causes include an enlarged submental or pretracheal/prelaryngeal lymph node and a thyroid isthmus nodule. Cystic causes include a thyroglossal duct cyst, dermoid cyst, cold abscess, subhyoid bursitis, laryngocele and a cystic lesion of the thyroid isthmus. A thyroglossal duct cyst typically moves with swallowing and protrusion of the tongue; examination and targeted neck imaging help distinguish the causes.

### Q8: DD of acute scrotal swellings

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Differential diagnoses of acute scrotal swelling include testicular torsion, acute epididymo-orchitis, hematocele, pyocele, acute funiculitis and a strangulated oblique inguinal hernia. Testicular torsion is a time-critical diagnosis and must be excluded urgently; sudden severe pain, a high-riding testis and an absent cremasteric reflex support it. Clinical examination and Doppler ultrasonography can help when they do not delay necessary exploration.

### Q9: DD of tongue ulcers

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Differential diagnoses of tongue ulcers include traumatic, inflammatory, infectious, premalignant and malignant lesions. Traumatic causes include a frenular ulcer after local trauma and an ulcer from a sharp tooth. Inflammatory or infectious causes include aphthous or dyspeptic ulcers, multiple small herpetic ulcers, oral lichen planus, tuberculosis, syphilis and chronic superficial glossitis. Premalignant lesions include leukoplakia and erythroplakia; benign lesions include papilloma. A persistent ulcer, especially on the lateral tongue, an indurated or raised ulcer, or an ulcer with cervical nodes requires biopsy to exclude squamous cell carcinoma.

### Q10: CP of DVT

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Deep vein thrombosis may be asymptomatic or present with unilateral leg pain, swelling and tenderness. Examination may show warmth, erythema, pitting edema and dilated superficial veins. Compare both limbs and assess for risk factors and signs of pulmonary embolism. Homan sign is unreliable and should not be used to exclude DVT; confirm suspected disease with a validated clinical assessment and venous duplex ultrasonography.

### Q11: Hard signs of vascular injury

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Hard signs of arterial injury include active or pulsatile external bleeding, an expanding or pulsatile hematoma, a palpable thrill or bruit, and distal ischemia with absent pulses. Ischemic findings include pallor, pain, progressive coldness, paresthesia and paralysis. These findings warrant urgent vascular assessment and operative control or repair as indicated.

### Q12: Importance of preoperative laboratory investigations

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Preoperative laboratory investigations help identify medical conditions that require treatment before surgery and reduce avoidable perioperative morbidity and mortality. They provide a baseline and help assess anemia, bleeding risk, renal or metabolic problems and other relevant comorbidity. The findings also help the team anticipate anesthesia-specific risks such as airway problems, malignant hyperthermia, nausea and vomiting, or postoperative delirium. The preoperative assessment should explain material risks to the patient and support informed consent.

### Q14: Contents of femoral sheath

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** key
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** The femoral sheath is formed anteriorly by transversalis fascia and posteriorly by iliac fascia. It contains three compartments: the lateral compartment contains the femoral artery, the intermediate compartment contains the femoral vein, and the medial compartment is the femoral canal, which contains loose connective tissue, lymphatics and the deep inguinal node of Cloquet. The femoral nerve lies outside the sheath, lateral to it.
