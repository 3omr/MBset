### Q1: Describe the mechanics and protective mechanisms of the inguinal canal.

**Model Answer:** 1) Obliquity of the canal: The deep and superficial rings do not lie opposite each other (separated by 4 cm), so increased intra-abdominal pressure presses the anterior and posterior walls together like a valve. 2) Shutter mechanism of the conjoint tendon (internal oblique and transversus abdominis arches): Contraction pulls the arch down towards the inguinal ligament, closing the canal. 3) Ball-valve mechanism of the cremaster muscle: Contracts and pulls the spermatic cord and testis up, plugging the superficial ring. 4) Slit-valve mechanism of external oblique: Contraction of crus fibers narrows the superficial inguinal ring.

### Q2: Enumerate the risk factors for deep vein thrombosis (DVT) (Virchow's Triad).

**Model Answer:** Virchow's Triad: 1) Stasis of blood flow: Prolonged immobilization, bed rest >3 days, major surgery (>30 min, especially pelvic, hip, or knee arthroplasty), long air travel, stroke, CHF. 2) Endothelial / Vessel wall injury: Trauma, fractures, surgical manipulation, intravenous cannulae, vasculitis. 3) Hypercoagulability: Malignancy, pregnancy and postpartum, oral contraceptive pills and HRT, obesity, nephrotic syndrome, inherited thrombophilias (Factor V Leiden, Prothrombin G20210A, Antithrombin III deficiency, Protein C/S deficiency, Antiphospholipid syndrome).

### Q3: Describe the primary survey of trauma in a patient with maxillofacial trauma.

**Model Answer:** Follow ATLS protocol: A - Airway with cervical spine protection: Assess airway patency, suction blood, vomit, teeth, and bone fragments; jaw thrust (avoid chin lift/neck extension); maintain in-line spinal immobilization; if airway compromised by massive hemorrhage or edema, perform emergency definitive airway (endotracheal intubation, cricothyroidotomy). B - Breathing: Assess chest expansion, RR, auscultate breath sounds, give 100% high-flow oxygen, rule out tension pneumothorax or flail chest. C - Circulation with hemorrhage control: Direct pressure to bleeding facial wounds, pack oral/nasal cavities if needed, establish two large-bore IV lines (16G), fluid resuscitation with warmed crystalloids. D - Disability: Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), pupillary size and reactivity. E - Exposure / Environment: Undress completely to assess other injuries, keep patient warm to prevent hypothermia.

### Q5: Enumerate the types and differential diagnosis of dermoid cysts.

**Model Answer:** Types: 1) Congenital dermoid cyst: Sequestration dermoid (occurs at lines of embryonic fusion, e. g. outer angle of orbit, midline of neck/sternum), Tubo-dermoid (e. g. thyroglossal cyst), Teratomatous dermoid (ovary, testis). 2) Acquired dermoid cyst (Implantation dermoid): Caused by puncture wound driving epidermal cells into deeper tissue (e. g. tailor, gardener, fingers/palms). Differential diagnosis: Sebaceous cyst, subcutaneous lipoma, neurofibroma, ganglion, cold abscess, mucocele, hemangioma.

### Q7: Classifications of hand infections.

**Model Answer:** 1) Superficial infections: Paronychia (infection of nail fold), Subungual hematoma/abscess, Apical space infection, Carbuncle/furuncle of dorsum. 2) Deep fascial space infections: Pulp space infection (Whitlow / Felon), Thenar space infection, Midpalmar space infection, Hypothenar space infection, Web space infection (collar-button abscess). 3) Tendon sheath infections: Acute suppurative tenosynovitis (Kanavel's four signs), Ulnar bursa infection, Radial bursa infection (horseshoe abscess). 4) Deep tissue/bone: Osteomyelitis, septic arthritis, necrotizing fasciitis.
