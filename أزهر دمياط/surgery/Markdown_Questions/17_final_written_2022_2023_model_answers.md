### Q2: Risk factors for deep vein thrombosis (DVT).

### Q6: Pathogenesis of dry gangrene, line of demarcation, and causes of hematuria.

**Model Answer:** Dry gangrene: Gradual progressive arterial occlusion in presence of intact venous drainage, typically in distal extremities; tissue becomes mummified, black, dry, and shriveled. Line of demarcation: Inflammatory zone of hyperemic granulation tissue between viable tissue and dead gangrenous tissue. Hematuria causes: Renal (stones, glomerulonephritis, trauma, RCC), Ureteric (stones, stricture), Bladder (cystitis, transitional cell carcinoma, schistosomiasis, trauma), Prostatic (BPH, prostate cancer), Urethral (urethritis, trauma).

### Q9: Criteria of severe burns and treatment of major burns.

**Model Answer:** Severe burn criteria: >20% TBSA partial thickness in adults (>10% in children/elderly), >5% full-thickness burns, burns involving face, hands, feet, genitalia, perineum, or major joints, inhalation injury, high-voltage electrical burns, burns with major trauma. Treatment: ABCs, Parkland formula (4 mL x kg x %TBSA Ringer's lactate in first 24h, half in first 8h), Foley catheter monitoring urine output (0. 5-1 mL/kg/h), analgesia, tetanus prophylaxis, wound care with topical antimicrobials (silver sulfadiazine), early excision and skin grafting.

### Q10: Comparison between subcutaneous lipoma and sebaceous cyst.

**Model Answer:** Lipoma: Adipose tissue origin, soft lobulated swelling, slippery edge, overlying skin normal without punctum, free from skin, simple enucleation. Sebaceous cyst: Epidermal retention origin, smooth round cystic swelling, attached to skin at punctum (black dot), no slippery edge, prone to abscess formation, excised with skin ellipse containing punctum.

### Q16: Classification of surgical wounds.

**Model Answer:** 1) Clean (Class I): Uninfected, no inflammation, respiratory/alimentary/genital/urinary tract not entered (infection rate <2%, e. g. hernia repair, thyroidectomy). 2) Clean-contaminated (Class II): Viscus entered under controlled conditions without unusual contamination (infection rate <10%, e. g. elective cholecystectomy, gastric surgery). 3) Contaminated (Class III): Open fresh accidental wounds, gross spillage from GI tract, acute non-purulent inflammation (infection rate 15-20%, e. g. acute cholecystitis, appendicitis). 4) Dirty-infected (Class IV): Old traumatic wounds with devitalized tissue, existing clinical infection or perforated viscera (infection rate >30%, e. g. peritonitis from perforated diverticulitis, drainage of abscess).
