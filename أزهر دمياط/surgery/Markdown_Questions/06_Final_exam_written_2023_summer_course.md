### Q1: Male patient 25y old, presented to emergency room with kitchen knife stabbed in right calf muscle, with 2000ml blood loss. (4 marks) a) Enumerate precautions of blood transfusion? b) What are TYPES of shock can be expected for this patient. Mention management of one of them? c) What are manifestations of tetanus infection for this patient?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Treat hemorrhagic shock from blood loss: control bleeding, give oxygen, establish large-bore IV access, send blood for grouping and crossmatch, and resuscitate with warmed blood products as indicated. Before transfusion, confirm patient identity and compatibility, check the unit and expiry, use a suitable filter and line, record baseline observations, begin slowly, and monitor for reactions. Other possible shock types include septic shock if the wound becomes infected and neurogenic shock if there is associated injury. Tetanus may cause trismus, risus sardonicus, neck and abdominal rigidity, painful stimulus-induced generalized spasms, opisthotonus, dysphagia, and autonomic instability.

### Q2: As regard cancer; What are? (4 marks) a) Definition b) Etiology c) Comparison between benign and malignant tumors d) Diagnosis

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Cancer is a clonal malignant neoplasm caused by uncontrolled proliferation of abnormal cells, with invasion of surrounding tissue and potential metastasis. Etiology includes tobacco and alcohol, chemical carcinogens, ionizing or ultraviolet radiation, oncogenic infections, inherited cancer-predisposition mutations, chronic inflammation, and hormonal or lifestyle factors. Compared with benign tumors, malignant tumors are less circumscribed, invade adjacent tissue, may recur, show atypia and abnormal mitoses, and can metastasize. Diagnosis uses history and examination, appropriate imaging and endoscopy, and tissue confirmation by biopsy with histopathology; staging investigations are then selected according to the suspected primary site.

### Q3: Male patient 50y old, smoker, presented with cervical lymph node swelling. How can you approach diagnosis? (4 marks)

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Take a history of duration and progression, pain, fever, weight loss, night sweats, tobacco and alcohol exposure, previous malignancy, and ear, nose, throat or respiratory symptoms. Examine all cervical nodes, the oral cavity, oropharynx, larynx, thyroid, skin and other nodal areas, and look for a primary tumor. Investigate with blood tests as indicated, ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration or core biopsy, and imaging of the neck and chest; perform directed endoscopy or panendoscopy when an upper aerodigestive primary is suspected. If needle sampling is nondiagnostic, obtain an excisional biopsy and arrange staging based on histology.

### Q4: Obese, diabetic female patient 50y old, past history of right modified mastectomy, presented with right upper limb edema (4 marks): (a) Enumerate expected causes? (b) Mention approach to diagnosis?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Expected causes include post-mastectomy lymphatic disruption or radiotherapy causing lymphedema, recurrent tumor or axillary nodal disease obstructing lymphatics or veins, upper-limb deep venous thrombosis, cellulitis, and less commonly trauma or systemic fluid-retaining disease. Assess onset, progression, pain, fever, venous catheter history and cancer treatment; compare both arms and examine the skin, pulses, veins, axilla and chest wall for infection or recurrence. Measure arm circumference, request duplex ultrasound if thrombosis is possible, and investigate suspected recurrence with breast or axillary imaging and biopsy of a suspicious lesion. Use blood tests and cardiac, renal or hepatic assessment when systemic edema is suspected.

### Q5: Enumerate (4 marks): (a) Causes of incisional hernia? (b) Complications of hernia?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Causes of incisional hernia include wound infection, inadequate closure or early suture failure, emergency surgery, poor tissue healing, obesity, malnutrition, diabetes, smoking, steroid use, and raised intra-abdominal pressure from coughing, constipation, ascites or heavy straining. Complications include enlargement, pain, irreducibility, intestinal obstruction, strangulation with ischemia or gangrene, and skin ulceration or breakdown.

### Q7: Male patient 40 y old, presented with sulphoric acid injury to the face and neck (3 marks) a) What are the first aid management? b) Enumerate other indications of admission to hospital of burned patient?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Protect rescuers, remove contaminated clothing and jewelry, brush away dry chemical, then irrigate the skin and eyes copiously with clean running water; do not attempt chemical neutralization. Assess airway, breathing and circulation, provide analgesia, cover the irrigated burn with a clean dry sheet, and arrange urgent burn care. Other reasons for hospital admission include a large or deep burn; burns involving the eyes, hands, feet, genitalia, perineum or major joints; suspected inhalation injury; electrical or chemical burns; circumferential burns; associated trauma or significant comorbidity; uncontrolled pain; or inadequate home support.

### Q8: As regard breast abscess, mention? (a) Diagnosis (b) Differential diagnosis (c) Treatment

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Breast abscess is diagnosed from a painful, tender, fluctuant breast mass, often with erythema and fever; use ultrasound to confirm a collection and guide aspiration, and culture aspirated pus. Differential diagnoses include mastitis without abscess, an infected cyst, galactocele, and inflammatory breast carcinoma, especially when findings persist or are atypical. Treat with analgesia, continued breast emptying or feeding when appropriate, and antibiotics covering likely staphylococci; drain the collection by ultrasound-guided needle aspiration or incision and drainage when large, loculated, or not resolving, and reassess to exclude an underlying malignancy.
