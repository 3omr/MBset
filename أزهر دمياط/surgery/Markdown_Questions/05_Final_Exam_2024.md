### Q1: What are the general principles of surgery for hand infection?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Principles are elevation and splintage of the hand, analgesia, removal of rings, assessment of neurovascular status, and prompt antibiotics when cellulitis or systemic infection is present. Obtain cultures when feasible. Drain any abscess promptly, debride necrotic tissue, remove a foreign body, and treat the involved space or tendon sheath; do not delay surgery for deep-space infection or flexor tenosynovitis. Check tetanus status and begin early hand therapy once acute infection is controlled.

### Q2: Compare between benign and malignant lesions?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Benign lesions are usually slow-growing, well circumscribed or encapsulated, expansile rather than invasive, and do not metastasize; their cells are well differentiated and recurrence after complete excision is uncommon. Malignant lesions tend to grow rapidly, infiltrate and destroy adjacent tissues, show atypia and poor differentiation, may recur after excision, and can spread through lymphatics or blood. Exceptions occur, so histopathology is definitive.

### Q3: Enumerate causes of salivary gland swellings?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Salivary-gland swellings may be inflammatory or infective (acute bacterial sialadenitis, viral mumps, chronic sialadenitis, Sjogren disease); obstructive (sialolithiasis or duct stenosis); neoplastic (benign pleomorphic adenoma/Warthin tumor or malignant salivary tumors); cystic (retention cyst or ranula); or systemic/non-neoplastic (HIV-associated disease, sarcoidosis, and sialadenosis associated with diabetes, alcohol use, or malnutrition). Regional lymph nodes and adjacent-organ lesions can mimic a gland swelling.

### Q4: Enumerate general and local complications of burn?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Local complications include burn-wound infection, progressive tissue necrosis, edema, painful scarring, hypertrophic scars and contractures; circumferential burns may cause limb ischemia or restrict chest expansion, and facial or inhalational injury may obstruct the airway. General complications include burn shock and fluid/electrolyte loss, hypothermia, acute kidney injury, respiratory failure/ARDS, sepsis, stress ulceration, hypermetabolism and catabolism, and multiorgan failure.

### Q5: A 50-year-old female with diabetes mellitus had hysterectomy 4 years ago. She now has reducible swelling with impulse with cough at the site of the hysterectomy scar. Enumerate predisposing factors and treatment?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** This is an incisional hernia. Predisposing factors include wound infection or poor closure, obesity, diabetes, malnutrition, smoking, steroid or immunosuppressive therapy, increasing age, emergency surgery, and raised intra-abdominal pressure from cough, constipation, ascites, or urinary obstruction. Optimize weight, nutrition, glucose control, smoking, and cough/constipation before elective repair. Repair the defect, usually with mesh reinforcement, by an appropriate open or laparoscopic approach; urgent operation is required if obstruction or strangulation develops.

### Q6: A 30-year-old male presents with trismus. He gives history of trauma to lefi foot few days ago. What are your management?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Trismus several days after a contaminated foot wound is generalized tetanus until proved otherwise. Admit to intensive care with a low-stimulation environment, monitor and protect the airway, control spasms (usually benzodiazepines, with ventilation if needed), debride and clean the wound, and give human tetanus immune globulin (typically 500 IU IM for treatment) plus metronidazole. Start an active tetanus-toxoid-containing vaccine at a separate site because tetanus does not confer immunity. Treat autonomic instability and provide nutrition and supportive care.

### Q7: Male child 10-year-old presented with reddish edematous painful scrotal swelling. Write expected history and examination to reach the diagnosis? What is treatment of the most dangerous cause?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Take the onset and duration of pain, trauma, fever, nausea/vomiting, urinary symptoms, prior episodes, and vaccination/sexual history; examine both testes for lie, tenderness, swelling, cremasteric reflex, inguinal hernia, and abdominal findings. Sudden severe pain, nausea/vomiting, a high or transverse testis, and an absent cremasteric reflex suggest torsion. Testicular torsion is the most dangerous cause: obtain immediate urologic assessment and urgent scrotal exploration with detorsion and bilateral orchidopexy; Doppler ultrasound may help if diagnosis is uncertain but must not delay surgery.

### Q8: Describe diagnosis and treatment offearly breast cancer?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Diagnose by triple assessment: history and examination of both breasts and regional nodes, age-appropriate imaging (diagnostic mammography with targeted ultrasound, with MRI for selected indications), and image-guided core-needle biopsy for histology and ER/PR/HER2 testing. Stage the disease and discuss at a multidisciplinary team. For operable early disease, offer breast-conserving surgery with clear margins plus radiotherapy when suitable, or mastectomy when indicated; assess the axilla, usually with sentinel-node biopsy if clinically node-negative. Add endocrine, HER2-targeted, and/or chemotherapy according to stage, pathology, and biomarkers.

### Q9: Mention differential diagnosis, investigations of cervical lymphadenopathy and treatment of one of them?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Differential diagnosis includes reactive or bacterial lymphadenitis, tuberculosis and other granulomatous infection, lymphoma, metastatic head-and-neck or thyroid cancer, and less often congenital cysts or salivary/thyroid lesions. Investigate with history and full head-and-neck examination, CBC and targeted infection tests, ultrasound, and needle sampling (FNAC or core biopsy); use CT/MRI and chest imaging when indicated, and excisional biopsy if lymphoma is suspected or needle sampling is nondiagnostic. For confirmed tuberculous lymphadenitis, treat with a full guideline-based multidrug anti-tuberculous regimen and monitor response.

### Q10: Compare between arterial thrombosis and arterial embolism?

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Arterial embolism is usually sudden, with abrupt severe ischemia in a previously asymptomatic limb; pulses may be normal in the other limb, and a source such as atrial fibrillation or recent myocardial infarction may be found. Thrombosis usually occurs in a limb with pre-existing atherosclerotic disease or chronic ischemic symptoms; pulses may be reduced in both limbs and collaterals may make onset less abrupt. Both require urgent limb-viability assessment, analgesia and immediate IV unfractionated heparin unless contraindicated, followed by revascularization. Embolic occlusion is often treated by embolectomy; thrombosis may require thrombectomy, bypass, or endovascular treatment of the underlying lesion.
