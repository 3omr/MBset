### Q1: Mechanics of inguinal canal.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** The inguinal canal is oblique, and its deep and superficial rings do not lie opposite one another, so raised intra-abdominal pressure does not act directly on the rings. Contraction of the internal oblique and transversus abdominis lowers the conjoint tendon like a shutter toward the inguinal ligament, narrowing the canal. The posterior wall is reinforced medially by the conjoint tendon, while the cremasteric and transversalis-fascia sling mechanisms help close the deep ring during straining.

### Q2: Risk factor of DVT.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** DVT risk follows Virchow's triad: venous stasis (immobility, bed rest, long travel, heart failure), endothelial injury (surgery, trauma, venous catheters), and hypercoagulability (malignancy, pregnancy or estrogen therapy, thrombophilia, prior VTE). Increasing age, obesity, smoking, and dehydration add risk.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Use the trauma primary survey: A, secure the airway while protecting the cervical spine; with major maxillofacial trauma, suction blood and debris and prepare early for a definitive airway, including a surgical airway if necessary. B, assess and treat life-threatening breathing problems. C, control hemorrhage and restore circulation with large-bore IV access and blood products when indicated. ### Q5: Types and D.D of dermoid cyst.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Dermoid-type cysts are classified histologically as epidermoid, true dermoid (with skin appendages), and teratoid cysts; they may be congenital inclusion cysts or acquired implantation cysts. The differential depends on site and includes an epidermal/sebaceous cyst, thyroglossal duct cyst, branchial cyst, ranula, cystic hygroma, lipoma, and an enlarged lymph node.

### Q7: Classifications of Hand infection.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Hand infections are classified by site: superficial infections include paronychia, felon (pulp-space infection), and cellulitis; deep infections include pyogenic flexor tenosynovitis, web-space (collar-button) abscess, thenar or mid-palmar space abscess, septic arthritis, and osteomyelitis. Bite wounds and infected wounds may involve more than one compartment.

### Q8: Risk factors of cancer breast.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Important breast-cancer risk factors are female sex and increasing age; a personal history of breast cancer or high-risk lesions; family history and pathogenic BRCA1/BRCA2 or related variants; prior chest irradiation; prolonged estrogen exposure (early menarche, late menopause, nulliparity or late first birth); alcohol use; postmenopausal obesity; and combined menopausal hormone therapy. Risk is reduced by earlier first birth and breastfeeding.

### Q9: Complications of wound healing.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Complications of wound healing include infection, hematoma or seroma, delayed healing, wound dehiscence or incisional hernia, chronic sinus or fistula, hypertrophic scar or keloid, contracture, adhesions, and chronic ulceration. They may result from poor perfusion, infection, excessive tension, malnutrition, diabetes, smoking, or immunosuppression.

### Q10: Complications of hernia.

**Correct Answer:** -
**Answer Source:** derived
**Source Pages:** 1
**EXP:** Hernia complications include irreducibility (incarceration), intestinal obstruction, strangulation with ischemia or gangrene, inflammation of the sac or contents, and trauma or ulceration of the overlying skin.
