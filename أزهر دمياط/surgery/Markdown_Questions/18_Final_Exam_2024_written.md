### Q1: What are the general principles of surgery for hand infections?

**Model Answer:** 1) Early surgical drainage under general anesthesia or regional block (never local infiltration to prevent spread). 2) Exsanguinating tourniquet for a bloodless operating field. 3) Incisions must never cross flexor skin creases perpendicularly (use mid-lateral or zigzag Brunner incisions). 4) Thorough debridement of all necrotic tissue and pus collection. 5) Culture and sensitivity swab from pus. 6) Postoperative elevation and immobilization in position of function / safe position. 7) Targeted empirical IV antibiotic therapy. 8) Early active physiotherapy once acute inflammation resolves to prevent joint stiffness.

### Q2: Compare between benign and malignant lesions.

**Model Answer:** Benign lesions: Slow rate of growth, encapsulated or well-circumscribed, non-invasive (pushes surrounding tissue), cells closely resemble normal parent tissue (well-differentiated), normal mitotic figures, absence of necrosis or hemorrhage, never metastasize, amenable to local excision with cure. Malignant lesions: Rapid progressive growth, non-encapsulated with ill-defined infiltrative margins, locally invasive and destructive, cellular pleomorphism, nuclear hyperchromatism, high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio, abnormal and atypical mitoses, central necrosis and hemorrhage, capacity for regional lymph node and distant hematogenous metastasis, high risk of recurrence unless radical clearance achieved.

### Q3: Enumerate causes of salivary gland swellings.

**Model Answer:** 1) Inflammatory / Infective: Acute suppurative sialadenitis (Staph aureus), viral sialadenitis (mumps, CMV), chronic sialadenitis, tuberculosis, actinomycosis. 2) Obstructive: Sialolithiasis (salivary stones, commonest in submandibular gland), duct stricture, mucus retention cyst (ranula). 3) Benign neoplasms: Pleomorphic adenoma (commonest), Warthin's tumor (adenolymphoma), monomorphic adenoma, hemangioma. 4) Malignant neoplasms: Mucoepidermoid carcinoma, adenoid cystic carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, malignant mixed tumor. 5) Autoimmune / Systemic: Sjogren's syndrome, Mikulicz syndrome, sarcoidosis. 6) Sialosis (metabolic / nutritional non-inflammatory enlargement, e. g. alcoholism, diabetes).

### Q4: Enumerate general and local complications of burns.

**Model Answer:** General: Burn shock (hypovolemic), inhalation injury and acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), burn wound sepsis, acute kidney injury (ATN from hypoperfusion/myoglobinuria), Curling's stress ulcer, paralytic ileus, hypermetabolic catabolic state. Local: Burn wound infection, conversion to full-thickness necrosis, compartment syndrome from circumferential eschar, hypertrophic scars, keloids, joint contractures, Marjolin's ulcer (squamous cell carcinoma).

### Q5: A 50-year-old female with diabetes mellitus had a hysterectomy 4 years ago. She now has a reducible swelling with an impulse on cough at the site of the hysterectomy scar. Enumerate the predisposing factors and treatment.

**Model Answer:** Diagnosis: Incisional hernia. Predisposing factors: Patient factors: uncontrolled diabetes mellitus (impaired collagen synthesis/healing), obesity, increased intra-abdominal pressure (chronic cough, constipation), poor nutritional status. Surgical/Wound factors: postoperative surgical site infection (most important factor), wound dehiscence/hematoma, inappropriate suture technique/material, improper closure under tension. Treatment: 1) Preoperative preparation: optimize glycemic control, weight reduction, treat chronic cough/constipation, stop smoking. 2) Surgical repair: Open or laparoscopic mesh repair (sublay / retromuscular mesh repair or onlay / preperitoneal) using polypropylene mesh with at least 5 cm overlap beyond the defect margins. Direct suture repair alone has a high recurrence rate and should be avoided.

### Q6: A 30-year-old male presents with trismus (lockjaw). He gives a history of trauma to his left foot a few days ago. Outline the diagnosis and management.

**Model Answer:** Diagnosis: Tetanus (caused by Clostridium tetani neurotoxin, tetanospasmin). Management: 1) Admit to quiet, dark intensive care unit (ICU) to minimize sensory stimulation and spasms. 2) Airway protection: early endotracheal intubation or tracheostomy and mechanical ventilation for severe spasms. 3) Neutralize unbound toxin: administer Human Tetanus Immunoglobulin (HTIG) 3, 000-6, 000 IU IM. 4) Eradicate organism: IV Metronidazole (preferred) or Penicillin G for 10-14 days. 5) Wound debridement: thoroughly debride and excise the foot wound after antitoxin administration. 6) Control spasms and rigidity: IV Benzodiazepines (Diazepam / Midazolam), neuromuscular blockade if needed. 7) Autonomic dysfunction control: IV labetalol or magnesium sulfate. 8) Active immunization: administer full tetanus toxoid vaccination course (tetanus disease does not confer immunity).
