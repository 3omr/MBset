Renal Histology Written Questions 2026
B. Answer the following questions:
1.​ Discuss the histological structure of the renal cortex.​
Consists primarily of renal corpuscles and convoluted tubules:
a.​ Cortical Labyrinth surrounding medullary rays.
b.​ Renal columns of Bertini between adjacent renal pyramids.
2.​ Classify nephrons and describe their differences.
a.​ Cortical: renal corpuscle located in the outer cortex.
b.​ Juxtamedullary: renal corpuscle located in the inner cortex or corticomedullary
junction.
3.​ Describe the structure of the renal corpuscle.
a.​ A rounded structure consisting of the glomerulus and the Bowman’s capsule.
b.​ Each one has two poles: the vascular and the urinary.
4.​ Explain the structure of podocytes.
a.​ Modified simple squamous epithelium in the visceral layer of the Bowman’s
capsule.
b.​ Gives rise to primary processes and secondary processes (or pedicles, which
cover much of the capillary surface).
c.​ Contain actin microfilaments, making them contractile.
5.​ Define the renal filtration barrier and describe its components.
a.​ It is the barrier that separates the blood inside the glomerular capillaries from the
glomerular filtrate inside Bowman’s space.
b.​ Components:
i.​
Fenestrated endothelium of the glomerular capillaries.
ii.​
Basal lamina (lamina rara and lamina densa).
iii.​
Filtration slit diaphragm bridging the filtration slits between adjacent
pedicels.
6.​ Describe the histological structure of the proximal convoluted tubule.​
Proximal and Distal Convoluted Tubules.
7.​ Discuss the structural differences between the thin and thick segments of the loop
of Henle.
a.​ Thin segments:
i.​
The descending limb, the loop itself, and part of the ascending limb.
ii.​
Lined by low cuboidal or simple squamous cells.
iii.​
Few organelles indicating a passive role in ionic movement.
b.​ Thick segments:
i.​
The rest of the ascending limb.
ii.​
Lined by cuboidal cells.
iii.​
Larger diameter and thicker wall.
8.​ Discuss the differences between cortical and medullary collecting tubules.
a.​ Cortical collecting tubules:

9.​ Discuss the structure of the juxtaglomerular apparatus.
a.​ Juxtaglomerular (JG) cells:
i.​
Modified smooth muscle cells mainly in the afferent arteriole but few in the
efferent.
ii.​
Show the following changes: rounded nuclei and PAS-positive secretory
granules (renin).
b.​ Extraglomerular mesangial cells (Lacis cells).
c.​ Macula densa:
i.​
Modified cells of DCT.
ii.​
Show the following changes: columnar cells, apical nuclei, basal Golgi
complexes, and no basement membrane.
10.​Describe the structure of the urinary bladder.
a.​ Mucosa: transitional epithelium and CT corium (or lamina propria).
b.​ Musculosa: inner longitudinal, middle circular, and outer longitudinal smooth
muscles.
c.​ Serous layer (superior surface covered by mesothelium of peritoneum) or
adventitia (inferior part) of fibroelastic CT carrying blood vessels, nerves, and
lymphatics.
11.​Describe the histological structure of the ureter.
a.​ Mucosa = urinary bladder (transitional epithelium and lamina propria) +
star-shaped lumen.
b.​ Musculosa:
i.​
Middle and lower parts = urinary bladder (inner longitudinal, middle
circular, and outer longitudinal smooth muscles).
ii.​
Upper part contains only two layers in the musculosa (inner longitudinal
and outer circular).
c.​ Adventitia = urinary bladder (of fibroelastic CT carrying blood vessels, nerves,
and lymphatics).
12.​List components of a nephron.
a.​ Renal corpuscle.
b.​ Proximal convoluted tubules.
c.​ Loop of Henle.
d.​ Distal convoluted tubules.
13.​Describe the structure of mesangial cells.​
Stellate-shaped cells with multiple processes. They have small, darkly stained nuclei and
basophilic cytoplasm rich in lysosomes.

C. Define the following structure:
1.​ Nephron.
a.​ The nephron is defined as the structural and functional unit of the kidney.
b.​ Consists of:
i.​
Renal corpuscle.
ii.​
Proximal convoluted tubules.
iii.​
Loop of Henle.
iv.​
Distal convoluted tubules.
2.​ Glomerulus.​
The glomerulus is a rounded tuft of blood capillaries inside the renal corpuscle.
3.​ Renal pelvis.​
The renal pelvis is the expanded upper end of the ureter located within the hilum of the
kidney. It divides into two or three major calyces.
4.​ Podocyte.
a.​ Modified simple squamous epithelium in the visceral layer of the Bowman’s
capsule.
b.​ Gives rise to primary processes and secondary processes (or pedicles, which
cover much of the capillary surface).
c.​ Contain actin microfilaments, making them contractile.
5.​ The juxtaglomerular apparatus.​
The JG apparatus is a structure located at the vascular pole of the renal corpuscle. It is
composed of three main parts:
a.​ Juxtaglomerular (JG) cells.
b.​ Extraglomerular mesangial cells (Lacis cells).
c.​ Macula densa.
6.​ Vasa recta.​
Long, thin vessels that arise from the efferent glomerular arterioles of juxtamedullary
nephrons.
7.​ Renal lobule.​
Central medullary ray and the closely associated cortical tissue on either side of it.
8.​ Renal pyramids.​
Conical structures forming the bulk of the renal medulla. Their bases are at the
corticomedullary junction, and their apex forms the renal papilla.
9.​ Columns of Bertini.​
Extensions of cortical tissue that project between adjacent renal pyramids.
10.​Blood renal barrier.​
It is the barrier that separates the blood inside the glomerular capillaries from the
glomerular filtrate inside Bowman’s space.

3.​ Compare the male and female urethra in terms of structure.
a.​ The male urethra consists of three segments: prostatic (lined by urothelium),
membranous (lined by stratified and pseudostratified columnar epithelium), and
penile (lined by stratified columnar and stratified squamous epithelium).
b.​ The female urethra is a 3- to 5-cm-long tube lined initially with transitional
epithelium that then transitions to non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium
continuous with skin.
