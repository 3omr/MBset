### Question 19

Enumerate the three primary types of dehydration.

**Correct Answer**: -
**EXP**: 1. Isotonic dehydration: Equal loss of water and electrolytes (serum osmolarity normal 280-295 mOsm/L).
2. Hypertonic dehydration: Water loss exceeds electrolyte loss (serum osmolarity > 295 mOsm/L, hypernatremia).
3. Hypotonic dehydration: Electrolyte loss exceeds water loss (serum osmolarity < 280 mOsm/L, hyponatremia).

### Question 20

Explain the difference between Osmolarity and Osmolality.

**Correct Answer**: -
**EXP**: - Osmolarity: The number of osmoles of solute per liter of solution (Osm/L). It is affected by temperature and pressure because liquid volume changes with temperature.
- Osmolality: The number of osmoles of solute per kilogram of solvent (Osm/kg H2O). It is independent of temperature and pressure, and is the standard physiological measurement in clinical medicine.

### Question 21

Explain why Methotrexate is used as an anti-cancer agent.

**Correct Answer**: -
**EXP**: Methotrexate is a folic acid structural analogue that competitively inhibits the enzyme dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR). This inhibition prevents the conversion of dihydrofolate to active tetrahydrofolate (THF), which is an essential one-carbon carrier required for de novo synthesis of thymidylate (dTMP) and purine nucleotides. Depletion of THF halts DNA and RNA synthesis, arresting rapidly dividing cancer cells in the S-phase and inducing apoptosis.

### Question 22

Define Gout.

**Correct Answer**: -
**EXP**: Gout is a metabolic disorder of purine metabolism characterized by chronic hyperuricemia (serum urate > 6.8 mg/dL) resulting in the precipitation and deposition of monosodium urate monohydrate crystals in synovial joints, periarticular tissues, and kidneys, leading to recurrent episodes of acute painful inflammatory monoarthritis and the formation of tophi.

### Question 23

Discuss the mechanism of the control of RBF and GFR when arterial blood pressure (ABP) increases from 100 to 180 mmHg by the tubuloglomerular feedback (TGF) mechanism.

**Correct Answer**: -
**EXP**: When arterial blood pressure rises from 100 to 180 mmHg, initial elevation in renal perfusion pressure tends to increase GFR. This delivers an increased volume of tubular fluid and NaCl to the loop of Henle and into the macula densa cells of the early distal tubule. The macula densa cells detect the increased luminal NaCl concentration via the Na+-K+-2Cl- cotransporter (NKCC2). Intracellular uptake of NaCl leads to ATP release and its extracellular breakdown into adenosine. Adenosine binds to A1 adenosine receptors on vascular smooth muscle cells of the adjacent afferent arteriole, causing vasoconstriction of the afferent arteriole. Afferent constriction increases renal vascular resistance, reducing glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure back to normal levels and maintaining RBF and GFR constant across the autoregulatory range.

### Question 24

Enumerate physiological factors that shift potassium ions into cells (regulators of plasma K+).

**Correct Answer**: -
**EXP**: 1. Insulin: Stimulates Na+/K+-ATPase pump activity.
2. Beta-2 adrenergic stimulation (e.g., epinephrine, salbutamol): Promotes cellular K+ uptake via cAMP and Na+/K+-ATPase.
3. Aldosterone: Promotes cellular uptake and renal excretion.
4. Alkalosis (decreased extracellular H+): Causes H+ to exit cells in exchange for K+ entering cells via H+/K+ antiporters.

### Question 28

Enumerate four predisposing factors for urinary tract infection (UTI).

**Correct Answer**: -
**EXP**: 1. Female gender: Due to a shorter urethra located anatomically close to the warm, moist perineal and anal regions.
2. Urinary tract obstruction and stasis: Such as renal calculi, benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), or urethral strictures.
3. Instrumentation and catheterization: Indwelling urinary catheters provide a surface for bacterial biofilm formation.
4. Metabolic and immune impairment: Such as diabetes mellitus (glycosuria promotes bacterial proliferation) and immunosuppression.

### Question 32

Explain the clinical and laboratory diagnosis of Hydatid disease (Echinococcus granulosus).

**Correct Answer**: -
**EXP**: 1. Imaging modalities: Abdominal ultrasound and CT scans are the primary diagnostic tools, demonstrating characteristic unilocular or multilocular fluid-filled cysts with daughter cysts, internal hydatid sand, or calcified cyst walls (water-lily sign).
2. Serological testing: Detection of specific anti-Echinococcus antibodies using Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) or Indirect Hemagglutination (IHA).
3. Skin test: Casoni intradermal test demonstrating immediate hypersensitivity reaction (historical).
4. Caution: Diagnostic needle aspiration is strictly contraindicated due to the high risk of anaphylactic shock and secondary cystic dissemination from leakage of hydatid fluid.

### Question 33

Mention the pharmacodynamic principles underlying the clinical use of:
a. Indapamide in hypertension.
b. Thiazide diuretics in Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus (DI).

**Correct Answer**: -
**EXP**: a. Indapamide in hypertension: Indapamide is a thiazide-like sulfonamide diuretic that exerts a dual antihypertensive mechanism: (1) Natriuresis by inhibiting the Na+/Cl- cotransporter in the cortical distal convoluted tubule, lowering plasma and extracellular fluid volume; (2) Direct arteriolar vasodilation by blocking calcium channels in vascular smooth muscle and stimulating vasodilatory prostaglandin (PGI2) synthesis.
b. Thiazides in Diabetes Insipidus: Thiazides paradoxically reduce polyuria in DI by inducing mild natriuresis and extracellular fluid volume depletion. This contraction stimulates compensatory hyper-reabsorption of sodium and water in the proximal convoluted tubule, thereby decreasing the total volume of tubular fluid delivered to the distal nephron and collecting ducts, leading to a marked reduction in daily urine output.

### Question 34

Provide the pharmacological rationale for each of the following clinical precautions:
a. Why fluoroquinolones are not recommended for uncomplicated UTIs in children.
b. Why sulfamethoxazole should be used cautiously in renal impairment.
c. Why diabetic patients must monitor blood glucose closely when starting thiazide or loop diuretics.

**Correct Answer**: -
**EXP**: a. Fluoroquinolones in children: Fluoroquinolones (such as ciprofloxacin) cause damage to developing cartilage in weight-bearing joints (arthropathy and chondrotoxicity) in growing children.
b. Sulfamethoxazole in renal impairment: Sulfamethoxazole and its acetylated metabolites are cleared primarily by the kidneys. Decreased GFR causes drug accumulation, increasing the risk of precipitation in renal tubules (crystalluria, acute renal failure) and severe systemic toxicity.
c. Diuretics and blood glucose in diabetes: Thiazide and loop diuretics cause hypokalemia, which diminishes potassium-dependent insulin secretion by pancreatic beta cells and increases peripheral insulin resistance, leading to clinically significant hyperglycemia.

### Question 35

Mention the structural adaptations to function of Juxtaglomerular (JG) cells.

**Correct Answer**: -
**EXP**: Juxtaglomerular (JG) cells are specialized modified smooth muscle cells located primarily within the tunica media of the afferent glomerular arteriole at the vascular pole of the renal corpuscle. Their adaptations include:
1. Epithelioid transformation: They lose their contractile myofibrils and assume a rounded, secretory epithelioid appearance with abundant rough endoplasmic reticulum and prominent Golgi complexes.
2. Zymogen granules: Cytoplasm is packed with membrane-bound osmiophilic granules containing inactive prorenin and active renin.
3. Intrinsic baroreceptor function: They act as stretch receptors sensitive to changes in transmural perfusion pressure across the afferent arteriolar wall, releasing renin in response to decreased renal arterial pressure.

### Question 36

Microbiological aspects of urinary tract infections:
a. Identify the causative pathogen: A Gram-negative pleomorphic bacillus, highly motile with characteristic swarming motility on blood agar and strong urease positivity.
b. Mention two viral etiologies of urinary tract infections.
c. Enumerate two major risk factors for bacterial UTIs.
d. State two scientific reasons why Chlamydia trachomatis is classified as a bacterium and not a virus.

**Correct Answer**: -
**EXP**: a. Pathogen: Proteus mirabilis (or Proteus vulgaris).
b. Viral causes of UTI: Adenovirus (causes acute hemorrhagic cystitis in children) and BK polyomavirus (causes hemorrhagic cystitis in renal transplant recipients).
c. Risk factors: Female gender/sexual intercourse and indwelling urinary catheterization/urinary outflow obstruction.
d. Reasons Chlamydia is a bacterium: (1) It contains both DNA and RNA simultaneously (viruses possess only one type of nucleic acid); (2) It possesses a true bacterial cell envelope containing peptidoglycan and LPS, reproduces by binary fission, contains bacterial 70S ribosomes, and is susceptible to antibacterial antibiotics (such as azithromycin and doxycycline).
